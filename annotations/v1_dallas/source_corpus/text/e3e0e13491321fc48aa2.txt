ABSTRACT 
  The purpose of this Exploratory Descriptive Qualitative Systematic Narrative Review 
Study is to explore the impact of bullying on the mental health and well-being of Black youth in 
Ontario. This study attempts to disseminate valuable knowledge regarding implications of bullying 
and the need for adequate mental health supports for Black youth in Ontario. These youths represent 
a vulnerable group due to both their race and culture, a phenomenon otherwise known as Double 
Jeopardy (Millar and Brookes, 2021). Data was rigorously gathered using a descriptive qualitative 
systematic tool to explore the mental health presentations and the frequency with which Black 
youth encounter bullying as reflected in peer-reviewed articles. The outcomes of this study have 
the capacity to provide valuable insights for focused interventions, enhancement of supportive 
health systems and producing policy recommendations. The ultimate goal would be to enhance 
mental health care services and promote overall well-being of this marginalized population in 
Ontario.  
 
 
 
Keywords: Bullying, Black Students, Mental Health, Suicide, Ontario.

1. Introduction 
Contemporary Canadian society celebrates diversity as a core value that promotes inclusion, 
kindness, and leadership, which form the pillar of Canada’s national identity (Fleras, 2019). 
Renowned globally for its commitment to fostering a multicultural landscape, Canada places a 
significant emphasis on health promotion, recognizing the significant impact of social determinants 
on individual well-being (Weldon & Hoffman, 2021). By extension, the province of Ontario serves 
as a representative of Canada’s rich cultural diversity, shaped by increased immigration (Abawi, 
2019). However, amidst this celebration of diversity and inclusion, a parallel reality persists – the 
prevalence of bullying, particularly among marginalized groups (Campbell, 2021). Among these 
groups are Black youth, who represent a demographic that has historically faced unique challenges, 
rooted in systemic inequities and social prejudices (Henderson et al., 2019). Understanding the 
impact of bullying on the mental health and well-being of Black youth in Ontario is not only 
important for addressing the immediate consequences but also for advancing a more inclusive and 
equitable society aligned with Canadian values (Cahil, Dadvand, & Gowing, 2021). 
The landscape of contemporary Ontario reflects the outlook of Canada's multicultural 
society, shaped by successive immigration from every corner of the globe, which enriches 
Ontario’s social and cultural environment, fostering a sense of belonging and community (Abawi, 
2019). Nevertheless, within this vibrant progress, the rates of bullying overshadow the well-being 
of many, particularly ethnographically Black youth who struggle with intersecting identities and 
experiences of discrimination (Campbell, 2021). 
Furthermore, historically, Canada has championed diversity and inclusion as fundamental 
principles, acknowledging the contributions of various ethnic, cultural, and racial groups to the 
nation's fabric (Fleras, 2019). The nation’s commitment to kindness and leadership is evident in its 


 
13 
 
efforts to promote social cohesion and ensure equal opportunities for all its citizens (Kagedan, 
2020). However, despite these commendable efforts, bullying remains a pervasive issue, 
undermining the very principles Canada espouses (Campbell, 2021). Considering these 
complexities, this research seeks to undertake a descriptive qualitative systematic narrative review 
to demonstrate the dynamics of bullying among Black youth in Ontario. By exploring the multi-
faceted interplay between bullying, mental health, and well-being within this demographic, this 
study seeks to not only highlight existing challenges but also to inform targeted interventions and 
policies aimed at fostering a more inclusive and supportive environment for all youth in Ontario.  
 
2. Historical Background 
Bullying is the most common form of youth violence that has raised concerns for students, 
parents, teachers and mental health establishments alike, considering its physical, emotional, and 
psychological impacts. Several studies have shown that bullying among children and adolescents 
contribute to severe symptoms of mental health issues, especially self-harm, and suicide ideation 
(Bryson et al., 2021). Considering the growth of the use of the internet among young people, 
bullying is now diverse in contrast to traditional victimization and physical injuries (Modecki et 
al., 2014).  
Gender, racial or ethnic background, age, socioeconomic background, and personality have 
been found to be associated with bullying. Without intervention, bullying can lead to significant 
deterioration of mental health among high school students, which can persist into adulthood 
(Eyuboglu et al., 2021). Despite the recognition of bullying as a challenge in high schools across 
Canada, there are no specific provisions in the Criminal Code that prohibit bullying and its 
exhibition in varying forms like cyberbullying (Public Safety Canada, n.d). However, due to the  


 
14 
 
wide range of this behaviour, bullying that escalates to the level of criminal conduct is subject to 
remedies provided in the Criminal Code provisions. For instance, depending on the activity, 
criminal harassment is catered for by Section 264 of the Criminal Code. Other instances of bullying 
include incitement of hatred (section 319), intimidation (subsection 423(1)), uttering threats 
(section 264.1), and false messages (incriminating libel and slander), indecent or harassing 
telephone calls (section 372) (Government of Canada, n.d.). 
 
2.1 Current Background of the Phenomenon Identified 
Historically, Black communities in Ontario have endured systemic racism and 
discrimination, which manifest in various forms, including unequal access to education, 
employment, and social services (Williams et al., 2022). These structural inequities exacerbate the 
challenges faced by Black youth, rendering them more susceptible to bullying and victimization 
within their schools, neighborhoods, and online spaces. Studies like Xu et al. (2020) have 
consistently shown that Black youth experience higher rates of bullying compared to their peers 
from other racial or ethnic backgrounds, which often takes on multiple forms, including verbal 
harassment, physical aggression, cyberbullying, and microaggressions, all of which can have 
lasting impacts on their mental health and well-being.  
The intersectionality of race, gender, and socio-economic status further exacerbates the 
vulnerability of Black youth to bullying. For instance, Black girls may experience unique forms of 
bullying that intersect with gendered stereotypes and expectations, while those from low-income 
backgrounds may encounter additional challenges in accessing support services or resources to 
address bullying (Iruka & Hawkins, 2022). Consequently, the rise and increased use of social media 
and digital technologies has introduced new avenues for bullying and harassment, with Black youth 


 
15 
 
often targeted for their race or ethnicity in online spaces. In response, there are current efforts to 
seek action against bullying because it is amplifying the historical injustices of minorities like Black 
community in Canada. Organizations such as Parents of Black Children (PoBC) work toward 
common goals of ensuring sustainable, systemic and transformational change in education systems 
in Ontario. In their analysis of anti-Black racism (PoBC, n.d), PoBC (2022), congruent arguments 
were presented to the Ontario Human Rights Commission to advocate for Black students regarding 
anti-oppressive practices and anti-Black racism in the schools.  
A 2021 survey conducted by the Angus Reid Institute in collaboration with the University 
of British Columbia sheds light on the prevalence of racial and ethnic-based bullying among 
Canadian youth, particularly in school environments. The survey, which gathered responses from 
872 Canadian youth aged 12 to 17, revealed concerning statistics regarding the experiences of racial 
discrimination and exclusion within educational settings (Angus Reid Institute and the University 
of British Columbia, 2021). In their survey findings, Angus Reid Institute found that a significant 
portion of Canadian youth, approximately 58%, reported witnessing instances where children were 
insulted, bullied, or excluded due to their race or ethnicity while at school. Moreover, 14% of the 
respondents disclosed that they themselves had experienced such mistreatment firsthand.  
Notably, the study highlighted disparities in the prevalence of personal abuse based on race, 
with visible minority children being three times more likely and Indigenous children twice as likely 
as white children to report facing such incidents. This study developed through collaboration 
between the Angus Reid Institute and UBC, builds upon previous research efforts aimed at 
understanding and addressing issues of racism and discrimination in Canada. The insights garnered 
from the survey contribute to ongoing discussions and initiatives focused on combating anti-Asian 
racism and other forms of discrimination within Canadian society (Fletcher, 2021).

Sexual and gender differences are also significant considerations in bullying and 
victimization. According to Prokopenko and Hango (2022), seventy percent of adolescents between 
the ages of 15 and 17 acknowledged encountering various forms of bullying within the past year. 
Notably, sexually and gender diverse youth within the same age bracket faced the highest risk, with 
77% reporting experiencing at least one of the ten types of bullying behaviors assessed in the 
survey. In comparison, 69% of cisgender youth, defined as those exclusively attracted to a different 
gender, reported similar experiences (Prokopenko & Hango, 2022). 
2.2 Youth Development Versus The Impact of Bullying Onto Youth 
Bullying takes a toll on the mental and emotional well-being of youth, with significant 
implications for brain development, self-esteem, and overall growth. Research indicates that 
repeated exposure to bullying behaviors can adversely impact the developing brain, disrupting 
neural pathways and physiological processes critical for cognitive and emotional functioning. For 
instance, in their study, D’Urso, Symonds, and Pace (2021) found that experiencing bullying during 
early adolescence was primarily associated with several factors, including having a smaller social 
circle, greater family financial strain, and residing in neighborhoods characterized by increased 
levels of disorder.  
During adolescence, a period characterized by rapid brain development and neural 
plasticity, experiences of bullying can leave lasting imprints on the brain’s structure and function 
(Gale, 2016). Chronic stress resulting from bullying can trigger dysregulation of the hypothalamic-
pituitary-adrenal (HPA) axis, leading to alterations in stress hormone levels, such as cortisol, which 
in turn can impair neurodevelopmental processes. Moreover, the pre-frontal cortex may be 
particularly vulnerable to the effects of bullying. Fine and Hayden (2022) explain that the prefrontal 
cortex is responsible for executive cognitive functions like impulse control, decision-making, and 


 
17 
 
emotional regulation. Therefore, prolonged exposure to stressors associated with bullying can 
impede the maturation of this brain region, compromising an individual’s ability to navigate social 
interactions and regulate emotions effectively (Fine & Hayden, 2022). 
Significantly, bullying inflicts profound psychological harm, eroding self-esteem and 
sense of self-worth because victims of bullying often internalize negative perceptions of 
themselves, attributing blame and inadequacy to their own identities (Fernanda & Carla, 2023). If 
such a negative self-concept persists into adulthood, it hinders personal growth, interpersonal 
relationships, and overall well-being. On the other hand, the cumulative effects of bullying extend 
beyond immediate psychological distress, contributing to long-term mental health issues from 
depression, anxiety, to post-traumatic stress disorder. The toxic stress induced by bullying can 
alter neurobiological processes implicated in mood regulation and emotional processing, 
predisposing individuals to chronic mental health conditions later in life (Palamarchuk & 
Vaillancourt, 2022).  
2.3 Bitter Facts 
Bullying has been consistently linked to adverse mental health outcomes such as 
depression, anxiety, and even suicide (Wolke & Lereya, 2015). It is a very common form of youth 
violence in Canada and the US, and the interventions available have not managed to curb its growth. 
An accumulated research body found that 10-30% of adolescents report being bullying victims, 
while 5-13% admitted to being bullying perpetrators in school (Hong et al., 2022). More often than 
not, most researchers have generally focused on the effect of bullying on the whole population of 
young people with less inquiry into specific defining characteristics like race and ethnicity. 
Sapouna, De Amicis, and Vezzali (2023) support this latter argument by explaining that despite a 
growing body of research demonstrating that large numbers of minorities experience racist bullying 


 
18 
 
with regard to their race, ethnic and religious backgrounds, racist bullying only gained attention 
recently but as a specific bullying sub-type. In an exploration of racial and ethnic differences in 
bullying, Xu et al. (2020) found that African Americans were more likely to experience frequent 
verbal, physical and sexual victimization compared to their white and Latino counterparts. 
Therefore, by using Black high school students as participants, this research aided in understanding 
how bullying impacts Black students, as the mental health consequences might vary based on 
cultural and contextual factors. 
   
Sengupta (2019) reports the case of E.H., whose identity was concealed to protect him who 
was anxious and experienced nightmares because of the bullying he experienced at school. He says 
that he is always on guard because the racist taunting and threats about harming him scare him. 
From the n-word calling to escalated physical attacks, E.H. could only stay alert even alone at 
home. Sengupta (2019) reports that even after a video surfaced of him getting beaten, the 
perpetrators were only suspended, including him because they didn’t listen to him. This seems that 
the perpetrators often get away with school violence while the victim is victimized even further. 
For instance, E.H. says that he had five teachers following him at school, which made him look 
bad. 
On the other hand, ignoring the situation seems to indicate supporting the perpetrators. For 
instance, in Angus Reid Institute and University of British Columbia’s (2021) study, they explain 
that children who have encountered or observed racism within their school environment commonly 
report that teachers typically attempt to discourage such behavior and address it by speaking to the 
perpetrators. About 30% indicate that racist actions often lead to disciplinary measures, including 
punishment, suspension, or detention for the individuals involved. However, a quarter of the 


 
19 
 
children surveyed express that teachers either overlook racist behavior or are unaware of its 
occurrence. 
In another part of their study, Angus Reid Institute and UBC (2021) found that children 
from visible minority backgrounds are at a higher risk of encountering racism within the school 
environment. On the other hand, White students (47%) and Indigenous students (44%) are more 
inclined to report the absence of such behaviors at their schools compared to visible minority 
students (30%) (Angus Reid Institute and University of British Columbia, 2021). However, 
Indigenous students (18%) and visible minority students (26%) are respectively twice and three 
times as likely as those who do not identify as such (8%) to report experiencing race-based bullying, 
exclusion, or insults (Angus Reid Institute and University of British Columbia, 2021).  
Apart from the individual effects, bullying affects educational outcomes, i.e., it can impact 
students’ educational attainment, and Black students already face educational disparities. In their 
study, Kibriya, Xu and Zhang (2017) found that bullying victims score at least 0.22 standard 
deviation, which is lower than their peers in a standardized Math examination. Therefore, this 
research will explain the connections between bullying, mental health, and academic success and 
the recommended interventions. Still, there is the need for prevention and intervention and the role 
various persons can play in managing bullying, may it be policy formulation or change in schools' 
approach to bullying for complete eradication. In general, there are significant gaps in bullying as 
a concept that necessitate further exploration in regions with cultural diversity, like Ontario. 
Young adults subjected to victimization by bullies are likely to face psychological trauma 
risks, including loneliness, depression, anxiety and suicide ideation. On the other hand, bullying 
also affects bullies with mental health disorders. In their study, Källmén and Hallgren (2021) found 
that the bullied reported more emotional problems, while the bullies reported conduct disorders. 


 
20 
 
Again, the authors support that school may be an escape for mentally troubled children to express 
their stress on others. On the other hand, Zhang et al. (2021) associate bullying with social ability 
and development because it causes decreased self-confidence and self-esteem. Again, victimization 
can cause antisocial behaviour in adulthood, hence an increased risk for society as some of the 
personalities will resort to crime in the future. 
 
3. Study Rationale 
While a wealth of studies has been done to link the impact of bullying onto Black youth, 
from a pragmatic standpoint, a lot of efforts are still needed to tackle bullying in Ontario at the 
present time. This study is necessitated to provide a current picture of most recent findings found 
in literature regarding prevalence of bullying onto Black youth, as well as strong recommendations 
to be deducted for wide implementation.  
It could be possible that this research study highlights the intersectionality that may be 
ignored as a result of the attention directed towards racism with movements like Black Lives 
Matter. In other terms, the focus on systemic discrimination may act as a blanket to divert attention 
away from Black high school student bullying resulting from sexuality and gender. Morales, 
Grineski, and Collins (2019) recommend that research consider the intersectionality framework 
and study gender and race together as opposed to separate mechanisms of power because of the 
intersectionality that arises from oppression, exclusion, and marginalization. Therefore, in light of 
the intersectionality, this research will explain how Black high school students may also face 
intersecting forms of discrimination and bullying, including racial, gender-based, or sexual 
orientation-related harassment and their implications on their mental health.

4. Aim 
The primary aim of this research project is to obtain a deep and current understanding of 
the adverse effects of bullying on to overall health of youth with a particular emphasis on to their 
mental health status.  
Through the use of An Exploratory Descriptive Qualitative Systematic Narrative Review

5.3 Critique of Literature Review 
The literature review presented herein is a significant overview of the inquiry into the 
impact of bullying on the mental health and well-being of Black youth in Ontario.  
While each study has unique results, the common themes indicate that bullying has negative 
impacts on the youths’ mental health. Some of these studies show trends in mental health in the 
described population, while some have provided notable points that contributed to the research.  
The methodologies employed in these studies range from Descriptive Studies (surveys, 
questionnaires) and Qualitative Studies (interviews, focus groups), Systematic Reviews and Meta-
Analyses, which prove validity. Other resources (grey literature) have used policy reviews surveys, 
statistical reports, annual evaluation reports and case studies.  
The limitations in the grey literature include: internal validity and reliability issues, 
estimation problems, opinion-based and being politicized. On the other hand, the literature sources 
from scholarly and peer-reviewed materials have a challenge that participants are not randomly 
sampled because of the nature of the issue under investigation; and due to sampling limitations, 
there is limited generalizability to the rest of the population, as reflected on contemporary literature 
as well (Prabhu, 2020).  
With the consideration to literature available in regards to bullying in Ontario, the 
phenomenon is contributing to poor health outcomes among young people (deLara, 2019). In 
Canada, suicide constitutes 24% of all fatalities among individuals aged 15-24 (Agincourt 
Community Services Association and Toronto Youth Equity Strategy, n.d.). On the other hand, the 
stress involved in peer victimization is a long-standing issue that has gained significant recognition. 
According to Goemans, Viding, and McCrory (2023), there is a distinction between optimum 


 
26 
 
psychological functioning, like being sad, low-confidence, and distressing states like anxiety and 
depression and psychological states reflecting what is less than the former. 
In 2014, within the youth population of Toronto, 12% of students in grades 7 to 12 disclosed 
having seriously contemplated suicide within the previous year (City of Toronto, n.d.). A higher 
percentage of female students (18%) reported such contemplation compared to male students (6%) 
(City of Toronto, n.d.). Ontario is equally affected as Graham (2020) investigated student mental 
health and implicated students’ voices as the central issue in school-based effects of mental health. 
Their findings showed that: culture of silence, influenced by gender norms and stigma, significantly 
affected the mental health of the students in their high school (1), students highlighted several 
educational practices, both supportive and unsupportive, as having an impact (2), and peer 
relationships, particularly instances of bullying and involvement in peer-support groups, were 
identified as both supportive and unsupportive factors affecting the students’ mental health (3). 
Bullying can also result in enduring damage to self-esteem. Moreover, school bullying can 
significantly affect the mental well-being of teenagers. In their study, Nazir & Nesheen (2015) 
explain that experiencing bullying from an early age can have lasting effects that persist into 
adolescence. Until it is addressed, bullying affects school functioning and adjustment. As a matter 
of fact, when two groups of adolescents are involved in a study about bullying, there are significant 
outcomes in their mental health. In Aslan, Allen, and Tanhan’s (2021) study, adolescents in both 
victim and perpetrator groups exhibited notably lower levels of positive psychological outlook, 
reduced subjective well-being, and increased emotional and behavioural challenges in comparison 
to their peers who were not involved in such. 
A strong link exists between mental health and bullying (Källmén and Hallgren, 2021). 
Källmén and Hallgren (2021) say the latter is shown to be persisting to late adolescence for young 


 
27 
 
people bullied in school, leading to mental health problems into early adulthood. Significantly, 
intersectionality affects the health outcomes for victims of bullying. Using race as part of the 
intersectional class with youth as the population and bullying as the determining factor, students in 
the intersectional class are more likely to have higher odds of suicide ideation (Garnett et al., 2014).      
The intersectionality can also extend to social status, given Black communities are often 
among the marginalized communities. However, bullying can turn around for individuals with a 
higher social status, i.e., if a youth from a higher social status is a perpetrator of bullying at school 
physically, they may experience online bullying by youths who belong to a less social status (Park 
et al., 2022). 
Bullying is a product of the victimization process with various constructs. Suicidality 
increases with an increase in victimization frequency as belief-in others decreases (Fullchange & 
Furlong, 2016). Shireen et al. (2014) found that suicide ideation and suicide rates among young 
people have been linked to bullying and victimization because the victims are more likely to 
consider suicide than non-victims as much as 2 to 9 times more. While not extensively researched, 
harsh parenting and physical punishment are also among risky behaviours that can trigger mental 
health challenges. WHO (2022) explains that they are also forms of bullying, which is among the 
leading risks for mental health conditions.  
This section entailed reviewed what other studies have discussed about bullying. The results 
show that the available literature is in consistent agreement with the findings that bullying can lead 
to increased levels of stress, anxiety, and depression among victims, as well as feelings of isolation 
and low self-esteem. For instance, (Mayes et al., 2014) has explained that bullying can contribute 
to the development of mental health disorders and even suicidal ideation or attempts. Strøm et al. 
(2018) have also stated that although uncommon, the effects of bullying extend beyond the victims, 


 
28 
 
as perpetrators may also experience negative psychological outcomes such as guilt, shame, and 
increased aggression. Nevertheless, the inquiry into the impacts of bullying on the mental health of 
the perpetrators is insufficiently explored.

8. Study Design/Methodology 
8.1 Study Design 
The study design utilized a Descriptive Qualitative Systematic Narrative Review to describe 
and characterize the prevalence, number, and contributing causes of mental health issues in Black 
Youth in Ontario.  Descriptive qualitative designs are most preferred because they allow for an 
exploration of the lived experiences, perceptions, and contextual factors that influence mental 
health within this specific population, in this context (Hunter, McCallum, & Howes, 2019).  
 
8.2 Qualitative Arm Type 
Within the umbrella of a Descriptive Qualitative Study Design, A Systematic Narrative 
study type was used in this study. The strength of utilizing the arm type is that, by focusing on 
narratives, we can capture the holistic perspectives of participants and findings (Hunter, McCallum, 
& Howes, 2019), enabling a comprehensive understanding of the multifaceted nature of mental 
health challenges faced by Black youth in Ontario, as reflected on the literature identified for 
review. Additionally, it allows for the contextualization of research findings within the broader 
social, cultural, and environmental context (Hunter, McCallum, & Howes, 2019) because the 
author can then present the data in a narrative format to convey the complex interplay of factors 


 
30 
 
associated with youth mental health. 
The Systematic Review component ensures the examination of existing literature where the 
synthesizing of the findings from multiple studies, helps in identifying common themes, trends, 
and gaps in knowledge (Xiao & Watson, 2019). This comprehensive approach enhances the 
validity and reliability of the study’s findings, providing a solid foundation for informing 
interventions and policies. While the design allows for a comprehensive synthesis of existing 
literature, it is limited by the availability and quality of published studies.  
 
8.3 Target Group 
The target is studies with the racial element, i.e., Black youth, and location (Ontario). The 
ultimate goal is to address the impact of bullying onto Black youth, or those who are at risk of 
bullying and the subsequent mental health challenges. 
 
8.4 Inclusion and Exclusion Criteria 
To be eligible for this descriptive study, the studies used were to consider the race aspect in 
bullying, specifically Black youth. Studies addressed mental health issues among Black youth, 
including but not limited to depression, anxiety, stress, trauma, substance abuse, self-harm, suicidal 
ideation, and any other relevant mental health concerns on how they relate to bullying. Both 
qualitative and quantitative research studies were eligible.  
The exclusion criteria included studies that do not directly address mental health issues 
among Black youth. At the same time, studies focusing on unrelated topics were excluded. For 
example, studies solely focused on physical health were cut out.  
 


 
31 
 
8.5 Sampling Method  
The sampling method selected was theoretical sampling (Conlon et al., 2020). The strength 
of using theoretical sampling is that it leads to in-depth exploration of key concepts and 
relationships within a phenomenon. However, the iterative nature of the method can lead to 
complex data analysis processes (Vasileiou et al., 2018). 
The selection process of the articles was carried out independently based on the inclusion 
and exclusion criterion provided.  Studies were identified via databases. The total number of studies 
were 427 (n=427): PubMed (n=184), CINAHL (n=43), SCOPUS (n=57), Web of Science (n=54), 
MedLine (n=26), ScienceDirect (n=54), PsychInfo (n=9). Duplicate copies (n=94) were removed 
before they were screened, while those excluded at this stage based on their title and inclusion vs 
exclusion criteria were 155.  111 articles were excluded for not detailing a systematic review as 
shown in the flow chart. The remainder were assessed for eligibility and (n=9) were excluded at 
this stage for insufficient details, risk for bias, failure to address or review any of the research 
questions. The final number of articles used for this study were 58.  
 
8.6 Safest Sample Size 
After considering all processes from considering the content, full-text, database, and 
language as explained in the sampling method, only 12 studies were considered the safest. This 
was based on the minimum of 10 articles aligning with the topic of study. The selection of the 
studies considered the ROBIS tool for reviews because it compares overall risk of bias across 
selected reviews and allow for comparison and contrast of the studies’ findings (Whiting et al., 
2016).

8.7 The Recruitment Strategy  
A comprehensive search strategy with strict consideration to the inclusion and exclusion 
criteria was considered. The search was conducted independently by the researcher, therefore 
eliminating a possibility of disagreements during the selection process. All studies that are 
considered as systematic reviews, involved bullying, discussed mental health among young people, 
and those published in English were recruited for the study. These studies were evaluated so as to 
be aligned with my sample sizing method to ensure the study achieves anticipated saturation 
(Vasileiou et al., 2018).   
 
8.8 Duration of Data Collection  
The process of collecting articles that meet the inclusion criteria lasted between January to 
March 2024.

recommended databases, all studies were subjected to the complete process. The primary research 
used a range of techniques to mitigate bias in conducting this kind of research as: keeping organized 
notes, conducting a preliminary categorization of themes identified (Mackieson, Shlonsky, & 
Connoly, 2019).

9 studies addressed the prevalence of bullying across racial lines. 7 reviews discussed the

cultural backgrounds, which may contribute to increased tendencies toward depression and 
substance abuse among those targeted for ethnic-based harassment. 
This study section has reported that instances of derogatory name-calling and racially 
charged insults emerged as the prevailing forms of racist bullying, consistently highlighted across 
all focus groups and recognized as a significant issue by school administration, educators, and 
students.

9.1.4 Mitigating Factors 
Research Question: What are the mitigating factors of the effect of bullying unto Black 
youth? 
One study included the mitigation strategies (Colizzi, Lasalvia, & Ruggeri, 2020). Colizzi, 
Lasalvia, and Ruggeri (2020) contend that research indicates that expecting mental health 
professionals solely to manage promotion and prevention efforts in mental health would be 
impractical. Instead, Colizzi, Lasalvia, & Ruggeri (2020) explain there is a growing consensus that 
integrated and multidisciplinary approaches are necessary to expand intervention options and 
mitigate the likelihood of unfavorable long-term outcomes. This approach may also yield 
advantages in terms of reducing healthcare system expenditures (Colizzi, Lasalvia, & Ruggeri, 
2020). Nonetheless, Colizzi, Lasalvia, and Ruggeri (2020) say mental health professionals bear the 
scientific, ethical, and moral obligation to guide the various social, political, and healthcare entities 
engaged in addressing mental health needs during adolescence. 
9.2 Discussion of Findings 
The studies above have shown that bullying, particularly when it is racially motivated or 
targeted, can have profound and lasting effects on the mental health and overall well-being of 
youths and most likely extend into adulthood. Research has shown that racialized youth are 
disproportionately targeted for bullying based on their race or ethnicity, experiencing higher rates 
of name-calling, racial slurs, and other forms of racially motivated harassment compared to their 
peers. This persistent exposure to racism and discrimination can lead to feelings of isolation, low 
self-esteem, and psychological distress among them. 
One significant aspect of the impact of bullying on mental health is its prevalence. Studies 


 
42 
 
like Xi et al. (2020) indicate that a substantial proportion of racialized youth report experiencing 
bullying at some point during their school years. This prevalence highlights the urgent need for 
effective prevention and intervention strategies to address the issue. 
 
9.3 Data Analysis Method 
This study systematically extracted relevant qualitative data from included studies 
identified through the systematic review process, analyzed the extracted qualitative data using 
thematic analysis techniques, and synthesized the extracted data into a coherent narrative that 
provides a rich description.  
 
9.4 Limitations of this Study 
This study has the following limitations:  
Initially, the primary research papers eliminated several reviews that did not primarily target 
youth, were published before the last decade, lacked specification of systematic review 
methodology, or were published in languages other than English. It was recognized that this 
approach may lead to the exclusion of certain systematic reviews and their corresponding findings.  
Additionally, since the primary researcher exclusively considered peer-reviewed journal 
publications, there exists a potential for publication bias, where studies presenting positive results 
are more inclined to be published. Lastly, there was limited literature focusing on Ontario, which 
led to generalization of results from other locations, with implications for adoption in Ontario as 
well.

9.5 Possible Implications of Study  
This study has implications for mental health and addiction, especially on health promotion 
and prevention. Therefore, the findings can inform the development of evidence-based policies and 
interventions aimed at addressing bullying and promoting mental health among Black youth in 
Ontario, including implementing anti-bullying programs, enhancing cultural sensitivity and 
diversity training for educators and school staff.  
At the same time, it raises awareness about the unique experiences and vulnerabilities of 
Black youth with regards to bullying and mental health to facilitate advocacy efforts in prioritizing 
the needs of Black youth in anti-bullying initiatives and mental health services across Ontario.
