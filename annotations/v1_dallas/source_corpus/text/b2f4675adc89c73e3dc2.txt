Health insurance premiums are influenced by several factors, depending on the policy and the individual. With the rising cost of medical care, especially after the COVID-19 pandemic, having a reliable health insurance cover has become more important than ever in India.
While it serves as a financial safety net, understanding the cost of health insurance premiums is also a vital factor you should not overlook.
Continue reading to explore the 11 key factors that play a significant role in affecting health insurance premiums.
A health insurance premium factor is a variable used by the insurer to evaluate risk and determine the policy cost. These factors include personal attributes such as age, health status, and your geographical location. These help calculate the likelihood of a claim. Higher risk factors may result in higher premiums.

Several factors, such as your lifestyle habits, the type of plan you choose, and your family's medical history, affect your health insurance premium.
Let’s explore 11 key factors in more detail:
1. Age: Premiums generally increase with age, as older people are considered to be at higher risk of illness.
2. Type of Plan You Choose: Your medical insurance premium will vary depending on the type of plan you choose. A family floater plan will cost less compared to an individual health insurance policy.
3. Lifestyle Habits: Smoking, consuming alcohol, or leading a sedentary lifestyle can increase premium costs. These habits increase the chances of chronic conditions and other health complications.
4. Obesity or Body Mass Index: A higher BMI indicates a greater risk of health issues such as diabetes and hypertension. This further increases health insurance premiums.
5. Family Medical History: A history of critical illnesses, such as cancer or heart disease, in the family increases health insurance premiums.
6. Pre-Existing Medical Condition: The amount of your health insurance premium increases depending on pre-existing medical conditions that you have, along with their severity.
7. Sum Insured: A higher sum insured increases the health insurance premium, as insurers bear greater financial risk due to higher coverage benefits.
8. Geographical Location: Costs can vary by location. Metro cities like Mumbai, Delhi, or Bengaluru typically have higher treatment costs, which can lead to higher premiums.
9. The Nature of Your Work: If your job involves hazardous or high-stress work, it will also factor into the premium. Those with safer jobs, like office work, will pay lower premiums.
10. Policy Tenure or Duration: Long-term policies sometimes provide lower locked-in premiums and may also offer discounts from insurers.
11. Additional Coverage or Riders: If you opt for additional coverage, such as maternity, international, or hospital cash allowance, your medical insurance premium will increase.

Rising healthcare costs, insurance market competition, and the regulatory environment affect health insurance premiums.
Here are the key influential factors:
1. Insurance Market Competition: In a highly competitive market, insurers may offer lower premiums to attract customers.
2. Rising Healthcare Costs: Another significant factor in increased premiums is rising healthcare costs. The insurers try to adjust premiums to ensure they can cover medical claims.
3. Reinsurance Costs: If insurance companies spend more on reinsurance to avoid high-cost claims, they often raise premiums to offset the costs.
4. Economic Growth: As per the National Library of Medicine, there is a direct correlation between health insurance and economic growth. In other words, the national economic health directly influences the cost structure of insurance products.

Now that you know what the key factors are that affect your health insurance premium costs, choosing the right plan is also essential. Evaluate the coverage options, provider network, and out-of-pocket expenses before selecting a health insurance plan.
Here are the steps that will help you choose the right plan.
First, evaluate the coverage options. Assess the benefit scopes and specific services covered by the insurance company. Additionally, ensure the plan aligns with your medical needs.
Ensure that the plan’s network of hospitals includes your preferred hospitals, doctors, and specialists. With a broader network of hospitals, you get the flexibility in choosing providers.
Examine deductibles along with copayments to calculate your potential out-of-pocket expenses. By examining this, you can balance your premium costs with your ability to manage spending.
Lastly, you need to assess the plan's flexibility. This refers to how the plan manages out-of-pocket network care and out-of-pocket expenses. Moreover, flexible plans allow smoother access to specialised services.
