DETERMINANTS OF CAREER CHOICE AMONG FEMALE UNDERGRADUATES IN OBAFEMI AWOLOWO UNIVERSITY, NIGERIA

Bukola Oyebanji, Kolawole Aliyu,

DOI https://doi.org/10.5604/01.3001.0014.6880
GICID 01.3001.0014.6880
Keywords Career Choice, Undergraduates, Gender, Societal Expectations
Date of publication 2020/12/31
Issue IJONESS 2020;12(2):33-44

Abstract

Why females choose a career path is a stimulating question among social educators. Consequently, this study investigated the role of some intrinsic, extrinsic, and interpersonal variables in shaping the choice of career of female undergraduates. An Online Career Choice Survey questionnaire was administered to 1440 first to fifth-year female undergraduates randomly selected from Obafemi Awolowo University. Data obtained were subjected to ordinal logistic regression and descriptive analysis. Two intrinsic factors; prestige and personal interest were found to play a huge role in the choice of career. Among extrinsic factors, females were more likely to choose a career path that offers career advancement through training. Gender stereotypes and societal expectations as pro-jected by the media was a key interpersonal factor that could shape present-day career paths for young women. Taken together, these results bring forth the need for stakeholders in the educational system (teachers, counselors, principals) to provide career and professional development opportu-nities periodically to enable female students to make informed career decisions. Finally, given the huge influence of media in shaping future aspirations, there is a dire need for the media to portray women in traditionally male-dominated careers. Statement of the problem in general outlook and its connection with important scientific and practical tasks.

Choosing a career is an important road curve that must be negotiated by every individual in the quest to fulfilling their dreams. Career choice is often a complex decision for most undergraduates since it might serve as a key determinant of the kind of profession that they pursue in life. Many undergraduates, especially females, often are faced with uncertainty and stress as they make career choices. Many of them do not research adequately on the career they intend to choose, nor do they receive adequate guidance from school educators/counselors. Many female undergraduates go into unsuitable careers due to ignorance, inexperience, peer pressure, advice from friends, parents, and teachers, or as a result of prestige attached to certain jobs without adequate guidance and career counseling. According to Kerka (2003), career choice is influenced by multiple factors and determinants including personality, interest, self-concept, cultural identity, globalization, socialization, role model, social support, and available information and financial resources. Hewitt (2010) posits that factors influencing career choice can be either intrinsic, extrinsic, or both. Hewitt further states that most people are influenced by careers that their parents' favor, others follow careers that their educational choices have opened for them, some choose to follow their passion regardless of how much or little it will make them while others choose the careers that give high income. The choice of a career is routinely influenced by various internal and external factors such as ignorance, gender role, personal interest, parental/relative influences, advice from friends, religion, background, family values, and societal perception accorded by a profession (Issa and Nwalo, 2008). Career development, for most people, is a lifelong process engaging the world of work through choosing among available employment opportunities (Bandura et al., 2001). Globally, researchers have investigated factors influencing students' career choices in various fields of specialization. When compared with men, more women have high school diplomas, are enrolled in college, and receive bachelors' degrees (Ryan and Bauman, 2015). As closing the gender gap remains a top priority for the government, access to education is no longer an issue especially for younger women. However, despite the availability of educational and career options, more than 50 percent of young undergraduate women today are choosing female-dominated careers that are lower in status, prestige, and compensation (Stamarski and Son, 2015). For example, In Nigeria, despite women constituting the major segment of the population, they account for only 24.2% of the labor force (NBS, 2012). There are several studies on factors influencing career choice among students in Nigeria. For example, Odia and Ogiedu (2013) studied the factors affecting the studies of specific courses in Nigeria; while Pitan and Olugbenga (2014) studied the factors affecting student choice of courses. Yet, there are no studies, at least to the best of our knowledge, on the determinants of career choices among females’ undergraduates generally. We argue that one way to estimate this would be to survey a population of female undergraduates and ask questions to help understand the drivers of their current course of study and potential career paths. In other words, the objectives of this study were two folds. First, to investigate the drivers of course of study among female undergraduates in Nigeria, and second, to explore the systemic determinants of career choices among female undergraduate students’ findings.

Analysis of research where the solution of the problem was initiated.

Literature review

Women's occupational choice is influenced by many factors, including intrinsic, extrinsic, and interpersonal factors. Taylor and Buku (2006) stated that abilities, needs, and interests, stereotype/prestige, values, school/educational attainment, family/parental values, placement, and aspiration as career choice determinants. Trauth, Quesenberry & Huamg (2009) researched a multicultural analysis of factors influencing career choice for women in the information technology (IT) workforce. This article presented an analysis of cultural/actors influencing the career choices of women in the IT workforce. They employed the theory of the individual difference of gender and IT as a theoretical lens to analyze a qualitative data set of interviews with 200 women in different countries. The themes that emerged from this analysis speak to the influence of cultural attitudes about maternity, childcare, parental care, and working outside the home on a woman’s choice of an IT career. Also, several socio-cultural factors served to add further variation to gender cultural influences; gender career norms, social class; economic opportunity, and gender stereotypes about aptitude. These results lend further empirical support to the emergent individual differences theory of gender and IT endeavors to theorize within gender variation concerning issues related to gender and IT. They also point to areas where educational and workplace interventions can be enacted. This work was like others as far as purpose is concerned; however, it focused just on women in the Information Technology workforce. Borchert (2002) researched on career choice factors of high school students. It aimed at finding out the level of influence of personality, environment, and opportunities in making a career choice. The purpose of the study was to identify the most important factor within these three factors that high school senior students utilized in deciding upon career choices. The findings revealed personality factors as the most important in choosing a career. Environmental factors were not significant in making their choice, though they did not show outright disregard for them. Adjin (2004) researched career choice in a senior secondary school; a case study of secondary technical schools in the Volta region of Ghana and utilized descriptive research design. He focused on factors that influence career choice, proficiency of counselors in handling students’ vocational problems in making a career choice, and ways of improving career/vocational education. He made use of stratified sampling with a sample population of 200. The results of the study show that interest was the motivating factor that influenced career choice. Quinter and Edwards (2011) researched factors influencing students' career choices among secondary school students in Kenya. The purpose of this study was to examine factors influencing career choice among four secondary school students in Kenya. The findings of this study indicated that the availability of advancement opportunities and learning experiences are the most influential factors affecting career choices among students. While males reported learning experiences and career flexibility as the most influential factors, females, however, reported the availability of advancement opportunities and the opportunity to apply skills as the most influential factors. However, no variance was reported for persons influencing career choice by gender. Studies have separately examined the influences of each parent on the career choices of their sons or daughters and have found that mothers tend to have more influence on the career decisions/aspirations of their children than fathers. Muthukrishna and Sokoya (2008) found the mother as the most influential person adolescents talk to concerning their career choice. Mothers were cited as particularly influential because they provide the support that eases children's apprehensions about their careers. Bojuwoye and Mbanjwa (2006) reported that parents had a significant influence on career decisions, but mothers were most influential. This implies that mothers play a major role in the career choice of their children. Some of the variables that influence students' occupational goals include the family, level of parental education, school peers, personality, and socioeconomic status (Crockett and Bingham 2000). Mau and Bikos (2000) suggested that both parent education and income influence career aspirations. Some studies suggest that children are influenced in their career choice by socio-demographic factors and these factors include family, school, and peers (Kniveton 2004; Salami 2006). Salami (2006) found that family involvement is the most significant predictor of career choice in gender dominated occupations. Similarly, Kniveton (2004) found that the family provides information and guidance directly or indirectly and influences young people's career choices. For example, the parent offers appropriate support for certain occupational choices that tend to follow their own (Small and McClean 2002). Garrahy (2001) noted that schools are social institutions that reinforce gender-appropriate behavior, interest, and occupations. Such constructs including curricular subjects, quality of teaching, student participation in school activities, school practices, and policies and learning materials for the student were found to impact career choice among learners. In his study, Spade (2001) found that gender difference in the learners' experiences starts at pre-school and continues throughout their educational careers, Teachers like parents are viewed as key players in the career paths that young people eventually pursue especially females (Barnett 2007). Denga (2004) found that sex-role stereotypes exist among boys and girls in primary schools as they aspire to the traditional occupation. This implies that parents' and teachers' beliefs influence their children's self-perceptions of ability and consequently career choice. Studies show that some teachers encourage students to take certain subject options that are congruent with aptitudes and abilities that they identify (Falaye and Adams 2008).Bojuwoye and Mbanjwa (2006) found that career choice of tertiary students from previously disadvantaged schools is negatively impacted by lack of finance, lack of career information, poor academic performance, and unsatisfactory career counseling services. Another study by Maree and Beck (2004) indicates that disadvantaged communities, schools with career counseling programs were underutilizing the facility which was also viewed as too expensive. Adolescents are easily influenced by peers because they rely on their friends to provide validation of the choices that they make including career decisions. On the contrary, Bojuwoye and Mbanjwa (2006) found that peers were reported not to be marginally influential in career decision making among university students. Intrinsic Factors Influencing Career Choice: Intrinsic factors relate to interests and personality factors. In a broader sense, they are basic and essential features that form part of an individual. Most people would like to do interesting work. Interest is of course, very personal and two people may be interested in the same activity for different reasons. Values and aspirations are also considered important since the importance or usefulness one attaches to his job and individual's desire or ambition to achieve inspires him to excel in his job. Value systems do change over time so what is right for you now may not be appropriate in 5 or 10 years. Values do become more important as we get older. Examples of values autonomy (making your own decisions, independence of action) authority, (having influence over others), variety (change and diversity in tasks, places, people), service (helping or caring for others), economic reward (high salary, property), prestige (having achievements recognized), social interaction (having pleasant, friendly contacts), creativity (being original, developing new concepts). Temperament and personality factors were identified as most important in choosing a career among high school students (Borchert, 2002). Also, the study of Annan (2006) revealed that personality type was the most influential factor that influenced career choice among senior secondary students. Career choices motivated by intrinsic factors tend to be intellectually stimulating. A choice is intrinsically motivated if one is free in making a career decision based on innate tendency rather than the environment or interpersonal factor. Extrinsic Factors Influencing Career Choice: Extrinsic factors include the availability of jobs and how well an occupation pays or brings benefits. They also include those essential features as a result of the individual's associations. People choose jobs that are easy to access. For instance, in a community where the people are predominantly farmers or fishermen, some people may choose to become farmers or fishermen because that is what is available. To some, because of a lack of access to other jobs that they cherish, they go in for any available job. Others go in for jobs that come their way or due to circumstances. The study of Quinter and Edwards (2011) indicates that the availability of advancement opportunities and learning experiences are influential factors affecting career choices among students. While males reported learning experiences are the most influential factors; females, however, report the availability of advancement opportunities as the most influential factors. People go in for jobs they feel may offer them further opportunities for education or training. Education or training enables the worker to acquire skills, knowledge, attitudes, and abilities that help him to do his present work effectively. The school an individual attend goes a long way to influence one's occupation. The level of individual attainment also counts. One who enrolls and studies in a medical school will likely become a medical doctor and one who attends the school of journalism is likely to become a journalist. Interpersonal Factors Influencing Career Choice: Interpersonal factors include the influence of parents and significant others. Choices of students' careers are influenced by parents and guardians. They provide the basic needs of the students and support them in their education or training to achieve their aims. They have the greatest opportunity to rehab their minds from the early stages of their life. The work of Ferry (2006) revealed that the cultural and social context of family and community were found to be instrumental in how youth learn about careers and were influential in the choice process. Fellow students or friends who are close associates also influence the individual students in making their choices. They are made up of people who share something in common; age social level, academic level, values, and beliefs and considered as equals. They also influence the choice of the program leading to their choices. Teachers and school counselors influence students' choice of career. They serve as mentors, coaches, provide students with career information and help them to clear their doubts, and equip them with many skills. They empower students with wisdom, help the individual to wipe out the stain of ignorance, and get serious with everything he/she is doing as they grow. The media is another source that influences students' choice of career. The media here means those people involved in the production of various means of mass communication. Media portrayals affect an individual's choice of career. Steinke et al. (2009) found out that before viewing televised scientist characters, girls had lower views of their current but not future academic science self-views than did boys. Individuals have been seen to follow career choices based on the media’s interpretation of the profession.

Research Questions

This study aims at assessing factors impacting female undergraduates' career choices. The study will contribute significantly to society as it would inform parents, educators on where female undergraduates place most of their trust in the career selection process and sensitize the public as to proper gender socialization. Furthermore, the findings of this study will be a valuable addition to the existing literature on career choice and it will also provide female undergraduates an opportunity to contribute to their situations as far as a career choice is concerned. The following questions will be addressed 1. What are the intrinsic factors influencing female undergraduates' choice of career? 2. What are the extrinsic factors influencing the career choice of female undergraduates? 3. What are the interpersonal factors influencing the career choice of female undergraduates? Significance of the study: The significance of this study is premised on the fact that female undergraduate students encounter problems in making appropriate career choices, thus, the significance of this study are that: 1. female students can pursue their career choice of interest without undue influence 2. school counselors, teachers are encouraged to provide effective career counseling 3. encourage the media to eliminate gender stereotyping and encourage women to pursue different career paths 4. energize the public, government, and other stakeholders to provide programs and opportunities for effective career growth and development.

Applying Social Constructivism Theory

Social Constructivism is a sociological theory of knowledge that applies the general philosophical constructivism into the social. The concept has a long theory in sociological and philosophical thought, but the term was coined by Peter Berger and Thomas Luckmann. The theory focuses on the processes by which anybody of knowledge comes to be socially accepted as reality. By “reality construction” they mean the process whereby people continuously create through their actions and interactions, a shared reality that is experienced and objectively factual and subjectively meaningful. The concept was Mead's ideas of socialization and interaction and this way, some aspects resemble ideas in Russian cultural psychology, wherein groups construct knowledge for one another collaboratively creating a "small" culture of shared artifacts with shared meanings. When one is immersed within a culture of this sort, one is learning all the time about how to be a part of that culture on many levels. It emphasizes that culture plays a large role in the cognitive development of a person. The basic tenet of this theory is that it focuses on the interpretations, meanings, and dispositions of individuals to social reality, that is the meanings individuals ascribe to a social phenomenon to create social reality, Thus, it emphasizes that knowledge is not passively accumulated, but is the result of active cognizing by the individuals. The theory opines that cognition organizes and makes sense of one’s experience and is not a process to render an accurate representation and “knowing” as root in social, cultural, and language usage and thus is a shared rather than individuals experience. Social constructivism argued that career choice among female undergraduate depends on how a person constructs it as a social fact. The theory argued that most female undergraduates see themselves as being isolated from certain career prospects through their subjective actions and interactions with their peer groups in society. Social Constructivism posits that the career choice of female undergraduate is determined by constructs of individual female undergraduate in the society because females are often seen as inferior to the male. This construct might reduce the level of investment that guardians or parents invest in such female since they would end up married to their male partner and depend on their partners for most of their needs. Social constructivism explains that most female undergraduates focus their attention on the female-dominated career that is lower in prestige, status compensation because the competition on such career options will not be rigorous as a male-dominated career option.

Aims of paper. Methods.

Materials and Methods

Study Design: This is a survey descriptive research. Population: Obafemi Awolowo University students constituted the population utilized of interest. A sample of 1440 students was used for this study. Sampling Techniques: Multi-stage sampling technique was used for this study. The use of this sampling technique was considered appropriate because it involved choosing samples stage by stage. Female undergraduates from all levels (100-500) totaling 1440 were randomly selected. Instrumentation: The research instrument used in this study was the Online Career Choice Questionnaire (OCCQ). The study utilized this survey to obtain information on the determinants of career choice among female undergraduates in Nigeria. The survey comprised of four sections; the first section focused on the socio-demographic characteristics of respondents. The second section sought to inquire into the intrinsic factors influencing career choice among female undergraduates. The third explored information on the extrinsic factors influencing the career choice of female undergraduates. The fourth section evaluated interpersonal factors influencing career choice among female undergraduates and any other factors influencing career choice among female undergraduates. Procedures for Data Collection and Administration: The instruments were administered by the researcher. The completed questionnaires were submitted online. Of the 1440 questionnaires, only 1420 were properly filled and fit for statistical analysis. This constitutes a 98.6% response rate. Procedure for Data Analysis: The analysis of data on information collected from the respondents was done using ordinal logistic regression and descriptive analysis. The descriptive statistics were used to analyze the demographic and socio-economic composition of the respondents and inferential statistics was utilized to show associations and differentials in responses of the respondents. Pie-charts were plotted using the ggplot2 package in R studio version 3.6.1. (R Core Team, 2018). In addition, ordinal regression was performed in R studio using the packages Mass, Foreign, and HMisc.

Exposition of main material of research with complete substantiation of obtained scientific results. Discussion.

Table 1 - 5 shows the socio-demographic characteristics of the respondents. 58.45 percent of the respondents were within the age range of 20-24, 20.42 percent were within the age range of 25-29, 14.09 percent were within the age range of 15-19 while 7.04 percent were aged 30 and above. This means that the respondents aged 20-24 years were largely represented in the study. Also, 44.37 percent of the respondents were at 400 level, 28.87 percent at 300 level, an equal percentage of 10.56 percent at 200 and 500 levels and 5.64 percent are in 100 level. The table further indicated that 41.55 of the respondent’s fathers were self-employed, 22.54 percent were civil servants, 13.38 percent were artisans and the remaining 11.97 percent consisted of lawyers, farmers, bankers and soldiers. The table further showed the occupation of respondents’ mothers – 28.87 were traders, 23.95 percent were teachers, 22.53 percent were civil servants, and the remaining 24.65 percent included clergies, bankers, farmers and lawyers. In terms of religion, most of the respondents – 54.93 percent practice Christianity, 38.73 percent practice Islam while 6.34 percent are traditionalists. Table 6 provides the odds ratios and confidence intervals for each set of variables under intrinsic, extrinsic, and interpersonal factors that were investigated for their influential role in determining the career choice among female undergraduates. For intrinsic factors, the results demonstrate that personal interest (P < 0.001) and prestige (P = 0.003) were key determinants of career choice among female undergraduates. Holding everything else constant, the odds, of a female undergraduate, being more likely (i.e., very great versus no) to choose a career due to its associated prestige was 93% higher than an unprestigious career. Similarly holding constant all other variables, a female undergrad is more likely (OR = 3.91) to choose a career path due to personal interest. Only training was significant (P = 0.04) among extrinsic factors considered. Female undergraduates were 56% more likely to follow a career path that encourages or promises personal advancement/development and training down the line compared to that promises no training (holding constant all other variables). The Job condition, associated promotion, salary, job security, and job status seemed not to be significant (P > 0.05). Among possible interpersonal factors that could play an influential role in the career choice among female undergraduates, media was chiefly significant (P < 0.0001). Holding all other variables constant, the odds of being more likely to choose a career path due to the media frenzy was 3.07 times more than that of a career with no media frenzy. Intriguingly, the odds of being more likely to choose a career path due to relative perception was 54% more times than when no relative was involved. Surprisingly, father, mother, and teachers were less likely to statistically (P >0.05) influence the career choice of a female undergraduate.

Discussion

Career choice is influenced by several factors which are categorized under intrinsic, extrinsic, and interpersonal in this research study. Intrinsic factors such as intellect, interest, prestige, self-esteem, skill and value were explored to determine the crucial factors that affect career choice of female undergraduates. The study found out that Prestige and Interest are the most critical factors. This is supported by Adjin (2004) and Amedzor (2003) with the assertion that interest and prestige influence the students’ choice of career. Interestingly, many of the respondents alluded to their interest in working in several industries such as Banking and Insurance due to the perceived prestige associated with them (Personal Communication). Many of the respondents believe that interest and prestige may be positively correlated. Extrinsic factors such as job condition, promotion, salary, security, status, and training (opportunity for career advancement) were investigated and only the opportunity for career advancement through training showed a significant influence in affecting career choice of female undergraduates. This agreed with Quinter and Edwards (2011) who opined that the availability of advancement opportunities and learning experiences are the most important factors affecting career choices among students. Father, media, mother, peer, relatives, and teachers’ influences were the Interpersonal factors evaluated in this research study. Amongst these factors, the media was found to have a significant influence on the choice of career for female undergraduates. Certain projections by the media of societal expectation for women has long affected career choice of young female adults. Also, the proliferation of Social Media in Nigeria and its power to influence young minds is becoming a powerful platform in influencing women's aspiration to pursue certain career opportunities.

Conclusions.

The study concludes that female students' career choices are influenced by various factors such as prestige, career interest, and the media. Based on this, the study recommends that female undergraduates should be provided with the all valuable resources to pursue their career interests. All stakeholders in the educational system – government, teachers, parents, and guidance need to come on board to create effective career programs propelling young women to pursue their career interests. Also, the media needs to change the narrative by projecting successful women in traditionally male-dominated roles. There is a need to sensitize students on the relevance of different disciplines and the opportunities and benefit of diverse careers.
