Poverty, a lack of accessible and affordable housing, and high healthcare costs significantly increase the risk of homelessness for people with disabilities.
Key Factors Influencing Homelessness
• Poverty and Low Incomes: Adults with disabilities experience poverty at more than twice the rate of people without disabilities. Policies that allow subminimum wages for disability benefit recipients and strict asset caps on programs like Supplemental Security Income (SSI) make it difficult to build financial savings for emergencies.
• Lack of Accessible Housing: Only a tiny fraction of available housing has proper physical modifications, such as ramps or wheelchair accessibility, making safe and stable housing hard to find.
• Housing Discrimination: Landlords and housing providers sometimes enforce indirect discrimination, ignore accommodation requests, or hold negative stereotypes against disabled applicants.
• Unaddressed Healthcare and Support Needs: Without stable access to medical care, personal care assistants, or behavioral health support, managing a disability independently becomes harder, leading to housing loss.
• Inaccessible Emergency Services: Many standard homeless shelters lack the physical layout, sensory accommodations, or trained staff required to support individuals with physical or cognitive disabilities.
