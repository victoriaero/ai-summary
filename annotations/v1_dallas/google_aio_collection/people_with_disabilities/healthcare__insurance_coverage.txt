Income and asset limits, employment status, and state-specific Medicaid policies heavily influence health insurance coverage for people with disabilities.
Key Factors Influencing Coverage
• Income and Asset Thresholds: Public programs like Medicaid often require low income and strict asset limits, meaning that earning above a certain threshold can disqualify an individual.
• Employment Status: Many adults with disabilities experience lower employment rates or lack employer-sponsored private insurance, forcing a heavy reliance on public programs like Medicaid and Medicare.
• State Residence: Geographic location and state policy choices—such as whether a state has expanded Medicaid or implemented Medicaid Buy-In programs—create major disparities in coverage and uninsurance rates.
• Type and Severity of Disability: Functional needs, such as requirements for long-term care, activities of daily living (ADL) assistance, or specialized mental health support, influence eligibility pathways and the specific scope of covered benefits.
• Definitions of Medical Necessity: Restrictive definitions in insurance policies often fail to cover chronic equipment, therapies, or in-home attendant care, treating coverage systems as if they only address short-term acute care.
