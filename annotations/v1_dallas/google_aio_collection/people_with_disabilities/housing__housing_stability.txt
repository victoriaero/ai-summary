AI OVERVIEW TEXT
============================================================

Housing stability for people with disabilities is primarily influenced by financial constraints, physical accessibility barriers, and systemic discrimination.
Financial Factors
• Fixed Incomes and Low Wages: Many individuals rely on inadequate government benefits like Supplemental Security Income (SSI), which fails to cover basic rental costs nationwide.
• Severe Cost Burden: Millions of renters with disabilities pay more than 30% of their limited income on rent, leaving them vulnerable to eviction.
• Healthcare Expenses: High out-of-pocket medical and care costs reduce the funds available for housing.
Physical and Environmental Factors
• Shortage of Accessible Homes: There is a critical lack of wheelchair-accessible or universally designed housing options, leading to long waitlists.
• Need for Home Modifications: A lack of funding or resources for physical home adaptations prevents individuals from safely remaining in their living spaces.
• Caregiver and Support Transitions: Aging or shifting family caregivers can lead to sudden housing instability if supportive services are cut or unavailable.
Systemic and Social Factors
• Housing Discrimination: People with disabilities frequently face illegal discrimination from landlords and housing providers, creating barriers to securing and keeping a home.
• Disproportionate Impact on Communities of Color: Black and Hispanic renters with disabilities—particularly Black women—experience higher rates of housing insecurity and eviction.
• Underfunded Public Assistance: Years of underfunding in public housing and federal programs (like Section 811) limit the availability of safe, subsidized options, often resulting in unnecessary institutionalization or homelessness.
