Multiple systemic, environmental, and behavioral factors influence the disproportionate rates of arrest and incarceration for people with disabilities in the criminal justice system.
Key Factors Influencing Incarceration
• The School-to-Prison Pipeline: Children with disabilities—particularly those with intellectual, developmental, or emotional needs—face school suspensions at significantly higher rates and enter juvenile detention at rates up to five times their representation in public schools. A lack of proper identification, accommodations, and support in educational settings heavily drives this early contact with the legal system.
• Misinterpretation by Law Enforcement: Actions or communication styles related to a disability (such as neurodivergence, deafness, or intellectual and developmental disabilities) are frequently misunderstood by untrained police officers as aggression, non-compliance, or guilt, quickly escalating peaceful situations into arrests.
• Lack of Community Safety Nets: The structural dismantling of public health infrastructure, affordable housing, and accessible community-based mental health care has turned local jails and prisons into default holding facilities for individuals experiencing crises or homelessness.
• Procedural and Legal Barriers: Defendants with intellectual or developmental disabilities often face obstacles during court proceedings. They may be deemed poor witnesses, struggle to understand complex legal rules, or lack effective, knowledgeable legal advocacy, limiting their access to due process and fair trials.
• Recidivism and Re-entry Barriers: Incarcerated individuals with disabilities routinely face discrimination, lack of accommodations in vocational or educational programs, and inadequate discharge planning (such as missing prescriptions or delayed access to Medicaid and SSDI), which sharply increases their risk of re-incarceration upon release.
