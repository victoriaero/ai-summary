Medical evidence, income limits, and age are the main factors that influence government disability benefit approvals like SSDI.
Medical Evidence and Documentation
• Objective proof: The Social Security Administration (SSA) requires detailed records from doctors, hospitals, and specialists.
• Duration: The condition must last or be expected to last for at least 12 consecutive months or result in death.
• Blue Book listings: Approval is faster if the condition matches an impairment listed in official guidelines.
• Functional limits: Treating physician statements that outline specific work restrictions carry heavy weight.
Income and Work History
• Substantial Gainful Activity (SGA): Earning above a specific monthly limit (such as $1,690 in 2026) generally disqualifies an applicant.
• Work credits: Programs like Social Security Disability Insurance (SSDI) require a sufficient employment history and paid taxes into the system.
Vocational and Personal Factors
• Age: Older applicants (ages 50 to 54 and 60+) face a higher approval rate because retraining for new work is harder.
• Education and skills: Limited education and a background in heavy, physical labor increase approval chances if the applicant can no longer perform those tasks.
