Misinterpretation of disability-related behaviors as resistance, a lack of specialized officer training, and communication barriers heavily influence police use of force against people with disabilities.
Key Factors Influencing Use of Force
• Misinterpretation of Behaviors: Actions stemming from developmental or intellectual disabilities—such as avoiding eye contact, repetitive movements ("stimming"), or slow response times—are frequently mistaken by officers for non-compliance or physical resistance.
• Communication Barriers: Without alternative communication methods or effective tools to bridge understanding, tense encounters can rapidly escalate into physical force.
• Lack of Standardized Training: Many law enforcement agencies lack sufficient, standardized training on recognizing and properly responding to intellectual, developmental, or mental health disabilities.
• Intersectionality: Marginalization is magnified when disability intersects with race, class, or gender, increasing the baseline risk of severe or fatal police violence.
• Denial of Accommodations: The removal or restriction of essential assistive devices (such as wheelchairs, canes, or communication aids) during encounters can impair an individual's movement or ability to comply, compounding the risk of conflict.
