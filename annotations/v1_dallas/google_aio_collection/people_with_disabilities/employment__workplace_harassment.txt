Workplace harassment for people with disabilities is heavily influenced by negative employer attitudes, unsupportive workplace cultures, and intersectional demographic factors.
Key Influencing Factors
• Employer and Coworker Perceptions: Misconceptions that disabled employees are less productive, cost too much money, or possess limited capabilities drive a hostile climate.
• Lack of Disability Awareness: Ignorance or a lack of training among managers and staff regarding various physical, mental, or neurodivergent conditions often leads to unintentional or overt microaggressions and harassment.
• Accommodations Friction: The process of requesting or utilizing reasonable accommodations—such as modified equipment, flexible schedules, or accessible technology—frequently triggers backlash, mockery, or deliberate sabotage by coworkers and supervisors.
• Intersectional Demographics: Experiences of discrimination and harassment worsen when a disability intersects with marginalized identities based on race, gender, age, social class, or sexual orientation.
• Visibility and Disclosure: Perceived disability status and whether a condition is hidden or obvious dictate how people are treated; forced or unwanted public disclosure of a medical condition also acts as a primary catalyst for mistreatment.
• Toxic Workplace Culture: Organizations that fail to enforce anti-harassment policies, dismiss "banter" or offensive jokes, or neglect to penalize inappropriate behavior create an environment where harassment thrives.
