Access to housing for people with disabilities is primarily influenced by severe affordability gaps, a critical lack of physical accessibility, and systemic discrimination.
Key Factors
• Affordability and Poverty: Adults with disabilities experience poverty at more than twice the rate of people without disabilities. Many rely on fixed incomes like Social Security or Supplemental Security Income, which fall far below median market rents.
• Lack of Accessible Stock: Nationwide, very few homes feature crucial physical adaptations. Less than 5% of housing accommodates moderate mobility needs, and under 1% suits wheelchair users.
• Discrimination and Stigma: Landlords sometimes use illegal screening, discriminatory rental practices, or false occupancy claims. This happens despite protections under the Fair Housing Act.
• Support Services: A major barrier is the lack of community-based supportive services delivered directly into private homes. This forces some individuals into institutional or segregated settings.
• Assistance Gaps: Federal housing assistance programs remain heavily underfunded. Long waiting lists leave many eligible individuals without vouchers or public support.
• Aging Caregivers: Many adults with intellectual or developmental disabilities live with elderly family members. When those primary caregivers age or pass away, housing instability spikes rapidly.
