Several key financial, social, and institutional factors influence college access and admission for people with disabilities.
Financial and Resource Factors
• Cost of Documentation: Affluent families can more easily pay for medical evaluations and professional testing required to prove a disability, while lower-income students often struggle to get necessary records.
• Socioeconomic Disparity: Poorer students with disabilities face a double disadvantage and enter college at much lower rates due to a lack of resources for ongoing care and preparation.
Transition and Self-Advocacy
• K-12 to College Shift: High schools handle special education automatically, but colleges require students to self-advocate and formally disclose their condition to get help.
• Transition Planning: High school transition plans that explicitly name future college accommodations help students successfully secure support after graduation.
Campus Culture and Attitudes
• Faculty and Staff Attitudes: Some instructors view accessibility accommodations as an unfair advantage or extra work rather than an essential right, which creates hidden hurdles for students.
• Sense of Belonging: Students who do not feel welcomed on campus within their first eight weeks face a much higher risk of dropping out.
Physical and Digital Accessibility
• Inaccessible Facilities: Many older campus buildings, transport systems, and housing options lack full physical mobility access.
• Digital Divides: Online learning tools and class websites often use technology that is not compatible with screen readers or other assistive devices.
