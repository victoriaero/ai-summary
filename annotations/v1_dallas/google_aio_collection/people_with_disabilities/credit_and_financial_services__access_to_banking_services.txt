Access to banking and credit services for people with disabilities is shaped by economic, physical, digital, and attitudinal factors.
Physical and Digital Accessibility
• Infrastructural barriers: Physical branches often lack accessible parking, ramps, automatic doors, or ADA-compliant transaction counters and ATMs.
• Digital gaps: Online banking portals and mobile apps frequently lag behind physical spaces in accessibility, failing to work properly with screen readers, voice commands, or other assistive technologies.
• Communication hurdles: In-person and call center staff may lack training to communicate effectively or demonstrate empathy toward customers with sensory or speech disabilities.
Economic and Financial Factors
• Income and poverty disparities: People with disabilities experience higher rates of unemployment and poverty, reducing their overall demand for or ability to maintain traditional bank accounts.
• Perceived creditworthiness: Financial institutions often misjudge the risk profile of individuals relying on disability-related income, leading to exclusionary lending practices or an unmet need for credit.
• Lack of tailored products: Traditional loans often fail to align with irregular or non-traditional income streams, pushing consumers toward high-cost alternatives like payday loans or pawn shops.
Knowledge and Awareness
• Financial literacy gaps: Lower access to inclusive financial education leaves many individuals unaware of specialized tools, such as tax-advantaged ABLE accounts for disability-related savings.
• Stigma and bias: Institutional bias and negative connotations regarding disability can discourage customers from engaging formal banking systems.
