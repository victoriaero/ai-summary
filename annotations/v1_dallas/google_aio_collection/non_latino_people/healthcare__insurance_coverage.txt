Insurance coverage for non-Latino populations (such as White, Black, Asian, and American Indian/Alaska Native individuals) is primarily influenced by employment status, income level, age, and education.
Key Factors Influencing Coverage
• Employer-Sponsored Insurance (ESI): Access to a job that offers health benefits is a major driver. White and Asian adults generally have higher rates of ESI compared to Black, American Indian/Alaska Native, and Native Hawaiian/Pacific Islander adults, who are more frequently in jobs without health benefits.
• Income and Poverty Levels: Household income determines eligibility for public programs like Medicaid or subsidies through the Affordable Care Act (ACA) marketplace. Lower-income individuals face higher uninsured rates if they live in states that have not expanded Medicaid.
• Age Patterns: Young adults (ages 19 to 34) across all racial and ethnic groups experience higher rates of uninsurance than older adults. Coverage rates improve with age as individuals transition into stable employment or approach Medicare eligibility at age 65.
• Education and Wage Levels: Higher educational attainment and higher wages strongly correlate with increased access to private and employer-provided health plans.
• Geographic Location: State-level policies, local labor markets, and the availability of rural healthcare infrastructure significantly affect whether public or private insurance options are accessible.
