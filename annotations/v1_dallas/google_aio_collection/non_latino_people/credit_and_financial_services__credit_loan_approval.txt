Standard financial and credit approval processes evaluate specific, measurable financial metrics rather than race or ethnicity.
Core Financial Factors
Lenders base credit and loan approvals on objective financial criteria:
• Credit History and Score: Payment history, length of credit, and existing credit mix.
• Debt-to-Income (DTI) Ratio: Comparison of monthly debt payments to gross monthly income.
• Income and Employment Stability: Verification of steady, reliable income sources.
• Collateral or Assets: Down payments, cash reserves, or property value used to secure the loan.
Contextual and Systemic Influences
While formal underwriting rules do not include race, broader economic research highlights disparities in underlying factors that affect approval rates across demographic groups:
• Credit Visibility: Having a "thin" credit file or no traditional credit score impacts approval odds.
• Wealth and Savings Gaps: Disparities in accumulated household wealth influence down payment sizes and reserve amounts.
• Alternative Data: Traditional scoring models often exclude rent and utility payments, which can disadvantage individuals who rely heavily on non-traditional payment histories.
