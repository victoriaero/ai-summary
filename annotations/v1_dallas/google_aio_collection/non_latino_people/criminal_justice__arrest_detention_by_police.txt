AI OVERVIEW TEXT

Legal and extra-legal variables strongly influence arrest and detention rates for non-Latino groups (such as White and Black individuals) within the criminal justice system.
Legal Factors
• Crime Severity: The presence of felony charges rather than minor infractions greatly increases the likelihood of booking and formal detention.
• Criminal History: Prior arrest records and active probation or parole statuses significantly raise the odds of being arrested or held pretrial.
• Offense Type: Allegations involving violent acts or weapon possession trigger immediate custodial arrest far more often than property or low-level drug offenses.
Extra-Legal and Environmental Factors
• Spatial Concentration of Crime: Police deployment heavily impacts arrest rates, which cluster tightly in specific high-crime urban neighborhoods rather than evenly across entire cities.
• Socioeconomic Disadvantage: Concentrated poverty, limited neighborhood resources, and lower median incomes correlate strongly with elevated police contact and subsequent arrests.
• Officer Discretion and Racial Disparities: Disparities remain evident even when controlling for behavior; for instance, studies show Black individuals face higher arrest and pretrial detention rates than White individuals for similar conduct, such as low-level drug offenses.
