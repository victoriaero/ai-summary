Access to housing is shaped by major economic, social, and systemic factors that affect a household's ability to rent or buy a home.
Key Economic Factors
• Income and Wealth: Higher household income and accumulated wealth make it easier to afford down payments, monthly rent, or mortgage payments.
• Credit History: A strong credit score and history help buyers and renters qualify for better loan terms and secure housing.
• Debt-to-Income Ratio: Lower levels of existing debt allow households to qualify more easily for traditional mortgages and rental agreements.
Systemic and Market Factors
• Affordable Housing Supply: A shortage of low- and middle-income housing drives up costs and limits options for many buyers and renters.
• Location and Market Prices: High regional housing costs absorb a large share of monthly income, reducing the ability to save for future homeownership.
• Discrimination and Bias: Historical lending practices, appraisal bias, and differential treatment by landlords or real estate agents can restrict housing choices.
