Police use of force for non-Latino groups (such as White and Black individuals) is influenced by a combination of situational, neighborhood, and behavioral factors.
Key Factors Influencing Police Use of Force
• Situational and Suspect Behavior: The immediate demeanor, level of compliance, and possession of a weapon by the individual involved are primary drivers that escalate police response and force.
• Neighborhood Characteristics: Police are more likely to deploy coercive actions and higher levels of force in areas marked by economic disadvantage, concentrated poverty, and high crime rates.
• Disparities and Demographics: Research shows that even when adjusting for specific contextual elements, Black non-Latino individuals experience non-lethal uses of force, threats, and police contact at significantly higher rates than White non-Latino individuals.
• Officer Perceptions and Training: Split-second decision-making, implicit biases, and departmental policies on less-lethal versus lethal tactics shape how officers gauge threats and respond during escalating encounters.
