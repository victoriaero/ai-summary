Several key structural, economic, and social factors influence the use of preventive healthcare services for non-Latino populations (such as non-Latino white and Black individuals):
• Health Insurance Coverage: Having health insurance and continuous coverage is one of the strongest predictors of whether a person receives routine screenings, wellness visits, and vaccinations.
• Usual Source of Care: Having a regular primary care provider or a consistent medical home greatly increases the likelihood of engaging in preventive care and having positive provider-patient communication.
• Socioeconomic Status (SES): Income level, employment benefits (such as paid time off to visit a doctor), and education directly affect a person's ability to afford and prioritize preventive services.
• Health Literacy: Understanding the purpose, availability, and scheduling of routine screenings (like colorectal or breast cancer checks) heavily influences participation.
• Systemic and Racial Disparities: Disparities persist across racial groups; for instance, research shows differences in utilization rates for annual wellness visits, vaccines, and cancer screenings between white and Black beneficiaries depending on insurance models like Medicare Advantage versus traditional Medicare.
