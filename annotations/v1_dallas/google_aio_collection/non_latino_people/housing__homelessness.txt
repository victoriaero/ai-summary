General economic and systemic barriers, including a severe lack of affordable housing, low incomes, and the lingering effects of structural discrimination, heavily influence homelessness for non-Latino populations (such as Black, White, and Native American individuals).
Main Factors Influencing Homelessness
• Poverty and Income Gaps: Low wages and a lack of financial resources are primary predictors of housing instability and homelessness across non-Latino groups. Deep poverty disproportionately impacts specific non-Latino communities, such as Black and Native American populations.
• Housing Affordability and Costs: High rent burdens and a shortage of deeply affordable housing developments force many low-income individuals out of stable housing.
• Systemic and Historical Discrimination: Institutional racism, historical housing policies like redlining, and ongoing discrimination create segregated communities with lower access to well-funded schools, stable jobs, and economic mobility.
• Health and Behavioral Challenges: Untreated mental health challenges, physical disabilities, and substance use issues increase vulnerability to losing housing, especially when coupled with weak community and social safety nets.
• Criminal Backgrounds: Past involvement with the justice system creates severe barriers to securing permanent housing, even when individuals have housing vouchers or rental assistance.
• Social Network Breakdown: Fragile or broken family and social networks mean individuals have fewer informal support systems (like "doubling up") to fall back on during financial crises.
