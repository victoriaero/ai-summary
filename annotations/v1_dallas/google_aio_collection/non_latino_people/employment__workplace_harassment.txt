Workplace harassment for non-Latino employees is driven by general organizational and interpersonal risk factors identified by bodies like the Equal Employment Opportunity Commission (EEOC) rather than ethnicity-specific targeting.
Key Organizational Risk Factors
• Power Dynamics: Imbalances where supervisors or managers use domineering or intimidating behaviors to maintain control create a fear-based environment.
• Lack of Diversity: Workplaces with low demographic diversity can make individual minority or out-group workers more vulnerable to targeted bullying and isolation.
• Non-Conforming Cultures: Environments featuring "rough and tumble" dynamics, heavy teasing, or crude banter often normalize demeaning remarks and ostracize individuals who do not fit in.
• Hierarchical and Low-Wage Settings: Workers at the bottom of an organizational hierarchy or those with precarious, non-union contracts face heightened vulnerability due to perceived powerlessness.
Interpersonal and Social Influences
• Personal Insecurities: Individual supervisors or co-workers struggling with low self-esteem or personal stress may project aggressive or controlling behaviors onto peers.
• External Social Discourse: Heated societal discussions regarding politics, current events, or cultural topics often spill into office spaces, coarsening daily social interactions and breeding friction.
• Intersectional Vulnerabilities: Factors such as gender, age (younger workforces), and socioeconomic standing interact with workplace culture to raise or lower the likelihood of experiencing abusive conduct.
