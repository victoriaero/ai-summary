Housing stability for non-Latino populations—including non-Hispanic Black, White, and Asian households—is influenced by a combination of economic, structural, and historical factors. Research from the U.S. Department of the Treasury and academic studies highlight key determinants:
Economic and Wealth Factors
• Income and Savings: Household income and liquid assets dictate a resident's ability to make timely rent or mortgage payments and absorb unexpected employment shocks.
• Intergenerational Wealth: Disparities in inherited wealth and family financial support significantly affect down-payment readiness and the ability to avoid severe cost burdens.
• Housing Costs and Cost Burden: Households spending more than 30% of their income on housing are considered cost-burdened. Non-white non-Latino groups, particularly Black renters, experience higher rates of severe cost burdens compared to white counterparts.
Systemic and Structural Factors
• Historical and Continuing Discrimination: Legacies of redlining and discriminatory lending practices have created long-standing residential segregation and lower rates of real housing appreciation in historically marginalized neighborhoods.
• Appraisal and Mortgage Bias: Disparities in home appraisals—where homes in minority neighborhoods are more frequently appraised below contract prices—limit refinancing and wealth generation.
• Criminal Justice and Intersectional Disadvantages: Past criminal justice contact or prior evictions create structural barriers across housing and safety-net systems, disproportionately impacting specific non-Latino demographics like older Black adults.
