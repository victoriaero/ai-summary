Harassment and bullying for non-Latino students in educational settings are influenced by an intersecting combination of demographic, social, and environmental factors.
Key Influencing Factors
• Other Marginalized Identities: According to research highlighted by Boston University, students belonging to other minority groups—such as Black, Asian American, Pacific Islander, or multi-racial youth—face elevated risks of bias-based harassment tied specifically to their race, skin color, or ethnicity.
• Religious and Cultural Markers: Studies on Religious Minorities in Schools indicate that visible cultural or faith practices (such as distinctive attire, dietary restrictions, or prayer times) make Jewish, Muslim, Arab, Sikh, and other religious minority students distinct targets for cultural and religious intolerance.
• Academic and Extracurricular Involvement: Research noted in the National Institutes of Health shows that minority students (including African American and Asian American peers) are often at a higher risk of school victimization when they are heavily involved in academic success or specific extracurricular and athletic activities.
• General and Environmental Vulnerabilities: General risk factors—such as perceived safety and school climate—play a major role; schools with high rates of reported discipline issues, visible conflict, or an overall lack of supportive diversity policies experience higher frequencies of identity-based bullying.
• Peer Dynamics and Social Integration: Peer networks, social isolation, and pressures surrounding peer acceptance significantly predict involvement in bullying across all racial and ethnic groups.
