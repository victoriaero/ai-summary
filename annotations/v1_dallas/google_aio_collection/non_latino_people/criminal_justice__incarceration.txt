AI OVERVIEW TEXT
============================================================

Socioeconomic disadvantage, prior criminal history, and the severity of the alleged offense are primary factors influencing incarceration rates for non-Latino individuals in the criminal justice system.
According to research from organizations like the Sentencing Project and public health studies on recidivism and legal contact, incarceration outcomes are shaped by a combination of legal, economic, and social variables:
Key Influencing Factors
• Socioeconomic Status: Poverty, homelessness, and inadequate access to stable employment or healthcare significantly increase the baseline risk of arrest and subsequent incarceration.
• Criminal History: Prior incarceration and a recorded history of past arrests serve as strong independent predictors for future or prolonged jail and prison sentences.
• Offense Severity and Type: The classification of the charge (felony versus misdemeanor) and the specific nature of the crime—such as violent offenses versus drug-related or property conduct—largely dictate pre-trial detention and final sentencing lengths.
• Substance Use and Health: Active stimulant use disorders, untreated mental health symptoms, and lack of access to community-based addiction treatment programs heavily correlate with repeated criminal justice contact.
• Social Support Networks: Limited access to financial resources, stable housing, or strong community safety nets reduces an individual's ability to secure pretrial release or private legal representation.
