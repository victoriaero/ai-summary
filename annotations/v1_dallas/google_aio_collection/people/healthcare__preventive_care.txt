AI OVERVIEW TEXT
============================================================

Several key personal, economic, and systemic factors influence whether people receive preventive care in healthcare settings.
Economic and Insurance Factors
• Health Insurance Coverage: Having health insurance—and particularly coverage that fully pays for routine checkups and screenings—strongly increases the use of preventive services.
• Cost and Affordability: Out-of-pocket costs, copays, and hidden fees often deter patients from scheduling regular wellness visits or tests.
• Socioeconomic Status: Individuals with lower income or unstable housing often face higher barriers and utilize fewer primary and preventive care services.
Patient and Demographic Factors
• Age and Gender: Age dictates many screening timelines, and older adults or specific demographics have different utilization rates. Younger groups often seek less routine care.
• Personal and Family Health History: A known family history of chronic illness or genetic risk factors often motivates patients to seek targeted preventive measures.
• Health Literacy and Education: Understanding how lifestyle choices impact long-term health empowers individuals to engage in proactive disease prevention.
Healthcare System and Provider Factors
• Access to a Regular Provider: Having a usual primary care doctor and continuity of care strongly improves adherence to preventive screening schedules.
• Frequency of Visits: Patients who have more routine ambulatory or primary care visits are far more likely to stay up to date on vaccines and tests.
• System Engagement Tools: Enrollment and active use of digital tools like patient portals help remind and connect patients to needed care.
