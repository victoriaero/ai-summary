A combination of economic, systemic, and policy factors heavily influences access to housing for individuals and families.
Economic Factors
• Affordability and Income: High living costs, low wages, and a widening wealth gap make it difficult for lower-income households to afford rent or save for a down payment. Renters often pay more than 30% of their income on housing.
• Shortage of Affordable Units: A general deficit in the National Alliance to End Homelessness inventory of low-cost rental options leaves many people vulnerable to housing instability.
• Rental Assistance Limitations: Underfunded federal or local rental assistance programs mean that housing vouchers do not reach everyone who qualifies.
Systemic and Social Barriers
• Housing Discrimination: Source-of-income discrimination (such as refusing housing vouchers), along with systemic racism and marginalization, restricts choices for protected or disadvantaged groups.
• Zoning and Land Use Laws: Local exclusionary zoning practices and "Not In My Backyard" (NIMBY) opposition limit the construction of multi-family or low-income housing in many neighborhoods.
• Health and Support Needs: Individuals facing severe mental illness, substance use disorders, or physical disabilities often encounter a shortage of accessible housing options and specialized Supportive Housing for People with Disabilities Program (Section 811) resources.
Policy and Historical Factors
• Justice and Foster Care Systems: Structural flaws in institutional discharges—such as releasing individuals from incarceration or the foster care system without adequate housing support—contribute significantly to housing insecurity.
• Historical Disparities: Past discriminatory practices like redlining have created enduring racial and economic segregation that impacts modern generational wealth and homeownership access.
