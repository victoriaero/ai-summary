Workplace harassment is influenced by a combination of organizational structures, cultural norms, power dynamics, and environmental risk factors identified by organizations like the U.S. Equal Employment Opportunity Commission (EEOC).
Organizational and Structural Factors
• Power Disparities: Significant gaps in power between managers and subordinates can make vulnerable employees fear retaliation or doubt that complaints will be addressed.
• Isolated or Remote Work: Workplaces or shifts physically distant from leadership supervision or performed entirely alone increase the risk of targeted behavior.
• Precarious Job Security: Low wages, short-term contracts, or understaffing create an environment where employees feel they cannot speak out without risking their livelihood.
• Monotonous or Low-Intensity Tasks: Boredom or repetitive tasks can sometimes foster inappropriate banter or bullying as a way to pass time.
Cultural and Workforce Demographics
• Homogenous Workforces: Work environments lacking in diversity can isolate minority workers, while majority workers may feel uncomfortable or threatened by those they perceive as different.
• Cultural and Language Differences: Segregated work groups or language barriers can prevent employees from understanding workplace norms or knowing their legal rights, making them susceptible to exploitation.
• Non-conforming Workplace Cultures: "Rough and tumble" or single-sex-dominated environments often tolerate crude or demeaning remarks, establishing harmful workplace norms.
• Young or Tip-Dependent Workforces: Industries that employ younger workers or rely heavily on customer tips can pressure employees to tolerate inappropriate behavior to maintain customer satisfaction or income.
External and Environmental Influences
• Tolerated Alcohol Consumption: Social events or work environments that encourage drinking can lower inhibitions and increase incidents of misconduct.
• Coarsened Social Discourse: Heated public debates or external social tensions can bleed into the workplace, making disrespectful conduct seem more acceptable.
