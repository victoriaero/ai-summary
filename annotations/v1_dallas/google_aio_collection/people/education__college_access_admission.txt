Multiple interconnected academic, financial, geographic, and social factors strongly influence college access and admission.
Academic Preparation and Evaluation
• High school performance: Grades, grade point averages (GPAs), and the rigor or strength of the high school curriculum (such as AP or honors courses) are universally rated as the top deciding criteria for four-year institutions.
• Standardized testing: While many schools adopt test-optional policies, high scores on exams like the SAT and ACT remain significant differentiators, though access to quality test-prep often favors wealthier students.
Socioeconomic and Financial Influences
• Family income and wealth: Higher socioeconomic status directly correlates with higher rates of college enrollment and persistence, specifically at four-year universities [0.6.14, 0.6.11].
• Cost and financial aid: The overall cost of tuition, availability of scholarships, and navigating the Free Application for Federal Student Aid (FAFSA) heavily dictate whether prospective students can afford or even apply to college [0.6.1, 0.6.4, 0.6.12].
Guidance and Mentorship
• School counselors and teachers: Regular interactions with secondary school counselors and supportive educators significantly boost college knowledge, application rates, and financial aid completion [0.6.1, 0.6.8].
• Social capital: First-generation or at-risk students often lack familial navigation experience or institutional guidance, creating additional barriers to entry.
Geography and Institutional Access
• Proximity to campuses: The physical location of colleges and distance from a student's residence heavily influence enrollment, especially for students who cannot afford relocation or long commutes.
• Institutional priorities: Factors like geographic diversity, socioeconomic representation, and institutional recruitment targets also shape who ultimately gets accepted.
