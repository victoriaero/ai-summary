Multiple system, human, and patient-related factors shape patient safety in healthcare settings.
System and Organizational Factors
• Staffing and Workload: Safe nurse-to-patient ratios, manageable shift hours, and adequate staffing prevent fatigue-driven errors.
• Resource Availability: Access to proper medical equipment, personal protective gear, and sustained facility funding reduces procedural risks.
• Workflow and Coordination: Clear care transitions, organized handoffs, and streamlined clinical processes stop communication gaps during patient transfers.
Human Factors and Teamwork
• Communication: Clear, concise exchanges among medical staff—and with patients—prevent medication mistakes and wrong-site procedures.
• Staff Well-being: Burnout, physical fatigue, and high stress levels degrade cognitive performance and increase oversight risks.
• Psychological Safety: A non-punitive culture where workers feel safe reporting mistakes or voicing concerns without fear of punishment leads to continuous learning.
Technological and Environmental Factors
• Health IT Usability: Functional electronic health records (EHRs) and reliable digital medication administration systems prevent data entry or dosing mix-ups.
• Hygiene and Sanitation: Strict adherence to infection control standards, such as hand hygiene and sterile bandaging, stops healthcare-associated infections.
Patient-Related Factors
• Health Literacy: Patients who fully understand their diagnoses, treatment regimens, and discharge instructions can better spot inconsistencies.
• Engagement: Active patient participation in safety checks (like verifying surgical sites or asking about medications) acts as a final protective buffer.
