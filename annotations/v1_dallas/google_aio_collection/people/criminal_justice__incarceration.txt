Several interconnected social, economic, and policy factors influence incarceration rates within the criminal justice system.
Socioeconomic Status
• Poverty: People with low incomes often live in neighborhoods with higher police presence, increasing their chances of arrest.
• Legal Representation: Low-income individuals frequently cannot afford private attorneys and must rely on overworked public defenders, which can lead to higher conviction and incarceration rates.
• Unemployment: A lack of stable employment and unmet basic needs strongly correlate with higher risks of criminal justice involvement.
Systemic and Racial Disparities
• Disproportionate Impact: Black people, Native Americans, and other marginalized groups face higher rates of arrest and imprisonment.
• Over-Policing: Communities of color often experience more intensive law enforcement practices, leading to a higher volume of recorded offenses and subsequent convictions.
Policy and Sentencing Laws
• Punitive Legislation: Harsher drug laws, mandatory minimum sentencing, and "tough on crime" policies enacted over the last several decades significantly increased prison populations.
• Pretrial Practices: High bail amounts and financial fees keep many non-convicted individuals in jail while awaiting trial, which increases the likelihood of an eventual prison sentence.
Health and Substance Abuse
• Substance Abuse: A strong link exists between illegal drug or alcohol use and criminal legal contact, with many incarcerated individuals dealing with active addiction.
• Mental Health: Correctional facilities frequently function as default care providers because many people behind bars experience untreated mental health disorders.
