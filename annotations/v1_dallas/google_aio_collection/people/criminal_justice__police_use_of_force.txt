AI OVERVIEW TEXT

Suspect behavior and the immediate threat level are the most significant factors influencing police use of force during a criminal justice encounter.
Suspect Behavior and Encounter Characteristics
• Suspect Resistance: Active resistance or physical aggression from a suspect is the strongest predictor of an officer using physical force.
• Weapon Presence: The display or use of a weapon by a suspect dramatically increases the likelihood and level of force used by police.
• Severity of the Crime: Under legal standards like the Federal Law Enforcement Training Center Graham Factors, the seriousness of the alleged offense plays a major role in determining reasonable force.
• Flight or Evasion: Attempting to flee from custody or evade arrest can prompt officers to escalate their physical response.
Legal and Departmental Frameworks
• Objective Reasonableness: The landmark Supreme Court standard (Graham v. Connor) dictates that force must be judged from the perspective of a reasonable officer on the scene, factoring in the immediate threat.
• Department Policies: Individual agency rules, managerial organization, and clear guidelines shape how and when officers are permitted to escalate or de-escalate force.
• Use-of-Force Continuum: Many agencies utilize a graded framework—ranging from officer presence and verbalization to empty-hand control and less-lethal methods—to guide tactical decisions.
Demographic and Systemic Disparities
• Demographic Disparities: Research indicates that people of color, particularly Black individuals, experience higher rates of police use of force even when controlling for other variables like local crime rates.
• Intersectionality: Individuals at the intersection of multiple marginalized identities—such as low-income status, minority race, or living with a mental illness—face disproportionately higher rates of forceful police interactions.
