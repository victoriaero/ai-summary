Housing stability is influenced by a combination of financial resources, personal health, legal protections, and broader economic and structural conditions.
Financial Factors
• Income and Employment: Steady wages and reliable employment allow households to make consistent rent or mortgage payments.
• Housing Affordability: Spending a disproportionate amount of income on rent or housing costs leaves fewer resources for food, healthcare, and emergencies.
• Savings and Debt: Limited savings or unexpected financial shocks make it difficult to absorb emergencies without falling behind on housing costs.
Health and Personal Circumstances
• Physical and Mental Health: Chronic illnesses, disabilities, or mental health crises can impact earning potential and daily functioning, while a lack of stable housing further exacerbates health issues.
• Life and Family Changes: Traumatic events, domestic instability, or family disruptions can destabilize a household's living situation.
• Social Support: Access to reliable friends, family, or community networks provides a safety net during crises.
Structural and Legal Conditions
• Rental Market Conditions: Low vacancy rates, rising rents, and a general lack of affordable housing options drive instability and increase the risk of overcrowding.
• Legal Protections and History: Past evictions, legal disputes with landlords, or criminal justice involvement create major barriers to securing and maintaining permanent housing.
• Government and Community Support: The availability of public assistance, housing vouchers, and proactive, trauma-informed property management help prevent unwanted disruptions like evictions.
