The decision by police to detain or arrest a person is primarily influenced by legal standards like probable cause and reasonable suspicion, alongside situational, officer, and neighborhood factors.
Legal Factors
• Probable Cause: Officers must have trustworthy facts or evidence showing a person likely committed a crime to make a full arrest.
• Reasonable Suspicion: Officers need a specific, factual basis to briefly stop and detain someone for an investigative check.
• Crime Severity: More serious offenses reduce officer discretion and heavily increase the likelihood of formal arrest or booking.
Situational and Environmental Factors
• Victim Presence: Incidents that involve a present victim or a citizen demanding police action are much more likely to end in an arrest.
• Suspect Demeanor: Uncooperative or hostile behavior by a suspect can negatively influence an officer's discretion.
• Neighborhood Context: Research shows arrest rates vary by location, often tied to precinct crime rates, community concerns, and local demographics.
Officer and Social Characteristics
• Officer Discretion: Individual traits, background, and department policies shape how an officer chooses to resolve an encounter.
• Systemic Disparities: Studies from organizations like the Prison Policy Initiative show that Black and Latinx individuals, poor residents, and people with mental health or substance use disorders face disproportionate rates of stops and arrests.
