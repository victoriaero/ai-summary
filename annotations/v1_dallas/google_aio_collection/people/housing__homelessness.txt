The transition from stable housing into homelessness is primarily driven by a combination of severe economic pressure, sudden personal crises, and systemic housing shortages.
Economic and Housing Pressures
• Severe Rent Burden: Renters paying more than 30% to 50% of their income on housing are highly vulnerable to displacement when costs rise.
• Income Loss: Sudden job termination, reduced work hours, or stagnant wages that fail to keep pace with inflation make maintaining rent impossible.
• Eviction: Formal or informal eviction notices strip individuals and families of their current residence with very few low-cost alternatives available.
Personal and Family Crises
• Domestic Violence: Fleeing an abusive household often forces individuals to leave immediately without financial reserves or a safe place to go.
• Health Emergencies: High medical debt or debilitating chronic health issues can quickly drain savings and interfere with the ability to work.
• Behavioral Health: Untreated mental health conditions or substance use disorders can destabilize employment and strain personal support networks.
Systemic and Structural Factors
• Lack of Assistance: Underfunded rental assistance programs and shrinking public safety nets leave people without a buffer during emergencies.
• Institutional Discharges: Inadequate discharge planning for individuals leaving foster care, hospitals, or correctional facilities frequently leads directly to housing instability.
• Systemic Discrimination: Source-of-income discrimination and historic housing inequities restrict marginalized groups from securing or maintaining affordable options.
