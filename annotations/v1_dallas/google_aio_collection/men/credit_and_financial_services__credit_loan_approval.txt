Lenders evaluate credit and loan approval based on financial and creditworthiness metrics rather than an applicant's gender.
Key Factors Influencing Credit and Loan Approval
• Credit Score: A numerical score (typically 300 to 850) showing your past borrowing and repayment habits. Higher scores mean lower risk.
• Debt-to-Income Ratio (DTI): The comparison of your monthly debt payments to your gross monthly income. Lenders prefer a lower DTI to ensure you can afford new payments.
• Income and Employment Stability: Proof of a steady, reliable income confirms you have the financial means to repay the borrowed amount.
• Payment History: Your track record of paying past bills and loans on time. This makes up a major portion of standard credit scoring models.
• Credit Utilization: The amount of available revolving credit you are currently using. Keeping balances low relative to your limits helps approval odds.
• Collateral or Down Payment: Valuable assets pledged to secure a loan, which reduce the lender's risk.
