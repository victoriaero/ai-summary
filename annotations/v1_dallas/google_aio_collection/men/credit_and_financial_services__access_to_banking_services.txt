Income level, credit history, and cost of services are the main factors that influence access to banking and credit services for individuals, including men, within the financial system.
Key Economic and Financial Factors
• Income and Wealth: Lower-income individuals face the highest barriers, often lacking checking or savings accounts or having subprime credit scores.
• Credit History: A lack of traditional credit history or low credit scores restrict access to mainstream loans and affordable credit products.
• Service Costs: High maintenance fees, minimum balance requirements, and transaction costs pose a heavy financial burden relative to earnings.
Structural and Technological Barriers
• Documentation Requirements: Strict identification and compliance rules (such as KYC or Know Your Customer policies) can exclude individuals lacking proper paperwork.
• Digital Access and Literacy: A reliance on online and mobile banking creates roadblocks for those without internet access, smartphones, or digital skills.
• Physical Infrastructure: The distance to physical bank branches or a lack of local financial institutions reduces in-person access.
Trust and Social Factors
• Institutional Trust: Skepticism or past negative experiences with formal financial institutions lead some consumers to avoid banks voluntarily or involuntarily.
• Social Networks: Social capital and community connections heavily influence awareness of and guidance toward inclusive banking products like Bank On-certified accounts.
