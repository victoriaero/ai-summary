Several organizational, cultural, and social risk factors influence workplace harassment for men, including non-conformity to traditional gender norms, power imbalances, and specific workplace cultures.
According to guidelines and insights from the U.S. Equal Employment Opportunity Commission (EEOC) and legal analyses, the primary factors driving or influencing harassment against men in employment involve:
Key Risk Factors
• Non-Conformity to Workplace or Gender Norms: Men who do not match traditional or hyper-masculine stereotypes—such as being perceived as less physically or emotionally tough—are often more vulnerable to targeted teasing, bullying, or sexual harassment.
• Gender-Dominant or "Rough and Tumble" Cultures: Workplaces dominated by a single sex or crude, demeaning banter often establish hostile norms that devalue individuals who do not participate in or fit the dominant behavioral mold.
• Power Disparities: Hierarchical structures where high-status individuals hold unchecked authority can lead to the exploitation or harassment of lower-status employees, regardless of gender.
• Social and Cultural Stigma: Societal expectations regarding male strength and control often make men hesitant to report abuse due to fear of not being believed, being mocked, or damaging their professional reputation.
• Homogenous Workforces: Work environments with a historic lack of diversity can breed isolation for minority individuals or create environments where majority groups feel threatened by anyone perceived as different.
