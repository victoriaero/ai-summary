Legal, behavioral, and situational factors—primarily the level of suspect resistance and the perceived immediate threat—heavily influence police use of force involving men in the criminal justice system.
Key Factors Influencing Use of Force
• Suspect Resistance and Behavior: Research consistently shows that active or aggressive physical resistance by a suspect is the strongest predictor of higher levels of police force.
• Legal Standards (Graham v. Connor): Under U.S. constitutional law, force must be objectively reasonable based on the Graham Factors, which include the severity of the crime, whether the suspect poses an immediate threat to officers or others, and whether the suspect is actively resisting or attempting to evade arrest.
• Demographics and Intersecting Identities: Statistical studies note that demographic variables like age, race, and socioeconomic status intersect with sex; young men, particularly racial minorities or those with mental health or substance abuse challenges, experience higher statistical rates of police force encounters.
• Situational Context: The ratio of suspects to officers, environmental stressors, and the presence of weapons heavily shape the split-second tactical decisions made by law enforcement.
