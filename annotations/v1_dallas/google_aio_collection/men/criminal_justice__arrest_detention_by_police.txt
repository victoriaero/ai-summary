Police arrest and detention decisions for men in the criminal justice system are primarily influenced by situational conditions, suspect demeanor, neighborhood context, and legal or extralegal characteristics.
Situational and Legal Factors
• Offense Severity: Officers have less discretion when handling severe or violent felonies, making arrest much more likely regardless of the suspect's background.
• Presence of Victims: Incidents involving active victims or complainants who wish to press charges frequently result in formal arrest.
• Suspect Demeanor: Hostile, uncooperative, or aggressive behavior by a male suspect often escalates an encounter from a warning or citation to physical detention or arrest.
• Criminal History: A documented prior record increases an officer's perception of risk and blameworthiness, raising the likelihood of booking.
Officer and Departmental Characteristics
• Responding Personnel: Research highlighted by the Bureau of Justice Assistance indicates that incidents involving a higher number of responding officers, female officers, or college-educated officers statistically show a greater likelihood of ending in an arrest.
• Discretion and Policies: Mandatory arrest laws (such as in certain domestic dispute calls) remove officer discretion and mandate detention.
Neighborhood and Environmental Context
• High-Crime Areas: Intensive police deployment and targeted patrol strategies in specific neighborhoods increase the rate at which minor or low-level offenses are detected and acted upon.
• Racial and Demographic Disparities: Studies show that arrests occur at higher rates in impoverished or racially diverse neighborhoods, reflecting systemic patterns in deployment and community interactions.
