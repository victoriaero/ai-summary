Men transition from stable housing into homelessness primarily due to economic hardship, job instability, and personal or relational crises.
Economic and Financial Factors
• Job Loss and Underemployment: Sudden termination, reduced work hours, or stagnant wages make it difficult to pay rising rents.
• Severe Rent Burden: Spending more than half of income on housing leaves men with no financial cushion for emergencies like car repairs or medical bills.
• Poverty: A lack of well-paying employment opportunities keeps low-income individuals in persistent financial instability.
Personal and Health Challenges
• Substance Use Disorders: Unmanaged addiction frequently strains personal resources and relationships, leading to housing loss.
• Mental Health Conditions: Untreated issues like depression or severe psychological distress disrupt employment and daily functioning.
• Physical Health Crises: Chronic illness or disabilities can result in unmanageable medical debt and job termination.
Systemic and Life Disruptions
• Justice System Re-entry: Men leaving jail or prison often face strict landlord discrimination and a lack of transitional support.
• Relationship Breakdowns: Divorce, family separation, or the weakening of informal support networks remove vital co-living financial safety nets.
• Inadequate Institutional Discharge: Leaving hospitals, military service, or institutional care without proper housing placement contributes significantly to risk.
