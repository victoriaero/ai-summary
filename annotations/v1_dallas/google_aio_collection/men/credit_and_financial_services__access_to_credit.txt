Standard financial and credit scoring factors—such as payment history, debt levels, and credit history length—primarily determine access to credit for men, though broader socioeconomic elements also play a role.
Key Credit Scoring Factors
• Payment history: Paying bills on time accounts for roughly 35% of a standard FICO score and heavily influences loan approval.
• Amounts owed: Total debt and credit utilization ratios dictate how much additional risk a lender perceives.
• Length of credit history: Longer histories with established accounts offer lenders more data to evaluate reliability.
• Credit mix and new credit: Holding a diverse portfolio of credit types (installment loans, credit cards) alongside a low frequency of recent hard inquiries supports better access.
Socioeconomic and Systemic Influences
• Income and financial capacity: A borrower's stable income, employment status, and debt-to-income ratio directly measure their capacity to repay debt.
• Early-life and geographic environment: Research indicates that childhood environment, neighborhood upward mobility, and local socioeconomic factors indirectly shape adult financial behaviors and long-term credit health.
• Algorithmic calibration: While traditional credit scoring models are legally gender-neutral and do not use gender as a direct input, studies suggest that predictive efficacy and scoring outcomes can exhibit nuanced distributional differences across demographic lines.
