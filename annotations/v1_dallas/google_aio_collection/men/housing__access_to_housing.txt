Single men face distinct barriers to housing stability, driven by allocation disparities, fewer gender-specific emergency resources, and higher rates of criminal justice involvement.
Key Factors Influencing Access
• Resource and Funding Allocation: Although a majority of individuals experiencing homelessness are male, many shelter beds and targeted interventions prioritize women, families, and domestic violence survivors.
• Stricter Shelter Requirements: Single men frequently face higher entry barriers, limited general shelter capacity, and fewer transitional housing options compared to family-focused programs.
• Criminal Justice Involvement: Men experiencing homelessness have a higher likelihood of interactions with law enforcement due to policies criminalizing public or street homelessness, which obstructs paths to stable housing.
• Health and Behavioral Factors: Chronic homelessness among men is frequently compounded by co-occurring structural vulnerabilities, untreated psychiatric illnesses, or substance use disorders.
• Social Isolation: Men often experience higher rates of relational disconnection and a lack of informal safety networks, reducing their access to temporary shared housing during a crisis.
