Financial stability, employment status, personal health, and legal history are the primary factors that influence housing stability for men.
Economic and Financial Factors
• Income and Job Security: Low wages, erratic work hours, or job loss make it difficult to pay rent or mortgages on time.
• Housing Cost Burden: Spending more than 30%—or severely more than 50%—of monthly income on housing leaves little room for emergencies, increasing the risk of eviction or foreclosure.
• Savings and Credit: A lack of savings limits options for security deposits, utility setup costs, and unexpected home or rental repairs.
Health and Wellness
• Physical and Mental Health: Chronic illnesses or disabilities can limit a man's ability to work consistently or maintain a clean, safe living environment.
• Substance Use and Mental Illness: Co-occurring severe mental illness and substance use disorders pose significant barriers, though targeted engagement with support services like Assertive Community Treatment greatly improves stability.
Legal and Systemic Barriers
• Incarceration History: Past involvement in the criminal justice system often leads to landlord discrimination and disqualification from public housing programs.
• Eviction Records: Prior evictions restrict future rental options and increase the risk of recurrent housing crises or homelessness.
Social and Personal Support
• Social Isolation: A lack of reliable friends or family to lean on during temporary financial hardship removes a critical safety net against losing housing.
• Doubling Up Dynamics: Staying temporarily with friends or relatives can help short-term, but overcrowding or conflicts over household expenses can lead to sudden displacement.
