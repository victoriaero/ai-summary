Several interconnected factors influence harassment and bullying for men in education, affecting both male students and male educators.
Factors Affecting Male Students
• Gender Norms and Stereotypes: According to UNESCO, failure to conform to traditional masculine gender norms (such as exhibiting vulnerability, lack of physical aggressiveness, or specific aesthetic/cultural preferences) often triggers "gender policing" and targeted harassment.
• Physical and Visible Aggression: Research on bullying dynamics indicates that boys are statistically more likely to experience and perpetrate direct, physical, and verbal bullying compared to the indirect or relational tactics more commonly reported among girls.
• Power Hierarchies and Status: Peer victimisation among young men frequently stems from attempts to establish dominance, social status, or conformity within classroom hierarchies.
Factors Affecting Male Educators and Staff
• Institutional and Environmental Stressors: High-stakes testing, under-resourced environments, and organizational crises increase overall stress levels in schools, which correlates with higher rates of verbal threats or hostile behaviors directed at staff by students and parents.
• Perceptions of Authority and Vulnerability: Male teachers can face unique challenges regarding support systems, administrative backing, and social expectations to manage aggressive incidents single-handedly without acknowledging personal well-being or burnout.
