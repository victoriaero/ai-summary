Several behavioral, social, and psychological factors influence the hiring and recruitment outcomes for men in the labor market.
Application Behavior and Confidence
• Broader Application Criteria: Men are statistically more likely to apply for a job when they meet roughly 60% of the listed qualifications, whereas women often wait until they meet closer to 100% of the requirements. This broader net results in a higher overall volume of applications submitted by men.
• Active Networking and Referrals: Men are proactive in seeking professional referrals. Because employee referrals heavily influence hiring decisions, asking connections for introductions gives male candidates an advantage in getting noticed.
Recruiter Perceptions and Bias
• Perceived Competence and Traits: Research on hiring tendencies indicates that decision-makers sometimes associate men with competence-driven or leadership-oriented traits during initial profile screenings.
• Recruiter Demographics: Studies show that male recruiters or hiring managers sometimes display an in-group preference, contacting or favoring male applicants more frequently in mixed-gender roles.
• Stereotypical Appearance: Candidates who project a stereotypically masculine look or present traits aligned with traditional leadership stereotypes can receive favorable ratings in competitive application reviews.
