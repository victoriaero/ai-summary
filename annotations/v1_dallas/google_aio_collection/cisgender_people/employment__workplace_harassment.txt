Several key organizational, cultural, and individual factors influence the risk and occurrence of workplace harassment for cisgender individuals (people whose gender identity aligns with the sex they were assigned at birth), particularly when intersecting with gender dynamics, sexual orientation, or other characteristics.According to data and guidance from the U.S. Equal Employment Opportunity Commission (EEOC) and workplace sociological research, the primary risk factors include:Homogenous or Imbalanced Workforces: Workplaces with a historic lack of diversity or those dominated by a single gender can foster environments where majority employees feel threatened by perceived differences or where minority employees (such as cisgender women or cisgender sexual minorities) face vulnerability and isolation.Permissive and Hierarchical Cultures: Environments with permissive leadership—where reporting harassment is perceived as pointless due to a lack of investigation or punishment—are primary predictors of harassment. Hierarchical structures further exacerbate this by placing immense power in the hands of supervisors over subordinates.Non-Conformity to Traditional Norms: Workplaces characterized by "rough and tumble," crude, or demeaning banter often promote workplace norms that devalue certain individuals. Cisgender employees who do not conform to traditional gender presentations or stereotypes (including cisgender LGBQ+ workers) experience heightened rates of verbal, physical, or sexual harassment compared to their heterosexual peers.Coarsened Social Discourse: Heated societal discussions regarding current events outside the organization can bleed into the workplace, making inappropriate or harassing conduct appear more acceptable or common among co-workers.Intersection with Sexual Orientation: Cisgender individuals who are lesbian, gay, or bisexual (LGBQ+) report significantly higher lifetime rates of workplace harassment and sexual harassment compared to heterosexual cisgender peers, often leading to "covering" behaviors or hiding their identities to avoid hostility.If you would like, let me know:Are you looking for factors related to a specific industry or type of workplace?Do you want strategies on prevention and organizational policies?
