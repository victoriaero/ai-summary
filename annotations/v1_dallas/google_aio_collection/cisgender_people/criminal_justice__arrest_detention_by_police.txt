Police decisions to arrest or detain cisgender individuals are driven by a combination of legal requirements, situational dynamics, demographic factors, and organizational policies. While legal criteria serve as the primary baseline, police officers possess significant discretion, which is heavily influenced by extra-legal variables.
1. Legal Factors
Legal variables are the most consistent predictors of arrest across all demographics:
• Severity of the Offense: Felonies and violent crimes drastically increase the likelihood of arrest compared to misdemeanors or infractions.
• Strength of Evidence: The presence of physical evidence, reliable eyewitness accounts, or direct officer observation strongly compels an arrest.
• Criminal History: Individuals with an active warrant, prior convictions, or existing flags in the police database face a much higher probability of being detained.
2. Situational Dynamics
The specific environment and context of the police encounter alter the outcome:
• Suspect Demeanor: Hostility, resistance, or a perceived lack of cooperation significantly increases the likelihood of arrest or the use of force. Conversely, compliance or displays of distress (such as crying) can sometimes elicit leniency.
• Victim Preference: In incidents involving an active victim (e.g., domestic disputes or assault), the victim's explicit desire to press charges heavily sways the officer's decision.
• Presence of Responding Officers: Incidents with a higher number of responding officers or the presence of an supervising officer frequently correlate with higher arrest rates.
3. Demographic & Intersectional Factors
Criminological research highlights how race, socioeconomic status, and cisgender variations affect outcomes:
• The Chivalry Hypothesis vs. Evil Woman Thesis: Historically, cisgender women have experienced lower arrest rates than cisgender men for similar low-level offenses due to systemic perceptions of vulnerability (the chivalry hypothesis). However, if a cisgender woman’s behavior violates traditional gender norms (e.g., displaying extreme aggression), they may face harsher treatment (the evil woman thesis).
• Race and Ethnicity: Cisgender people of color, particularly Black and Hispanic individuals, face disproportionately higher rates of stops, searches, and arrests compared to their white counterparts under identical legal circumstances.
• Socioeconomic and Neighborhood Context: Arrests are statistically more frequent in low-income or historically marginalized neighborhoods. Police deployments are often denser in these areas, raising the statistical frequency of encounters.
4. Organizational & Systemic Factors
Departmental structure dictates localized enforcement habits:
• Mandatory Arrest Policies: In many jurisdictions, laws mandate an arrest during domestic violence calls if probable cause exists, stripping away individual officer discretion.
• Departmental Priorities: Institutional pressure to meet specific enforcement quotas, combat local drug epidemics, or clear specific precincts directly increases detentions for targeted offenses.
