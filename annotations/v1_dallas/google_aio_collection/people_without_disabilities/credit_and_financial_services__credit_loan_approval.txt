Lenders evaluate standard financial metrics to determine credit and loan approval for all applicants.
Core Factors Influencing Approval
• Credit Score and History: Your credit score is the primary metric lenders use to gauge your risk of default. They review your credit report for past payment performance, defaults, bankruptcies, and length of credit history.
• Income Level: Lenders verify your regular earnings to ensure you have sufficient incoming cash flow to cover regular living expenses plus the new loan payments.
• Debt-to-Income (DTI) Ratio: This measures the percentage of your monthly gross income that goes toward paying debts (like mortgages, student loans, or credit cards). A lower DTI ratio signals a higher capacity to take on new debt.
• Employment History: Consistent, stable employment history shows lenders that your income source is reliable over the life of the loan.
• Collateral or Down Payment: For secured loans—such as mortgages or auto loans—the value of the asset you pledge as collateral or the size of your down payment helps offset the lender's risk if you fail to repay.
