College access and admission for students without disabilities are primarily influenced by academic preparation, socioeconomic status, family background, and institutional admission policies.
Academic Preparation and Performance
• GPA and Course Rigor: High school grade point averages (GPA) and enrollment in rigorous courses like Advanced Placement (AP) or International Baccalaureate (IB) programs are top predictors of admission.
• Standardized Testing: Scores from exams like the SAT or ACT historically play a major role in evaluating college readiness, though many institutions have adopted test-optional policies.
Socioeconomic and Demographic Factors
• Family Income and Wealth: Higher household income provides better access to private tutoring, test preparation courses, and college counseling resources.
• Parental Education Level: First-generation college students often face informational barriers regarding application deadlines, financial aid, and campus cultures compared to peers with college-educated parents.
• Race and Geographic Location: Systematic disparities in K-12 funding and neighborhood resources can affect the quality of schooling and extracurricular opportunities available to students.
Institutional and Non-Academic Factors
• Extracurricular Activities: Leadership roles, community service, sports, and arts involvement help applicants demonstrate well-rounded skills and personal character.
• Admission Policies: Legacy status, institutional priorities, geographic diversity goals, and early decision or early action programs significantly tilt acceptance odds.
