For students without disabilities, harassment and bullying in educational settings are primarily influenced by social, individual, and environmental risk factors rather than disability status. According to guidelines from StopBullying.gov, common influencing factors include:
• Perception of Difference: Students who are viewed as different from their peers—such as in their appearance, clothing, body weight, race, religion, or sexual orientation—face a higher risk of targeted harassment.
• Perceived Vulnerability: Being perceived as physically weak, passive, or less able to defend oneself creates an imbalance of power that bullies exploit.
• Social Isolation: Socially isolated youth or those with very few supportive friends lack peer protection, making them more vulnerable targets.
• Emotional and Mental Health Status: Students who experience anxiety, depression, or low self-esteem are statistically at a higher risk of peer victimization.
• Environmental and Institutional Factors: Intolerant school climates, lack of adult supervision, or unaddressed behavioral infractions by staff can enable bullying by signaling low risk for the aggressor.
