Workplace harassment for people without disabilities is heavily influenced by organizational culture, workforce demographics, and broader social dynamics as outlined by the U.S. Equal Employment Opportunity Commission (EEOC).
Key Risk Factors
• Homogenous Workforces: Workplaces with a historic lack of diversity or isolated minority employees can create environments where majority workers feel threatened by differences or where minority individuals feel vulnerable.
• Non-Conformity to Workplace Norms: Cultures that tolerate crude, demeaning, or "raunchy" banter—often seen in rough-and-tumble or single-sex-dominated settings—promote behavior that devalues individuals.
• Cultural and Language Differences: Segregated workforces or the presence of diverse groups with different nationalities can lead to exploitation or friction if workers do not understand their rights or workplace expectations.
• Coarsened Social Discourse: Heated discussions of national or local current events outside the office can bleed into the workplace, making inappropriate or harassing conduct seem more acceptable.
• Isolated Work Situations: Work areas, shifts, or remote settings far removed from leadership oversight increase the likelihood of inappropriate targeting.
• Power Imbalances: Harassers in positions of authority make employees less likely to report misconduct due to fears of retaliation or organizational inaction.
