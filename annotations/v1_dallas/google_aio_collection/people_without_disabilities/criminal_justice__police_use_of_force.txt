The most predictive factor influencing police use of force for people without disabilities is the level of physical resistance and aggressive behavior shown by the suspect during the encounter.
Suspect Behavior and Legal Variables
• Level of resistance: Active aggression or attempts to resist arrest strongly increase the likelihood and degree of force used by officers.
• Presence of a weapon: Being armed or suspected of carrying a weapon elevates the perceived threat, leading to higher levels of physical or lethal force.
• Substance use: Intoxication or active drug and alcohol use during an incident frequently escalates the tension and risk of physical force.
Demographics and Extralegal Factors
• Race and ethnicity: Studies note that individuals belonging to certain racial or ethnic minority groups often experience a higher statistical likelihood of police force, even when controlling for other variables.
• Age and sex: Younger individuals and males are statistically more likely to experience higher levels of police force due to behavioral correlations and enforcement patterns.
Environmental and Departmental Factors
• Neighborhood context: Police encounters occurring in areas with higher rates of violent crime or concentrated economic disadvantage often feature elevated levels of force.
• Department policies: Agency guidelines, supervisory practices, and managerial organization shape how officers are trained to de-escalate or respond to perceived threats.
