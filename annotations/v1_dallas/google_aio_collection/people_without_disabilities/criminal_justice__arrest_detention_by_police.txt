For individuals without disabilities in the criminal justice system, police decisions regarding arrest and detention are primarily driven by a combination of legal, situational, demographic, and contextual factors. Because officers exercise significant discretion, these decisions are shaped by the immediate environment of the encounter rather than accommodations or disability-related behavioral interpretations.
According to criminological research, the main factors influencing arrest and detention for the general population are categorized below:
Legal Factors (The Focal Concerns)
• Offense Severity: This is the strongest predictor of arrest. Officers have very little discretionary leeway when a felony or violent crime has occurred compared to minor misdemeanors or infractions.
• Prior Criminal Record: Individuals with a documented criminal history face a much higher probability of being arrested and booked rather than released with a citation.
• Statutory Mandates: Departmental or state policies, such as mandatory arrest laws for domestic violence incidents, remove officer discretion entirely.
Situational Dynamics
• Presence and Demands of a Victim: Criminological studies, such as research published in the Journal of Criminal Justice, indicate that arrests are far more likely when a victim is present at the scene and explicitly requests that charges be pressed.
• Suspect Demeanor: Hostility, disrespect, or a lack of compliance toward law enforcement heavily influences an officer’s decision to escalate an encounter to a formal arrest.
• Strength of Evidence: The immediacy and quality of physical evidence or credible eyewitness testimony directly determine whether probable cause is established on the spot.
Extralegal & Socioeconomic Demographics
• Socioeconomic Status: An individual's perceived social class strongly correlates with arrest outcomes. Data compiled by the U.S. Department of Justice indicates that individuals perceived as lower-class face significantly higher arrest rates during stops than those perceived as middle or upper-class.
• Racial and Ethnic Disparities: Extensive documentation from organizations like the Prison Policy Initiative demonstrates that Black and Hispanic individuals face disproportionately higher stop, search, and arrest rates compared to white individuals for identical offenses, such as drug possession.
Environmental and Organizational Context
• Neighborhood Demographics: Arrests are statistically more frequent in areas with higher concentrations of poverty or larger minority populations, even when controlling for the specific nature of the incident.
• Officer Characteristics: The background of the responding officer plays a role. Multi-level modeling reveals that female officers and college-educated officers are statistically more likely to conduct formal arrests during dispatched incidents.
• Number of Responding Officers: Incidents that draw a larger police presence or backup are more prone to ending in an arrest or physical detention.
