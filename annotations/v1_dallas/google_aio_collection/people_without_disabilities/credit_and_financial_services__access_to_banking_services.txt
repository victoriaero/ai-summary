Access to traditional banking and credit services for the general population is primarily influenced by income levels, credit history, geographic location, and technological access.
Income and Financial Stability
• Low income: Earning a low wage makes it hard to pay high monthly bank fees or maintain required minimum balances.
• Paycheck-to-paycheck living: Having little to no emergency savings can lead to overdrafts or account closures.
• Alternative services: People with low cash flow often rely on costly alternatives like check cashers and payday lenders instead of standard banks.
Credit History and Scoring
• Lack of credit history: Young adults or people new to the financial system often lack the credit score needed to qualify for traditional loans or credit cards.
• Poor credit rating: Past financial hardships or defaults create a barrier to getting approved for affordable credit.
• Co-signer availability: Younger or low-income consumers often need a co-signer to build a positive credit file.
Geographic and Physical Location
• Banking deserts: Rural areas and low-income urban neighborhoods often lack physical bank branches, making in-person banking difficult.
• Transportation: A lack of reliable public transit or personal transport limits visits to distant bank locations.
Technology and Digital Literacy
• Internet access: Online and mobile banking require a stable internet connection and a smartphone, which may not be available in all households.
• Digital skills: A lack of familiarity with digital interfaces can prevent people from using modern financial apps safely.
Demographics and Language
• Immigrant status: Moving to a new country without a domestic credit history or local identification creates major hurdles.
• Language barriers: Financial documents or online platforms that do not offer multiple languages block full participation.
