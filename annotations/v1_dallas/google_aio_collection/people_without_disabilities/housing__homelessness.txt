The primary factor influencing homelessness for people without disabilities is the severe shortage of affordable housing combined with stagnant wages that fail to keep pace with rising rent costs.
According to the National Alliance to End Homelessness, broader economic and structural forces drive housing instability rather than individual circumstances alone.
Economic and Structural Factors
• High Housing Costs: Rent prices frequently exceed what low-income households earn, leaving individuals vulnerable when unexpected expenses arise.
• Poverty and Unemployment: Job loss, reduced work hours, business closures, or lack of local employment opportunities can quickly exhaust personal savings and lead to missed rent or mortgage payments.
• Decline in Public Assistance: Insufficient federal funding, underfunded rental assistance programs, and declining public safety net benefits reduce the cushion available for families and individuals facing financial shocks.
Personal and Social Circumstances
• Domestic Violence: Fleeing an abusive household forces many individuals and children out of stable environments with nowhere else to go.
• Family and Social Disruption: Relational breakdown, eviction from a shared family home, or a lack of an informal support network (such as friends or family who can provide temporary "couch-surfing") can trigger sudden homelessness.
• Systemic Disparities: Marginalization and racial inequities create distinct demographic disadvantages, meaning certain communities experience higher rates of housing instability than the general population.
