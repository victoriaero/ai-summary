Access to government safety-net benefits for individuals without disabilities—such as cash assistance or food support—is primarily influenced by income limits, work rules, and administrative hurdles.
Key Economic Factors
• Income and Assets: Most programs are means-tested. Applicants must prove their income and savings fall below specific state or federal limits.
• Work Requirements: Programs like Temporary Assistance for Needy Families (TANF) often require adults to look for work immediately or participate in job training to keep benefits.
Administrative and Structural Barriers
• Administrative Burden: Complex application forms, long wait times, and frequent recertifications make it hard to apply and stay enrolled.
• Information Gaps: Many eligible people do not know which programs exist or how to qualify due to confusing rules and poor outreach.
• Geography: People living in rural or remote areas face shortages of local offices and poor internet access, which lowers enrollment rates.
