Several key demographic, economic, and systemic factors influence the use of preventive healthcare services for people in the general population (without disabilities):
Demographic and Personal Factors
• Age: Older adults often utilize routine preventive screenings more frequently, though specific age-based guidelines dictate screening rates.
• Gender: Statistics consistently show that females utilize adult preventive health services at higher rates than males.
• Race and Ethnicity: Racial and ethnic minority groups experience lower utilization rates of guideline-recommended preventive services compared to white populations.
• Health Status: Individuals with existing chronic diseases or active health conditions are significantly more likely to engage in preventive care and routine monitoring.
Socioeconomic and Financial Factors
• Income and Salary: Higher monthly salary and household income generally correlate with better access to and utilization of preventive care.
• Insurance Coverage: Having health insurance—and understanding how preventive services are covered—directly increases screening and wellness visit rates compared to having limited medical aid or being uninsured.
• Education Level: Higher health literacy and education levels improve a person's ability to perceive, seek, and engage with preventive healthcare recommendations.
Systemic and Environmental Factors
• Geography and Urbanization: Urban residents often have different access levels compared to rural residents, with rural populations sometimes facing provider shortages or travel barriers.
• Primary Care Access: Having a designated primary care physician (PCP) and an ongoing source of regular care greatly increases the likelihood of receiving routine check-ups and vaccines.
