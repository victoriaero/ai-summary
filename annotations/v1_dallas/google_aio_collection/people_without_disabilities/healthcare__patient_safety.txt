Patient safety for people without specific physical, cognitive, or sensory disabilities is shaped by system-wide organizational factors, human behavior, and individual patient engagement.
Human and Team Factors
• Communication: Clear, open communication among doctors, nurses, and family members prevents medical errors.
• Teamwork: Coordinated efforts across healthcare units reduce risks during critical interventions.
• Staff Well-being: Provider fatigue, burnout, and high stress levels directly lower safety performance.
• Cognitive Bias: Clinical misjudgments or delayed diagnoses can happen when providers rush or rely on assumptions.
System and Technological Factors
• Staffing Levels: Adequate nurse-to-patient ratios ensure proper monitoring and timely care delivery.
• Health Information Systems: Functional electronic health records (EHR) and safe barcode medication administration systems prevent prescription or dosing mistakes.
• Safety Culture: Facilities that reward staff for reporting errors openly—rather than using a blame model—fix hazards faster.
• Resource Availability: Access to reliable medical tools, standard protective equipment, and functional infrastructure protects patients from hospital-acquired harm.
Patient-Related Factors
• Health Literacy: Patients who understand their medical instructions and diagnoses follow treatment plans more accurately.
• Active Engagement: Individuals who ask questions, verify medications, and participate in their care rounds reduce adverse events.
• Adherence: Sticking to prescribed medication and discharge guidance prevents readmissions or complications.
