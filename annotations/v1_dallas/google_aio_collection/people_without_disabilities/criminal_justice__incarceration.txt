Incarceration in the general criminal justice system is primarily driven by socioeconomic disadvantages, racial disparities, prior criminal history, and strict sentencing policies.
Key Influencing Factors
• Socioeconomic Status: Poverty restricts access to private legal counsel and prevents defendants from paying cash bail, which often results in prolonged pretrial detention and higher pressure to accept plea deals.
• Racial Disparities: Systemic biases and heavy policing in minority neighborhoods lead to disproportionate rates of stops, searches, arrests, and harsher sentencing lengths for people of color.
• Criminal History: Judges rely heavily on prior convictions and past criminal records when deciding whether to grant probation or impose a prison term.
• Offense Type and Severity: Violent offenses carry mandatory prison sentences, while rigid drug policies and mandatory minimum laws ensure incarceration even for many non-violent offenses.
• Quality of Legal Representation: Public defenders often face heavy caseloads and limited resources, which can restrict the time available to investigate cases or challenge evidence effectively.
