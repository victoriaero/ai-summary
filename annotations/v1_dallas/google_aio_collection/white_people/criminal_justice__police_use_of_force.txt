AI OVERVIEW TEXT

Police use of force involving White individuals in the criminal justice system is influenced by several situational, environmental, and institutional factors. While much research focuses on racial disparities—noting that people of color face higher rates of force—studies on general use of force identify core variables that shape police actions across demographics, including White suspects:
• Suspect Behavior and Resistance: The immediate level of threat, aggression, or physical resistance shown by an individual is the strongest predictor of whether an officer uses force and the level of force applied.
• Nature of the Incident: Encounters involving violent crimes, weapons, or felony calls inherently increase the likelihood of police using coercive or physical force.
• Environmental and Neighborhood Context: High-crime rates, localized economic disadvantage, and community instability influence the overall intensity of policing and the frequency of high-stress encounters in specific areas.
• Officer Discretion and Training: Individual officer decision-making, de-escalation training, and adherence to departmental use-of-force policies dictate how quickly and severely force is deployed in a given situation.
• Substance Use or Mental Health Crisis: Encounters where a subject is perceived to be under the influence of drugs/alcohol or experiencing a mental health crisis frequently elevate the risk of physical intervention.
