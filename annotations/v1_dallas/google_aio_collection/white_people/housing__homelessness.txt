Higher rental housing costs and general housing affordability crises are primary drivers influencing homelessness among White individuals.
While research shows that systemic racism and historical housing discrimination disproportionately impact Black, Indigenous, and other people of color (BIPOC), studies analyzing structural conditions affecting White homelessness point to several core economic and housing market factors:
• Housing Costs and Rent Pressures: Increases in rental housing costs show a strong positive association with higher rates of homelessness across White populations, demonstrating that housing affordability is a fundamental bottleneck.
• Poverty and Economic Hardship: Individual and household poverty, lack of a living wage, and low income levels significantly elevate housing instability and the risk of eviction or homelessness.
• Weak Social Safety Nets: Inadequate public assistance programs, lack of robust rental vouchers or housing subsidies, and gaps in healthcare or disability support systems leave low-income White individuals vulnerable when facing financial shocks.
• Employment and Labor Market Shifts: Fluctuations in race-specific employment rates and local job availability directly correlate with housing security for White workers.
