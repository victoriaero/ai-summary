Access to housing for White households is primarily influenced by historical wealth accumulation, intergenerational financial support, and systemic policy advantages.
Key Influencing Factors
• Intergenerational Wealth and Inheritance: White households benefit significantly more from family gifts, down-payment assistance, and inheritances, which lower barriers to home purchase and ownership stability.
• Historical Policy and Lending Advantages: Past federal housing programs, federally insured mortgages (such as FHA and G.I. Bill benefits), and suburban development subsidies historically favored White families while excluding people of color.
• Neighborhood Resource Concentration: Predominantly White neighborhoods often concentrate higher demand, greater property appreciation, and better public goods like funded schools and job access, reinforcing housing advantages.
• Zoning and Land-Use Regulations: Exclusionary zoning practices and local development restrictions in many affluent or suburban areas have historically preserved property values and limited affordable housing options, impacting overall market entry.
