For White people in the U.S. criminal justice system, incarceration is primarily driven by criminal history, offense severity, and socioeconomic factors rather than racial bias.
Key Factors Influencing Incarceration
• Prior Criminal History: A key baseline for sentencing guidelines across state and federal courts is an individual's past record. Prior convictions heavily dictate whether a judge imposes a prison sentence or alternative options like probation.
• Offense Severity and Type: The commission of violent offenses or high-level felonies carries statutory mandatory minimums or sentencing enhancements that restrict judicial discretion and make incarceration mandatory.
• Socioeconomic Status: Low income heavily impacts a defendant's outcome. Individuals who cannot afford private legal representation and must rely on underfunded public defenders, or those unable to pay cash bail for pretrial release, face higher rates of pre-trial detention and pressure to accept unfavorable plea deals.
• Geographic and State Policies: Incarceration rates vary significantly by state based on local sentencing laws, prosecutorial practices, and the presence of habitual offender statutes.
