AI OVERVIEW TEXT
============================================================

Preventive care utilization among White people in healthcare is primarily driven by socioeconomic status, insurance coverage, having a usual source of care, and overall health literacy.
Key Influencing Factors
• Insurance Coverage and Type: Having health insurance significantly increases the likelihood of receiving routine checkups and screenings, and coverage type (such as Medicare Advantage versus traditional Medicare) creates variations in preventive service usage.
• Usual Source of Care: White adults with an established primary care provider or a regular clinic are substantially more likely to receive age-appropriate preventive services like blood pressure checks, cholesterol screenings, and cancer immunizations.
• Socioeconomic Status: Higher household income and higher educational attainment are strongly correlated with greater access to resources, increased utilization of elective preventive measures, and reduced cost barriers to care.
• Perception of the Healthcare System: Research indicates that insured White individuals frequently report higher levels of trust and lower perceptions of discrimination within the medical system, which positively correlates with ongoing engagement with providers.
