AI OVERVIEW TEXT
============================================================

For White individuals in the U.S. criminal justice system, police arrest and detention decisions are primarily influenced by factors such as prior criminal history, the severity and type of the alleged offense, and the specific geographic or neighborhood context of the incident.
Key Influencing Factors
• Criminal History: Past criminal records and prior justice system contact strongly predict decisions regarding custodial bookings and pretrial detention for White defendants.
• Offense Severity and Type: The legal classification of the alleged crime—such as violent offenses, property crimes, or drug-related violations—directly impacts whether police execute an arrest rather than issuing a citation or warning.
• Geographic and Neighborhood Context: Spatial concentration of crime, local policing strategies, and neighborhood crime rates heavily affect overall police presence and the frequency of enforcement actions in specific areas.
• Pretrial and Financial Factors: During detention decisions following an arrest, ability to pay financial conditions or monetary bail heavily influences whether an individual is released or held.
