Socioeconomic status, geographic location, and credit history are the primary factors that influence access to banking and financial services for White individuals, similarly to general consumer populations.
Key Factors Influencing Access
• Income and Wealth: Higher household income and asset ownership directly increase the likelihood of holding traditional bank accounts and qualifying for mainstream credit products like mortgages.
• Credit Scores: Traditional financial institutions rely heavily on credit scores (such as FICO) to screen applicants; individuals with subprime or non-existent credit histories face higher barriers or exclusion.
• Geographic Proximity: Living in urbanized areas with high branch density improves physical access to traditional banks, whereas rural or economically depressed areas may lack nearby physical infrastructure.
• Digital and Technological Access: Access to internet and mobile banking tools is essential for modern account management, meaning those without reliable connectivity face functional hurdles.
