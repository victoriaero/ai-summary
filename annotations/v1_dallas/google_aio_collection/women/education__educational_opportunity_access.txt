Multiple socioeconomic, cultural, and structural factors significantly influence educational opportunities and access for women.
Socio-Cultural and Traditional Norms
• Gender roles: Families in many regions prioritize household chores or caregiving responsibilities for girls over formal schooling.
• Early marriage and pregnancy: Cultural expectations surrounding early marriage or adolescent pregnancy frequently force young women to drop out of school prematurely.
• Societal bias: Traditional beliefs about the perceived value or appropriateness of higher education for women can discourage female enrollment.
Economic Barriers
• Poverty and costs: Direct costs (tuition, books, supplies) and opportunity costs (lost household or labor output) make schooling prohibitive for low-income families.
• Resource allocation: When household resources are scarce, families may choose to invest limited funds in the education of male children rather than female children.
Structural and Environmental Factors
• Safety and infrastructure: Lack of secure transportation, unsafe school environments, and inadequate sanitation facilities (such as private restrooms for menstruating girls) prevent regular attendance.
• Representation: A shortage of female teachers and mentors in schools can negatively impact female engagement and retention.
• Geographic location: Rural or isolated areas often lack nearby higher-education or secondary institutions, limiting physical access.
