AI OVERVIEW TEXT
============================================================

Several structural, economic, and socio-cultural factors influence women's access to banking and credit services.
Economic and Income Factors
- Income disparities: Women globally earn less than men on average, which limits their capacity to save, build formal credit histories, and meet the minimum income thresholds required by traditional financial institutions .
- Lack of collateral: Traditional lending criteria often require land or property ownership. Because women are less likely to hold formal property titles, securing business or personal loans becomes difficult.
Institutional and Regulatory Barriers
- Prudential regulations: Strict risk-management and capital frameworks (such as Basel standards) can indirectly force banks to impose tougher credit requirements, disproportionately filtering out individuals without formal employment or credit records—a group where women are heavily represented .
- Discriminatory practices: Some traditional banking procedures historically demand a male co-signer, husband's signature, or male family member approval before granting credit or opening accounts.
- Complex documentation: Stringent Know Your Customer (KYC) rules and paperwork requirements create procedural hurdles for women who operate in informal economic sectors or lack formal identification.
Socio-Cultural Norms
- Gender bias and stereotypes: Financial institutions sometimes operate under unverified assumptions that women present higher default risks.
- Mobility and time constraints: Social expectations often place primary domestic and childcare responsibilities on women, restricting their physical mobility to travel to bank branches.
Financial and Digital Literacy
- Knowledge gaps: Lower average scores in financial literacy assessments hinder women from navigating complex loan products, comparing interest rates, or engaging confidently with formal financial service providers.
- Digital divide: While financial technology (Fintech) and mobile banking help bypass physical branch barriers, disparities in smartphone ownership and digital access can still exclude marginalized or rural women.
