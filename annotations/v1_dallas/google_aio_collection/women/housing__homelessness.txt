Homelessness among women is driven by a combination of severe economic pressures, domestic trauma, and gender-specific caretaking responsibilities.
Economic and Structural Factors
- Affordable Housing Shortage: A lack of low-cost housing options remains a primary driver for housing loss across all populations.
- The Gender Wage Gap: Women often earn less than men, leaving them with fewer personal financial reserves to handle economic shocks.
- Caretaking Responsibilities: Women are disproportionately responsible for raising children and caring for aging relatives, which limits their working hours and capacity to generate income.
- Lack of Affordable Childcare: High childcare costs make it difficult for single mothers to maintain steady employment without spending most of their wages on care services.
Personal and Relational Circumstances
- Domestic and Sexual Violence: Abuse is a leading cause of homelessness for women, forcing many to flee unsafe home environments immediately without alternative financial resources.
- Fear of Child Protective Services: Many women delay or avoid seeking emergency shelter or housing assistance out of fear that their children will be removed due to their unhoused status.
- Relationship Breakdowns: Divorce, separation, or the death of a primary breadwinning partner can instantly destabilize a woman's housing security, particularly for older women on fixed incomes.
The "Hidden Homeless" Phenomenon
- Couch-Surfing and Temporary Living: Women are more likely to experience hidden homelessness by staying temporarily with friends, living in cars, or moving between family members rather than sleeping in public spaces . This makes standard counts under-represent the scale of female housing insecurity.
