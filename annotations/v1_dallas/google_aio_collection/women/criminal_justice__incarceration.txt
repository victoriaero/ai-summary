Socioeconomic disadvantage, high rates of past trauma, and drug or property offenses are the primary factors that influence the incarceration of women in the criminal justice system.
Key Factors Influencing Women's Incarceration
- Poverty and Economic Pressure: Many incarcerated women come from disadvantaged backgrounds where low income, unemployment, and financial instability drive survival-based or low-level economic crimes like shoplifting or minor property offenses .
- Trauma and Abuse: A large majority of incarcerated women have extensive personal histories of physical, sexual, or domestic abuse and violence, which strongly correlates with subsequent mental health challenges and justice involvement.
- Drug and Property Offenses: Women are disproportionately incarcerated for non-violent crimes compared to men, specifically drug and property offenses, often worsened by mandatory minimum sentencing and strict drug policies.
- Co-Offending and Relationships: Women frequently enter the criminal justice system due to their associations with male partners who engage in criminal behavior, sometimes facing charges related to co-offending.
- Racial Disparities: Women of color—particularly Black, Hispanic, and Native American women—face systemic disadvantages, higher rates of community violence, and harsher sentencing outcomes compared to white women.
