Women in education—whether serving as teachers, staff, or academic faculty—experience harassment and bullying influenced by a combination of gender dynamics, institutional structures, and power imbalances. Data from organizations like the Workplace Bullying Institute indicate that education and healthcare often report high concentrations of workplace hostility, with women disproportionately targeted.
Institutional and Organizational Factors
- Power Hierarchies: Junior faculty, early-career teachers, and non-tenured staff are especially vulnerable to top-down bullying and microaggressions from administrators, principals, or senior professors who control evaluations, promotions, and workloads .
- Permissive Culture: A lack of clear, consistently enforced anti-bullying policies or unresponsive human resource departments creates a vacuum of accountability, leaving targets feeling that reporting abuse will yield no change or provoke retaliation.
- Low Administrative Support: Poor leadership and a lack of institutional backing or social support from superiors strongly correlate with higher rates of unresolved workplace conflict and targeted hostility.
Gendered and Intra-Gender Dynamics
- Female-Dominated Workfields: Research on educational settings notes that despite being female-dominated, institutional spaces often feature intense intra-gender hostility—where women target other women through covert psychological bullying, social exclusion, or professional undermining .
- Scarcity and Competition: Perceived scarcity regarding promotions, tenure track positions, or leadership roles can drive aggressive behaviors, gatekeeping, and malicious peer competition.
- Sexual Harassment and Misogyny: Surveys by groups like Education Week and teacher unions show that a significant percentage of female educators face verbal harassment, physical threats, or sexual misconduct from students, parents, or colleagues who undermine female authority in the classroom.
Intersecting Vulnerabilities
- Marginalized Identities: Women of color, early-career educators, and LGBTQ+ staff experience compounded microaggressions and bias-motivated behaviors that intersect with gender, increasing their overall risk of targeted exclusion .
