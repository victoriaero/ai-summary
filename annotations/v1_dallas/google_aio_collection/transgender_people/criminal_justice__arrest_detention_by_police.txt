Transgender individuals face heightened risks of arrest and detention in the criminal justice system due to a combination of systemic bias, socioeconomic vulnerabilities, and targeted policing practices. [1, 2] 
According to research from the [Williams Institute](https://williamsinstitute.law.ucla.edu/) and advocacy organizations like the [Advocates for Trans Equality](https://transequality.org/), several core factors influence these disproportionate outcomes:
## Key Factors Influencing Arrest and Detention

* Implicit Bias and Officer Discretion: Law enforcement officers frequently exercise broad discretion based on gender presentation, leading to unjustified stops, harassment, or assumptions of illegal activity simply for being visibly transgender in public (often termed "walking while trans"). [1, 3, 4] 
* Criminalization of Survival and Poverty: Due to systemic discrimination in housing, employment, and education, transgender people—particularly transgender women of color—experience high rates of homelessness and are overrepresented in the underground economy, including survival sex work. Vague loitering and solicitation laws are frequently used to target and arrest them. [2, 3, 4] 
* Disproportionate Public and Police Entanglements: Studies show that transgender and gender-nonconforming individuals experience higher rates of interaction with police following calls for service, and those matching suspect descriptions face an elevated risk of subsequent arrest. [5] 
* Intersectional Discrimination: The overlapping impacts of racism, transphobia, and economic marginalization compound vulnerability, resulting in harsher enforcement actions and higher baseline rates of arrest for Black and Latine transgender individuals. [1, 6]
