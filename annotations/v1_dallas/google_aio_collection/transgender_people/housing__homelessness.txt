Transgender individuals experience homelessness at roughly eight times the rate of the general U.S. population, driven by overlapping economic, social, and systemic factors. [1] 
## Family Rejection and Lack of Support

* Family conflict: Rejection or abuse by family members is one of the leading causes of housing instability, particularly forcing transgender youth to run away, get kicked out, or leave home prematurely. [2, 3, 4] 
* Loss of safety nets: Without a foundational family or social support network, young adults and older adults alike lack a buffer against sudden financial emergencies. [4, 5] 

## Economic Insecurity and Discrimination

* Employment barriers: Transgender individuals frequently face hiring discrimination and a deficit of trans-inclusive workplaces, forcing many into lower-paying jobs. [6] 
* Housing affordability: Due to lower incomes, transgender households are disproportionately housing cost-burdened—often spending 50% or more of their income on housing—which drastically increases the risk of eviction and homelessness. [6, 7] 
* Rental discrimination: Landlords may deny housing applications, quote higher prices, or refuse to show available rental options when an applicant discloses their gender identity. [6] 

## Barriers in Shelter and Social Systems

* Exclusionary shelters: Traditional emergency housing and shelters often fail to accommodate or affirm transgender people, sometimes turning them away or forcing them into facilities based on their sex assigned at birth rather than their gender identity. [8, 9] 
* Safety concerns: Fear of harassment, verbal abuse, or physical and sexual assault within non-inclusive shelter systems leads many transgender people to avoid formal shelters entirely and experience unsheltered homelessness instead. [9, 10] 
* Mismatched identification: Outdated identification documents that do not match a person's lived name or gender presentation create administrative hurdles when applying for housing, jobs, or public assistance. [11, 12] 

## Intersecting Marginalization

* Systemic racism: Indigenous and Black transgender individuals experience lifetime rates of homelessness significantly higher than their white counterparts, compounding racial and gender-based discrimination. [1, 2] 
* Trauma and health disparities: Elevated rates of violence, mental health challenges, and barriers to accessing necessary gender-affirming healthcare further destabilize housing situations. [10, 11, 13]
