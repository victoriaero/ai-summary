Several key individual, cultural, and systemic factors influence workplace harassment and discrimination against transgender employees. [1] 
## Core Influencing Factors

* Workplace Culture and Norms: Harassment is more common in environments with strict traditional gender roles or "masculinity contest" cultures that reject non-conforming behavior or expression. [1, 2] 
* Visibility and Disclosure: Employees who are openly transgender, or those undergoing a gender transition while employed, often face heightened risks of negative reactions, invasive questioning, or targeted mistreatment from coworkers and supervisors. [3, 4] 
* Lack of Inclusive Policies: Organizations that fail to implement explicit anti-harassment protections, inclusive restroom access, or protocols for using correct names and pronouns often foster environments where bias goes unchecked. [5, 6] 
* Intersectionality: Transgender people of color, nonbinary workers, and those with intersecting marginalized identities experience compounded discrimination and higher rates of severe mistreatment. [7, 8] 
* Legal and Political Ambiguity: Shifts in federal or state enforcement guidelines can create uncertainty around worker rights, which may reduce an employee's confidence in reporting harassment and embolden hostile behavior. [9]
