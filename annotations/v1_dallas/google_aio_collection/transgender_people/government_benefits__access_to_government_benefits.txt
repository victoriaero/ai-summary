Access to government benefits for transgender people is directly shaped by evolving federal and state policy restrictions, administrative discrimination, and compounding socioeconomic hurdles. [1, 2, 3] 
## Policy and Regulatory Barriers

* Healthcare Exclusions: Recent federal directives, such as the 2025 Office of Personnel Management (OPM) guidance, restrict gender-affirming care coverage under the [Federal Employee Health Benefits Program](https://williamsinstitute.law.ucla.edu/publications/fehb-changes-trans-workers/). Similar federal rules affect Essential Health Benefits under the Affordable Care Act. [2, 4] 
* State-Level Disparities: State policies dictate whether local Medicaid programs cover necessary care. While some states mandate inclusive coverage, others ban specific medical treatments or enforce restrictions that limit access. [5, 6] 

## Systemic and Interpersonal Discrimination

* Office Mistreatment: Studies by public health and legal researchers show that nearly 17% of transgender and gender-diverse adults face verbal harassment or denial of equal treatment when visiting public assistance or government benefit offices. [1] 
* Fear and Distrust: Past experiences of hostility create deep mistrust toward government agencies. This discourages individuals from applying for food assistance like SNAP or Medicaid. [1, 7] 

## Socioeconomic and Intersectional Factors

* Poverty and Unemployment: Transgender individuals experience higher rates of poverty, homelessness, and unemployment compared to the general population. [1] 
* Intersecting Identities: Compounding discrimination based on race, disability, or geographic location creates even greater barriers. Transgender people of color often face higher rates of food insecurity and under-enrollment in public programs due to these intersecting factors. [1, 3, 7]
