Housing stability for transgender people is heavily influenced by systemic discrimination, economic insecurity, and a lack of affirming social and legal support. [1, 2] 
## Key Influencing Factors

* Housing and Employment Discrimination: Transgender individuals frequently face rejection, higher rent quotes, or evictions from landlords due to their gender identity. Discrimination in the workplace also limits income and job retention, leading to higher rates of poverty. [2, 3, 4, 5] 
* Family Rejection: Rejection by family members is a leading driver of housing instability and homelessness, particularly among transgender youth who may be forced out or run away due to mistreatment. [6, 7, 8] 
* Barriers to Shelter and Public Services: Many emergency and homeless shelters are sex-segregated or require identity documents matching a person's sex assigned at birth. This often exposes transgender residents to harassment or violence, causing many to avoid shelters and remain unsheltered. [4, 9] 
* Economic and Rent Burdens: Gender-expansive households are statistically more likely to rent rather than own homes and frequently spend 50% or more of their income on housing costs compared to cisgender households. [3, 9, 10] 
* Intersecting Vulnerabilities: Factors such as youth, racial minority status, physical or mental disabilities, and chronic health conditions (like HIV) compound housing precarity and increase the duration of homelessness. [11, 12] 
* Community Resilience and Support: Access to queer- and trans-affirming community networks, chosen-family structures, and peer housing groups serve as vital buffers against complete displacement. [13]
