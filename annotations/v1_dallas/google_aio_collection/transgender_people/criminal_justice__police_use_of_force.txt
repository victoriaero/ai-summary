Multiple intersecting factors—including race, perceived engagement in sex work, and explicit transphobia—significantly influence police use of force and misconduct against transgender individuals. [1, 2] 
## Key Influencing Factors

* Race and Ethnicity: According to reports from the American Civil Liberties Union (ACLU) and academic research, Black, Native American, and other transgender people of color face the highest statistical risk of experiencing physical and sexual violence by law enforcement. [2, 3, 4] 
* Perception of Sex Work: Law enforcement officers frequently rely on biased profiling, assuming transgender individuals (especially trans women of color) are engaged in sex work based on appearance, location, or carrying items like condoms. This alleged involvement heavily increases the probability of searches, arrests, and physical force. [2, 5, 6] 
* Gender Identity and Expression: Gender nonconformity is frequently misconstrued by officers as deceptive or deviant behavior, leading to aggressive surveillance, unwarranted detentions, and verbal or physical escalation. [6] 
* Disclosure or Visibility: When police know or perceive a person is transgender during an encounter, rates of reported harassment, misgendering, physical abuse, and sexual assault rise dramatically compared to general interactions. [2, 4, 7] 
* Environmental Context: Living in heavily policed or marginalized neighborhoods raises the frequency of routine stops, which statistically compounds the exposure to potential use-of-force incidents. [8]
