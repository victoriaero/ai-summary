Multiple interconnected social, institutional, and policy factors heavily influence educational opportunity and access for transgender people.
## School Climate and Interpersonal Safety

* Harassment and Bullying: According to research highlighted by the [US Department of Education](https://www.ed.gov/media/document/factsheet-supporting-transgender-youth-school-june-2021-112270.pdf), transgender students experience higher rates of verbal and physical harassment, leading to feelings of unsafety and increased absenteeism. [1] 
* Staff and Peer Attitudes: Direct behaviors, acceptance, or refusal to use affirmed names and pronouns by educators and peers strongly shape a student's mental well-being and daily participation. [2] 
* Cisinormativity: Entrenched societal and institutional assumptions of a strict gender binary create unwelcoming or hostile environments for gender-diverse youth. [3] 

## Institutional Policies and Infrastructure

* Gender-Segregated Facilities: The lack of accessible, non-binary, or privacy-respecting facilities—such as restrooms, locker rooms, and housing—creates physical barriers and exclusion on campus. [4, 5] 
* Administrative and Record-Keeping Barriers: Rigid institutional data systems that fail to reflect a student’s affirmed name, gender marker, or confidential identity status complicate registration, financial aid (such as FAFSA), and campus integration. [2, 5, 6] 
* Inclusive Resources and Curriculum: The presence or absence of LGBTQ+-inclusive curricula, active resource centers, and mental health support directly correlates with a student's sense of belonging and academic retention. [5, 7] 

## Broader Legal, Political, and Socioeconomic Factors

* Shifting Policy Landscapes: Changing federal and state executive orders or legislative restrictions regarding gender identity recognition and administrative updates impact campus safety and resource access. [6] 
* Family and Financial Support: Disproportional rates of family rejection or housing instability lead to financial hardship, forcing many transgender students to rely solely on personal income or face hurdles in securing grants, loans, and stable living arrangements. [8, 9] 
* Intersectionality: Compounding marginalization—such as facing simultaneous discrimination based on race, disability, or socioeconomic background—further amplifies barriers to educational attainment. [1, 9]
