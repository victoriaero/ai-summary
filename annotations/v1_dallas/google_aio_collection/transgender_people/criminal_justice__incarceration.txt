Transgender people face disproportionate rates of incarceration driven primarily by systemic socioeconomic discrimination, profiling by law enforcement, and the criminalization of poverty-related survival strategies. [1, 2] 
Here are the key factors that influence the overrepresentation of transgender individuals in the criminal justice system:
## Socioeconomic Disparities

* Poverty and Unemployment: Transgender people face high rates of joblessness, poverty, and housing insecurity because of widespread discrimination. [3] 
* Lack of Support: Rejection by families and communities leaves many transgender youth homeless at a young age, increasing their risk of contact with the legal system. [3, 4, 5] 

## Survival Economies

* Underground Work: Because traditional jobs are hard to find, some transgender individuals rely on underground economies for money.
* Survival Sex Work and Minor Offenses: Many are arrested for survival-related offenses, such as theft or sex work, which are targeted by police. [1, 5, 6] 

## Law Enforcement and Policing Bias

* Targeted Profiling: Police officers frequently profile transgender people, especially transgender women of color.
* Antiquated Laws: Police often use vague loitering laws to arrest people simply for being in public, a practice sometimes called "walking while trans". [1, 2, 6] 

## Racial and Intersectional Disparities

* Trans People of Color: The risk of arrest and imprisonment is much higher for Black, Hispanic, and Native American transgender people due to the combined impact of racism and transphobia. [2, 4, 7]
