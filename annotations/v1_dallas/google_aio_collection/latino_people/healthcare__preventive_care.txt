Several key systemic, economic, and cultural factors influence the use of preventive healthcare by Latino people in the United States.
Health Insurance and Access
• Uninsurance rates: Hispanics and Latinos have higher uninsured rates (roughly 17% to 19%) compared to the general U.S. population, which directly limits routine checkups and primary care use.
• Lack of a usual source of care: Many Latinos—particularly foreign-born or undocumented individuals—lack a primary care physician or regular medical home, leading a higher percentage to rely on urgent care or emergency rooms for routine needs.
Language and Communication
• Language barriers: Limited English proficiency hinders effective communication with providers, reduces health literacy regarding early prevention, and lowers overall healthcare utilization.
• Shortage of bilingual staff: A lack of available Spanish-speaking providers and medical interpreters creates friction and distrust in the medical system.
Economic and Occupational Constraints
• Paid leave disparities: Only about 56.6% of Hispanic and Latino workers have access to paid sick leave, making it difficult to schedule daytime preventive appointments around rigid work hours.
• Cost and poverty factors: Lower average income levels and high out-of-pocket costs deter individuals from seeking preventative screenings.
Cultural and Structural Factors
• Cultural dynamics: Health choices are often tied to family, tradition, and trusted community advice, making culturally sensitive providers vital for engagement.
• Systemic distrust: Past negative experiences, fear of discrimination, and anti-immigrant sentiment can discourage individuals from engaging with formal healthcare systems.
