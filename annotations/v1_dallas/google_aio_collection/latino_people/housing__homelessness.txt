Several structural, economic, and social factors influence housing instability and homelessness among Latino individuals.
Economic and Housing Affordability Pressures
• High Rent Burden: A large share of Latino renters are cost-burdened, meaning they spend more than 30% of their gross income on housing. Increases in rent strongly correlate with rising rates of doubled-up and unsheltered homelessness.
• Poverty Rates: Latino communities experience poverty at higher rates than non-Latino White populations, leaving households with fewer financial reserves to handle economic shocks.
• Job Loss and Unstable Employment: Sudden unemployment or lack of steady income often triggers housing loss, and ongoing barriers to stable employment make regaining housing difficult.
Doubled-Up Living and Hidden Homelessness
• Overcrowding: To manage economic strain, Latino individuals frequently live in "doubled-up" situations—sharing a single housing unit with multiple families or individuals.
• Lack of Formal Leases: Many enter homelessness directly from informal housing arrangements without a formal lease, which strips them of legal tenant protections against sudden evictions or illegal rent hikes.
• Risk Factors: Factors like recent immigration status, fewer years living in the U.S., and limited English proficiency increase the likelihood of relying on doubled-up living conditions that can eventually lead to literal homelessness.
Systemic and Administrative Barriers
• Underutilization of Assistance: Latino households often access housing resources and public assistance programs at rates below their proportion of the poverty population.
• Fear of Enforcement: Concerns regarding immigration status, deportation, and public charge rules deter many individuals from seeking government-backed housing or emergency social services.
• Language and Cultural Obstacles: Limited English proficiency, literacy barriers, and a lack of culturally responsive service delivery create profound disconnects when navigating mainstream housing and homeless support systems.
