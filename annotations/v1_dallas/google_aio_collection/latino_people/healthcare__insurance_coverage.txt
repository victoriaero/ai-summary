Several key legal, socioeconomic, and systemic factors influence health insurance coverage and healthcare access for Latino people in the United States.
Legal Status and Immigration
• Immigration Status: Undocumented immigrants and individuals with uncertain legal status face restricted eligibility for public programs like Medicaid and the Children’s Health Insurance Program (CHIP).
• Fear and Trust: Concerns about deportation and data-sharing between public agencies and immigration authorities create a chilling effect, deterring eligible family members from enrolling in coverage or seeking care.
• Foreign-Born Status: Research shows that being foreign-born is independently associated with higher odds of being uninsured, even when adjusting for income and education.
Employment and Income
• Job Type: Latino workers are disproportionately employed in industries and small businesses that do not offer employer-sponsored health insurance.
• Socioeconomic Status: Lower family incomes and higher poverty rates limit the ability to afford private health insurance premiums and high out-of-pocket costs.
• Medical Debt: Financial strain from medical or dental debt leads many individuals to delay or forgo necessary medical attention.
Systemic and Language Barriers
• Language Proficiency: Complex application processes for public coverage and insurance navigation hurdles are magnified for individuals with limited English proficiency.
• Cultural and Structural Obstacles: A shortage of bilingual and culturally competent healthcare providers and complicated administrative enrollment systems reduce overall engagement with the healthcare market.
