Access to housing for Latino people is shaped by economic gaps, language barriers, immigration status, and systemic discrimination in the housing and mortgage markets.
Economic and Financial Barriers
• Income and Wealth Gaps: Latino households often earn less and have less savings than white households. This makes it hard to pay for large down payments.
• Credit Access: Many Latino families face higher mortgage denial rates or lack traditional credit histories, partly due to reliance on self-employment or non-traditional income that standard banks struggle to verify.
• Lack of Generational Wealth: Fewer Latino buyers receive financial help or inheritances from family to buy a first home.
Language and Immigration Factors
• English Proficiency: People who speak English very well have higher homeownership rates. Language skills help people understand complex mortgage and rental documents.
• Immigration and Citizenship Status: Newer immigrants face strict documentation rules, such as social security number requirements, which block them from standard credit systems and rental programs. Fear of deportation or public charge rules also stops some from seeking public aid.
Housing Discrimination and Systemic Bias
• Market Discrimination: Studies show Latino buyers can face higher interest rates, stricter paperwork rules, and appraisal bias—where homes in minority neighborhoods are undervalued.
• Underrepresentation in Aid: According to the National Low Income Housing Coalition (NLIHC), Latino households access public housing and voucher programs at rates lower than their poverty levels suggest.
• Appraisal and Lending Bias: As highlighted by the U.S. Department of the Treasury, discriminatory lending and appraisal practices make buying and refinancing harder for Hispanic families. Research by the Joint Center for Housing Studies notes these systemic barriers block families from building housing wealth.
Social and Cultural Dynamics
• Multigenerational Living: Latino households are often larger and more multigenerational, which creates a high demand for larger homes that may be scarce or expensive.
• Country of Origin: Homeownership rates vary by country of origin, reflecting differences in community resources, average arrival times, and local economic integration.
