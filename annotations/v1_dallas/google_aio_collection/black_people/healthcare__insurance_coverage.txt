According to reports by the Kaiser Family Foundation (KFF) and the The Century Foundation, health insurance coverage for Black people is heavily influenced by a combination of geographic policy choices, income disparities, employment factors, and historical systemic inequalities.Geographic Policy and the Medicaid GapNon-expansion states: A disproportionate share of the U.S. Black population (about 37%) lives in Southern states that have chosen not to expand Medicaid under the Affordable Care Act (ACA).The coverage gap: Because of this, uninsured Black individuals are more likely to fall into a "coverage gap"—meaning their incomes are too high for traditional Medicaid in their state, but too low to qualify for Marketplace premium tax credits.Socioeconomic and Employment FactorsIncome levels: Household income plays a primary role in predicting insurance eligibility, and systemic economic inequalities mean Black Americans experience higher rates of poverty and lower average household wealth.Employer-sponsored coverage: Research cited by the American Bar Association indicates that Black Americans are about 10% less likely to hold employer-sponsored health coverage than white counterparts, even when working for large firms, due to occupational segregation and differences in job stability.Historical and Structural InfluencesStructural racism: Decades of racial discrimination in employment, housing, and the insurance industry itself have suppressed wealth accumulation and restricted access to stable, high-quality health plans.Workforce representation: A lack of diversity and persistent biases within the insurance brokerage and professional community can also impact outreach and culturally competent navigation of available health plans.If you'd like, I can provide more details on:Medicaid expansion state comparisonsSpecific health outcomes tied to these coverage gapsLet me know how you would like to proceed.
