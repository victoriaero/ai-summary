Several social, economic, and systemic factors influence access to and utilization of preventive care for Black people in the healthcare system.Social Determinants of HealthSocioeconomic barriers: Lower average income and wealth levels can make routine screenings, medications, and visits cost-prohibitive.Insurance and access: Black individuals experience higher rates of being uninsured or underinsured, and lack a usual source of care (USOC) like a primary care physician.Logistical challenges: Unpredictable work hours, lack of paid time off, transportation hurdles, and childcare needs create practical obstacles to attending routine appointments.Trust and Provider DynamicsMedical mistrust: Historical and ongoing experiences with systemic racism and discrimination in medical settings foster well-documented distrust in physicians and healthcare institutions.Provider diversity: Research highlighted by the Kaiser Family Foundation (KFF) indicates that racial concordance—having providers who share a patient's racial or ethnic background—improves patient trust, communication, and adherence to preventive recommendations.
