Understanding Healthcare Hesitancy in Black Patients: A Historical Perspective
2.13.24
•
ChenMed
The hesitancy for Black patients to receive healthcare can be attributed to a long history of medical exploitation and ongoing institutional, structural, and systemic racism. Healthcare professionals need to truly understand this historical background and its impact, as it helps explain the valid distrust many Black people have of the medical system. This skepticism can lead to delayed or missed care, raising the risk of illness and preventable diseases.
This article aims to inform medical professionals about these historical reasons, emphasizing how resolving these concerns is critical to improving health outcomes in the Black population.
Historical Roots of Mistrust
To properly serve and support the Black community, healthcare providers must have a thorough grasp of historical medical injustices. This insight is critical to ending the cycle of mistrust caused by past events.
Black patients’ reluctance to participate in the medical system partially stems from a history of medical abuses that are rooted in systemic racism. In the 1800s, J. Marion Sims
conducted unethical surgery
on enslaved women without their consent to find a cure for vesicovaginal fistulas. Sims, known as the "father of American gynecology," performed multiple procedures on these ladies, including one who underwent the surgery 30 times. The recognition of these abuses in recent years has led to calls for honoring the victims rather than their abusers.
The Tuskegee Syphilis Experiment is another prime example
highlighting the need for such understanding. Beginning in 1932 and lasting four decades, the study involved hundreds of Black men who, without informed consent, were left untreated for syphilis to track the disease's natural progression. The individuals involved in the study were deceitfully denied access to available treatment. This event is a stark reminder of the lengthy legacy of medical exploitation and neglect.
Impact of Systemic Racism and Discrimination
Past segregation laws exacerbated racial disparities within the healthcare system, limiting the access that Black people had and deserved to adequate medical care. These historical injustices have created a deep distrust in the healthcare system among Black Americans.
Unfortunately, systemic racism and discrimination in the healthcare system continue to have an impact on Black patients’ access to quality care.
The Civil Rights Act of 1964 ended legally sanctioned racial segregation in hospitals.
However, structural racism persists, particularly in academic health institutions, where a patient's insurance status and race often drive de facto segregation. These discrepancies have historically hindered Black people access to comprehensive medical treatment, fueling long-held, understandable skepticism of the healthcare system.
Academic Health Centers are now expected to actively participate in desegregating health professions education
. This requires a multifaceted approach that includes recruiting a diverse student body that is representative of the communities they serve and incorporating community leaders into the education process to ensure that the future healthcare workforce is trained in an environment that prioritizes public health and equity.
Confronting Racial Disparities in Healthcare Outcomes
Physicians are committed to treating all patients equally, yet disparities persist, particularly affecting the Black American population.
This group faces higher rates of illness, worse health outcomes, and earlier mortality compared to white individuals
. Factors like
socioeconomic disparities play a role
, but inherent racial biases within the healthcare system are also significant contributors. Such biases, whether conscious or not, can lead to discriminatory practices in patient care. The lack of diversity among healthcare professionals further exacerbates this issue, as patients often seek comfort in those with similar backgrounds and experiences. Negative experiences in healthcare settings can deter Black patients from engaging with the system, impacting their overall trust and health outcomes.
Strengthening Trust in Patient-Provider Relationships
Healthcare initiatives emphasize trust-building through increased communication and structural improvements in healthcare practices
. The initiatives are essential to repair the patient-provider connection in the Black community. Hospitals are implementing implicit bias training to combat
unconscious prejudices
. Furthermore,
initiatives like the 3rd Conversation program promote discussion outside clinical settings
, allowing for a better understanding of patient and clinician difficulties. These initiatives aim to overcome past mistrust and enhance healthcare results for Black patients by addressing individual provider behavior and institutional healthcare policy.
Key Strategies to Promote Healthcare Equity
Improving healthcare equity for Black communities requires intentional improvements to primary care access and quality
. Policies should prioritize universal and equitable health coverage, including initiatives to cut out-of-pocket expenses and expand Medicaid. Administrative obstacles must be reduced to make healthcare services more accessible. Social determinants of health must be addressed through housing, food security, and transportation investments. Anti-racist and culturally aware health communication, as well as active community participation, are critical for overcoming inequities and fostering inclusive health decision-making.
ChenMed's Commitment to Advancing Health Equity
ChenMed is committed to delivering outstanding care to all communities
, with a deep understanding of unique health challenges. Recognizing and understanding the historical background of healthcare inequities rooted in racism is absolutely crucial for healthcare professionals. By committing to ongoing education in understanding and unlearning systemic racism in healthcare, we are delivering informed and compassionate care to a community that has long been underserved.
Engagement outside of our clinics is equally important. By participating in community events, collaborating with local leaders, and fostering trust and respect—
we listen and learn from the experiences of our Black patients.
This approach further enhances our ability to offer empathetic and effective care.
Furthermore, advocating for anti-racist and systemic change within our practice and the wider healthcare system is imperative. Our actions reflect a commitment to racial equity, whether through policy lobbying or the implementation of training programs that address unconscious prejudices.
At
ChenMed
, we encourage all healthcare professionals to join these efforts. We are uniquely positioned to be catalysts for change, benefiting individual patients and the healthcare system. Together, we can work to make fair healthcare a reality in every community we serve.
