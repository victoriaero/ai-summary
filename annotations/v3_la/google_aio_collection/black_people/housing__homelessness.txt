Black people experience disproportionate rates of homelessness due to a combination of historical injustices, economic disparities, and systemic discrimination. According to the National Alliance to End Homelessness, Black Americans make up over 34% to 40% of the unhoused population despite representing roughly 13% of the general U.S. population.
Historical and Systemic Factors
• Discriminatory Housing Policies: Past federal practices like 1930s suburban exclusion and FHA redlining denied mortgages in Black neighborhoods, actively preventing generations from building home equity and wealth.
• Wealth and Income Gaps: Generational housing exclusion has resulted in a stark homeownership gap (around 44% for Black households versus 75% for white households), with Black families possessing a fraction of the median wealth of white families.
• Criminal Justice Disparities: Involvement in the criminal justice system—driven by disproportionate policing and sentencing—creates severe barriers to securing a lease. Research notes that formerly incarcerated individuals face a significantly higher risk of homelessness.
Economic and Market Pressures
• Affordable Housing Shortages: Black households are disproportionately represented among extremely low-income renters, leaving them vulnerable when housing costs rise and wages lag.
• Workforce Discrimination: Ongoing disparities in hiring, promotions, and hourly wages limit economic mobility and safety nets within the Black community.
• Unstable Living Conditions: Because of economic pressures, Black families are more likely to experience "doubling up" (overcrowded or shared living arrangements), which frequently precedes literal homelessness.
