Housing stability for Black individuals and families is heavily influenced by systemic economic, historical, and policy factors. According to reports from the U.S. Department of the Treasury and the Urban Institute, key drivers include:
📊 Economic Wealth and Income Disparities
• Wealth Gaps: Historic denial of wealth-building mechanisms limits down-payment funds.
• Income Constraints: Wage gaps restrict consistent rent or mortgage payments.
• Eviction Vulnerability: Lower savings raise the immediate risk of housing loss.
⚠️ Historic and Contemporary Discrimination
• Redlining: Past federal mapping practices devalued majority-Black neighborhoods.
• Appraisal Bias: Homes in Black communities are appraised at lower values.
• Lending Practices: Discriminatory credit and mortgage hurdles restrict ownership.
📌 Neighborhood Resources and Segregation
• Zoning Laws: Exclusionary zoning isolates communities from high-opportunity areas.
• Appreciation Deficit: Lower property appreciation reduces long-term equity growth.
• Environmental Hazards: Substandard conditions and hazards cluster in segregated zones.
