Patient safety for Black people in healthcare is influenced by systemic racism, provider implicit bias, socioeconomic barriers, and unequal access to high-quality facilities.
According to analyses from the Kaiser Family Foundation (KFF) and public health literature, several core levels and factors drive these safety and quality disparities:
Systemic and Institutional Factors
• Unequal Facility Access: Black patients are frequently admitted to lower-rated or under-resourced hospitals and medical facilities due to historical housing policies, residential segregation, and referral networks.
• Data Gaps: Many healthcare institutions fail to consistently track or disaggregate patient safety events and outcomes by race and ethnicity, making it harder to spot and fix disparities.
• Biased Clinical Tools: Some medical algorithms and artificial intelligence tools have historically utilized race-based adjustments (such as tests for kidney function) that can delay or compromise appropriate care.
Provider and Interpersonal Factors
• Implicit Bias and Dismissiveness: Unconscious or conscious bias by providers can lead to the dismissal of pain or symptoms, resulting in delayed diagnoses, missed conditions, and untreated complications.
• Lack of Workforce Diversity: A shortage of Black and minority healthcare professionals can reduce cross-cultural communication and patient comfort, impacting shared decision-making.
Socioeconomic and Patient Factors
• Financial and Insurance Barriers: Higher rates of uninsurance and lower average household incomes create hurdles in accessing new therapies, specialized care, and prescription medications.
• Medical Mistrust: Previous personal or historical experiences of discrimination within the medical system can lead to healthcare hesitancy, delays in seeking treatment, or reluctance to speak up during safety events.
