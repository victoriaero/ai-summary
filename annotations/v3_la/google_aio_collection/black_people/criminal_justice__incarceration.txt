According to the American Civil Liberties Union (ACLU), racial disparities in incarceration are driven by disparate treatment and systemic factors at every stage of the criminal legal system.
Key Factors Influencing Incarceration
• Biased Policing: According to the Prison Policy Initiative, Black Americans face higher arrest rates due to disproportionate levels of targeted traffic stops, pedestrian searches, and selective enforcement of drug laws despite similar usage rates compared to white counterparts.
• Pretrial Detention and Cash Bail: The Sentencing Project notes that financial requirements for cash bail disadvantage low-income people of color, increasing pressure to accept less favorable plea deals.
• Prosecutorial Discretion: Prosecutors are documented to file mandatory minimum or habitual offender charges more frequently against Black defendants and are less likely to offer pretrial diversion programs compared to similarly situated white defendants.
• Sentencing Disparities: Studies show that Black individuals often receive longer prison sentences than white individuals convicted of similar crimes with comparable criminal histories, and they are less likely to receive alternative probationary sentences.
• Punitiveness of Drug and Habitual Offender Laws: Policies stemming from the War on Drugs—such as mandatory minimums and severe penalties for specific substances—have disproportionately targeted and impacted Black communities.
• Underfunded Indigent Defense: Public defender systems in many jurisdictions lack adequate resources, limiting the robust legal representation available to low-income defendants.
