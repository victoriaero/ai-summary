Racial disparities in police arrests and detentions for Black individuals are influenced by a combination of systemic, geographic, and behavioral factors documented by research organizations like The Sentencing Project and the Vera Institute of Justice.
Key Factors Influencing Disparities
• Implicit and Explicit Bias: According to the Vera Institute of Justice, conscious and subconscious racial preferences among individual law enforcement actors can lead to more frequent stops, searches, and discretionary arrests of Black people compared to white people in similar situations.
• High-Volume Policing and Deployment: Police departments often deploy higher numbers of officers to economically disadvantaged and majority-Black neighborhoods. Increased police presence naturally generates a higher volume of stops, checks, and minor-infraction arrests.
• Concentrated Poverty and Neighborhood Context: Research highlighted by The Sentencing Project shows that historical disinvestment has led to elevated rates of poverty and violent victimization in many Black communities, which shapes the structural environments associated with higher rates of police contact.
• Discretionary Traffic and Pedestrian Stops: Law enforcement data shows that Black drivers and pedestrians experience a disproportionate number of investigatory stops and searches. These stops frequently result in bookings or arrests for low-level or secondary offenses discovered during the encounter.
• Cumulative Criminal Justice Policies: Prior criminal history—which can reflect historical systemic disparities in past arrests and convictions—often feeds into algorithmic risk assessments and police deployment strategies, compounding the likelihood of future detention.
