Several social, economic, and systemic factors influence access to and utilization of preventive care for Black people.
Socioeconomic and Structural Barriers
• Insurance Coverage: Black adults experience higher rates of being uninsured or underinsured compared to white adults, which directly limits routine checkups and preventive screenings.
• Income and Poverty: Black households face higher poverty rates, leading to cost-prohibitive out-of-pocket expenses for medical visits, medications, and diagnostic tests.
• Geographic Access: Many Black communities are located in health professional shortage areas or "food/health deserts" with limited public transportation and fewer nearby medical facilities.
Systemic and Interpersonal Factors
• Medical Mistrust: Historical mistreatment and present-day experiences of systemic racism and implicit bias in the healthcare system contribute to a foundational distrust of medical institutions and providers.
• Provider Diversity: Research indicates that patient-provider racial concordance—having providers who share a similar racial or ethnic background—improves patient trust, communication, and the utilization of preventive care. However, Black professionals remain underrepresented in the healthcare workforce.
• Health Literacy: Educational barriers and health information written at unnecessarily complex reading levels can make navigating insurance and preventive guidelines difficult.
