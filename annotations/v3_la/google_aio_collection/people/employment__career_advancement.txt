Career advancement for employed individuals depends on a mix of individual performance, strategic visibility, and workplace relationships. [1] 
## Key Performance and Skills

* Job Performance: Consistently delivering high-value work and meeting or exceeding goals sets the foundation for promotion.
* Skill Development: Continuously learning new technical and soft skills keeps workers adaptable and ready for higher-level roles.
* Proactive Engagement: Taking on "stretch opportunities" or challenging assignments outside regular duties demonstrates readiness for leadership. [1, 2, 3] 

## Visibility and Recognition

* Strategic Visibility: Sharing wins, volunteering for high-profile projects, and presenting work to leaders outside an immediate team ensures decision-makers notice contributions.
* Track Record: Documenting achievements and communicating value clearly helps managers justify promotions or pay raises. [1] 

## Relationships and Networks

* Professional Relationships: Forming genuine connections across departments and with key decision-makers builds internal advocacy.
* Mentorship and Sponsorship: Having a mentor to provide guidance or a senior sponsor to advocate for advancement opportunities accelerates career growth. [1] 

## Organizational Environment

* Company Culture: Supportive workplaces that offer clear internal career pathways, talent marketplaces, and professional development programs make advancement more achievable. [4, 5] 
* Manager Support: Regular constructive feedback, coaching, and active management help employees align their daily work with long-term advancement goals. [4, 5]
