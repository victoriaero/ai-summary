Housing loss and homelessness for people currently in housing are primarily driven by severe economic strain, housing cost burdens, and sudden life crises. [1, 2] 
## Economic and Housing Factors

* Severe Rent Burden: Spending more than 30% to 50% of income on rent leaves households vulnerable to housing loss when financial emergencies happen. [3, 4] 
* Stagnant Wages and Poverty: Inadequate wages that fail to keep pace with rising rental costs create continuous financial instability. [1, 5] 
* Loss of Income: Sudden job loss, reduced work hours, or the termination of employment can quickly make rent unaffordable. [2] 
* Decline in Public Benefits: Inadequate or reduced safety-net support, such as cash assistance or housing vouchers, leaves low-income households without backup resources. [6, 7] 

## Personal and Social Crises

* Domestic Violence: Fleeing an abusive partner or family violence often forces individuals—especially women and children—to leave home immediately without financial safety nets. [1, 3] 
* Health Emergencies and Medical Debt: Severe illness, disability-induced eviction, or high medical bills can drain household finances and trigger displacement. [1, 2, 8] 
* Untreated Mental Health and Addiction: Behavioral health challenges combined with a lack of supportive services can destabilize tenancy and strain housing retention. [5, 9] 

## Systemic and Institutional Failures

* Eviction and Legal Barriers: Formal and informal evictions, coupled with barriers from past criminal records, make maintaining or securing alternative housing difficult. [1, 2] 
* System Discharges: Inadequate transition and discharge planning for individuals leaving foster care, hospitals, correctional facilities, or mental health institutions increases the risk of immediate housing loss. [3]
