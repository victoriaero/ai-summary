Access to credit is directly shaped by individual credit history, systemic socioeconomic factors, and institutional barriers within financial services.
## 📊 Credit Score and Financial History

* Payment history: Tracks past on-time or missed payments (35% weight). [1] 
* Amounts owed: Measures total credit utilization and debt levels (30% weight). [1] 
* Length of credit history: Evaluates the age of oldest and newest accounts (15% weight). [1] 
* Credit mix: Considers the variety of credit types used (10% weight). [1] 
* New credit: Accounts for recent hard inquiries and newly opened lines (10% weight). [1] 

## 💡 Socioeconomic and Environmental Factors

* Childhood environment: Early-life neighborhood norms and regional upward mobility heavily shape adult repayment and borrowing patterns. [2, 3] 
* Income visibility: Lower-income consumers are disproportionately more likely to become "credit visible" via negative records like collections rather than traditional credit cards. [4] 
* Systemic disparities: Structural factors and historic lending exclusions create ongoing gaps in credit limits and interest rates regardless of individual repayment records. [2, 5] 

## ⚠️ Institutional and Technological Barriers

* Documentation hurdles: Strict tax or business paperwork requirements confuse or exclude low- and moderate-income applicants.
* Financial literacy: Gaps in understanding how to interact with traditional banks prevent successful product applications.
* Digital exclusion: A lack of reliable internet or smartphone access restricts usage of modern, app-based financial services.
* Immigrant status: The absence of a domestic credit history or language differences block entry into traditional financial systems. [6, 7]
