Policy decisions, socioeconomic disadvantage, and racial disparities are the primary factors that influence incarceration rates within the criminal justice system. [1, 2] 
## Policy and Sentencing Decisions

* Harsh Sentencing Laws: Measures like mandatory minimum sentences, "three strikes" laws, and the [War on Drugs](https://www.brennancenter.org/our-work/analysis-opinion/history-mass-incarceration) dramatically increased the length of prison terms. [3, 4] 
* Punitive Policing Practices: High-intensity policing in specific neighborhoods leads to higher arrest rates, directly funneling more individuals into the system. [2, 5] 
* Pretrial Detention: Local jails hold large populations of people who cannot afford bail while awaiting trial, increasing the likelihood of pressured plea deals and subsequent incarceration. [6] 

## Socioeconomic Disadvantage

* Poverty and Resources: Low-income individuals often live in under-resourced areas with heavy police presence and lack the financial means to hire private legal counsel, relying instead on overburdened public defenders. [5] 
* Education and Employment: Limited access to quality education, stable employment, and economic mobility strongly correlates with higher vulnerability to the criminal justice system. [4] 
* Substance Abuse and Mental Health: Untreated mental health disorders and drug dependence frequently intersect with criminal behavior, and the justice system often responds with incarceration rather than healthcare. [4, 7, 8] 

## Racial and Systemic Disparities

* Disproportionate Impact: Black, Hispanic, and Native American individuals experience significantly higher rates of arrest, harsher charging decisions, and longer prison sentences than white individuals for similar offenses. [1, 9, 10] 
* Historical and Social Narratives: Long-standing structural inequalities and historical policy choices have systematically targeted marginalized communities, locking them into cycles of disadvantage and over-incarceration. [1, 2]
