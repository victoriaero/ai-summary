According to the [U.S. Equal Employment Opportunity Commission (EEOC)](https://www.eeoc.gov/chart-risk-factors-harassment-and-responsive-strategies), workplace harassment is heavily influenced by organizational culture, demographic makeup, and specific environmental working conditions. [1, 2] 
## Workforce Demographics and Norms

* Homogeneous Workforces: A lack of diversity can cause minority groups to feel isolated, making biases or stereotypes go unchallenged.
* Cultural and Language Differences: Poorly managed diversity or segregated groups can lead to miscommunication and tension.
* Non-conforming Employees: Workers who do not fit traditional stereotypes or workplace norms face higher rates of targeting.
* Young Workforces: Teenage and young adult employees may be less familiar with professional boundaries, reporting procedures, and legal protections. [1, 3, 4, 5, 6] 

## Power and Value Disparities

* Power Imbalances: Large gaps between low-ranking staff and management leave vulnerable employees exposed to exploitation and fear of retaliation. [4, 6] 
* High-Value Employees: Top earners or critical rainmakers may have rules unenforced or face fewer consequences for misconduct due to their perceived value to the business. [4, 7] 

## Environmental and Operational Factors

* Customer Service Reliance: When pay or tips depend directly on client satisfaction, employees often feel forced to tolerate abusive behavior from customers. [4, 8] 
* Monotonous or Low-Intensity Tasks: Repetitive or boring work leaves workers with idle time, which can foster bullying or harassment as a form of venting. [3, 9] 
* Isolated Workspaces: Working alone or in remote settings eliminates witnesses, providing harassers with greater access and reduced accountability. [9, 10] 
* Tolerated Alcohol Consumption: Workplaces or events where alcohol flows freely reduce social inhibitions and consistently correlate with misconduct complaints. [5, 9] 
* Decentralized Workplaces: Retail locations or remote offices with distant leadership and limited oversight allow inappropriate behavior to go unchecked. [6, 9] 
* Coarsened Social Discourse: Crude or heated behavior in outside social environments often spills over and normalizes disrespect inside the job. [2, 4]
