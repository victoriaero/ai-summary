Financial stability, employment security, affordable housing costs, and personal health challenges are the primary factors that influence housing stability for people currently in housing. [1] 
## Financial and Economic Factors

* Income and Wages: Low or irregular income makes it hard to pay rent or mortgages on time. [2] 
* Housing Cost Burden: Spending more than 30%—or severely more than 50%—of household income on rent or housing leaves little money for food, utilities, and medical care. [3, 4] 
* Unexpected Expenses: Sudden medical bills, car repairs, or job loss can quickly trigger a housing crisis. [5, 6] 

## Health and Personal Circumstances

* Physical and Mental Health: Chronic illnesses, disabilities, or untreated mental health conditions can limit a person's ability to work or manage daily household responsibilities. [2, 6] 
* Substance Use: Co-occurring mental health and hard drug use disorders create severe personal and social barriers to maintaining a stable home. [2, 7] 
* Life Disruptions: Family changes, domestic violence, or personal trauma often destabilize living arrangements. [2, 5] 

## Social and Legal Support

* Social Support Networks: Having reliable friends, family, or community groups helps people navigate financial and housing emergencies. [6] 
* Legal History and Policies: Past evictions, legal disputes, or lack of tenant protections (like just-cause eviction laws) make it harder to secure and keep safe housing. [1, 6, 8] 
* Supportive Services: Access to rental assistance, counseling, and permanent supportive [housing initiatives](https://upward-mobility.urban.org/framework/neighborhoods/stability) helps prevent displacement. [7, 8]
