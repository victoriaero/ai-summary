Economic, structural, and social factors heavily shape a person's ability to access and keep stable housing. [1, 2] 
## Economic Factors

* Affordability and Costs: A severe shortage of low-cost rental homes and rising rent prices force many households to spend over 30% to 50% of their income on housing. [3, 4, 5] 
* Wages and Income: Stagnant wages that fail to keep pace with living expenses and inflation make it difficult to pay deposits, rent, and utility bills. [2, 4, 6] 
* Poverty and Wealth Gaps: Generational poverty and a wide racial wealth gap limit equal opportunities for renting or buying a home. [7, 8] 

## Structural and Policy Barriers

* Zoning and Development: Restrictive land-use rules, local opposition (NIMBYism), and high construction costs slow the creation of new affordable housing. [1, 4] 
* Public Assistance Deficits: Limited federal housing vouchers and underfunded safety-net programs leave the majority of eligible low-income families without aid. [1, 2, 4] 
* Systemic Discrimination: Historical practices like redlining, ongoing housing discrimination, and biases within the justice system disproportionately impact marginalized groups, including BIPOC and LGBTQ+ individuals. [2, 7] 

## Personal and Health Circumstances

* Health Challenges: Untreated mental illness, substance use disorders, and chronic physical disabilities can make maintaining housing difficult without proper support services. [2, 6, 9] 
* Life Disruptions: Domestic violence, family rejection, or aging out of foster care without resources significantly increase housing vulnerability. [1, 2]
