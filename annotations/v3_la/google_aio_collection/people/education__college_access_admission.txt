Socioeconomic status, academic preparation, and guidance resources are the primary factors that influence college access and admission. [1, 2] 
## Academic Readiness and Performance

* High school grades and curriculum strength: High school GPA and enrollment in rigorous courses like AP or honors are the most consistent predictors and heavily weighted criteria for admission. [3, 4] 
* Standardized testing: Standardized test scores still influence selective admissions and competitive placement, even at test-optional institutions. [4, 5] 
* K-12 preparation gaps: Under-resourced schools often offer fewer advanced courses, leading to higher remediation rates for low-income students and students of color. [2] 

## Socioeconomic and Financial Factors

* Family income and wealth: Wealthy families can afford private college counselors, specialized test preparation, and application support, giving students an unfair advantage. [2] 
* Affordability and financial aid: Rising tuition costs, debt anxiety, and complex FAFSA requirements create major barriers, with college cost cited as the top reason adults never enroll or stop out. [6, 7, 8] 
* Summer melt: Up to a quarter of accepted low-income students fail to matriculate because they lack support to complete administrative and financial aid tasks over the summer. [7] 

## Institutional and Guidance Support

* Counselor-to-student ratios: High national counselor caseloads limit the time staff can dedicate to individual postsecondary planning, disproportionately harming first-generation and low-income students. [2, 7] 
* Social and cultural capital: Access to mentorship, peer networks, and institutional knowledge about the college application process heavily dictates enrollment success. [6, 9, 10] 
* Institutional priorities: Colleges balance student bodies based on geographic representation, intended majors, socioeconomic diversity, and gender parity. [5, 11]
