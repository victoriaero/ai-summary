Patient safety in healthcare is influenced by a complex mix of systemic, human, technological, and patient-level factors. [1] 
## System and Organizational Factors

* Staffing and Workload: Inadequate staffing levels, high workloads, and long shifts lead to staff fatigue, burnout, and reduced vigilance. [1, 2] 
* Culture and Leadership: A blameless reporting environment, management support, and psychological safety encourage workers to report errors and speak up about risks. [2, 3, 4, 5] 
* Workflow and Coordination: Disruptions in care coordination, poor handoffs between shifts or units, and unclear processes increase the chance of medical errors. [1, 3] 

## Human and Provider Factors

* Communication: Breakdowns in communication among care teams, or between providers and families, are a leading cause of safety incidents. [1] 
* Cognitive Bias and Stress: Stress, cognitive fatigue, and inexperience impair clinical decision-making and performance. [1, 6] 
* Supervision: Insufficient oversight of trainees or delayed consultations raise the risk of adverse events. [7] 

## Technological and Environmental Factors

* Health IT Usability: Flaws or poor design in [World Health Organization (WHO)](https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/patient-safety) electronic health records and medication systems can cause data entry or administration errors. [1] 
* Equipment Availability: Shortages of standard clinical equipment or poorly designed physical layouts hinder safe workflows. [6, 8] 

## Patient-Related Factors

* Health Literacy: Limited understanding of diagnoses or treatment plans can result in non-adherence to medications or care instructions. [1] 
* Engagement: Patients who actively participate and communicate with their nurses and doctors experience safer care outcomes. [9, 10]
