Police use of force is primarily influenced by suspect behavior, legal standards, and departmental policies. [1, 2] 
## Suspect Behavior and Characteristics

* Suspect Resistance: Active or aggressive resistance by a suspect is the strongest predictor of police use of force. [1] 
* Severity and Threat: The seriousness of the crime and the immediacy of the threat posed to the officer or the public dictate the acceptable level of force. [3] 
* Impaired Judgment: Suspects exhibiting signs of mental illness, intoxication, or illicit drug use experience higher rates of police force due to perceived risks or non-compliance. [4] 
* Demographics: Studies indicate disparities where people of color, young males, and marginalized individuals face higher statistical rates of force. [1, 5] 

## Legal and Organizational Standards

* Objective Reasonableness: Under legal precedents like Graham v. Connor, force must be judged based on what a reasonable officer would do facing the same circumstances. [3, 6] 
* Departmental Policy: Clear agency guidelines, training standards on de-escalation, and supervision structures strongly shape how and when officers exercise discretion. [7, 8]
