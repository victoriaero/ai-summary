Homelessness and housing instability among homosexual and broader LGBTQ+ individuals are heavily influenced by family rejection, economic disparities, and systemic discrimination. According to research from the [Williams Institute at UCLA School of Law](https://williamsinstitute.law.ucla.edu/publications/lgbt-housing-instability/), LGBT adults and youth experience significantly higher rates of poverty and housing insecurity compared to cisgender and heterosexual peers. [1, 2, 3] 
## Major Factors Influencing Homelessness

* Family Rejection and Abuse: Family conflict, abuse, or being forced out of the home after coming out drives a large percentage of LGBTQ+ youth into homelessness. Studies note that LGBTQ+ youth are more than twice as likely to experience homelessness as non-LGBTQ+ youth. [2, 4, 5] 
* Housing and Lending Discrimination: Many jurisdictions lack comprehensive legal protections against discrimination based on sexual orientation in the rental and mortgage markets. Same-sex couples and individuals often face bias, lower loan approval rates, or higher fees from landlords and lenders. [1, 2, 3] 
* Economic Insecurity: Higher rates of poverty, unemployment, and wage gaps disproportionately affect the LGBTQ+ community, reducing access to affordable housing and homeownership. [1, 2, 3] 
* Unsafe or Inaccessible Shelters: Many emergency shelters and temporary housing options lack culturally competent staff or safe, inclusive environments, which can deter unhoused individuals from seeking or accepting shelter. [6, 7, 8] 
* Intersectionality and Systemic Biases: Marginalization is compounded for individuals who face overlapping biases based on race, ethnicity, or gender identity, with BIPOC (Black, Indigenous, and People of Color) LGBTQ+ individuals facing significantly elevated risks. [3, 9, 10]
