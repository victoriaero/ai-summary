Several key social, institutional, and environmental factors influence educational access and opportunities for homosexual and LGBTQ+ individuals. [1, 2] 
## School Climate and Harassment

* Bullying and Safety: Homosexual students frequently face verbal or physical harassment, leading many to feel unsafe at school.
* Attendance and Retention: Unsafe environments result in higher rates of absenteeism, skipping classes, and dropping out, which negatively impact overall academic performance. [3, 4] 

## Institutional Policies and Support

* Non-Discrimination Protections: The presence or absence of explicit school and legal non-discrimination policies regarding sexual orientation directly shapes whether students feel protected.
* Resource Groups: The availability of supportive student groups, such as Gay-Straight Alliances (GSAs), and dedicated campus resource centers fosters a sense of belonging. [1, 2] 

## Family and Social Background

* Family Acceptance: Caregiver and family acceptance or rejection heavily influences a student's mental health, stability, and motivation to pursue higher education.
* Geographic Relocation: Many LGBTQ+ students intentionally choose colleges in more welcoming cities or states to escape unsupportive local environments or unaccepting families. [1, 5] 

## Curriculum and Representation

* Inclusive Education: Curricula that integrate LGBTQ+ history, health, and social sciences reduce stigma and promote positive socioemotional outcomes.
* Staff Training: Schools with staff trained to act as supportive mentors or allies provide critical intervention against exclusion. [1, 6]
