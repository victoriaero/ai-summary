College access and admission for LGBTQ+ (homosexual and sexual minority) students are influenced by a combination of social, psychological, institutional, and legal factors that shape whether they aspire to, apply for, and successfully enter higher education. [1, 2, 3] 
## Pre-College Aspirations and Support

* High School Climate and Bullying: Anti-LGBTQ+ victimization, bullying, and a lack of protective policies in secondary schools often lead to higher rates of truancy, lower academic engagement, and reduced college aspirations. [1, 4] 
* Teacher and Peer Support: The presence of supportive educators and campus groups like [Gender and Sexuality Alliances (GSAs)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8916710/) strongly correlates with higher self-esteem and a greater likelihood of LGBTQ+ youth aspiring to attend college. [1] 
* Family Acceptance and Socioeconomic Background: Family rejection or acceptance plays a major role; studies note that sexual minority youth frequently weigh family dynamics when deciding whether to pursue higher education or move away from home. Disparities in parental education levels across different sexual orientation subgroups can also indirectly affect college preparation. [5, 6] 

## Institutional Environment and College Choice

* Perceived Campus Climate: LGBTQ+ applicants are significantly more likely than non-LGBTQ+ peers to select a college in a different city or state specifically to seek a welcoming, inclusive environment or to distance themselves from unsupportive families. [5, 7] 
* Visibility of Non-Discrimination Policies: The inclusion of sexual orientation in a university's [non-discrimination policies](https://www.higheredtoday.org/2017/04/10/lgbtq-students-higher-education/) and the presence of visible resource centers heavily influence an applicant's decision to apply and enroll. [8] 

## Legal and Policy Frameworks

* Federal Protections: Laws like [Title IX](https://www.evidencebasedmentoring.org/lgbtq-college-student-guide/) ban sex-based discrimination at federally funded colleges, which legally secures equal access to admissions, housing, and financial assistance for LGBTQ+ students. [2] 
* State-Level Contexts: Uneven legal protections and varying state legislation regarding LGBTQ+ rights create geographic disparities, making higher education more accessible or intimidating depending on the state. [8]
