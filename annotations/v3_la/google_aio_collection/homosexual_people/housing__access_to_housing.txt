Access to housing for LGBTQ+ individuals is heavily influenced by systemic discrimination, economic disparities, family rejection, and a patchwork of legal protections. [1, 2, 3] 
According to data from the [Williams Institute](https://williamsinstitute.law.ucla.edu/publications/lgbt-housing-instability/) and the [United Way L.A.](https://unitedwayla.org/news/housing-inequality-within-the-lgbtq-community/), several core factors shape these housing challenges: [1, 2] 
## Economic Disparities and Affordability

* Higher Poverty Rates: LGBTQ+ adults face a poverty rate of about 21.6%, compared to 15.7% for cisgender straight adults.
* Lower Homeownership: Only about 49% to 50% of LGBTQ+ adults own their homes, compared to roughly 64% to 70% of non-LGBTQ+ adults.
* Wage Gaps: Full-time LGBTQ+ workers often earn less on average, reducing their ability to save for deposits or mortgages. [1, 2] 

## Housing and Lending Discrimination

* Rental Bias: Landlords sometimes show same-sex couples fewer available units, quote higher rents, or refuse leases outright. Research highlighted by the [Urban Institute](https://archive-housingmatters.urban.org/articles/five-facts-about-housing-access-lgbt-people) shows that gay men and transgender applicants frequently encounter more hurdles than cisgender or heterosexual applicants.
* Mortgage Hurdles: Same-sex couples face 3% to 8% lower mortgage approval rates and higher borrowing fees in many lending markets. [1, 2, 4, 5] 

## Family Rejection and Youth Homelessness

* Coming Out: Strained relationships and rejection by family members force many young queer people out of their family homes.
* Disproportionate Risk: LGBTQ+ youth account for 20% to 40% of the total homeless youth population, putting them at an early disadvantage for long-term housing stability. [1, 6] 

## Legal and Intersectional Challenges

* Varying Laws: Federal and state anti-discrimination laws offer an uneven or incomplete shield against bias in renting and lending.
* Intersectionality: People of color and transgender individuals within the LGBTQ+ community experience compounded forms of discrimination, resulting in even lower homeownership rates and higher rates of housing insecurity. [1, 2, 3, 5, 7]
