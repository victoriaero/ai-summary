Several social, organizational, and individual factors influence the prevalence and severity of workplace harassment against homosexual and LGBTQ+ employees. [1, 2] 
## Intersectionality and Demographics

* Race and Ethnicity: According to data from the [Williams Institute](https://williamsinstitute.law.ucla.edu/publications/lgbt-workplace-discrimination/), LGBTQ+ employees of color experience higher rates of verbal harassment (36%) and lifetime discrimination compared to white peers (26%). [1, 2] 
* Gender Identity and Expression: Transgender and nonbinary workers report significantly higher rates of harassment (55%) than cisgender LGB employees. Additionally, individuals who do not conform to traditional gender presentations or stereotypes are more frequently targeted. [1, 3, 4] 

## Visibility and Disclosure ("Being Out")

* Openness at Work: Employees who are openly "out" to supervisors and coworkers report higher rates of observed discrimination and harassment (39%) compared to closeted peers (12%). [1] 
* Invisibility of Identity: Because sexual orientation is an invisible identity until disclosed, employees navigate complex decisions about whether to come out. Many engage in "covering" behaviors—suppressing aspects of their identity to blend in—to avoid potential hostility. [1, 4] 

## Cultural and Ideological Environment

* Religious and Moral Beliefs: Research highlights that 57% of workers attribute workplace mistreatment and hostile comments to the personal religious or moral beliefs of coworkers or managers, who may view equal treatment or expression as conflicting with their values. [2, 4] 
* Tone from Leadership: Organizations lacking explicit non-discrimination policies, formal LGBTQ+ support groups, or active diversity training often foster environments where casual homophobia, microaggressions, and offensive "jokes" go unchecked. [2, 5, 6]
