Patient safety for homosexual and broader LGBTQ+ individuals in healthcare is primarily influenced by provider bias, communication breakdowns, and a lack of culturally competent training. [1] 
## Provider Bias and Discrimination

* Fear of Mistreatment: Anticipated or experienced stigma from doctors and support staff causes many homosexual individuals to delay or completely avoid necessary medical care. [2, 3, 4] 
* Disrespectful Treatment: Patients frequently report feeling judged, treated rudely, or having personal or religious beliefs imposed upon them by medical staff. [2] 
* Medical Gaslighting: Studies indicate that sexual and gender minorities experience higher rates of medical-related dismissal or invalidation of their symptoms by clinicians. [5] 

## Communication and Psychological Safety

* Inadequate Disclosures: A lack of trust and psychological safety prevents patients from openly sharing private health information, sexual history, or lifestyle details. [1, 6] 
* Missed Diagnostics: Poor dialogue and fear of judgment lead directly to missed routine health screenings, delayed interventions, and improper diagnoses. [1, 6] 
* Heteronormative Assumptions: Providers often assume all patients are heterosexual, failing to ask relevant health questions or provide tailored risk assessments. [7] 

## Provider Knowledge and Systemic Gaps

* Training Deficits: A widespread shortage of education regarding sexual minority health needs leaves clinicians unaware of specific physical and mental health risks. [1, 8, 9] 
* Absence of Inclusive Data: Many health systems fail to routinely collect Sexual Orientation and Gender Identity (SOGI) data, which hinders targeted quality improvement and equitable care delivery. [1] 
* Intersectional Stigma: Safety risks are further magnified when sexual orientation intersects with race, socioeconomic class, geographic isolation, or age. [8, 9]
