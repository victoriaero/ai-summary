Multiple intersecting demographic, systemic, and situational factors influence police use of force and mistreatment involving homosexual and LGBTQ+ individuals in the criminal justice system. [1, 2] 
According to a national survey by the [American Civil Liberties Union (ACLU)](https://www.aclu.org/publications/policing-progress-findings-from-a-national-survey-of-lgbtq-peoples-experiences-with-law-enforcement) and research from the [Williams Institute](https://williamsinstitute.law.ucla.edu/publications/law-enforcement-lgbtq-people/), the primary drivers and correlates of disparate police force and aggressive enforcement include:
## Intersectional Demographics

* Race and Ethnicity: LGBTQ+ people of color—particularly Black, Brown, and Latine individuals—face significantly higher rates of aggressive police stops, physical force, and arrests compared to their white counterparts. [2, 3] 
* Gender Identity and Expression: Transgender, nonbinary, and gender non-conforming individuals experience disproportionately higher rates of hostile interactions, physical force, and searches than cisgender LGBQ+ individuals. [2, 3] 
* Socioeconomic Status: Homelessness or low household income heavily correlates with increased police contact and vulnerability to heavy-handed enforcement and profiling. [2, 4] 

## Systemic and Procedural Factors

* Biased Policing and Profiling: Vague laws (such as those regarding disorderly conduct, loitering, or public lewdness) grant individual officers wide discretion, historically leading to the profiling of queer individuals—such as young gay men or masculine-presenting queer women—as sex workers or gang members. [4] 
* Implicit and Explicit Bias: Research notes that underlying homophobic and transphobic attitudes among individual officers or within department cultures can shape field discretion and escalate hostility during civilian encounters. [5] 
* Impact of Criminalizing Legislation: The presence of localized or shifting criminalizing laws targeting queer and trans populations increases adversarial police contact, raising the likelihood of physical conflict or use of force. [2]
