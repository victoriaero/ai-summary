Housing stability for homosexual and LGBTQ+ individuals is influenced by economic disparities, systemic discrimination, family dynamics, and legal protections. Data from organizations like the [Williams Institute](https://williamsinstitute.law.ucla.edu/publications/lgbt-housing-instability/) and the [Urban Institute](https://www.urban.org/urban-wire/how-can-we-reduce-housing-instability-among-lgbtq-americans) highlight the core factors shaping these outcomes: [1, 2] 
## Economic Insecurity and Poverty

* Higher Poverty Rates: LGBT adults face roughly a 15% to 21.6% higher rate of poverty compared to cisgender heterosexual adults. [1, 3] 
* Lower Homeownership: Homeownership rates for LGBT adults hover around 49% to 50%, compared to roughly 64% to 70% for non-LGBT adults. [1, 3] 
* Wage Disparities: Earned income gaps and higher rates of underemployment limit the ability to build long-term savings and strong credit scores. [2, 4] 

## Discrimination and Bias

* Housing and Rental Bias: Landlords frequently show fewer available units or quote higher fees to same-sex couples and gay men during testing audits. [1, 5] 
* Lending Disparities: Same-sex couples experience 3% to 8% lower mortgage approval rates and face higher borrowing fees. [1, 3] 
* Shelter Discrimination: Homeless LGBTQ+ individuals often encounter hostile environments or outright denial of entry in traditional shelter systems. [1, 5] 

## Family Rejection and Youth Vulnerability

* Family Breakdown: Rejection based on sexual orientation or gender identity drives roughly 25% to 40% of unhoused LGBTQ+ youth from their family homes. [2] 
* Disproportionate Risk: LGBTQ+ youth make up 20% to 45% of the total homeless youth population, putting them at a severe disadvantage during critical education and employment years. [1, 6] 

## Legal Protections and Policy Patchwork

* Inconsistent Laws: Federal protections under the Fair Housing Act (following interpretations of sex discrimination) exist, but state and local anti-discrimination laws regarding sexual orientation remain a patchwork, causing many individuals to restrict their housing searches to more legally protective regions.
