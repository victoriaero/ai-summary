Several systemic, social, and legal factors influence the higher rates of arrest and detention for LGBTQ+ individuals within the criminal justice system. [1] 
## Discriminatory Policing and Profiling

* Biased Enforcement: Law enforcement officers often rely on stereotypes, occasionally equating non-conforming gender expression or homosexuality with criminality or deviancy. [2] 
* Proxy and Vague Laws: Vague statutes—such as disorderly conduct, loitering, and public lewdness—give officers broad personal discretion, leading to the disproportionate targeting of queer individuals and public profiling of youth of color or transgender people as sex workers. [2] 
* Targeting Public Spaces: Historical and contemporary policing of known gathering areas for queer individuals increases the frequency of police stops, searches, and subsequent arrests. [3, 4] 

## Socioeconomic Vulnerability and Survival Crimes

* Family Rejection: High rates of rejection by families lead many LGBTQ+ youth to experience homelessness (comprising roughly 40% of homeless youth populations). [5, 6] 
* Survival Economies: Due to systemic discrimination in housing and employment, marginalized individuals are often pushed into survival economies, engaging in activities like sex work, low-level drug sales, or survival theft to meet basic needs, which directly escalates arrest risks. [5, 7] 
* Status Offenses: Homeless or runaway queer youth are frequently detained for minor infractions related to lack of shelter, such as breaking curfews or loitering in public spaces. [5] 

## Intersecting Marginalization

* Race and Gender Identity: Disparities multiply for LGBTQ+ people of color and transgender individuals. Research from organizations like the [Prison Policy Initiative](https://www.prisonpolicy.org/research/lgbtq/) highlights that Black transgender individuals face exceptionally high lifetime rates of policing interference and incarceration. [4, 8] 
* School-to-Prison Pipeline: Zero-tolerance policies in educational environments disproportionately penalize LGBTQ+ youth, pushing them into the juvenile justice system earlier. [1, 3]
