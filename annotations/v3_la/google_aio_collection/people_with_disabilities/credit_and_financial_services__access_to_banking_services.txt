Access to banking and credit services for people with disabilities is significantly shaped by physical, digital, economic, and social factors. [1, 2] 
## 🏛️ Physical and Infrastructural Barriers

* Branch accessibility: Inaccessible physical layouts, heavy doors, or lack of ramps restrict branch visits. [2, 3] 
* ATM limitations: Cash machines often lack audio output, tactile keys, or wheelchair-accessible heights. [3, 4] 
* Transportation hurdles: Difficulty traveling to physical locations prevents routine in-person banking. [4] 

## 💻 Digital and Technological Gaps

* Inaccessible software: Websites and mobile apps frequently lack screen-reader compatibility or keyboard navigation. [5] 
* Authentication blocks: Security features like visual CAPTCHAs lock out visually impaired users. [3] 
* Digital divide: Lower smartphone or high-speed internet adoption limits remote banking usage. [6] 

## 💵 Economic and Credit Vulnerabilities

* Lower income profiles: High unemployment and reliance on fixed disability benefits restrict formal account demand. [1, 7, 8] 
* Credit discrimination: Lenders often penalize or underserved borrowers relying on non-wage disability income. [9, 10] 
* Predatory reliance: High fees drive vulnerable populations toward costly alternative services like check cashers. [8, 10] 

## 👥 Social, Staff, and Policy Factors

* Lack of training: Bank staff often lack awareness regarding assistive communication or disability accommodations.
* Stigma and autonomy loss: Cultural bias or forced reliance on third-party guardians compromises financial privacy and agency.
* Regulatory gaps: Weak enforcement of universal design standards leaves institutional compliance optional. [3, 11]
