Students with disabilities face a significantly higher risk of bullying and harassment in educational settings—often experiencing rates two to three times higher than their non-disabled peers. [1, 2] 
According to guidelines from [StopBullying.gov](https://www.stopbullying.gov/bullying/special-needs) and the [PACER Center](https://www.pacer.org/bullying/info/students-with-disabilities/), several key risk factors increase vulnerability to harassment: [2, 3] 
## Key Influencing Factors

* Perceived Differences and Alienation: Students are often singled out by peers as "different," which reduces their integration and makes it harder to form protective friendships. [3, 4] 
* Communication Challenges: Difficulty expressing needs, understanding social cues, or responding quickly to verbal and physical situations leaves students unable to easily deter or report aggressors. [3, 4] 
* Behavioral and Emotional Differences: Children with conditions involving emotional disturbances, attention deficit hyperactivity disorder (ADHD), or autism spectrum disorder (ASD) face heightened risks due to atypical behaviors or social skill challenges. [5, 6] 
* Physical and Functional Vulnerability: Physical disabilities, sensory impairments, or medical vulnerabilities (such as severe food allergies, epilepsy, or diabetes) create power imbalances that bullies exploit. [3, 6] 
* Lack of Self-Advocacy Skills: Limited tools or practice in self-advocacy prevent students from effectively stopping the behavior or seeking adult intervention independently. [4, 6] 

## Legal and Educational Context
Under federal civil rights laws—including Section 504 of the Rehabilitation Act and Title II of the Americans with Disabilities Act (ADA)—disability harassment that creates a hostile learning environment is illegal. When bullying interferes with a student's educational access, it may also constitute a violation of a Free Appropriate Public Education (FAPE), requiring schools to intervene immediately through mechanisms like Individualized Education Programs (IEPs) or 504 plans. [3, 7, 8, 9]
