Access to housing for people with disabilities is primarily influenced by affordability, physical accessibility, and systemic discrimination. [1, 2] 
## Affordability and Economic Barriers

* Fixed Incomes: Many individuals rely on Supplemental Security Income (SSI), which often falls significantly short of average market rents, leaving millions severely cost-burdened. [2, 3] 
* Housing Assistance Gaps: A large majority of low-income disabled households qualify for federal housing assistance but never receive it due to funding and availability shortages. [1, 3] 
* Added Expenses: Finding or retrofitting a home requires extra financial resources for modifications, specialized equipment, and supportive care services. [2] 

## Physical Accessibility Constraints

* Severe Supply Shortage: Fewer than 5% of U.S. housing units offer moderate accessibility features, and less than 1% accommodate full wheelchair access. [3] 
* Structural Obstacles: Basic entry steps, narrow doorways, non-adaptive bathrooms, and multi-family design violations routinely block independent living. [4, 5, 6] 
* Digital and Policy Barriers: Inaccessible property websites, unclear accommodation request processes, and rigid landlord policies make searching for a suitable home difficult. [6, 7] 

## Discrimination and Stigma

* High Discrimination Rates: Disability-based grievances consistently account for over half of all fair housing complaints filed with agencies like HUD. [8] 
* Direct and Indirect Bias: Landlords may overtly reject applicants, refuse reasonable modification requests, or subtly steer tenants away to avoid compliance costs. [7, 9] 
* Community Pushback: Local opposition often targets group homes or supportive multi-family housing developments intended for people with disabilities. [2, 10]
