Workplace harassment against people with disabilities is primarily driven by social stigma, poor organizational leadership, and friction over reasonable workplace accommodations. [1, 2] 
## Social Stigma and Misconceptions

* Unproductive stereotypes: Co-workers and managers sometimes incorrectly view disabled employees as less capable or less productive. [3, 4] 
* Perceived special treatment: Coworker resentment can build when teams misunderstand why a colleague receives flexible schedules or custom tools. [2] 
* Fear and ignorance: A general lack of education about different physical and mental conditions leads to awkwardness, teasing, or outright hostility. [3] 

## Accommodation Conflicts

* Denied requests: When an employer delays or refuses to provide basic tools or schedule adjustments, it creates daily frustration and a hostile atmosphere. [5] 
* Cost friction: Misinformed managers may view the cost or effort of an accommodation as a burden, projecting that frustration onto the employee. [3] 
* Sabotage: Unsupportive peers sometimes actively work against or mock the modified workstations or tech used by disabled workers. [6] 

## Workplace Culture and Leadership

* Toxic "banter": Offices that tolerate offensive jokes, intrusive questioning, or casual bullying create a culture where severe harassment thrives. [1] 
* Inaction from management: When supervisors ignore early signs of teasing or exclusion, bad actors feel empowered to escalate their behavior.
* Fear of retaliation: Employees often stay silent about harassment because they worry about losing their jobs, getting demoted, or facing worse treatment. [4, 7] 

## Disclosure and Visibility

* Forced disclosure: Pressuring a worker to publicly share private medical details before they feel ready breaches trust and invites targeting.
* Hidden disabilities: Workers with non-visible conditions (like chronic pain, anxiety, or autism) often face disbelief and accusations of faking when they ask for help. [8] 

To ensure workplaces follow the rules set by organizations like the [U.S. Equal Employment Opportunity Commission](https://www.eeoc.gov/youth/disability-discrimination), companies must enforce strict anti-harassment policies. [9, 10]
