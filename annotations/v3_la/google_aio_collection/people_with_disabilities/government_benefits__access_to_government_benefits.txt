Access to government benefits for people with disabilities depends on financial thresholds, medical criteria, work history, and administrative capacity. [1, 2, 3] 
## 💰 Income and Asset Limits

* Means-testing: Programs like Supplemental Security Income (SSI) and Medicaid require strict limits on income and countable resources (often capped at $2,000 for individuals). [4, 5] 
* Benefits cliffs: Earning slightly above allowed limits can trigger the total loss of critical healthcare and cash assistance. [1, 4] 
* Asset rules: Owning basic property or modest savings above thresholds can disqualify applicants. [4] 

## 🩺 Medical and Work Criteria

* Severity definitions: Programs like Social Security Disability Insurance (SSDI) require proof of an inability to engage in Substantial Gainful Activity (SGA) for at least 12 months. [3, 5] 
* Work history credits: SSDI eligibility depends directly on past employment and the accumulation of enough work credits through payroll taxes. [3, 5] 
* Impairment type: Mental health conditions and physical or musculoskeletal limitations are evaluated under strict, standardized federal medical listings. [6, 7] 

## ⚙️ Administrative and Systemic Barriers

* Processing capacity: Staffing shortages, communication loops, and agency backlogs delay approvals during critical medical crises. [2] 
* Digital access: Reliance on online portals disadvantages applicants with limited internet connectivity or unstable housing. [2] 
* Geographic and economic context: Local economic structures (such as heavy physical labor industries) and state-level policy variations influence application volumes and approval rates. [8]
