Access to credit for people with disabilities is primarily influenced by systemic economic barriers, structural limitations in banking infrastructure, and persistent lending biases. [1, 2] 
## 📉 Economic and Income Factors

* Lower employment rates: High unemployment reduces documented stable earned income. [3] 
* Reliance on fixed benefits: Dependence on programs like SSDI or SSI can be misunderstood by traditional underwriting models. [4, 5] 
* Higher baseline expenses: Medical and accessibility costs reduce monthly financial liquidity and savings. [1] 

## 💻 Infrastructure and Accessibility Barriers

* Inaccessible technology: Digital banking portals and mobile apps frequently lack screen-reader or assistive compatibility. [2] 
* Product design gaps: Mainstream institutions rarely offer micro-loans or flexible terms tailored to disability-related expenses. [1] 
* Physical and communication hurdles: Branch environments often lack physical accommodations or sign-language resources. [2, 3] 

## ⚠️ Systemic and Regulatory Challenges

* Credit scoring limitations: Traditional credit algorithms fail to reliably factor in non-traditional or fixed federal assistance streams. [4, 6] 
* Implicit discrimination: Despite protections under laws like the [Equal Credit Opportunity Act](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/rules-policy/regulations/1002/) (ECOA), informal bias persists during manual underwriting. [4] 
* Predatory reliance: Exclusion from traditional banks forces many to rely on high-cost payday loans and pawn shops. [7]
