Insurance coverage and healthcare access for people with disabilities are influenced by several interconnected socioeconomic, regulatory, and functional factors. [1, 2] 
## 📊 Key Factors Influencing Insurance Coverage

* ## 💵 Income and Asset Limits
* Medicaid thresholds: Public programs often require low income and limited assets.
   * Employment disincentives: Working individuals may risk losing benefits.
   * Buy-in programs: [Medicaid Buy-In](https://www.urban.org/research/publication/improving-health-coverage-working-people-disabilities) allows working adults to maintain coverage with higher earnings. [3] 
* ## 🩺 Disability Severity and Type
* Functional needs: Greater need for long-term care or [Activities of Daily Living (ADL)](https://journalistsresource.org/politics-and-government/disabilities-insurance-affordable-care-act/) support.
   * Subgroup disparities: Mental health or cognitive disabilities face higher uninsurance and access barriers.
   * Medical necessity definitions: Policies often target short-term care over chronic support. [4, 5, 6] 
* ## 📍 Geographic and Policy Variations
* State-level rules: Medicaid eligibility and scope vary significantly by location.
   * Regional gaps: Uninsurance rates differ widely between geographic areas.
   * Federal reforms: Legislation like the [Affordable Care Act (ACA)](https://www.kff.org/medicaid/5-key-facts-about-medicaid-coverage-for-people-with-disabilities/) expanded coverage pathways. [2, 4, 7, 8] 
* ## 👥 Demographics and Employment
* Employer-sponsored insurance: Lower employment rates limit private coverage access.
   * Intersectionality: Gender, race, and socioeconomic status compound coverage and care disparities.
   * Age brackets: Dependency shifts between private options, Medicaid, and Medicare. [1, 6, 7, 9, 10]
