Patient safety for people with disabilities is heavily influenced by communication barriers, provider knowledge gaps, physical inaccessibility, and systemic omissions in basic care. [1, 2, 3] 
## Key Influencing Factors

* Communication Barriers: Ineffective communication between providers and patients leads to misunderstandings, misdiagnosis, and difficulties in obtaining informed consent. [1, 4] 
* Diagnostic Overshadowing: Clinicians frequently commit errors in perception by attributing a patient's new or worsening symptoms entirely to their pre-existing disability rather than investigating an independent, treatable medical condition. [5] 
* Provider Knowledge Gaps and Training Deficits: Staff inexperience and lack of mandatory disability-awareness training often result in inappropriate treatment decisions, misuse of mental capacity laws, and inadequate responses to complex medical needs. [1, 6] 
* Physical and Structural Inaccessibility: Inaccessible facilities, heavy or non-adjustable examination tables, and a lack of proper mobility equipment elevate the physical risk of injury, missed physical screenings, and compromised clinical examinations. [2, 3, 4] 
* Omissions of Basic Nursing Care: Hospitalized patients with intellectual or developmental disabilities face higher rates of preventable harm due to delayed treatments, missed diagnostics, and unassisted feeding or hydration. [1, 7] 
* Flawed Incident Reporting: Healthcare systems frequently fail to flag disability status or track safety events specific to disabled populations, causing systemic safety issues to remain underreported and unaddressed. [1, 7]
