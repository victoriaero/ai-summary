AI OVERVIEW TEXT

Poverty, housing discrimination, and a severe shortage of physically accessible units are the primary factors driving homelessness among people with disabilities. [1, 2] 
People with disabilities experience poverty at double the rate of nondisabled individuals and face intersecting economic, physical, and systemic barriers that increase their risk of housing instability. [1, 2] 
## Economic and Income Factors

* High Poverty Rates: Disabled adults experience poverty at twice the rate of non-disabled adults, severely limiting their financial cushion. [1, 2] 
* Insufficient Benefits: Federal disability benefits, such as Supplemental Security Income (SSI), often fall well below the actual cost of market-rate rent. [1, 2] 
* Asset Limits: Public assistance programs often enforce strict asset limits, making it illegal or penalizing for recipients to save money for financial emergencies. [2, 3] 
* Employment Barriers: Higher rates of unemployment and subminimum wage policies for workers with disabilities limit upward economic mobility. [1, 3, 4] 

## Housing and Structural Barriers

* Lack of Accessible Units: Less than 5% of the overall housing stock is physically accessible for individuals with mobility impairments. [2] 
* Housing Discrimination: People with disabilities face disproportionate rates of discrimination by landlords, realtors, and housing providers who deny reasonable modification requests or reject applicants based on their source of income. [1, 2, 5, 6] 
* Support Service Gaps: A lack of in-home supportive care and community-based health services makes independent living difficult to maintain. [7, 8] 
* Inaccessible Emergency Shelters: Traditional emergency shelters frequently lack physical accommodations, adaptive bathrooms, or specialized staff, forcing many disabled individuals to remain unsheltered. [1, 3, 4] 

You can read more about these systemic barriers through the [California Department of Housing and Community Development](https://www.hcd.ca.gov/policy-and-research/intersectional-policy-work/people-disabilities) or analyze policy solutions via the [Center for American Progress](https://www.americanprogress.org/article/recognizing-addressing-housing-insecurity-disabled-renters/).
