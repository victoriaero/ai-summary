Multiple intersecting variables—including race, suspect gender, socioeconomic status, and officer characteristics—influence police use of force involving cisgender individuals.
Key Influencing Factors
• Race and Ethnicity: Racial disparities remain a primary driver in use-of-force incidents. Research indicates that Black and Latino men experience police use of force significantly more often than white individuals.
• Suspect Gender and Sex: Cisgender men, particularly Black and Latino men, experience higher rates of physical force and aggressive policing tactics compared to cisgender women. However, low-income cisgender women of color still face high rates of verbal threats, shouting, and harassment during involuntary stops.
• Socioeconomic Status: Lower household income (such as earning under $20,000 annually) increases the baseline exposure risk and likelihood of experiencing coercive police actions across racial demographics.
• Officer Characteristics and Culture: Studies on officer demographics show mixed results regarding direct use of physical force, but traditional organizational cultures (such as "machismo") and lower officer experience or education levels have been correlated with higher escalations and hostile confrontations.
• Context of the Encounter: The nature of the police stop—such as traffic stops versus street/pedestrian stops—alters how race and gender intersect, with racial disparities showing the sharpest contrast during involuntary street stops and apprehensions.
