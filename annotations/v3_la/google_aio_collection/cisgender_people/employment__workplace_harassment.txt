Workplace harassment for cisgender people is primarily influenced by organizational culture, power dynamics, intersectional identities like race and age, and non-compliance with traditional gender stereotypes.
According to guidelines and data from the U.S. Equal Employment Opportunity Commission (EEOC) and research organizations, several key factors shape these risks:
Organizational and Cultural Factors
• Male-Dominated Cultures: Workplaces where men hold disproportionate power and center norms around masculine behaviors can increase vulnerability to sexual or gender-based harassment.
• Homogeneous Workforces: A historical lack of diversity in an organization often correlates with a higher risk of inappropriate conduct, bullying, and hostile work environments.
• Power Disparities: Hierarchical imbalances—such as significant gaps in status or authority between supervisors and subordinate staff—foster an environment where harassment is less likely to be reported or penalized.
• Isolated Workplaces: Physical or operational isolation reduces visibility and peer oversight, creating greater opportunity for misconduct.
Intersectional and Individual Factors
• Intersectionality with Race and Age: Cisgender people of color, younger workforces (under age 35), and lower-income workers experience statistically higher rates of overlapping harassment and discrimination compared to white or older peers.
• Gender Expression and Stereotyping: Harassment can occur when an employee's appearance, mannerisms, voice tone, or behavior do not conform to traditional or rigid expectations of binary gender roles.
