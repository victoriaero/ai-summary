Economic security, household structure, and systemic factors like income and safety net access heavily influence housing stability for cisgender people.
Key Economic Factors
• Income and Employment: Stable, full-time wages determine the ability to pay rent or mortgages on time. Wage gaps and job loss increase housing vulnerability.
• Housing Costs: High rent burdens relative to income force households into overcrowding or eviction risk.
• Poverty Levels: Low-income households face much higher rates of housing distress and inability to maintain housing costs.
Household and Social Factors
• Household Composition: Single-parent households—predominantly led by cisgender women—face greater financial strain and higher risks of housing need compared to households led by cisgender men.
• Interpersonal Safety: Intimate partner violence and domestic abuse strongly disrupt housing stability, particularly for low-income women.
• Support Systems: Access to community resources, rental assistance, and safety nets buffers against eviction and homelessness.
