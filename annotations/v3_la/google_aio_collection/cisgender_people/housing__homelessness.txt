Poverty, lack of affordable housing, and severe economic distress are the primary factors that influence homelessness for cisgender people.
Economic and Financial Factors
• Severe Rent Burden: Spending more than 50% of a limited household income on rent leaves little to no money for emergencies, food, or medical care, leading quickly to eviction.
• Poverty and Unemployment: Job loss, low wages, underemployment, and a lack of reliable savings or emergency funds make maintaining stable housing impossible.
• Evictions: A formal or informal eviction history creates major barriers to passing tenant screening processes for new apartments.
Individual and Health Factors
• Mental Health Conditions: Untreated or severe mental health challenges can disrupt a person’s ability to maintain employment or manage household responsibilities.
• Substance Use Disorders: Active substance use struggles without adequate treatment support can strain personal and financial stability.
• Trauma and Domestic Violence: Escaping unsafe or abusive household environments often forces individuals—particularly cisgender women—into sudden homelessness.
Structural and Systemic Factors
• Lack of Social Safety Nets: Inadequate public assistance, long waitlists for housing vouchers, and a community shortage of low-income housing leave vulnerable individuals without a cushion.
• Disparities by Gender and Demographics: Research shows that cisgender women face higher rates of housing insecurity and core housing need than cisgender men, often driven by lower average earnings and caregiver burdens.
