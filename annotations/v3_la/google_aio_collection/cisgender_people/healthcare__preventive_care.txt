AI OVERVIEW TEXT
============================================================

Insurance coverage, income, and personal health beliefs heavily influence whether cisgender people access routine preventive care.
Preventive care utilization for cisgender individuals is shaped by several key categories of factors:
Economic and Resource Factors
• Health Insurance Status: Having coverage significantly increases the likelihood of receiving annual wellness visits, routine screenings (like mammograms or colonoscopies), and vaccinations.
• Cost and Affordability: Out-of-pocket expenses, high deductibles, and lack of insurance coverage for preventive services often deter patients from seeking routine care.
• Access to a Usual Source of Care: Having a designated primary care provider or medical home makes ongoing preventive health monitoring much more consistent.
Individual and Demographic Factors
• Age and Sex/Gender: Recommended screening schedules vary based on biological age and specific anatomical organs (e.g., cervical or prostate screenings), and utilization patterns differ historically between cisgender men and women.
• Health Beliefs and Literacy: Personal attitudes toward modern medicine, awareness of screening guidelines, and perception of personal risk strongly dictate proactive health behaviors.
• Race and Ethnicity: Systemic inequalities and historical or interpersonal experiences of bias can create gaps in how comfortable or able cisgender individuals from diverse backgrounds feel when accessing primary care services.
Systemic and Provider Factors
• Provider Communication: Patients are far more likely to engage in preventive measures when primary care clinicians actively discuss and recommend age-appropriate screenings and immunizations.
• Geographic and Structural Availability: Living in areas with a shortage of primary care physicians or residing in state policy environments that impact healthcare infrastructure affects the overall availability of preventive services.
