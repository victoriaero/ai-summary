Incarceration for cisgender people in the criminal justice system is primarily influenced by factors such as race, socioeconomic status, substance use disorders, and the specific type of alleged offense.
Key Influencing Factors
• Race and Ethnicity: Black and Hispanic individuals experience disproportionately higher rates of arrest and incarceration compared to white cisgender individuals due to systemic disparities and targeted policing.
• Socioeconomic Status: Poverty, lack of stable housing, and unemployment strongly correlate with higher justice system contact and an inability to afford bail or legal representation.
• Substance Use: Substance use disorders (SUD) are among the most common underlying medical or mental health conditions associated with arrests and subsequent incarceration for cisgender populations.
• Type of Offense: Offense severity and conviction type heavily dictate sentencing outcomes; for instance, women in state prisons are frequently incarcerated for drug or property offenses, while violent offenses carry a much higher likelihood of long-term imprisonment across all demographics.
