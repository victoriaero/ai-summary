Career advancement for non-Latino workers—including white, Black, Asian, and Indigenous professionals—is heavily shaped by human capital, access to professional networks, and broader systemic factors.
Key Factors Influencing Career Advancement
• Human Capital and Education: Higher levels of formal education, specialized degrees, and continuous skills training directly increase promotional opportunities and entry into high-paying professional sectors.
• Mentoring and Sponsorship: Relationships with senior leaders provide protégés with visibility, developmental feedback, and advocacy for leadership tracks.
• Professional Networks: Inclusion in informal and formal organizational networks allows employees to hear about unadvertised opportunities, build alliances, and secure executive support.
• Performance Evaluations and Bias: Performance appraisals can influence upward mobility, with subjective evaluation criteria sometimes introducing managerial biases that affect workers differently based on race and background.
• Systemic and Structural Dynamics: Historical patterns of occupational segregation, societal stratification, and varying levels of workplace discrimination create distinct structural advantages or headwinds across different racial and ethnic groups in the labor market.
