According to the [Prison Policy Initiative](https://www.prisonpolicy.org/research/racial_and_ethnic_disparities/), incarceration rates and criminal justice outcomes for non-Latino groups (such as Black and White Americans) are influenced by a combination of socioeconomic disparities, systemic policies, and legal decision-making. [1, 2] 
## Key Factors Influencing Incarceration

* Socioeconomic Disparities: Financial instability and under-resourced communities strongly correlate with higher rates of criminal justice contact, disproportionately affecting marginalized racial groups. [2] 
* Pre-existing Criminal History and Age: Prior incarceration history, younger age demographics, and statutory offense seriousness scores heavily shape sentencing and recidivism outcomes. [3, 4, 5] 
* Discretionary Legal Practices: Research highlighted by the [Sentencing Project](https://www.sentencingproject.org/reports/one-in-five-racial-disparity-in-imprisonment-causes-and-remedies/) shows that prosecutorial discretion, bail determinations, and the application of mandatory minimums or sentencing enhancements (such as gang or drug-zone rules) contribute to unequal penal outcomes. [1, 2] 
* Policing and Arrest Rates: Disparities in initial police stop frequencies, surveillance levels in specific neighborhoods, and arrest rates feed directly into higher subsequent rates of jail and prison admission. [1, 6, 7]
