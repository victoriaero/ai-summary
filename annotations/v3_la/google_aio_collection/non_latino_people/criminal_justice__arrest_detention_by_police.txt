Arrest and detention decisions for non-Latino individuals (such as White, Black, or Indigenous people) in the criminal justice system are influenced by legal severity, geographic crime concentration, and institutional practices. [1, 2, 3] 
## Key Factors Influencing Arrest and Detention

* Crime Severity and Legal History: The specific type of alleged offense—such as violent crimes versus minor infractions—and a person's prior criminal record heavily drive police decisions to arrest and court decisions to order pretrial detention. [1, 4] 
* ** Geographic Concentration ("Hot Spots")**: Arrests for all racial and ethnic groups are strongly concentrated within specific neighborhoods or block groups that experience higher overall crime rates. [2] 
* High-Volume Policing Strategies: Law enforcement allocation patterns in heavily policed or low-income areas increase the frequency of police-citizen contacts, which directly correlates with higher local arrest rates for residents in those zones. [5] 
* Pretrial Financial Conditions: For detained populations awaiting trial, the inability to pay cash bail frequently determines who remains in custody versus who is released, impacting defendants across non-Latino demographics. [6] 
* Demographic Variables: Studies analyzed by organizations like the [U.S. Commission on Civil Rights](https://www.usccr.gov) show that individual characteristics like gender (with males facing higher rates of referral and detention) and age also influence processing outcomes. [3, 6]
