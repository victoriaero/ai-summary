Economic hardship, severe shortages of affordable housing, and weak social safety nets are primary drivers of homelessness across populations, including non-Latino groups. [1] 
## Economic and Housing Market Factors

* High housing costs: Rapidly rising rents and a lack of deeply affordable housing force individuals and families out of stable housing when their income cannot keep pace. [1, 2] 
* Income constraints: Low wages, underemployment, and stagnant incomes relative to living expenses increase rent burdens and the risk of eviction. [1, 3] 
* Fragile social networks: A lack of personal financial reserves combined with impoverished or unsupported social networks prevents people from "doubling up" or relying on family during financial shocks. [2] 

## Systemic and Individual Vulnerabilities

* Health and behavioral challenges: Untreated or severe mental health conditions, physical disabilities, and limited access to adequate healthcare or insurance increase vulnerability to losing housing. [2, 3] 
* Justice system involvement: Past criminal justice backgrounds create significant barriers to securing rental housing, even when individuals possess housing vouchers or financial assistance. [2] 
* Disparities among subgroups: Within non-Latino populations, systemic marginalization heavily impacts specific demographics—such as Black Americans, Indigenous populations, and LGBTQ+ individuals—who experience homelessness at disproportionate rates due to historical wealth gaps and structural inequalities. [3, 4, 5]
