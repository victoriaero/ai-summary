Income level, employment status, family structure, education, and age are the primary measurable risk factors that explain differences in welfare and benefit participation among non-Latino racial and ethnic groups. [1] 
## Key Economic and Structural Factors

* Income and Poverty: Lower household income and higher rates of poverty directly increase eligibility and participation in means-tested safety-net programs like SNAP, Medicaid, and TANF. [1, 2] 
* Employment Status: Being employed or unemployed dictates access to specific work-linked benefits (like unemployment insurance), while low-wage employment often qualifies individuals for poverty-reduction credits such as the Earned Income Tax Credit (EITC). [3, 4] 
* Education: Lower overall levels of educational attainment correlate with lower earning power, which in turn raises the rate at which certain demographic groups qualify for public assistance. [1, 3] 
* Family Structure: Households with single parents or a higher share of dependent children often face higher economic vulnerability and qualify for structural family-support benefits. [1] 
* Age: Older adults interact with distinct government benefit structures, such as Social Security and Supplemental Security Income (SSI), based on their work history and retirement status. [5, 6]
