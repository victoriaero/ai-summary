Patient safety for non-Latino populations (including White, Black, Asian, and Indigenous patients) is heavily influenced by systemic social determinants, communication quality, and insurance status.
Key Influencing Factors
• Social Determinants of Health (SDOH): Negative social factors—such as unstable housing, low income, or lack of transportation—are directly linked to delayed medical care and lower quality treatment outcomes across racial groups.
• Insurance Coverage: Public insurance types like Medicaid strongly correlate with higher risks of inpatient safety events compared to private or commercial insurance.
• Provider Communication & Bias: Effective patient-provider communication reduces perceived discrimination and improves care satisfaction. Conversely, implicit bias or poor communication can lead to unaddressed symptoms or diagnostic errors.
• Institutional Location: The type of healthcare facility (such as under-resourced safety-net hospitals versus well-funded academic medical centers) introduces variability in safety protocols and complication rates.
• Demographic-Specific Disparities: For example, studies show that Black pregnant individuals face disproportionately higher rates of severe pregnancy-related complications compared to White individuals.
