## 📊 Factors Influencing Access to Credit
For non-Latino (specifically non-Hispanic white and Asian) populations, access to mainstream credit and financial services is primarily driven by socioeconomic foundations, geographic availability, and traditional scoring metrics.
## 💡 Core Financial Drivers

* Income Stability: Reliable, documented employment history directly supports debt-to-income (DTI) requirements. [1, 2] 
* Credit History Depth: Higher baseline rates of prime credit utilization establish robust, long-term credit scores early in adulthood. [2, 3] 
* Asset Ownership: Real estate and accumulated generational wealth provide collateral and financial safety nets. [1, 4] 
* Credit Visibility: Lower rates of being "credit invisible" (having no credit bureau history) allow for automated loan approvals. [5] 

## 🔎 Institutional & Geographic Factors

* Branch Proximity: Higher geographic density of traditional bank branches in majority-non-Latino neighborhoods facilitates easier physical access. [1] 
* Institutional Trust: Higher baseline trust in traditional banking systems encourages early engagement with mainstream financial products. [1, 5] 
* Scoring Alignment: Standardized credit-scoring models historically reflect wealth patterns more favorable to these demographic groups. [2, 5]
