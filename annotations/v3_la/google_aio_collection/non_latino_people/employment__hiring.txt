Hiring outcomes for non-Latino workers are shaped by standard labor market variables, educational attainment, and broader demographic trends.
## Key Factors Influencing Employment and Hiring

* Educational Attainment: Higher levels of formal education, such as a bachelor’s degree or professional certification, strongly correlate with better access to high-wage professional and corporate roles.
* Occupational Segregation: Labor markets are often divided by industry sectors. Non-Latino workers—depending on whether they belong to White, Black, Asian, or other demographic groups—frequently experience distinct concentration patterns across fields like tech, healthcare, management, or manual labor.
* Managerial Networks and Bias: Research indicates that the demographic profile, race, and gender of hiring managers can statistically influence the composition of new hires through referral networks or unconscious preferences.
* Criminal Background Checks: Strict corporate screening policies regarding criminal history can disproportionately impact various applicant pools depending on demographic disparities in the justice system.
* Corporate DEI Policies: The presence of formal [Diversity, Equity, and Inclusion (DEI)](https://www.pewresearch.org/social-trends/2023/05/17/diversity-equity-and-inclusion-in-the-workplace/) structures—such as standardized hiring criteria, blind resume reviews, and structured interviewing—can modify how organizations evaluate all candidates.
