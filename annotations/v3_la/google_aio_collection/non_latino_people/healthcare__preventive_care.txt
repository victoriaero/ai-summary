For non-Latino populations (such as Non-Hispanic White, Non-Hispanic Black, Asian, American Indian/Alaska Native, and Middle Eastern or North African adults), preventive healthcare utilization is shaped by a combination of structural, socioeconomic, behavioral, and individual factors documented in public health research. [1] 
## Core Factors Influencing Preventive Care in Non-Latino Groups

* Usual Source of Care (USOC): Having a regular primary care provider or a designated "medical home" is one of the single strongest predictors of receiving recommended annual checkups, blood pressure screenings, cholesterol checks, and cancer screenings across all racial and ethnic groups. [2, 3] 
* Health Insurance Coverage & Out-of-Pocket Costs: Access to private or public insurance significantly drives preventive service use. However, studies note that even with insurance, unexpected billing or charges applied to supposedly "free" preventive care can deter future visits. [2, 4] 
* Socioeconomic Status (Income & Education): Higher household income and educational attainment consistently correlate with higher rates of preventive service adherence (such as mammograms, colonoscopies, and routine immunizations). [5, 6] 
* Individual Health Behaviors & Psychological Factors: For Non-Hispanic White adults, non-adherence or delayed preventive care is frequently linked in studies to personal factors such as procrastination, depression, or fear of diagnosis rather than systemic language or documentation barriers. [7, 8] 
* Intersectional Racial/Ethnic Disparities (Sub-group Variations): Large-scale national surveys (such as data published in the [Springer Nature Link study on Preventive Care Adherence](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s40615-026-03159-9)) show that while Non-Hispanic White adults often serve as the comparative baseline with higher overall adherence to certain routine guidelines, specific non-Latino groups face distinct gaps:
* American Indian/Alaska Native (AIAN) adults face lower adherence to flu and shingles vaccinations (buffered in some cases by Indian Health Services access).
   * Non-Hispanic Black adults have high or comparable rates for certain screenings like blood pressure or Pap smears, but experience lower adherence or later diagnoses in other areas like shingles vaccination or specific cancer screenings.
   * Middle Eastern and North African (MENA) and Multiracial adults demonstrate significantly lower overall adherence to multi-item preventive care guidelines compared to White counterparts. [1, 3, 9, 10, 11] 
* Provider Dynamics and Sex/Race Concordance: Patient-provider communication, trust, and whether a clinician matches the patient's language or background influence comfort and follow-through on sensitive or invasive screenings (such as cervical or colorectal exams). [1]
