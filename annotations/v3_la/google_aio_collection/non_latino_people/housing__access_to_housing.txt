Access to housing for non-Latino groups—such as White and Black households—is heavily influenced by historical wealth gaps, income disparities, and mortgage market discrimination. [1, 2] 
## Wealth and Inheritance Gaps

* Family Wealth: White families often hold significantly more median wealth than Black or other minority families, making it easier to buy homes. [1, 2] 
* Inheritance: Financial gifts and inheritance help many White buyers purchase homes earlier or with larger down payments compared to other groups. [1, 3] 
* Income Levels: Lower median incomes for some minority groups limit the size of the mortgage they can qualify for. [2, 4] 

## Lending and Credit Barriers

* Mortgage Approval: Black and lower-income applicants can face higher loan denial rates and stricter credit requirements.
* Interest Rates: Some buyers receive higher interest rates based on credit scores and lending practices.
* Appraisal Bias: Homes in neighborhoods with higher populations of color are sometimes undervalued by appraisers, lowering property wealth returns. [1, 2, 5, 6] 

## History and Neighborhood Factors

* Past Policies: Past government actions like redlining shaped segregated neighborhoods and restricted property ownership.
* Zoning Rules: Exclusionary zoning laws limit the supply of affordable housing in many high-opportunity areas.
* Home Values: Neighborhood appreciation rates differ, which affects how much long-term wealth a homeowner can build. [1, 2]
