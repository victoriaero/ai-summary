A combination of gender-based behavioral norms, insurance coverage, and socioeconomic status strongly influences preventive healthcare utilization among heterosexual people. [1, 2, 3] 
## Key Influencing Factors

* Gender and Health-Consciousness: Women generally exhibit higher rates of preventive care engagement (such as routine blood pressure and cholesterol checks, Pap tests, and mammograms) compared to men. Men face internal barriers including traditional masculine norms, stoicism, and embarrassment, which lead to higher rates of delaying routine physicals and cancer screenings. [1, 2, 4, 5] 
* Insurance and Financial Access: Having private or public health insurance significantly increases the likelihood of receiving regular wellness counseling, routine vaccinations, and chronic disease screenings. Uninsured or underinsured heterosexual individuals face severe delays in care. [3, 6] 
* Socioeconomic Status: Higher education, stable income, and urban residence improve the frequency of routine care-seeking. Conversely, poverty, homelessness, and past histories of incarceration drastically reduce access to a regular source of healthcare. [3, 7, 8] 
* Systemic and Structural Barriers: Appointment availability, long wait times, and clinic environments geared heavily toward specific demographics can discourage routine visits, particularly for men navigating male-specific or general practice. [1]
