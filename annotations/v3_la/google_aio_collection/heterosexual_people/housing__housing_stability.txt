Income, employment status, and housing affordability are the primary factors that influence housing stability for heterosexual individuals and households. [1, 2, 3] 
## Economic Factors

* Household Income: Higher and steady earnings allow individuals to consistently pay rent or mortgages without facing severe cost burdens. [1, 2] 
* Employment Stability: Consistent job security prevents sudden drops in income that often lead to eviction or foreclosure. [1] 
* Housing Costs: Spending more than 30% (cost-burdened) or 50% (severely cost-burdened) of income on housing leaves little room for emergencies and increases instability. [1, 2] 

## Social and Personal Circumstances

* Marital and Relationship Status: Being married or living in a committed, shared-income relationship is strongly associated with higher rates of homeownership and lower rates of housing precarity. [4, 5] 
* Substance Use and Health: Chronic physical health issues, mental health challenges, or untreated substance use can disrupt employment and financial management. [6, 7] 
* Criminal Justice History: Recent incarceration creates severe systemic barriers, including landlord discrimination and disqualification from public housing assistance. [1, 4]
