## 📊 Factors Influencing Banking Access
Access to banking and credit services for individuals (including heterosexual people) is generally driven by socioeconomic, institutional, and behavioral factors rather than sexual orientation itself, as standard financial systems are structured around economic profiles. [1, 2] 

* ## 💵 Income and Wealth Stability
* Cash flow: Regular employment and verifiable income dictate loan approvals and account minimums.
   * Savings baseline: Higher liquid assets reduce the likelihood of being unbanked or underbanked. [1, 3] 
* ## 📈 Credit History and Scoring
* File depth: Having a track record with credit bureaus is necessary for prime lending rates.
   * Derogatory marks: Past defaults or bankruptcies restrict mainstream credit access, pushing consumers toward alternative financial services. [3, 4] 
* ## 🪪 Documentation and Identity Verification
* KYC compliance: Strict anti-money laundering rules require valid government IDs and proof of address.
   * Immigration status: Lack of domestic identification can prevent non-citizens from opening traditional accounts. [5, 6] 
* ## 📍 Geographic and Digital Infrastructure
* Branch proximity: Rural or low-income areas often experience physical bank deserts.
   * Digital connectivity: Reliable internet access and smartphone availability are required for modern mobile banking. [5, 7, 8] 
* ## 🗒 Financial Literacy and Trust
* Education: Understanding fee structures, interest rates, and compounding debt prevents financial exclusion.
   * Institutional trust: Past negative experiences or generational distrust in banks steer people toward cash or alternative services. [1, 9, 10]
