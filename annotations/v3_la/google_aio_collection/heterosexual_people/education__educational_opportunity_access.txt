## 1. Socioeconomic Status and Family Background

* Parental Education and Wealth: For heterosexual individuals, family origin strongly correlates with educational achievement. Lower maternal and paternal class levels or lack of parental degrees directly reduce the likelihood of higher education attainment. [4, 5, 6] 
* Resource Allocation: Wealthier families can afford private schooling, tutoring, and stable housing, whereas low-income individuals face underfunded schools dependent on local property taxes. [4, 7] 

## 2. Traditional Gender Norms and Disparities

* Heterosexual Men: This group frequently experiences lower overall educational attainment compared to both heterosexual women and male sexual minorities. These include classroom gender biases that track them away from STEM fields, and global cultural or family norms that historically prioritize educational investments in sons over daughters. ## 3. Institutional and Systemic Factors

* Macro-Opportunity Structures: Access is tied to the geographic distribution of institutions. Regional economic disparities, such as the divide between rural and urban school funding, heavily dictate whether a student can access top-tier universities. [4] 
* Curriculum and Teacher Quality: Systemic equity rests on institutional quality. Studies show that when students have access to rigorous course-taking tracks and highly skilled teachers, initial demographic or racial performance gaps narrow significantly. [12]
