Poverty, a lack of affordable housing, and sudden income loss are the primary factors that influence homelessness for heterosexual people. [1, 2, 3] 
General structural and personal factors drive housing instability across the broader population (which largely reflects heterosexual individuals, as they comprise the majority demographic in most general housing studies):
## Economic Hardships

* Severe Rent Burden: Spending more than half of a household income on rent leaves little to no money for emergencies, food, or medical care. [1, 2, 4] 
* Job Loss or Reduced Wages: Losing a primary source of income or working low-wage jobs that fail to keep pace with rising housing costs makes paying rent impossible. [2, 3, 5] 
* Generational Poverty: A lack of accumulated family wealth or savings reduces a person's safety net during financial crises. [5] 

## Housing Market Deficits

* Affordable Housing Shortage: A community-wide lack of low-cost housing units acts as the root driver of homelessness, functioning like a game of musical chairs where low-income earners lose out.
* Evictions: Landlord actions, rising lease rates, and formal evictions abruptly push stably housed individuals into homelessness. [2, 5, 6] 

## Health and Personal Crises

* Mental Illness: Untreated or severe mental health conditions can disrupt a person's ability to maintain employment and manage daily living tasks. [1, 2] 
* Substance Use Disorders: Active addiction can drain household finances and strain personal support networks, though it often deepens rather than solely initiates housing loss. [1, 2, 6] 
* Physical Health Crises: High medical debt or a disabling medical condition can lead to sudden job and income loss. [2] 

## Domestic and Family Instability

* Domestic Violence: Fleeing an abusive partner or unsafe home environment forces many individuals—particularly women and children—into sudden homelessness when alternative safe options or savings are unavailable. [1, 3] 
* Breakdown of Support Networks: The absence of family or community support systems to lean on during a crisis leaves individuals without a temporary place to stay (such as couch surfing) before ending up on the street. [1, 2]
