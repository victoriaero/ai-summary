The core factors that influence incarceration for heterosexual individuals in the criminal justice system are socioeconomic status, substance use disorders, prior criminal history, and demographic variables like race and gender. [1, 2] 
## Key Factors Influencing Incarceration

* Socioeconomic Disadvantage: Poverty, homelessness, lack of stable employment, and lower educational attainment heavily correlate with higher rates of arrest and incarceration. [2] 
* Substance Use and Mental Health: Unaddressed drug use, particularly of stimulants or opioids, and untreated mental health conditions significantly increase the likelihood of criminal legal involvement and subsequent confinement. [2] 
* Prior Record: A history of prior arrests or incarcerations is one of the strongest independent predictors for future or continued incarceration. [2] 
* Racial and Gender Disparities: Statistically, men and racial minorities (such as Black and Latinx individuals) experience disproportionately higher rates of arrest, harsher sentencing guidelines, and longer terms of incarceration compared to white or female defendants. [1, 2] 
* Social Support Networks: The presence or absence of stable social networks—such as family stability and access to financial or community resources—acts as a critical protective or risk factor. [2, 3]
