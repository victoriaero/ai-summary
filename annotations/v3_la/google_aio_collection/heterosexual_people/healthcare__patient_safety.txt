General patient safety factors influence heterosexual people in healthcare through universal systemic, communication, and social elements, though they do not face minority stress or identity-based discrimination.
## Universal Healthcare and Systemic Factors

* Communication Quality: Clear, empathetic and respectful interactions between providers and patients significantly reduce medical errors and improve safety outcomes. [1, 2] 
* Provider Competence and Training: Staff adherence to safety protocols, adequate medical knowledge, and proper technical execution prevent adverse events. [3] 
* Organizational Environment: Well-staffed facilities, efficient appointment scheduling, and safe hospital infrastructure minimize delays and systemic oversights. [4] 

## Social and Individual Determinants

* Socioeconomic Status (SES): Income, employment stability, and education levels directly impact access to quality care, health literacy, and the ability to navigate complex medical systems. [1, 4, 5] 
* Insurance Coverage: Adequate private or public insurance ensures timely access to preventive care and specialist treatments without coverage-related delays. [5, 6] 
* Biological and Sex Biases: General diagnostic safety can still be impacted by historical sex biases in medical research, device design, or the misattribution of physical symptoms to psychological causes. [7]
