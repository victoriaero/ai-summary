## Key Influencing Factors

* Income and Poverty Level: Most means-tested assistance programs—such as the Supplemental Nutrition Assistance Program (SNAP) and [Medicaid](https://www.census.gov/library/stories/2022/05/who-is-receiving-social-safety-net-benefits.html)—require an individual's or household's income to fall below a specific percentage of the federal poverty level. [2] 
* Family and Partnership Structure: Household size, marriage status, and whether dependent children are present heavily affect eligibility thresholds and benefit amounts, such as the Child Tax Credit or Temporary Assistance for Needy Families (TANF). [1, 2, 3, 4] 
* Employment Status: Past-year unemployment or work-hour history determines access to social insurance programs like unemployment compensation, while work requirements can condition ongoing eligibility for basic needs assistance. [1, 2, 5] 
* Demographic Factors: Intersecting traits like age, disability status, race, and gender norms shape eligibility for specific categorical safety net streams, such as Supplemental Security Income (SSI) for disabled or older adults. [1, 2, 3, 6, 7]
