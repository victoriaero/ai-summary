For heterosexual individuals in employment, workplace harassment is driven primarily by organizational culture, power dynamics, and demographic imbalances rather than discrimination based on sexual orientation. While sexual minorities face distinct risks tied to heterosexism, heterosexual employees are highly influenced by factors documented by agencies like the [U.S. Equal Employment Opportunity Commission (EEOC)](https://www.eeoc.gov): [1, 2, 3, 4, 5] 
## 1. Organizational & Environmental Factors

* Power Disparities: Environments with highly stark organizational hierarchies or where unchecked authority is granted to specific managers increase the risk of harassment, driven by the victim's fear of retaliation or job loss. [1, 2] 
* Workplace Culture & Tolerance: A lack of clear, enforced professional boundaries, an absence of accessible grievance procedures, or organizational tolerance of "bad behavior" heavily predicts hostile environments. [6, 7] 
* Isolated or Remote Environments: Shifts or physical workspaces operating away from leadership, such as night shifts or decentralized offices, have higher rates of target victimization due to lack of direct oversight. [5, 8] 
* Alcohol Consumption: Workplaces that tolerate or integrate alcohol consumption into professional networking, client dinners, or company events frequently report elevated harassment claims due to lowered inhibitions. [5, 9] 

## 2. Gender & Demographic Dynamics

* Skewed Gender Ratios: Heterosexual individuals suffer higher rates of sexual and gender-based harassment when they form a minority within a specific occupation.
* Heterosexual women face highly elevated risks in male-dominated fields (e.g., construction, engineering) as a means of enforcing social exclusion or gender roles.
   * Heterosexual men face higher risks of sexual harassment when working in heavily female-dominated sectors. [10, 11] 
* Age and Experience: Younger or entry-level employees, regardless of orientation, are disproportionately targeted due to perceived naivety, economic vulnerability, or unfamiliarity with corporate reporting systems. [1, 12] 

## 3. Industry-Specific Demands

* Client-Facing Roles: In service-oriented positions where financial compensation or employment tenure depends strictly on customer satisfaction (the "customer is always right" model), heterosexual workers are frequently pressured to tolerate inappropriate behavior from clients or third parties. [2, 9]
