## 📊 Factors Influencing Banking Access for White Individuals
Socioeconomic status, geographic proximity to mainstream institutions, and established credit history primarily influence banking and credit access for White individuals. [1, 2] 
## 💡 Core Determinants of Financial Access

* Income and Wealth Levels:
* Higher median household incomes and accumulated generational wealth heavily correlate with meeting bank balance requirements.
   * Lower-income White households face similar unbanked or underbanked vulnerabilities as other low-income demographics. [1, 2, 3, 4] 
* Geographic Proximity:
* Concentration of traditional bank branches in majority-White and affluent neighborhoods ensures physical access.
   * Rural or economically depressed White regions can experience local branch closures, leading to localized banking deserts. [1, 4] 
* Credit Scoring and History:
* Access to mainstream products (mortgages, auto loans, credit cards) relies on traditional credit scores.
   * Favorable historical integration into regulated financial markets reduces reliance on alternative financial services (AFS) like payday lenders. [5, 6, 7] 
* Institutional Relationships:
* Pre-existing customer ties with traditional lenders facilitate smoother access to emergency credit or business liquidity (e.g., historical lending biases favoring established networks). [5]
