Several academic, socioeconomic, and institutional factors shape college access and admission for White students in the United States. [1] 
## Academic Performance and Metrics

* High School GPA: Academic metrics, led by high school grade point average, remain the primary baseline factor for admissions across selective universities. [2] 
* Standardized Testing: Standardized exam results (such as the SAT or ACT) historically function as major evaluative criteria, though an increasing number of institutions have adopted test-optional policies. [3, 4] 

## Socioeconomic and Institutional Advantages

* Legacy Preferences: White applicants disproportionately benefit from legacy admissions policies because historical enrollment patterns mean their parents are more likely to have graduated from those specific institutions. [1] 
* Family Wealth and Resources: Higher average household wealth among many White families allows for better access to well-funded K-12 school districts, robust extracurricular activities, sports programs, and dedicated college-prep counseling. [1] 
* Application Networks and Navigation: Studies indicate that White high school seniors frequently rely on established familial and social networks for guidance on applications, and they tend to participate in campus visits at higher rates than several minority peer groups. [5]
