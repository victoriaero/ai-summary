Homelessness among White individuals is primarily driven by general economic pressures, housing market conditions, and personal safety net failures rather than race-specific systemic housing discrimination. [1, 2] 
## Economic and Housing Market Factors

* Affordable Housing Shortage: A severe lack of low-cost housing options and rising rents relative to local wages force many low-income households out of stable housing. [1] 
* Income Loss and Job Instability: Sudden job loss, layoffs, business closures, or medical leaves can rapidly drain personal savings, leading to missed rent or mortgage payments. [1] 
* Poverty and Income Inequality: General economic disadvantage and regional poverty strongly correlate with White homelessness, particularly in rural and suburban areas. [2, 3] 

## Health and Personal Circumstances

* Mental Health and Substance Use: Untreated severe mental illness or substance use disorders can destabilize employment and interpersonal support systems. [1] 
* Medical Debt: High healthcare costs and inadequate insurance coverage can consume a household's limited income, leaving insufficient funds for housing. [1, 4] 
* Domestic Violence: Physical abuse or unsafe home environments frequently force individuals to flee stable housing without an immediate alternative. [1] 

## Geographic and Structural Realities

* Rural and Suburban Concentration: While White Americans experience lower overall per-capita rates of homelessness nationally than many minority groups, a large share of White individuals experiencing homelessness reside in rural or smaller suburban communities where resources and shelters are often underfunded or scarce. [3, 5, 6]
