Criminal history and the severity of the alleged offense are the primary legal factors influencing police arrest and detention decisions for White individuals in the criminal justice system. [1] 
## Legal and Case Factors

* Criminal History: Prior record strongly predicts pretrial detention and arrest outcomes, often serving as a primary baseline used by judges and law enforcement to assess risk or culpability. [1] 
* Offense Severity: The gravity and type of the alleged crime (such as violent offenses versus minor or drug-related infractions) directly dictate the likelihood and degree of formal booking and detention.

## Extralegal and Systemic Factors

* Geographic Location and Neighborhood Context: Local police deployment strategies, crime rates, and neighborhood economic characteristics influence the frequency of police contact and proactive enforcement in specific communities.
* Socioeconomic Status: Access to private legal counsel, stable employment, and financial resources affects outcomes like securing pretrial release or managing conditions related to bail and fees.
* Substance Use and Mental Health: Unaddressed addiction or behavioral health crises frequently increase the probability of repeated law enforcement encounters and subsequent custodial arrests.
