Credit and loan approval decisions for White applicants—like all applicants under U.S. federal law (such as the Equal Credit Opportunity Act)—are based on race-blind financial and credit risk metrics rather than demographic identity. [1, 2] 
## 📊 Core Financial Factors

* Credit Scores (FICO/VantageScore)
* Payment history (on-time payments vs. delinquencies).
   * Credit utilization ratio (revolving debt relative to limits).
   * Length of credit history and mix of credit types. [3] 
* Income & Employment Stability
* Verifiable, stable gross monthly income.
   * Duration of employment in the current field.
* Debt-to-Income Ratio (DTI)
* Comparison of total monthly debt obligations against gross monthly income.
* Collateral & Assets
* Down payment size and property appraisal value (for mortgages).
   * Liquid reserves available after closing or purchase. [4] 

## 📈 Structural & Contextual Influences

* Generational Wealth & Intergenerational Support
* Access to familial financial backing for down payments or co-signing. [5] 
* Historical & Geographic Accumulation
* Higher average baseline rates of historical prime-credit access linked to geographic and socioeconomic environments. [3, 6]
