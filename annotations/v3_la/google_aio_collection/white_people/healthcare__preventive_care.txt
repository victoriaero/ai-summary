Several key socioeconomic, structural, and personal factors influence the use of preventive healthcare services among White adults in the United States.
## Core Influencing Factors

* Socioeconomic Status (SES): Higher levels of income and education strongly correlate with increased utilization of routine checkups, screenings, and vaccinations. Financial stability allows for better affordability of out-of-pocket costs and copays. [1, 2] 
* Health Insurance Coverage: Having private health insurance or supplemental coverage significantly increases the likelihood of receiving regular preventive care, whereas being uninsured leads to a sharp decline in service utilization. [2, 3] 
* Usual Source of Care: Having a regular primary care provider or a medical home is one of the strongest predictors for receiving timely preventive screenings, such as blood pressure checks, cholesterol tests, and cancer screenings. [2, 4] 
* Geographic Location and Access: Rural versus urban residency affects provider availability, travel distance to medical facilities, and the local density of healthcare infrastructure.
* Age and Gender: Older adults and women generally utilize routine preventive care and wellness visits at higher rates compared to young adult men.
* Perceptions and Trust: Higher levels of trust in the healthcare system and positive perceptions of care access strongly encourage adherence to preventive guidelines. [2]
