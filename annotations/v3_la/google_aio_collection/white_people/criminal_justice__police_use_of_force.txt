Police use of force involving White individuals in the criminal justice system is influenced by several situational, demographic, and socioeconomic factors.
## Key Factors Influencing Use of Force

* Subject Resistance: The level of physical resistance or aggression shown by an individual during an encounter is a primary driver for officers escalating force. [1] 
* Socioeconomic Status: Low income (such as earning under $20,000 annually) increases the statistical risk and exposure to police use of force for White individuals. [2] 
* Gender and Age: Being male significantly increases the likelihood of experiencing police use of force. For White individuals, specific age brackets (such as adults aged 35 and older) also show elevated risk profiles in certain demographic studies. [2] 
* Neighborhood Crime Rates: High-crime neighborhoods and areas with elevated rates of violent offenses lead to more frequent police-public contacts, raising the overall likelihood of use-of-force incidents regardless of demographics. [1] 
* Officer Discretion and Department Policy: The presence or absence of strict departmental de-escalation guidelines, standardized tracking, and tactical training heavily influence how individual officers assess threats and apply force. [3, 4, 5]
