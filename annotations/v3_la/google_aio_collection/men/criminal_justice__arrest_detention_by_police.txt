Police decisions to arrest or detain men in the criminal justice system are influenced by legal standards, situational circumstances, and officer discretion. [1, 2] 
## Legal and Constitutional Factors

* Reasonable Suspicion: Law enforcement requires reasonable suspicion of criminal activity to legally conduct a temporary investigative detention or [California POST Learning Domain 15](https://post.ca.gov/portals/0/post_docs/basic_course_resources/workbooks/LD_15-V5.0.pdf) stop. [2, 3] 
* Probable Cause: An official arrest requires probable cause, meaning officers possess sufficient facts or evidence to believe a crime has been or is being committed by the suspect. [2, 4] 
* Offense Severity: The presence of felony charges or major criminal violations strongly increases the legal likelihood of booking and formal arrest compared to minor infractions. [5, 6] 

## Situational and Behavioral Dynamics

* Suspect Demeanor: Behavioral factors—such as perceived suspicious demeanor, active resistance, or disrespect toward officers—significantly elevate the probability of being taken into custody. [6, 7] 
* Nature of the Incident: Self-initiated police encounters, property-related calls, and incidents involving identified victims increase the statistical likelihood of an arrest decision. [1, 8] 

## Officer and Neighborhood Characteristics

* Officer Discretion: Research from the [Bureau of Justice Assistance](https://bja.ojp.gov/library/publications/understanding-police-decisions-arrest-impact-situational-officer-and) shows that incident-level factors account for the most variation in arrest decisions, followed by individual officer traits (such as education level or tenure) and neighborhood context. [1, 8] 
* Demographic Disparities: Studies on booking practices note that extralegal disparities persist across race and demographic lines, independent of basic legal controls. [5, 8]
