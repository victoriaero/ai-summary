Systemic, socioeconomic, and psychological factors significantly influence the rates and experiences of incarceration for men within the criminal justice system. According to analyses by the [Vera Institute of Justice](https://www.vera.org/ending-mass-incarceration/causes-of-mass-incarceration) and [The Sentencing Project](https://www.sentencingproject.org/reports/one-in-five-racial-disparity-in-imprisonment-causes-and-remedies/), policy choices, policing strategies, and socioeconomic stressors shape these outcomes. [1, 2, 3, 4] 
## Socioeconomic and Structural Factors

* Poverty and Employment: Low income, unstable housing, and lack of a high school diploma or GED strongly correlate with higher rates of arrest and imprisonment. [5, 6] 
* Racial Disparities: According to the [Prison Policy Initiative](https://www.prisonpolicy.org/research/racial_and_ethnic_disparities/), Black and Latino men face disproportionately higher rates of police stops, harsher pretrial detention decisions, and longer mandatory minimum sentences compared to white men. [1, 7] 
* Community Disadvantage: Over-policing in low-income neighborhoods and minority communities leads to higher rates of recorded offenses and subsequent incarceration. [1, 4] 

## Legal and Policy Drivers

* Punitive Sentencing Laws: Policies originating from the "War on Drugs" and mandatory minimum sentencing have historically targeted low-level offenses and marginalized populations. [1, 4] 
* Pretrial Detention: Financial barriers like cash bail disadvantage low-income defendants, often forcing unfavorable plea deals that result in incarceration. [1] 

## Psychological and Behavioral Factors

* Trauma and Abuse: Studies on incarcerated young men show high pre-incarceration rates of physical abuse, weapon threats, and witnessing neighborhood violence. [3] 
* Substance Use: Recent drug or stimulant use heavily predicts both initial and repeated contact with the criminal legal system. [8] 
* Masculinity Norms: Adherence to rigid definitions of toughness can constrain emotional vulnerability and discourage healthy help-seeking or coping mechanisms. [3]
