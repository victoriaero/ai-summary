Several key social, economic, and psychological factors influence educational opportunity and access for men and boys, affecting their performance, aspirations, and college completion rates. [1] 
## Key Influencing Factors

* Masculine Identity and Peer Norms: Developing masculine identities that view school compliance or academic engagement as "uncool" or feminine can drive male underperformance and lower educational aspirations. [1] 
* Behavior and Discipline Disparities: Early behavioral challenges and disciplinary issues in K-12 settings significantly impact academic tracking and account for a notable share of the gender gap in college graduation. [1] 
* Lack of Male Educators: A severe underrepresentation of male teachers—particularly minority male educators—reduces relatable role models, which can foster feelings of alienation and disengagement among boys. [2] 
* Socioeconomic Status and Intersectionality: Economic pressures, working-class backgrounds, and the immediate need to earn money often push young men to bypass higher education in favor of entering the workforce early. [3] 
* Family and Cultural Expectations: Pressures to fulfill traditional breadwinner roles quickly can heavily shape choices around whether to invest time in long-term academic pathways or trade skills. [3, 4]
