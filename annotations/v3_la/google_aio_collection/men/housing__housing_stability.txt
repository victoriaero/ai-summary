Housing stability for men is primarily influenced by financial resources, ongoing access to supportive services, and physical or mental health management. [1, 2, 3] 
## Financial and Economic Factors

* Income and Employment: Steady employment and reliable income protect against rent burdens and evictions.
* Housing Affordability: Spending less than 30% of income on rent or housing costs prevents severe cost burdens.
* Rental Assistance: Subsidies and housing vouchers lower the risk of losing a home or facing foreclosure. [2, 3, 4, 5] 

## Health and Wellness

* Mental Healthcare: Managing conditions like depression or anxiety supports long-term residential continuity.
* Substance Use Treatment: Addressing addiction reduces a major risk factor tied to housing loss.
* Physical Disabilities: Chronic health issues require accessible living environments and medical stability. [3, 6, 7, 8, 9] 

## Social and Support Systems

* Supportive Services: Engagement with programs like Assertive Community Treatment (ACT) helps maintain stable housing.
* Social Networks: Having family, friends, or community resources provides a safety net during financial crises.
* Legal and Advocacy Help: Access to legal aid prevents unlawful evictions and resolves landlord disputes. [1, 3, 9, 10]
