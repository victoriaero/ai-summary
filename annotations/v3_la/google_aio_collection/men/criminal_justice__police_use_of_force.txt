Police use of force involving men in the criminal justice system is primarily influenced by legal, situational, behavioral, and demographic factors. [1, 2, 3] 
Under the landmark Supreme Court ruling [Graham v. Connor](https://www.congress.gov/crs-product/LSB11336), law enforcement interactions are evaluated using an objective reasonableness standard based on specific situational triggers. [3, 4] 
## Situational and Legal Factors

* Immediate Threat: Whether a suspect poses an immediate danger to the safety of officers or others is considered the most critical factor governing the level of force applied. [1] 
* Severity of the Crime: The gravity of the suspected offense being investigated or used as the basis for a stop or seizure directly shapes the permissible degree of police action. [1, 3] 
* Resistance or Flight: Active resistance to arrest or attempts to evade custody by fleeing heavily influence the escalation or de-escalation of physical intervention. [1, 3] 

## Behavioral and Health Factors

* Mental Illness and Substance Use: Behavioral health conditions or intoxication often contribute to erratic actions or an inability to comply with verbal commands, raising the likelihood of a forceful encounter. Studies note that individuals with untreated mental health issues face a significantly elevated risk of adverse outcomes during police contact. [2, 5] 
* Suspect Actions: Research from bodies like the [National Institute of Justice](https://nij.ojp.gov) indicates that suspect behavior and active physical resistance are among the strongest direct predictors of whether police will deploy physical force. [6] 

## Demographic and Systemic Disparities

* Gender Disparities: Men—and particularly young men of color—experience statistically higher rates of police use of force and fatal encounters compared to other demographic groups, reflecting broader racial and gendered disparities in policing. [5, 7] 
* Officer Characteristics and Training: Variations in an officer's level of experience, tactical training, and de-escalation protocols play a role in how rapidly situations escalate or whether alternative methods are successfully deployed. [2, 8, 9]
