Multiple socioeconomic, demographic, and legal factors influence health insurance coverage and utilization for men, with young and low-income adult men experiencing the highest uninsured rates. [1, 2] 
## 📅 Age and Life Stage

* Young adults (ages 18–34): Peak uninsured demographic due to transitioning off parental plans or lower workplace coverage uptake. [2, 3] 
* Older men (ages 50–64): Face higher anxiety over coverage affordability, though government programs like VA care become more prevalent. [3, 4] 
* Senior men (65+): Nearly universal coverage driven by Medicare eligibility.

## 💰 Socioeconomic Status and Employment

* Income level: Lower-income men are disproportionately uninsured despite living in households with full-time workers. [2] 
* Employment type: Full-time formal employment strongly correlates with employer-sponsored private insurance access. [5] 
* Education and wealth: Higher educational attainment and wealth categories positively predict insurance enrollment and utilization. [5, 6] 

## ⚖️ Legal and Regulatory Frameworks

* The Affordable Care Act (ACA): Prohibits insurers from altering premiums based on gender or pre-existing medical history. [7, 8] 
* Mandated benefits: ACA rules dictate that plans cover essential preventive services (such as blood pressure, diabetes, and colorectal screenings) with zero copays for insured men. [9] 
* Geographic location: State-level Medicaid expansion decisions heavily dictate coverage availability for low-income, childless adult men. [2] 

## 🔎 Behavioral and Perceptual Factors

* Healthcare literacy: Low understanding of out-of-pocket costs and covered services spikes avoidance and affordability concerns.
* Utilization disparities: Men report higher rates of lacking a primary care physician compared to women, lowering proactive care engagement. [4, 10]
