Access to housing for men experiencing housing insecurity or chronic homelessness is primarily influenced by a combination of structural economic shortages, criminal legal system involvement, and unaddressed health challenges. [1, 2, 3] 
## Structural and Economic Factors

* Affordable housing shortages: A severe lack of low-cost housing and stagnant wages make it difficult for low-income men to secure or maintain stable independent living. [3, 4] 
* Poverty and employment barriers: Job insecurity, lack of savings buffers, and low-wage work limit financial stability. [3, 4] 
* Criminal legal involvement: A history of incarceration creates steep systemic hurdles to passing tenant background checks and securing formal leases. [3, 5] 

## Health and Personal Circumstances

* Substance use and mental health: Comorbid psychiatric disorders and untreated addiction frequently compound housing instability among chronically unhoused men and can act as barriers within traditional shelter environments. [1, 2, 6] 
* Social isolation: A lack of robust informal family or community support networks often leaves men with fewer safety nets during personal or economic crises. [1] 
* Chronic physical conditions: Aging, disabilities, and limited access to ongoing healthcare exacerbate the risk of prolonged homelessness. [1, 4]
