Domestic violence is the leading cause of homelessness for women, heavily driven by economic inequality and a shortage of affordable housing. [1, 2] 
## Key Factors Influencing Women's Homelessness

* Domestic and Interpersonal Violence: Fleeing an abusive partner forces many women and their children out of safe shelter, and financial abuse often destroys a survivor's credit and independence. [3, 4] 
* Gender Wage Gaps and Poverty: Persistent pay gaps mean women compound wealth slower, face higher rent cost burdens, and are more likely to be single mothers supporting families on lower incomes. [1, 5, 6] 
* Affordable Housing Shortages: Rising rent costs outpace stagnant wages, leaving low-income women vulnerable to eviction when housing stock is limited. [7] 
* Childcare and Employment Barriers: A lack of affordable, consistent childcare restricts women from maintaining stable employment or completing job training programs. [7] 
* Hidden Homelessness: Women are more likely to experience "hidden homelessness"—couch surfing or staying in unsafe living situations—to avoid the extreme physical dangers of sleeping on the streets. [8, 9] 
* Systemic and Intersectional Disparities: BIPOC and LGBTQIA+ women face amplified risks due to compounding impacts of racism, discrimination, and historical wealth gaps. [5]
