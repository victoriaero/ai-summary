Multiple structural, economic, and behavioral factors influence women's access to credit and financial services, often leading to systemic disparities in loan approvals and borrowing amounts. [1, 2, 3] 
## ⚖️ Structural and Legal Barriers

* Property rights: Unequal or restricted ownership of land and immovable assets limits formal collateral. [4, 5] 
* Legal capacity: Legal restrictions in certain jurisdictions impede independent contracting or opening formal bank accounts. [6] 
* Identification hurdles: Stringent or unequal documentation standards make obtaining a national ID or passport harder for women. [7] 

## 📉 Economic and Asset Disparities

* Collateral focus: Traditional underwriting relies heavily on physical assets, penalizing cash-flow-rich but asset-poor women-led businesses. [4, 5] 
* Wealth gaps: Lower overall net worth and smaller business sizes reduce the maximum capital traditional lenders are willing to extend. [8] 
* Sector concentration: Overrepresentation in lower-growth service and retail sectors restricts access to high-tier business financing. [8] 

## 🔎 Institutional Bias and Scoring

* Lender bias: Unconscious or explicit screening assumptions frequently demand extra guarantors or male board members. [2, 9] 
* Limited credit history: Standard credit-scoring models exclude microloans, mobile money, or utility payment histories. [1, 7] 

## 💡 Literacy and Social Norms

* Digital literacy: Lower access to and training in digital financial tools restricts alternative borrowing avenues. [5, 10] 
* Social restrictions: Restrictive cultural norms limit physical mobility and direct interaction with formal financial institutions. [7, 9]
