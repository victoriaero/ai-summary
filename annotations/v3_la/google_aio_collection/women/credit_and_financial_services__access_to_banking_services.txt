Multiple structural, economic, legal, and social factors shape women's access to banking, credit, and financial services globally. [1, 2, 3] 
## ⚖️ Legal and Property Rights

* Collateral deficits: Unequal land and property ownership rights restrict access to formal. [1, 4] 
* Asset bias: Traditional lending heavily favors immovable assets like real estate. [1] 
* Legal hurdles: Married women in certain jurisdictions face legal travel or ID restrictions. [5] 

## 📉 Economic and Income Disparities

* Lower earnings: Persistent gender wage gaps limit regular savings and account usage. [3] 
* Informal work: Concentration in informal sectors prevents credit history creation. [5, 6] 
* Smaller loans: Micro-ticket financing needs are frequently ignored by commercial banks. [4] 

## 🧠 Literacy and Digital Gaps

* Financial knowledge: Lower familiarity with complex bank products and formal application processes. [1, 7] 
* Digital exclusion: High costs for mobile handsets and connectivity restrain digital banking. [8, 9] 
* Technological capability: Lower confidence in utilizing digital financial platforms independently. [8] 

## 🏛️ Institutional and Regulatory Barriers

* Rigid KYC rules: Strict identification and anti-money laundering mandates complicate onboarding.
* Product design flaws: Traditional services ignore time poverty and caregiving responsibilities.
* Leadership gaps: Underrepresentation of women in financial C-suites reduces gender-centric product innovation. [4, 9, 10, 11, 12]
