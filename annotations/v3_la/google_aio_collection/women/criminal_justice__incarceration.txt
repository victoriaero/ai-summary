Multiple interconnected socioeconomic, psychological, and systemic factors influence the incarceration of women in the criminal justice system. [1, 2] 
According to reports from organizations like the [Prison Policy Initiative](https://www.prisonpolicy.org/reports/pie2024women.html) and the [Sentencing Project](https://www.sentencingproject.org/fact-sheet/incarcerated-women-and-girls/), female incarceration rates have grown rapidly over recent decades, driven by distinct pathways into the justice system. [3, 4] 
## Key Factors Influencing Female Incarceration

* Poverty and Economic Instability: Many women enter the justice system due to low-level, nonviolent offenses tied to survival, such as property crimes or drug offenses, compounded by the gender wage gap and homelessness. Inability to afford high money bail keeps a large share of unconvicted women locked in local jails while awaiting trial. [2, 3, 4, 5] 
* Trauma and Abuse: Over 80% to 90% of justice-involved women have extensive histories of childhood trauma, physical abuse, or intimate partner violence. Some arrests involving domestic disputes stem from complex dynamics where women defending themselves are mistakenly identified as primary aggressors under mandatory arrest policies. [6, 7, 8] 
* Substance Use and Mental Health: High rates of co-occurring disorders—including post-traumatic stress disorder (PTSD), depression, and severe substance addiction—frequently precede incarceration, often acting as unaddressed coping mechanisms rather than violent intent. [2, 5, 9] 
* Structural Racism: Black, Native American, and Latinx women face disproportionately higher rates of arrest and imprisonment compared to white women, driven by segregated poverty, biased policing practices, and harsher drug sentencing policies. [3, 10]
