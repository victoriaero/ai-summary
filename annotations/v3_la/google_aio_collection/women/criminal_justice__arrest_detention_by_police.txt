Arrest and detention rates for women in the criminal justice system are primarily influenced by mandatory domestic violence laws, racial disparities, punitive drug policies, and socioeconomic poverty. [1, 2, 3] 
## Mandatory Arrest Laws and Domestic Violence

* Self-defense criminalization: Strict domestic violence rules often require police to make an arrest when responding to a call.
* Dual arrests: Women defending themselves from abusers are frequently arrested alongside their attackers.
* Victim status blurred: Police sometimes fail to recognize who initiated the violence, turning victims into defendants. [1] 

## Racial and Demographic Disparities

* Higher stop rates: According to the [Prison Policy Initiative](https://www.prisonpolicy.org/blog/2019/05/14/policingwomen/), Black women face traffic stops 17% more often than white women.
* Unequal arrest rates: Black women are arrested during traffic stops at triple the rate of white women (4.4% compared to 1.5%).
* Surveilled neighborhoods: Police patrol minority neighborhoods more heavily, which raises the chance of arrest for local women. [2, 4, 5] 

## Substance Use and Drug Enforcement

* War on drugs impact: Aggressive enforcement of low-level drug crimes drives a fast-growing share of female arrests.
* Trauma connection: Many women use drugs to cope with past abuse or trauma.
* Addiction penalties: Mandatory minimum sentences for drug possession pull women into the detention system instead of offering medical help. [1, 6] 

## Poverty and Survival Crimes

* Economic pressure: Extreme poverty forces some women to commit nonviolent property crimes, engage in sex work, or sell drugs.
* Police contact: Visible survival strategies in public spaces increase encounters with law enforcement. [3] 

## Officer Discretion and Situational Factors

* Credibility judgments: Officers may judge a woman's behavior or mental health state as noncompliant, making arrest more likely.
* Officer demographics: The presence of supervisors or specific peer dynamics during a patrol can change how an officer exercises discretion. [7, 8]
