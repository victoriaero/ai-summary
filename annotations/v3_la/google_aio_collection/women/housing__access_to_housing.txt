Multiple economic, social, and policy factors significantly influence housing access and stability for women. [1] 
## Economic Factors

* Gender Wage Gap: Women earn less on average than men, reducing their overall purchasing power and housing choices.
* Higher Rent Burden: Women spend a higher percentage of their income on rent and face higher rates of eviction.
* Credit and Financing Barriers: Women experience more difficulty accessing mortgages, face higher interest rates, and encounter systemic lending biases ("pinklining"). [1, 2, 3, 4, 5] 

## Social and Personal Factors

* Domestic and Family Violence: Intimate partner violence forces many women to flee their homes, serving as a primary driver of female homelessness and housing insecurity. [1, 6, 7] 
* Single Parenthood: Single mothers face disproportionate poverty rates, making affordable housing and childcare harder to balance. [1, 3] 
* Hidden Homelessness: Women are more likely to experience "hidden homelessness"—staying in unsafe, temporary, or overcrowded conditions with acquaintances to avoid sleeping on the streets. [8] 

## Legal and Policy Factors

* Discriminatory Practices: Landlord discrimination, sexual harassment, and biased zoning laws limit safe and affordable housing options.
* Property Rights: Unequal inheritance laws and property rights in various regions tie a woman's security of tenure to her marital or familial status. [9, 10]
