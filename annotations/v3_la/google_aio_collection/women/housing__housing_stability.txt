Housing stability for women is driven by a combination of economic resources, safety considerations, and systemic factors. [1, 2] 
## 📊 Economic Security and Affordability

* Income Disparities: Wage gaps reduce women's purchasing power and ability to afford rising rents.
* Cost Burden: Female-headed households face disproportionately higher rates of rent burden.
* Rental Arrears: Accumulated debt severely increases the likelihood of displacement. [2, 3] 

## ⚠️ Gender-Based Violence and Safety

* Intimate Partner Violence (IPV): Fear of abuse or lack of safe options traps women in unstable or dangerous environments.
* Post-Separation Risk: Leaving an abuser often triggers sudden material and psychological housing instability.
* Shelter Constraints: Temporary shelters provide vital safety but can lack long-term privacy and space. [3, 4] 

## 🔎 Systemic and Intersectional Barriers

* Eviction Disparities: Women—particularly Black and Latinx women—experience higher rates of eviction filings.
* Discriminatory Policies: Identification mandates and systemic exclusion restrict access to public assistance.
* Property Rights: Unequal legal, credit, and inheritance structures limit independent tenure security. [2, 5, 6] 

## 💡 Psychological and Social Support

* Ontological Security: Stability relies on emotional comfort, neighborhood cohesion, and a true sense of "home".
* Social Capital: Access to trusted community networks and family support mitigates the risk of homelessness.
* Integrated Services: Case management and behavioral health access help sustain long-term tenancy. [1, 7, 8]
