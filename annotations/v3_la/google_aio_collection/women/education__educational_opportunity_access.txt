Multiple economic, social, and structural factors influence educational opportunity and access for women globally.
## Economic Factors

* Poverty and Costs: Families with limited financial resources often prioritize the education of boys over girls when they cannot afford school fees, supplies, or uniforms.
* Opportunity Costs: Girls are frequently expected to contribute to household income or take on heavy domestic work, reducing the time available for schooling. [1, 2, 3, 4] 

## Social and Cultural Norms

* Harmful Gender Stereotypes: Societal expectations often steer girls away from math, science, and technology (STEM) toward humanities or domestic roles, which can limit lucrative career paths. [3, 5] 
* Early Marriage and Pregnancy: Child marriage and a lack of supportive re-entry policies for adolescent mothers force many young women out of school permanently. [6, 7] 
* Discriminatory Legal and Power Structures: Unequal legal rights in areas like inheritance, work permissions, and domestic violence protections reinforce systemic disadvantages. [8] 

## Structural and Health Barriers

* Safety and Conflict: Violence, long travel distances to schools, and conflict-affected environments dramatically increase dropout rates and risks for female students.
* Infrastructure Deficits: A lack of private sanitation facilities and inadequate access to menstrual hygiene management products (period poverty) cause girls to miss significant instructional time.
* The Digital Divide: Disparities in access to digital devices, internet connectivity, and technical training hinder participation in modern educational settings. [1, 2, 9]
