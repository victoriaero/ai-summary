Pain dismissal, systemic clinical biases, and communication breakdowns are key factors that influence patient safety for women in healthcare. [1, 2] 
## Pain Management and Dismissal

* Symptom underestimation: Women experience pain more severely and frequently than men, yet their reports of pain are routinely dismissed or minimized by providers. [2] 
* Delayed diagnoses: Conditions with severe pain profiles, such as endometriosis or high-pain gynecological procedures, face severe diagnostic delays and unmanaged discomfort. [2] 
* Racial disparities: Intersectional racial biases leave minority women—particularly Black women—at an even higher risk of having critical symptoms and pain ignored. [2] 

## Clinical Bias and Systemic Inequality

* Provider assumptions: Historical medical research has male-centric baselines, leading to a lack of recognition for how conditions manifest uniquely or differently in female physiology.
* Minoritized groups: Clinician bias heavily impacts minoritized women, worsening preventable adverse events and lowering the quality of maternal and general care. [3] 
* Access barriers: Elective and specialized care backlogs (such as in gynaecology) disproportionately affect women in deprived or lower-resource regions. [2] 

## Human Factors and Obstetric Safety

* Communication breakdowns: High-stress clinical settings like labor and delivery suffer from noisy environments, fatigue during long shifts, and delayed recognition of clinical abnormalities. [1] 
* Team collaboration: Safe obstetric and gynecological outcomes rely heavily on open team communication, situational awareness, and avoiding unnecessary medical interventions. [1, 4]
