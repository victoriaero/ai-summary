Power imbalances, male-dominated hierarchies, and permissive workplace cultures are the primary factors that influence workplace harassment for women in employment. [1] 
## Key Risk Factors
According to the [U.S. Equal Employment Opportunity Commission (EEOC)](https://www.eeoc.gov/chart-risk-factors-harassment-and-responsive-strategies) and studies on gender dynamics, several organizational and cultural conditions heighten the risk of harassment: [1, 2] 

* Power Disparities: Harassment thrives where supervisors or executives hold significant control over an employee's wages, scheduling, assignments, or promotions, making victims fearful of retaliation. [3, 4, 5] 
* Permissive or Toxic Cultures: Environments that fail to punish misconduct, dismiss complaints, or foster leadership accountability create an atmosphere where harassers feel emboldened. [1, 3] 
* Customer-Facing and Tip-Dependent Roles: Jobs in hospitality, retail, or service where pay is tied to customer satisfaction often pressure women to tolerate inappropriate behavior from clients. [4, 6] 
* Isolated Work Environments: Remote locations, late-night shifts, hotel housekeeping, or solitary workspaces reduce the presence of witnesses. [2, 6] 
* Alcohol Consumption: Work-related social gatherings or party atmospheres where alcohol flows freely consistently correlate with increased harassment incidents. [2, 4, 6] 
* Homogenous or Extreme Demographics: Workplaces dominated by a single demographic can reinforce unchaperoned stereotypes, while rapidly changing or young workforces may lack proper onboarding regarding acceptable boundaries. [2, 4, 6]
