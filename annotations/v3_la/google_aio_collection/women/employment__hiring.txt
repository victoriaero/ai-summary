Several key psychological, cultural, and structural factors influence the hiring and recruitment process for women in the workforce. [1, 2, 3] 
## 1. Job Description Language and Requirements

* Masculine-Coded Words: Terms like "aggressive," "assertive," or "ninja" in job posts can turn away female applicants.
* Strict Qualification Matching: Women often feel they must meet 100% of listed requirements before applying, whereas vague or inflated criteria increase self-doubt.
* Precise Thresholds: Clear, objective qualifications reduce ambiguity and encourage more women to apply. [2, 4, 5, 6] 

## 2. Unconscious Bias and Paternalism

* Paternalistic Discrimination: Employers sometimes assume certain jobs are too dangerous or unsuitable for female candidates (e.g., night shifts) unless safety measures are explicitly guaranteed. [1] 
* Behavioral Double Standards: Capable women in male-dominated roles may face penalties if they use strong self-promotion or powerful language, violating traditional gender expectations. [7] 
* Appearance Biases: Studies note that subjective screening can introduce biases based on physical appearance or assumptions about "culture fit". [7, 8] 

## 3. Workplace Flexibility and Benefits

* Remote and Hybrid Work: Flexibility is a major priority for retention and attraction, heavily weighted by female candidates balancing household or family responsibilities. [3, 9] 
* Pay Transparency: Publishing clear salary ranges builds trust and significantly increases application rates from women. [4, 9] 
* Childcare Access: The availability of family planning, parental leave, and childcare directly influences whether women enter or stay in the formal labor market. [3] 

## 4. Recruitment Practices and Culture

* Structured Interviews: Standardized questions and pre-defined skill ratings help eliminate implicit bias compared to open-ended "culture fit" chats. [5, 7, 9] 
* Diverse Interview Panels: Including women in interview loops and featuring female leaders in company branding improves candidate comfort and trust. [9]
