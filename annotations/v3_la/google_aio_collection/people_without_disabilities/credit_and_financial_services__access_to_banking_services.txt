Access to banking and credit services for the general population is primarily driven by income levels, credit history, and digital or geographic proximity. [1, 2, 3, 4] 
## 📊 Income and Affordability
Financial stability directly determines whether a person can open and maintain formal accounts. [4, 5] 

* Low income restricts account opening
* High monthly maintenance fees create barriers
* Lack of minimum balance requirements helps inclusion [4, 5, 6] 

## 📈 Credit History and Scores
Lenders evaluate past financial behavior to determine credit eligibility. [4, 7] 

* Subprime scores limit loan approvals
* Thin credit files restrict credit access
* Positive repayment history builds upward mobility [2, 4, 7] 

## 💡 Financial Literacy and Trust
Understanding financial products builds confidence in formal banking systems. [2] 

* Financial education increases bank usage
* Mistrust in institutions deters account creation
* Complex bank terms cause avoidance [2, 5] 

## 📌 Technology and Geography
Physical and digital infrastructure dictates how easily consumers reach services. [1, 4, 5] 

* Branch distance reduces rural banking access
* Internet connectivity drives mobile banking adoption
* Lack of digital tools excludes remote users [1, 4, 5]
