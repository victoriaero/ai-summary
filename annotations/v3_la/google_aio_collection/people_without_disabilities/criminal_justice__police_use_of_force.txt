Police use of force for individuals without disabilities is mainly driven by situational dynamics like suspect resistance, weapon presence, and the seriousness of the offense. [1, 2] 
## Suspect Behavior and Resistance

* Active resistance: Fighting, pulling away, or trying to escape.
* Verbal hostility: Threats or aggressive language toward officers.
* Compliance levels: Refusing lawful orders during a detention or arrest. [2] 

## Presence of Weapons

* Possession: Carrying a firearm, knife, or blunt object.
* Implicit threats: Reaching into pockets or a waistband during a stop. [1, 3] 

## Substance Use

* Intoxication: The influence of alcohol or drugs during an encounter.
* Impaired judgment: Heightened unpredictability or volatile reactions. [1] 

## Crime Severity

* Felonies vs. misdemeanors: Violent or serious crimes raise the legal threshold and likelihood for physical intervention.
* Scene urgency: Fast-moving crimes requiring immediate containment or capture. [2] 

## Neighborhood Environment

* Disadvantaged areas: High-crime neighborhoods where [police encounter higher stress and volatility](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4929689/).
* Encounter type: Dispatched calls versus officer-initiated street stops. [4]
