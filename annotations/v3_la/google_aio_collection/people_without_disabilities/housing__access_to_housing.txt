## Key Factors Influencing Housing Access

* Affordability and Fixed Incomes: Many individuals rely on fixed incomes like Supplemental Security Income (SSI), which frequently falls short of average market rents. Research from the [Urban Institute](https://www.urban.org/research/publication/people-disabilities-living-us-face-urgent-barriers-housing) notes that disabled individuals experience higher rates of poverty and are often "priced out" of stable housing. [2, 3, 4, 5] 
* Physical Accessibility Shortages: A very small percentage of the overall housing stock meets basic accessibility needs, such as step-free entries, wider doorways, or adaptable bathrooms. According to the [Equal Rights Center](https://equalrightscenter.org/ada-35-inaccessible-housing/), older buildings and multi-family complexes frequently lack elevators or universal design features. [2, 3, 6] 
* Landlord Discrimination and Bias: Despite legal protections under the Fair Housing Act, housing providers sometimes deny reasonable modification requests. Others hold negative assumptions or refuse service animals and necessary structural accommodations. [2, 7] 
* Gaps in Federal Assistance: A large majority of low-income disabled renters who qualify for federal housing assistance do not receive it due to underfunded programs and long waitlists. [3, 8]
