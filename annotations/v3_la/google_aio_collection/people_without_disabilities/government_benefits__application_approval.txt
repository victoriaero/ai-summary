✅ Government benefits for people without disabilities (such as unemployment, SNAP/food assistance, TANF cash aid, or low-income housing) are primarily influenced by financial, employment, and demographic factors rather than medical evidence.
## 📊 Key Financial and Income Factors

* 💵 Gross and Net Income: Earnings must fall below specific program limits relative to the Federal Poverty Level (FPL).
* 🏦 Asset and Resource Limits: Savings, vehicles, and property holdings cannot exceed caps (e.g., $2,750 for general SNAP households or lower/higher depending on the state).
* 📉 Household Size: Income thresholds scale upward based on the total number of legal dependents and co-habitants.

## 🗓 Employment and Work Requirements

* 📌 Work History / Hours: Programs like TANF or SNAP ABAWD (Able-Bodied Adults Without Dependents) mandate working or training 20+ hours weekly.
* 🔎 Job Search Compliance: Unemployment benefits require proving active, documented weekly applications and availability for work.
* ⚠️ Involuntary Separation: Unemployment approval depends on being laid off or fired without gross misconduct (quitting usually disqualifies applicants).

## 💡 Administrative and Demographic Criteria

* 📍 Residency and Citizenship: Applicants must prove state/local residency and legal immigration or citizen status.
* 🗒 Documentation Accuracy: Missing wage stubs, identity proof, or delayed recertification paperwork cause immediate technical denials.
* 📅 Ages of Dependents: TANF or child-care subsidies factor in the exact age of youngest children (e.g., exemption limits for school age).
