Academic preparation, family financial background, and personal aspirations are the primary factors influencing college access and admission for students without disabilities. [1, 2] 
## Academic Preparation

* High school curriculum: Rigorous coursework, including honors, Advanced Placement (AP), or International Baccalaureate (IB) classes, builds the academic foundation required for college admissions.
* Standardized testing: Scores from tests like the SAT or ACT often serve as benchmarks for college readiness and merit-based selection.
* Grade point average (GPA): Cumulative high school performance remains a primary metric reviewed by admissions committees.

## Socioeconomic Status (SES) and Finances

* Family income: Wealth dictates a family's ability to pay for tuition, application fees, and standardized test preparation materials.
* Financial aid and loans: Knowledge of and access to grants, scholarships, and federal loans heavily shape whether students apply and enroll.
* Cost-benefit analysis: Students and families weigh anticipated future earnings against the total debt acquired to fund a degree. [1] 

## Family and Social Support

* Parental expectations: Higher educational aspirations and career goals from parents strongly motivate students to pursue post-secondary paths.
* Guidance counseling: High school counselors and college-prep programs provide critical information regarding applications, deadlines, and financial aid.
* Peer and community networks: Encouragement from friends, mentors, and community networks shapes college predisposition and choice. [1] 

## Institutional and Policy Factors

* Admissions policies: Criteria such as holistic reviews, affirmative action guidelines, or test-optional policies alter who gets accepted.
* Geographic location: Proximity to higher education institutions and local college marketing affect application rates.
