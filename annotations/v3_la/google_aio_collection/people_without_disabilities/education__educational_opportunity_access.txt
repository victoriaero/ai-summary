Educational opportunity and access for general students are primarily driven by household income, geographic location, family background, and school funding quality. [1] 
## Socioeconomic Status

* Household Income: Families with higher incomes can afford better schools, tutoring, and college prep resources.
* Poverty: Low-income students face financial strain, forcing some to work or delay their education.
* Basic Needs: Lack of stable housing or nutrition lowers attendance and academic performance. [2, 3] 

## Geographic Location

* Rural vs. Urban: Rural schools often lack advanced courses, modern labs, and certified teachers.
* The Digital Divide: Reliable internet access and personal computers are necessary for modern learning, but rural and poor areas often lack proper digital infrastructure.
* Transportation: Long travel distances or poor public transit limits regular school attendance. [1, 4, 5] 

## Family and Social Support

* Parental Education: Parents with college degrees tend to guide children more effectively through the application and enrollment process.
* Home Environment: A quiet study space and active parental involvement boost student motivation and achievement.
* Community Networks: Access to mentors and professional networks helps students learn about career and higher education pathways. [6] 

## School Resources and Funding

* Public Funding: Schools rely on local taxes, meaning low-income neighborhoods often have underfunded classrooms and fewer resources.
* Teacher Quality: Well-funded districts attract and retain highly trained teachers, improving student outcomes.
* Class Size: Smaller classes allow teachers to give individualized attention and support. [7]
