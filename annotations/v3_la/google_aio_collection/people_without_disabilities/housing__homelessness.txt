Economic hardship, severe housing affordability gaps, and personal or relational crises primarily drive homelessness for individuals without disabilities. [1, 2, 3] 
## Structural and Economic Factors

* High Housing Costs: Rising rents that outpace stagnant wages leave low-income households severely cost-burdened, meaning they spend more than 30% to 50% of their income on housing. [4, 5, 6] 
* Poverty and Low Wages: Inadequate minimum wages, job insecurity, underemployment, and sudden job loss make it impossible to absorb financial shocks. [1, 7] 
* Decline in Public Assistance: Shrinking safety-net programs, inadequate housing vouchers, and a shortage of low-income units heighten vulnerability. [4, 8, 9] 

## Relational and Personal Crises

* Domestic Violence: Fleeing an abusive household forces many individuals and families—especially women and children—out with no immediate alternative shelter. [1, 5, 7] 
* Family Breakdown: Relationship dissolution, conflict, or severe family rejection (frequently affecting youth or LGBTQ+ individuals) remove vital social safety nets. [1, 2, 3, 5] 
* Debt and Financial Shocks: Unmanageable personal debt, unexpected emergency bills, or formal evictions trigger sudden housing loss. [2, 6, 7] 

## Systemic Factors

* System Failures: Inadequate transitions or support when leaving foster care, juvenile justice, or correctional facilities without secure housing lined up. [5, 8] 
* Discrimination: Systemic racism and marginalization restrict equitable access to stable employment, fair housing, and financial resources. [4, 5, 10]
