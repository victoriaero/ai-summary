Perceived differences, social standing, and emotional vulnerability are the primary factors that influence harassment and bullying among students without disabilities in educational settings. [1] 
## Key Risk Factors
According to guidance from [StopBullying.gov](https://www.stopbullying.gov/bullying/at-risk), students without disabilities are more likely to experience bullying when they possess specific risk factors: [1] 

* Perceived Differences: Standing out from peers due to physical appearance (being overweight or underweight), wearing glasses, wearing different clothing, or being new to a school. [1] 
* Economic Status: Being unable to afford trendy clothing, school supplies, or items that peers consider "cool". [1] 
* Perceived Vulnerability: Being viewed as weak, passive, or unable to defend themselves. [1] 
* Mental Health Challenges: Experiencing depression, anxiety, or low self-esteem, which can signal vulnerability to potential bullies. [1] 
* Social Standing: Having fewer friends, being less popular, or struggling to integrate into existing peer groups. [1] 
* Interpersonal Difficulties: Having trouble getting along with others, or occasionally displaying behavior perceived as annoying, provocative, or attention-seeking. [1]
