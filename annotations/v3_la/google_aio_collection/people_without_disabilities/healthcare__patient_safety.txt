Patient safety for people without specific disabilities is shaped by communication, organizational systems, human behaviors, and patient engagement. [1, 2] 
The primary factors influencing patient safety in general healthcare include:
## Communication and Interaction

* Nurse-Patient Communication: Clear, open interaction between staff and patients serves as one of the strongest predictors of positive safety measures and effective care assessment. [2] 
* Teamwork and Coordination: Smooth handoffs and clear communication among doctors, nurses, and support teams prevent medical errors and oversights. [1, 3] 

## Patient Engagement and Literacy

* Health Literacy: Patients who understand their treatment plans, medications, and medical procedures are less likely to experience preventable adverse events. [1, 4] 
* Active Participation: Willingness and confidence to ask questions, verify procedures, and engage in safety activities improve overall outcomes. [2] 

## Organizational and System Factors

* Staffing and Workload: Adequate staffing levels, manageable shift hours, and minimized staff fatigue or burnout reduce cognitive bias and clinical errors. [1, 3, 5] 
* Safety Culture: Institutions that foster a blame-free reporting environment encourage staff to report near-misses and learn from adverse events. [5, 6] 
* Resource Availability: Access to standard medical equipment, clean physical environments, and functioning health information technology (like electronic health records) supports safe care delivery. [1, 3, 5]
