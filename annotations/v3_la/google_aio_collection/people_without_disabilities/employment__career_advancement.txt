Career advancement for people without disabilities is primarily driven by education, professional networks, job performance, and access to leadership training opportunities.
## Education and Continuous Learning

* Higher Education: Holding a bachelor's degree or higher training significantly increases the likelihood of promotions and entry into higher-paying roles.
* Skill Development: Continuous professional development, certifications, and mastering emerging technical or managerial skills accelerate career growth.

## Performance and Experience

* Job Performance: Consistently exceeding performance metrics and delivering high-quality work builds a strong professional reputation.
* Tenure and Experience: Accumulated on-the-job experience and successful handling of complex projects prepare workers for leadership responsibilities.

## Professional Networks and Mentorship

* Internal Networking: Building relationships with decision-makers and senior leaders increases visibility for promotion opportunities.
* Mentorship and Sponsorship: Having a mentor or a senior sponsor helps navigate corporate structures and advocate for career advancement.

## Organizational Culture and Strategy

* Career Self-Management: Proactively setting goals, seeking feedback, and volunteering for high-visibility assignments drive progression.
* Company Policies: Transparent promotion criteria, leadership development programs, and supportive corporate cultures encourage upward mobility.
