Access to government safety net benefits (like SNAP, Medicaid, or unemployment insurance) for people without disabilities is primarily influenced by economic, administrative, and geographic factors. [1, 2, 3] 
## Key Influencing Factors

* Income and Asset Limits: Programs are strictly means-tested, meaning earnings or savings above specific thresholds disqualify applicants. [4, 5] 
* Administrative Burdens: Complex paperwork, frequent recertification rules, and long application processes create barriers that reduce enrollment. [1] 
* Geography and Infrastructure: Rural residents often face limited broadband internet access, digital illiteracy, and long travel distances to reach physical agency offices. [2] 
* Work Requirements: Mandatory employment and reporting rules condition aid on maintaining specific work hours, which can cut off individuals experiencing job instability. [3, 6] 
* Agency Funding and Staffing: Government staffing cuts and processing backlogs lead to communication delays and lost paperwork. [1, 7]
