Incarceration in the criminal justice system is driven by core socioeconomic, racial, and systemic factors that increase the risk of arrest and detention.
## Key Factors Influencing Incarceration

* Socioeconomic Disadvantage: Poverty and unstable housing or homelessness significantly raise the risk of coming into contact with law enforcement and being unable to afford pretrial bail. [1, 2] 
* Education Gaps: Lower rates of high school completion and limited literacy strongly correlate with a higher likelihood of encountering the criminal legal system. [3] 
* Racial Disparities: Racial and ethnic minorities experience disproportionate rates of police surveillance, arrests, and harsher sentencing outcomes. [4, 5] 
* Early System Contact: Harsh school disciplinary policies, suspensions, and early juvenile detention multiply the long-term risk of future adult incarceration. [6, 7]
