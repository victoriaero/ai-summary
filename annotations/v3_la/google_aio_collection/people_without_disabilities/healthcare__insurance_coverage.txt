Healthcare insurance coverage for people without disabilities is primarily driven by employment status, income level, and geographic location.
## 💼 Employment Status

* Employer-sponsored plans: Over 64% of working-age adults without disabilities get coverage through a job. [1] 
* Full-time vs part-time: Full-time workers secure continuous private plans more easily.
* Job industry: High-wage sectors offer better health benefits than low-wage hourly jobs.

## 💵 Income and Poverty Level

* Federal Poverty Level (FPL): Determines eligibility for public assistance or marketplace subsidies.
* Medicaid access: Low-income adults without disabilities qualify for Medicaid in states that expanded the program.
* Affordability gaps: Middle-income earners who do not qualify for Medicaid may struggle with high-premium private plans.

## 📍 Geographic Location

* State policy differences: Medicaid expansion varies significantly depending on the state of residence.
* Regional variations: Southern states often report higher uninsurance rates compared to the Northeast. [2] 
* Local market competition: Rural areas offer fewer private insurance options than major metropolitan centers.

## 📊 Demographic and Social Factors

* Age brackets: Younger adults (ages 18–29) face higher uninsurance rates before transitioning to stable careers.
* Race and ethnicity: Minority populations experience systemic disparities in private coverage access.
* Marital status: Married individuals frequently share or access dual-income family insurance policies. [3]
