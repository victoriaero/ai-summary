Housing stability for people without disabilities is primarily influenced by economic resources, housing affordability, and systemic socioeconomic factors. [1] 
## 💡 Economic and Financial Factors

* Income level: Consistent earnings determine the capacity to pay rent or mortgages on time.
* Cost burden: Spending over 30% of household income on housing increases instability risks.
* Employment stability: Job security directly supports ongoing rental or mortgage payments.
* Savings and debt: Emergency funds prevent displacement during unexpected financial shocks. [1, 2] 

## 🏠 Housing Market and Structural Conditions

* Affordable housing supply: Shortages of low-cost housing units drive severe cost burdens.
* Rental market availability: Low vacancy rates limit alternative options during price hikes.
* Geographic location: Neighborhood safety, local job markets, and transit access affect long-term retention. [1, 2, 3, 4] 

## ⚖️ Systemic and Social Determinants

* Legal history: Past evictions or criminal records create barriers with landlords.
* Racial and social disparities: Discriminatory practices disproportionately impact marginalized renters.
* Support systems: Access to community resources, legal aid, and emergency rental assistance mitigates displacement. [1, 2, 3, 5, 6, 7]
