Several organizational and social risk factors influence workplace harassment, including in environments involving employees without disabilities. According to the [U.S. Equal Employment Opportunity Commission (EEOC)](https://www.eeoc.gov), general risk factors that increase the likelihood of harassment include: [1] 

* Homogenous Workforces: Work environments lacking in diversity can make minority or different employees more susceptible to targeted conduct. [1, 2] 
* Power Disparities: Imbalances in authority allow individuals in power to engage in or overlook misconduct, where subordinates feel unsafe reporting due to fear of retaliation. [1, 2] 
* Isolated or Decentralized Workplaces: Locations or shifts remote from leadership and physical supervision reduce managerial oversight and accountability. [1, 2] 
* Young Workforces: Younger or newer employees often lack onboarding outreach and are treated as easier targets by harassers. [1, 2] 
* Customer Service Roles: High tolerance for the "customer is always right" mindset exposes workers to external harassment from clients or customers. [1, 2] 
* Monotonous or Isolated Work: Routine duties or isolated tasks can foster boredom, tension, and coarsened social dynamics among staff. [1, 2] 
* Alcohol Consumption: Work-related events involving alcohol lower inhibitions and increase the incidence of inappropriate behavior. [1, 2]
