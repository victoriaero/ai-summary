Multiple systemic, economic, and interpersonal factors influence homelessness and housing instability for transgender individuals.
## Key Factors Influencing Transgender Homelessness

* Family and Interpersonal Rejection: Rejection by family members or romantic partners is a primary driver of homelessness, particularly for [LGBTQ+ youth](https://www.thetrevorproject.org/research-briefs/homelessness-and-housing-instability-among-lgbtq-youth-feb-2022/), many of whom run away or are forced out of their homes due to their gender identity. [1, 2, 3] 
* Housing and Employment Discrimination: Transgender individuals experience high rates of discrimination by landlords, leading to unfair denials, evictions, and workplace bias that limits their ability to earn a stable income. [2, 4] 
* Economic Instability and Poverty: Due to employment discrimination and limited access to career advancement, trans people—especially transfeminine individuals and transgender people of color—face disproportionate rates of severe poverty and spend over half their income on housing. [5, 6, 7, 8] 
* Barriers in Emergency Shelters: Traditional shelters often misgender residents, deny access based on gender identity, or fail to provide safe, inclusive environments, which forces many transgender people to avoid shelters and remain unsheltered. [2, 9, 10] 
* Rigid Identification Requirements: Mismatches between a person's legal identification documents (showing a non-affirming name or gender marker) and their actual appearance or lived identity create bureaucratic hurdles when applying for housing, jobs, or medical assistance. [6, 8] 
* Intersectional Stigma: Rates of homelessness are significantly higher among Indigenous, Black, and multiracial transgender individuals due to the intersection of racism, transphobia, and economic inequality. [1, 5, 10]
