Transgender people face a significantly higher risk of arrest and detention due to systemic profiling, socioeconomic marginalization, and discriminatory enforcement of conventional gender expectations by law enforcement. [1, 2, 3] 
## Key Factors Influencing Arrest and Detention

* Gender Perception and Profiling: According to recent data highlighted by the [Williams Institute](https://williamsinstitute.law.ucla.edu/publications/police-public-arrests-trans-people/), individuals perceived as transgender or gender nonconforming face heightened police scrutiny, higher search rates despite equivalent rates of contraband discovery, and arrest rates roughly twice those of cisgender individuals. Officers often subject those violating conventional gender norms to more punitive outcomes. [1, 2] 
* Involvement in Underground Economies: Due to widespread employment discrimination, high rates of homelessness, and poverty, many transgender individuals—particularly transgender women of color—survive through underground economies like sex work. Vague policing ordinances (such as "loitering with intent to solicit") give officers wide discretion, making survival sex work a primary driver of arrests. [3, 4, 5] 
* Racial and Economic Disparities: Intersectionality heavily compounds arrest risk. Low-income transgender people and transgender people of color experience disproportionate rates of over-policing, street harassment, and pre-existing incarceration history, which strongly predicts future entanglements with the legal system. [3, 5, 6, 7] 
* Public Visibility and Harassment: Transgender individuals are frequently more vulnerable to being on the street due to housing instability or family rejection. Studies show they face harassment and spurious arrests simply for being visible in public spaces. [8]
