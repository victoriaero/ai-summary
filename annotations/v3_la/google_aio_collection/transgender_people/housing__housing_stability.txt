Housing stability for transgender people is significantly influenced by a combination of economic, social, and systemic factors that often create compounding barriers to maintaining safe and permanent housing. [1, 2] 
## Key Factors Influencing Housing Stability

* Economic Insecurity and Poverty: Transgender individuals experience disproportionately high rates of poverty and severe rent burdens due to systemic wage gaps and employment discrimination. Transgender women, particularly trans women of color, face some of the steepest wage disparities and employment barriers, limiting their ability to afford stable housing. [2, 3, 4, 5] 
* Interpersonal and Family Rejection: Many transgender youth and adults experience housing instability or literal homelessness after being forced out or running away from unsupportive family environments. The breakdown of family and social support networks remains an initial and primary driver of displacement. [6, 7, 8] 
* Housing and Rental Discrimination: Open discrimination by landlords—such as direct lease denials, harassment, or eviction based on gender identity—contributes heavily to housing insecurity. Transgender renters are frequently subjected to severe cost burdens or forced into unsafe living arrangements. [2, 5, 9, 10] 
* Barriers in Shelters and Safety Nets: Traditional emergency and transitional housing programs often enforce rigid gender-segregated policies or lack staff trained in cultural competence. This can result in the mistreatment, misgendering, or physical vulnerability of transgender residents, leading many to avoid official shelters entirely. [3, 10] 
* Rigid Identity Document (ID) Mandates: Administrative hurdles and mismatching names or gender markers on legal identification documents block equitable access to public assistance, employment verifications, and subsidized housing programs. [3, 5] 
* Intersection of Stigma and Race: Transgender people of color, Native/Indigenous individuals, and those with disabilities experience compounded vulnerability driven by the overlapping impacts of transphobia, transmisogyny, and systemic racism. [2, 6, 9]
