Socioeconomic discrimination, housing instability, and targeted policing heavily drive the disproportionate rates of incarceration for transgender people in the criminal justice system. [1, 2] 
## Key Factors Influencing Incarceration

* Socioeconomic Discrimination: Widespread bias in housing, education, and employment forces many transgender individuals—especially transgender people of color—into severe poverty and homelessness. [1, 2] 
* Reliance on Underground Economies: Due to systemic exclusion from legal employment, individuals may rely on survival economies like sex work or the informal drug trade, which increases their risk of criminalization. [3, 4] 
* Overpolicing and Profiling: Law enforcement frequently targets marginalized groups through biased surveillance, profiling, and disproportionate stops or arrests. [5] 
* Identification Barriers: Difficulties updating gender markers on legal identification documents often "out" transgender people during routine police interactions, escalating tension or scrutiny and increasing the likelihood of arrest. [3, 4, 6] 
* Racial Disparities: Intersecting racial bias means Black and Native American transgender individuals experience vastly higher rates of criminal legal system contact and incarceration than white transgender people. [2, 7]
