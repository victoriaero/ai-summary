Harassment and bullying of transgender people in education are influenced by a combination of school policies, social norms, environment, and intersectional identities. [1, 2, 3] 
## Key Influencing Factors

* School Policies and Climate: Attendance at schools with restrictive or anti-LGBTQ+ policies correlates with significantly higher rates of verbal and physical harassment. Conversely, inclusive environments featuring Gay-Straight Alliances (GSAs), gender-neutral bathrooms, and affirming educator training reduce vulnerability. [1, 2, 4] 
* Cisnormativity and Gender Non-Conformity: Rigid societal expectations that align gender expression strictly with biological sex at birth drive bias-based bullying. Students who visibly challenge these norms face higher targeting. [3, 5] 
* Intersectional Identities: Compounding stigmas—such as being a transgender youth of color, having a disability, or navigating unsupportive regional or socioeconomic contexts—amplify the risk and severity of victimization. [1, 4, 6] 
* Support Systems: A lack of family acceptance, peer support, or institutional intervention leaves transgender students isolated and more vulnerable to poly-victimization and academic disruption. [4, 7, 8]
