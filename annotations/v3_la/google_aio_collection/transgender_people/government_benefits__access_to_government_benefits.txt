Several structural, legal, and social factors influence access to government benefits (such as Medicaid, SNAP, and public housing) for transgender people. [1, 2] 
## Legal and Policy Restrictions

* State and Federal Rules: Coverage policies vary significantly by region and administrative directives. Recent federal and state rules have moved to restrict or ban gender-affirming care under public programs like Medicaid and the Federal Employee Health Benefits (FEHB) program. [1, 3, 4, 5, 6] 
* Geographic Disparities: Where a person lives dictates whether state Medicaid programs explicitly include or exclude gender-affirming treatments. [4] 

## Administrative and Identification Barriers

* Inaccurate ID Documents: Difficulties in obtaining updated or accurate legal identification documents that match a person's gender identity create major administrative roadblocks when applying for public assistance. [2, 5] 
* Intake Processes: Non-inclusive intake forms and a lack of standardized data collection on gender identity can lead to under-enrollment and misallocation of support. [7, 8] 

## Discrimination and Stigma

* Mistreatment by Staff: Fear of discrimination or direct verbal harassment and denial of equal treatment by agency staff discourage transgender individuals from applying for or maintaining benefits. [2, 7] 
* Intersectionality: Transgender people of color, low-income individuals, and those living in rural areas face compounding economic barriers and higher rates of poverty, increasing their reliance on—yet complicating their access to—public assistance. [2, 7, 9]
