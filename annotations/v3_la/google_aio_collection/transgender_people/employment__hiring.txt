Hiring and employment outcomes for transgender people are heavily influenced by systemic bias, legal frameworks, and structural workplace factors.
## Key Factors Influencing Transgender Employment

* Implicit and Explicit Bias: According to research from the [Williams Institute at UCLA School of Law](https://williamsinstitute.law.ucla.edu/wp-content/uploads/Trans-Workplace-Discrimination-Nov-2024.pdf), a substantial portion of transgender individuals experience hiring discrimination or report that their gender identity negatively affects hiring and promotion decisions. Studies like those highlighted by the [DC Office of Human Rights](https://ohr.dc.gov/page/transemployees) indicate that employers sometimes favor less-qualified cisgender candidates over better-qualified transgender applicants. [1, 2] 
* Resume Disclosures and Pronouns: Disclosing non-binary or trans-inclusive markers, such as pronouns on resumes, can trigger adverse employer responses, with research showing lower positive response rates in certain regions or male/female-dominated industries. [3, 4] 
* Legal Protections and Patchworks: While federal interpretations (such as Title VII under the U.S. Supreme Court ruling in Bostock v. Clayton County) prohibit employment discrimination based on gender identity, inconsistencies in state-level enforcement and local political climates still leave many workers vulnerable or fearful of exposure during background checks and reference verifications. [5, 6, 7] 
* Administrative and Application Hurdles: Discrepancies between legal names, birth markers, and chosen names on prior employment records or background checks can involuntarily "out" an applicant, leaving their hiring prospects dependent on a manager's personal attitudes. [7] 
* Industry Safety and Representation: According to a report by [McKinsey & Company](https://www.mckinsey.com/featured-insights/diversity-and-inclusion/being-transgender-at-work), safety concerns and a lack of visible representation lead many transgender job-seekers to avoid certain industries entirely, disproportionately steering them toward lower-paying service sectors. [8, 9] 
* Workplace Inclusivity Measures: Companies that lack clear non-discrimination policies, inclusive restroom access, or training on gender identity often struggle with retention and fail to attract diverse talent. [10, 11]
