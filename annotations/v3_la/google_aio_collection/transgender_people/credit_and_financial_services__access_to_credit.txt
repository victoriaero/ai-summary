Access to credit for transgender people is heavily influenced by legal name changes, systemic wage gaps, and documented discrimination in financial services. [1, 2] 
## 🔤 Legal Name and Credit File Discrepancies

* File fragmentation: Updating a legal name can cause credit files to split or fragment across bureaus. [3] 
* Deadnaming on reports: Former names exposed on credit checks can involuntarily out a person and trigger bias. [3] 
* Score drops: Administrative credit file mismatches can lower credit scores by 100 points or more. [3] 

## 📉 Economic Insecurity and Wage Gaps

* Lower earnings: Transgender individuals often face a pronounced wage gap, earning significantly less than cisgender peers. [4, 5] 
* Family rejection: Losing family financial support after coming out forces reliance on high-interest credit cards. [1] 
* Medical and legal expenses: Out-of-pocket costs for gender-affirming care and legal name changes strain liquidity. [1] 

## ⚠️ Institutional Discrimination and Bias

* Direct bias: Studies show transgender respondents experience high rates of discrimination in banking and lending. [2] 
* Account barriers: Outdated name or gender marker policies in financial systems block smooth product access. [4] 
* Underbanking: Transgender households experience higher rates of being unbanked or underbanked. [1]
