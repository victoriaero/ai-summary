Transgender people face unequal access to housing mainly due to direct discrimination, family rejection, poverty, and unsafe emergency shelter rules. [1, 2, 3] 
## Discrimination and Eviction

* Lease denials: Landlords often refuse to rent apartments or homes to people because of their gender identity. About 20% of transgender individuals report facing housing discrimination. [3, 4, 5] 
* Unfair evictions: Roughly 10% to 11% of transgender people report being forced out of their homes or evicted due to their gender expression. [3, 4] 
* Low homeownership: Only about 25% of transgender adults own a home, compared to much higher rates for the general population. [6] 

## Family Rejection and Youth Homelessness

* Loss of support: Many transgender youth and adults face rejection from their parents and relatives after coming out.
* High youth rates: Up to 40% of homeless youth in the United States identify as LGBTQ+, often driven by this family breakdown. [3, 7, 8] 

## Poverty and Lower Incomes

* Pay gaps: Transgender workers, especially transgender women, earn significantly less money per dollar compared to cisgender workers (people whose gender matches the sex they were assigned at birth).
* Higher poverty: Economic instability and high job discrimination make it hard to pay for rent, deposits, and monthly housing costs. [1, 6, 7] 

## Shelter and Safety Net Barriers

* Shelter mistreatment: Many public and emergency shelters misgender transgender residents, place them in unsafe areas, or turn them away entirely. [3, 4] 
* Policy changes: Shifts in federal rules regarding equal access in federally funded shelters can create confusion and increase the risk of being denied safe housing. [9, 10] 

## Identity Documents

* Mismatch issues: Official identification cards (like driver's licenses or passports) that do not match a person's lived name or gender can block access to public assistance, rental applications, and housing programs. [2] 

## Intersectional Factors

* Compounding bias: Transgender people of color and transfeminine individuals face higher rates of housing instability due to the combined impact of transphobia and racism. Data from research groups like the [National Alliance to End Homelessness](https://endhomelessness.org/resources/research-and-analysis/housing-instability-among-gender-expansive-people-and-paths-forward/) and the [Williams Institute](https://williamsinstitute.law.ucla.edu/publications/lgbt-housing-instability/) show that these overlapping prejudices deepen housing inequality.
