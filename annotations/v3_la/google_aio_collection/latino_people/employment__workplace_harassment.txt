Multiple interconnected systemic, organizational, and social factors influence workplace harassment for Latino people in employment.
## Key Influencing Factors

* Language Barriers and Accent Discrimination: Language differences or speaking English with an accent often expose Latino workers to derogatory remarks, stereotyping, or exclusion, and limit their ability to report abuse. [1, 2, 3] 
* Immigration Status and Legal Vulnerabilities: Undocumented or first-generation workers frequently face severe exploitation, wage theft, and threats of deportation or retaliation regarding their immigration status from employers. [1, 4] 
* Occupational Segregation: Overrepresentation in low-wage, undervalued industries—such as agriculture, cleaning, hospitality, and construction—places Latino individuals in environments with fewer institutional safeguards and higher rates of labor violations. [1, 3, 5] 
* Intersectionality and Marginalized Identities: Intersecting identities significantly compound risk; for instance, Latinx LGBTQ+ employees experience substantially higher rates of lifetime harassment and discrimination compared to their non-LGBTQ+ peers. [6, 7] 
* Power Dynamics and Organizational Lacks: Hierarchical imbalances, lack of workplace diversity, remote or temporary contract statuses, and absence of culturally responsive reporting channels discourage victims from raising concerns due to fear of firing. [1, 5, 8, 9]
