Multiple economic, structural, and immigration-related factors influence housing instability and homelessness for Latino people, according to research from the [UCLA Latino Policy & Politics Institute](https://latino.ucla.edu/research/redefine-latinx-homelessness/) and the [National Alliance to End Homelessness](https://endhomelessness.org/blog/examining-latino-homelessness/). [1, 2] 
## Economic and Housing Factors

* Hidden Homelessness (Doubled-Up Living): Latinos experience high rates of overcrowding and doubled-up living arrangements due to economic hardship, often lacking formal leaseholder protections and facing sudden eviction risks. [1, 3] 
* Escalating Rents and Wages: High costs of living, low wages, and wage theft heavily burden Latino households, driving them closer to literal homelessness. [4, 5] 
* Job Loss: Sudden unemployment combined with ongoing barriers to re-employment frequently precipitates the descent into housing loss. [6] 

## Systemic and Immigration Factors

* Immigration Status: Fear of deportation, concerns regarding the public charge rule, and exclusion from federal relief programs prevent many mixed-status families from seeking timely government assistance. [1, 7] 
* Language and Literacy Barriers: Limited English proficiency and a scarcity of bilingual staff or culturally responsive outreach make official housing programs difficult to navigate. [5, 8] 
* Distrust and Stigma: Cultural stigmas surrounding homelessness and a general lack of trust in municipal or public service systems lead many to avoid formal safety nets entirely. [2, 8]
