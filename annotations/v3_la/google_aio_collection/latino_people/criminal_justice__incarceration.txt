Latino individuals are incarcerated at roughly 2.9 times the rate of white adults in the U.S. criminal justice system, driven by a combination of systemic, economic, and institutional factors. [1] 
## Key Influencing Factors

* Sentencing Bias and Perceived Ethnicity: Research shows that individuals perceived or labeled as Hispanic in criminal justice records are nearly twice as likely to be sentenced to prison as white individuals, even when controlling for crime severity and criminal history. This points to the impact of ethnic stereotypes and biases among legal decision-makers. [2] 
* Bail and Pretrial Detention: Latinos are roughly 33% less likely to be able to meet bail requirements, which leads to higher rates of pretrial detention. Being detained before trial often puts defendants at a disadvantage during plea-bargaining and court proceedings. [3, 4, 5] 
* School-to-Prison Pipeline: Latino and African American students make up about 70% of students arrested or referred to law enforcement through school discipline policies. Harsh disciplinary practices in under-resourced schools push youth into early contact with the justice system. [3, 5, 6] 
* Economic and Employment Scarcity: Limited economic mobility, concentrated neighborhood poverty, and employment scarcity correlate strongly with higher rates of arrest and conviction for property and non-violent offenses. [3, 4, 6, 7] 
* Immigration Status and Enforcement Policies: Policies that criminalize migratory status and increase local collaboration with immigration enforcement (such as ICE) amplify the risk of detention and family separation for undocumented or mixed-status communities. [3, 6, 8] 
* Language and Systemic Barriers: Many local criminal justice agencies lack certified bilingual staff, consistent translation of legal materials, and language access services, hindering fair representation and navigation of legal rights. [8] 
* Data Deficiencies: Inconsistent ethnic data collection across state and local law enforcement agencies often miscategorizes Latinos as "white," which hides the true scale of ethnic disparities and complicates targeted policy reform. [1, 9, 10] 
* Health and Substance Use Treatment Gaps: High rates of untreated mental illness and substance use disorders—compounded by a lack of culturally and linguistically appropriate healthcare resources—increase the risk of repeat criminal justice contact. [4]
