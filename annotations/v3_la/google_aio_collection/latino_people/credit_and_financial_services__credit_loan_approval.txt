Several key financial, structural, and behavioral factors influence credit and loan approval for Latino consumers and business owners:

* Debt-to-Income (DTI) Ratio: DTI is one of the top lender-reported reasons for mortgage and loan denials among Latino applicants (with rates around 16.8% to 24.3% depending on the study and loan type), driven frequently by higher debt burdens or underemployment relative to income. [1, 2, 3] 
* Credit History and Score Depth: Payment history, credit utilization, and credit length heavily dictate approvals. Historical exclusions, reliance on cash transactions in certain communities, and fewer traditional credit-building opportunities mean some Latino applicants have thinner or non-traditional credit files. [4, 5, 6, 7] 
* Collateral and Down Payments: Insufficient collateral or asset reserves often impact loan evaluations, particularly for larger loans or mortgages where wealth gaps limit cash-on-hand for down payments. [2, 8] 
* Neighborhood and Regional Disparities: Loan origination rates and approval probabilities can vary based on the racial concentration and economic history of the census tract or metropolitan area where the property or business is located, as analyzed via data from the [Federal Reserve](https://www.federalreserve.gov/econres/feds/how-much-does-racial-bias-affect-mortgage-lending.htm). [4, 7, 9] 
* Use of Non-Traditional Lenders (Fintechs and Alternative Finance): Latino consumers adopt financial technology (fintech) at high rates (up to 92%) due to language access, alternative data utilization, and lower barriers, though some alternative products carry higher rate spreads or subprime terms. [10, 11] 
* Business Financing Gaps: For Latino-owned small businesses—which request financing at high rates—approval disparities persist for loans exceeding $50,000 despite strong underlying business performance metrics. [12]
