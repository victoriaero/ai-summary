Socioeconomic status, school funding disparities, and language barriers significantly influence educational opportunities and access for Latino students. [1, 2] 
## Socioeconomic and Family Factors

* Income and Family Support: Many Latino students come from low-income households and face pressure to support their families financially, which can limit schooling time or higher education plans. [3, 4] 
* Parental Education: Educational attainment varies; children of immigrant-only parents often encounter higher hurdles due to parents having less familiarity with the U.S. school system. [5, 6] 
* Early Childhood Education: Lower participation rates in affordable preschool programs create initial school-readiness gaps in early elementary grades. [1, 2] 

## Institutional and Systemic Barriers

* School Funding and Segregation: Latino students frequently attend underfunded, urban, or segregated schools that offer fewer advanced placement (AP) math and science courses. [1, 2, 7] 
* Language and Cultural Obstacles: Spanish-dominant language barriers and a lack of translation services or culturally competent counseling hamper student progress and parent engagement. [1, 3, 6] 
* Educator Bias: Implicit or explicit bias from teachers and staff can impact academic tracking, discipline practices, and referrals to gifted programs. [6] 

## Higher Education Access

* Affordability and First-Generation Status: A high percentage of college-bound Latino students are first-generation and rely heavily on two-year colleges or underinvested [Hispanic-Serving Institutions (HSIs)](https://lulac.org/education_equitable_access/) due to financial and institutional constraints.
