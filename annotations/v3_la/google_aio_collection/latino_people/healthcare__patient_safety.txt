Language barriers, insurance coverage gaps, and provider communication quality are primary factors influencing patient safety and healthcare experiences for Latino individuals. [1, 2, 3] 
## Key Factors Influencing Patient Safety and Care Quality

* Language and Communication Barriers: About 28% of Hispanic/Latino individuals age 5 and older speak English "less than very well," which creates risks for misunderstanding medical instructions, misdiagnoses, and delayed care. Poor provider-patient communication is a major driver of perceived discrimination and lower healthcare satisfaction. [1, 2] 
* Insurance and Economic Access: Latino individuals and foreign-born immigrants experience disproportionately higher uninsured rates, leading to delayed medical care, reliance on emergency settings, and poorly controlled chronic conditions like hypertension or diabetes. [3, 4] 
* Documentation Status and Fear: Undocumented immigrants face severe systemic barriers, including residency verification rules, fear of deportation, and chronic stress that negatively impact health-seeking behavior and utilization of preventive safety resources. [5] 
* Social Determinants of Health (SDOH): Negative social determinants—such as lower income, unstable housing, and localized poverty—strongly correlate with delayed medical treatment and suboptimal care quality. [6] 
* Interpersonal Discrimination and Bias: Studies show that a substantial portion of Latino patients experience ethnicity-based discrimination, most frequently reporting that providers do not listen to them or treat them discourteously. [1]
