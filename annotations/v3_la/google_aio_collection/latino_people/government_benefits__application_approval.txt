Administrative burdens, immigration status concerns, and language barriers significantly influence the application and approval of government benefits for Latino families. [1, 2] 
## Immigration Status and Mixed-Status Households

* Mixed-Status Dynamics: Many Latino children are U.S. citizens, but live with noncitizen or undocumented family members. This creates confusion over who qualifies for assistance. [3, 4, 5] 
* Chilling Effects and Fear: Anti-immigrant rhetoric and enforcement policies create a culture of fear. Families often avoid applying for safety-net programs like SNAP or Medicaid out of fear it could trigger deportation or negatively impact immigration status. [2, 6, 7] 
* Public Charge Concerns: Past changes and ongoing misinformation surrounding the "public charge" rule lead eligible citizens and lawful residents to forgo benefits. [2, 7] 

## Administrative and Systemic Barriers

* Complex Rules: Navigating means-tested criteria, sponsorship rules, and verification procedures (such as SAVE systems) presents steep hurdles. [8, 9] 
* Misconceptions about Eligibility: Many low-income Latino and immigrant parents mistakenly believe their household income or lack of full citizenship disqualifies their eligible children from aid. [5] 
* Outreach and Navigation Gaps: A lack of culturally tailored assistance and community navigation resources reduces application rates. [2] 

## Language and Literacy

* Limited English Proficiency: Complex application forms and notices frequently lack accessible translations or adequate language support. [2, 4, 8] 
* Literacy Constraints: Varying levels of reading and writing proficiency in either English or Spanish make it difficult to complete and maintain benefit documentation. [3]
