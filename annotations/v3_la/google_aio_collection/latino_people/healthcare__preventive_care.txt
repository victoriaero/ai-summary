Several key barriers and structural factors influence the use of preventive healthcare among Latino people.
## Access and Socioeconomic Factors

* Lack of health insurance: Latino individuals experience some of the highest uninsured rates in the U.S., making routine checkups and preventive screenings unaffordable. [1] 
* Immigration and legal status: Undocumented immigrants face severe legal, financial, and systemic barriers that reduce their likelihood of having a usual source of care. [2, 3] 
* Workplace inflexibility: Many Latino workers lack paid sick leave or flexible scheduling, which makes attending daytime preventive appointments difficult. [4, 5] 
* Poverty and education: Lower income levels and educational inequities limit health literacy and the resources needed to navigate the healthcare system. [4, 6, 7] 

## Language and Communication Barriers

* Limited English proficiency (LEP): Patients who are more comfortable speaking Spanish or another language may struggle to understand medical advice or communicate effectively with providers. [8, 9] 
* Scarcity of bilingual staff: A shortage of bilingual medical professionals and readily available interpreters can lead patients to avoid or delay care. [4, 8, 9] 

## Cultural and Systemic Factors

* Family and community dynamics: Health decisions are often guided by family trust, traditions, and collective support systems. [4, 5] 
* Distrust in the system: A lack of representation of Latino professionals in healthcare settings can increase patient mistrust and fear of mistreatment. [10] 
* Reliance on urgent care: Due to accessibility issues, many turn to emergency rooms or urgent care clinics for acute needs rather than establishing a continuous relationship with a primary care doctor. [7, 10]
