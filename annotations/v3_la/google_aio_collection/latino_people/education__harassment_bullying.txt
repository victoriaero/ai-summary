Multiple intersecting factors—including race and ethnicity, immigrant status, language barriers, and gender or sexual orientation—influence harassment and bullying for Latino students in education. [1] 
According to research and reports from organizations like the [Lumina Foundation](https://www.luminafoundation.org) and studies highlighted by educational researchers, key influences include:
## Key Influencing Factors

* Immigrant and Citizenship Status: Recent immigrants and newcomers face higher rates of bias and intracultural bullying from peers regarding their citizenship status or English proficiency. [2] 
* Language Differences: Students who are English language learners are frequently targeted for teasing and harassment by peers with stronger English skills. [2] 
* Program Type in Higher Education: Research from the [Gallup and Lumina Foundation Study](https://news.gallup.com/opinion/gallup/510323/hispanic-students-feeling-discrimination-school.aspx) shows that Hispanic post-high school students experience discrimination and harassment at two to three times higher rates in short-term credential and certificate programs compared to traditional associate or bachelor's degree programs. [3, 4] 
* Academic and Extracurricular Involvement: Studies note that Latino students involved in certain academic or athletic extracurriculars, or those achieving high academic success, may experience increased peer victimization or unique social pressures within school environments. [5] 
* Intersectionality: Vulnerability increases when race/ethnicity intersects with gender, immigrant generation status (US-born vs. foreign-born), and sexual orientation. [1, 6]
