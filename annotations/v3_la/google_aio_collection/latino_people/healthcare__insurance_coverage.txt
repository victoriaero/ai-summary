Several structural, economic, and legal factors influence health insurance coverage rates and adequacy for Latino people in the United States, according to research from organizations like the Kaiser Family Foundation and the [Center on Budget and Policy Priorities](https://www.cbpp.org/blog/eliminating-structural-barriers-can-improve-latino-peoples-access-to-health-coverage).
## Key Influencing Factors

* Employment-Based Coverage Gaps: Latino individuals are less likely to receive health insurance through an employer compared to non-Latino white individuals, often working in industries that do not offer affordable benefits. [1, 2] 
* Immigration Status and Eligibility: Strict immigration-related rules bar certain noncitizens, particularly undocumented immigrants, from enrolling in federal programs like Medicaid or Affordable Care Act (ACA) marketplaces. [3] 
* Fear and Chilling Effects: Concerns that public program enrollment or utilization might negatively impact immigration status or family members create a barrier to signing up for available coverage. [3] 
* Socioeconomic Status and Income: Lower average family incomes and wealth disparities limit the ability to afford private premiums, deductibles, and out-of-pocket medical costs. [4, 5] 
* Nativity and Citizenship: Foreign-born Latino individuals experience significantly higher uninsurance rates compared to U.S.-born Latino individuals. [6] 
* Administrative and Procedural Hurdles: Complex enrollment processes, language barriers, and a lack of outreach regarding available assistance programs reduce uptake. [3]
