H.R. 1 Changes & Work Requirements

Details on federal Medicaid, SNAP, and health coverage updates under H.R. 1.

On this page

    On this page

      What Providers & Clients Need to Know

      Recent federal legislation (H.R. 1) introduces changes to public assistance, Medicaid eligibility rules, and Supplemental Nutrition Assistance Program (SNAP) work requirements. These changes may affect eligibility, renewal requirements, and access to health coverage for some New Yorkers.

      Medicaid Changes

      Beginning January 1, 2027, some adults with Medicaid coverage will need to complete 80 hours per month of qualifying activities to maintain their coverage. Qualifying activities may include work, education, job training, community service, or other activities permitted under the new federal requirements.

      Some people are exempt from the Medicaid work requirements. The complete list of exceptions and requirements is available through the New York State Department of Health and NY State of Health.

      Examples include certain individuals who are:

      • Participating in a program to help with drug or alcohol use
      • Pregnant or recently pregnant
      • Living with a qualifying physical or mental health condition that makes it difficult to work
      • Receiving Medicaid because of a disability
      • Caring for a child under age 14 or a person with a disability
      • Enrolled in or entitled to Medicare
      • Veterans who meet specified requirements
      • Under age 19 or age 65 and older

       

      SNAP Changes

      Federal changes also affect SNAP work requirements for certain adults known as Able-Bodied Adults Without Dependents (ABAWDs).

      Generally, an ABAWD who is subject to the SNAP time limit must work, participate in a qualifying employment or training activity, or complete another qualifying activity for at least 80 hours per month to continue receiving SNAP benefits beyond the applicable time limit.

      There are exceptions from the ABAWD requirements. Treatment for substance use disorder may be relevant to an individual's eligibility or exemption status, depending on the applicable SNAP rules and the individual's circumstances.

      New York's SNAP requirements have also been affected by federal changes and litigation. Current requirements and exceptions can change, so individuals should check the latest information from the New York State Office of Temporary and Disability Assistance (OTDA).

      Information for Clients and Individuals

      If you receive Medicaid or SNAP and are currently in treatment or recovery, your health and basic needs come first.

      Many people are exempt from Medicaid work requirements, including individuals with certain medical conditions, disabilities, caregiving responsibilities, pregnancy, postpartum status, and other circumstances established under federal and State law.

      Individuals receiving treatment for a substance use disorder may also qualify for an exemption.

      Find the complete and most current list of exceptions and eligibility information from the NYS Department of Health's Changes to Medicaid Coverage webpage.

      Guidance for Addiction Service Providers

      Providers can help minimize disruptions in care by preparing early and supporting individuals through upcoming eligibility changes.
      Prepare Your Organization
      • Review your Medicaid and SNAP payer mix.
      • Review eligibility verification policies and procedures.
      • Coordinate with your Electronic Health Record (EHR) and billing vendor regarding batch eligibility verification.
      • Review ePACES/EMEVS eligibility verification processes.
      • Review self-pay, uninsured, and sliding fee policies.
      • Develop an internal communication plan for staff.
      Support Individuals
      • Identify clients who may be affected by Medicaid or SNAP changes
      • Help individuals keep their contact information current in the Medicaid system.
      • Assist individuals with Medicaid renewals and eligibility redeterminations.
      • Maintain documentation that may support exemption requests when appropriate.
      • Share educational materials and available resources with clients.
      • Develop an external communication plan for clients and community partners.
