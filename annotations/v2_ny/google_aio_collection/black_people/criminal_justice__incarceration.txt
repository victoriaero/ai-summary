According to research from organizations like the American Civil Liberties Union (ACLU) and The Sentencing Project, these factors operate at every single stage of the legal process.Policing and Law Enforcement PracticesDisproportionate Stops and Searches: Law enforcement officers stop Black pedestrians and drivers at significantly higher rates than white individuals, often with less cause, increasing the likelihood of subsequent arrests.Targeted Drug Enforcement: The punitive enforcement strategies of the War on Drugs heavily focused on urban, low-income neighborhoods, leading to higher rates of drug-related arrests for Black Americans despite similar usage rates across races.Use of Discretion: Biased policing practices and vague criteria—such as gang databases—frequently misallocate resources and increase police contact in minority communities.Prosecution and Pretrial DisparitiesCharging Decisions: Prosecutors exercise wide discretion and are statistically more likely to file mandatory minimum charges or invoke habitual offender laws against Black defendants than similarly situated white defendants.Plea Bargaining and Bail: Financial burdens like cash bail disadvantage low-income defendants, disproportionately people of color, forcing higher rates of pretrial detention and increased pressure to accept unfavorable plea deals.Diversion Disparities: Prosecutors are more likely to offer diversion programs or lenient plea agreements to white defendants rather than Black or Latine individuals for comparable offenses.Sentencing and Judicial TreatmentLengthier offenses.Sentencing Enhancements: Reliance on past criminal history as a baseline for sentencing often compounds earlier systemic biases, resulting in harsher cumulative penalties.Socioeconomic and
