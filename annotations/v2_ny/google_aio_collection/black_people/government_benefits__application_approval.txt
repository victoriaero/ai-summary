Several key structural, economic, and administrative factors influence the application and approval processes for government benefits among Black individuals and communities. Research from organizations like the Center on Budget and Policy Priorities (CBPP) and the Center for Law and Social Policy (CLASP) highlights how policies and systemic trends shape these outcomes.
Administrative Burdens and Work Requirements
• Strict Rules: States with large Black populations often implement stricter rules and higher administrative hurdles for programs like Unemployment Insurance and Temporary Assistance for Needy Families (TANF).
• Work Mandates: Onerous work requirements and arbitrary time limits create distinct barriers that disproportionately cut off families of color.
• Geographic Disparities: Black children and families are statistically more likely to live in states where benefit payouts are lower and programs reach fewer people in poverty.
Economic and Labor Market Factors
• Higher Need: Persistent systemic economic inequality, lower median household wealth, and higher unemployment rates mean Black workers and families rely more heavily on safety-net programs like SNAP and federal disability benefits.
• Economic Downturns: Application and entry rates into safety-net and disability programs among Black workers fluctuate significantly in response to broader economic recessions and downturns.
Institutional and Social Perceptions
• Stigmatizing Narratives: Public assistance policies are sometimes shaped by historical racial stereotypes and resentment, which drive political support for harsher administrative burdens.
• Access and Navigation: Challenges involving delivery system efficiency, difficult-to-navigate application processes, and limited localized outreach act as structural deterrents during the application phase.
