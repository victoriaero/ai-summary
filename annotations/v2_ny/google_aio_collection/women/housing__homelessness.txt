For women who are currently housed, the transition into homelessness is rarely caused by a single event. Instead, it is driven by a complex intersection of interpersonal trauma, systemic economic inequality, and structural housing barriers. Organizations like the National Alliance to End Homelessness and the Downtown Women's Center highlight several distinct risk factors that uniquely threaten women's housing stability.
The primary factors influencing homelessness for housed women include:
Interpersonal & Safety Factors
• Intimate Partner Violence (IPV): Domestic abuse is the leading cause of homelessness among women. Survivors are frequently forced to choose between staying in a violent home or fleeing into immediate housing instability.
• Financial Abuse: Abusers often sabotage a woman's economic independence by controlling bank accounts, ruining her credit score, or causing gaps in her employment, making it nearly impossible for her to secure or maintain an independent lease.
• Family Breakdown: Divorce, separation, or the sudden loss of a partner can abruptly eliminate a household’s primary source of income, quickly leading to eviction.
Economic & Caretaking Pressures
• The Gender Wealth & Wage Gap: Women consistently earn less on average than men, meaning a significantly higher percentage of their income goes toward rent. This leaves them with minimal savings to survive financial emergencies.
• Childcare Costs: Single mothers often face a compounding financial crisis. High childcare costs can restrict a woman’s ability to work full-time or attend job training, making them exceptionally vulnerable to falling behind on rent.
• Caregiving Demands: Women disproportionately shoulder unpaid caretaking duties for children or aging relatives, which reduces their overall earning potential and labor market mobility.
Systemic & Institutional Barriers
• Lack of Affordable Housing: As rental costs rise faster than average wages, low-income women face severe rent burdens, frequently spending more than half of their income on housing.
• Housing Discrimination: Landlords occasionally refuse to rent to survivors of domestic violence or single mothers, citing stigmas or arbitrary credit requirements that penalize victims of financial abuse.
• Intersectional Systemic Inequities: Due to long-standing systemic disparities, BIPOC and LGBTQIA+ women experience disproportionately higher rates of housing insecurity and eviction.
Health & Support Networks
• Weak Social Support Systems: A lack of robust social or family networks leaves women without an emergency safety net—such as a place to temporarily stay—during a personal or financial crisis.
• Untreated Mental and Physical Health Challenges: Chronic health conditions or severe trauma-related mental illnesses (like PTSD from prior abuse) can interfere with a woman's ability to maintain steady employment and retain housing.
