Multiple structural, legal, social, and economic factors influence women's access to banking and credit services:
• Legal and Property Rights: Unequal rights to own land, inherit property, or manage assets limit women's ability to provide traditional collateral for loans. In some regions, women also face legal restrictions on traveling outside the home or obtaining formal identification without a male family member's permission.
• Identification (KYC) Hurdles: Strict Know Your Customer (KYC) rules often disadvantage women. Married women in multiple countries face extra administrative hurdles to get national ID cards or passports compared to men.
• Collateral and Credit History Gaps: Financial institutions heavily rely on immovable property or formal credit histories. Because women frequently work in informal sectors, run smaller micro-enterprises, or handle utility/mobile accounts that are not reported to credit bureaus, building a formal credit score is harder.
• Digital and Technological Divides: Lower rates of smartphone ownership, higher mobile internet costs, digital illiteracy, and poor connectivity restrict women from safely utilizing digital banking or mobile money platforms.
• Biased Risk Perceptions and Product Design: Lenders often view women borrowers through high-risk assumptions, leading to unfavorable terms like higher interest rates or shorter repayment windows. Many financial products are built with a "male-default" design that ignores women’s cash-flow realities, time poverty, and domestic care responsibilities.
• Mobility and Infrastructure Barriers: Physical bank branches or agent networks are often located far from home. Combined with safety concerns, travel costs, and time constraints, physical distance heavily discourages regular banking.
• Representation Gaps: Financial institutions and regulatory bodies feature a severe lack of women in leadership and frontline agent roles, which reduces trust and comfort for female customers.
