Access to government benefits for women is primarily influenced by structural economic burdens, legal frameworks, and the level of female representation in policymaking.
Key Influencing Factors
• Unpaid Care Work: Women perform over 75% of unpaid domestic and caregiving labor, limiting the time available for formal employment and reducing qualification metrics for work-tied benefits.
• Informal Employment Concentration: A large percentage of working women are concentrated in the informal economy or low-wage jobs, frequently excluding them from social insurance programs.
• Policy Representation: Higher female representation in parliaments and government ministries directly correlates with increased public spending on health and education, shaping how welfare policies prioritize women's needs.
• Legal and Regulatory Frameworks: Discriminatory property, inheritance, and labor regulations restrict women from independently controlling economic assets, which are often prerequisites or qualifiers for social assistance.
• Design and Implementation of Budgets: The adoption of gender-responsive budgeting helps determine whether public funds effectively target systemic inequalities or reinforce existing gaps.
