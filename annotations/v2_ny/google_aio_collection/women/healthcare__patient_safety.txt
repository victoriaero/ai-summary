Patient safety for women in healthcare is influenced by clinical biases, systemic gaps in pain management, reproductive health considerations, and intersectional factors.
Key Influencing Factors
• Clinician Bias and Dismissal of Symptoms: Women are more likely to have their physical symptoms, particularly acute and chronic pain, dismissed or underestimated by healthcare providers.
• Racial and Intersectional Inequities: Racial biases compound safety risks, leaving historically minoritized patients—such as Black women—particularly vulnerable to diagnostic delays, maternal morbidity, and unmanaged pain.
• Gaps in Reproductive and Gender-Specific Care: Conditions unique or disproportionate to women (such as endometriosis or gynaecological procedures) often face long diagnostic delays, systemic under-resourcing, and unmanaged procedural pain.
• Physiological and Environmental Work/Care Factors: Facility design, temperature regulation, and chemical or physical exposures during distinct reproductive phases (like pregnancy, menstruation, or menopause) affect both female patients and healthcare workers, impacting overall care quality and safety frameworks.
• Systemic and Workforce Pressures: High workloads, poor communication among healthcare teams, and staff burnout contribute heavily to preventable medical errors and compromised safety standards in specialized women's health units.
