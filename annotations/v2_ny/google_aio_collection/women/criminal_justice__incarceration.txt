Poverty, trauma, and harsh drug laws are the main factors that drive the rising incarceration rates of women in the criminal justice system.
According to reports from organizations like the Prison Policy Initiative and the Council on Criminal Justice, several key elements influence why women enter and remain in the criminal justice system:
Socioeconomic and Financial Pressures
• Poverty and Survival: Many women are arrested for low-level property or drug offenses driven by extreme poverty, economic marginality, or attempts to support their families.
• Inability to Pay Bail: Roughly 60% of women held in local jails are unconvicted and awaiting trial, primarily because they cannot afford high money bail amounts.
• Fines and Fees: Minor municipal citations, technical probation infractions, and unpaid court fees frequently trigger jail admissions or long-term confinement.
Trauma, Abuse, and Mental Health
• Childhood Trauma: Over 90% of justice-involved women report experiencing severe childhood trauma, physical abuse, or neglect.
• Intimate Partner Violence (IPV): More than 70% report histories of intimate partner violence. Some violent offense charges stem from self-defense, coercion, or being misidentified as the primary aggressor during domestic disputes.
• Substance Use and Mental Health: Rates of post-traumatic stress disorder (PTSD), depression, and chemical dependency are significantly higher among incarcerated women than their male counterparts.
Systemic and Co-Defendant Factors
• Co-Defendant Sentencing: Women involved in crimes alongside male partners often receive equal punishment despite playing a lesser or non-violent role in the actual act.
• Racial Disparities: Black and Latina women face disproportionately high rates of imprisonment compared to white women, reflecting broader systemic inequalities in policing and sentencing.
• Technical Violations: Probation and parole revocations for minor administrative or non-criminal rule-breaking regularly send women back to prison.
