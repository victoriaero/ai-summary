Arrest and detention rates for women are heavily influenced by mandatory domestic violence laws, aggressive enforcement of low-level drug offenses, and police discretion shaped by gendered behavioral expectations.
According to research from organizations like the Council on Criminal Justice and the Bureau of Justice Assistance, several key factors drive police decisions to arrest or detain women.
Legal and Policy Factors
• Mandatory Arrest Laws: Laws requiring arrests in domestic violence calls often result in the arrest of female victims who act in self-defense or get caught in dual-arrest situations.
• Punitive Drug Policies: Strict enforcement of low-level drug crimes tied to addiction has increased drug-related arrests for women faster than for men.
• Legalistic Policing: A shift toward formal processing means police are less likely to resolve minor incidents informally.
Behavioral and Social Perceptions
• Gender Norms: Police may view women who display aggression or non-conformity as "uncontrollable" or erratic, which increases the chance of formal arrest.
• Trauma and Mental Health: High rates of trauma, post-traumatic stress disorder (PTSD), and mental health conditions can affect how women react during police encounters.
• Substance Use: Intoxication or active addiction can lead officers to misinterpret a woman's behavior as dangerous or uncooperative.
Environmental and Officer Factors
• Neighborhood Context: Studies show that arrest rates vary by location, with higher formal arrest responses in certain urban or minority neighborhoods.
• Officer Characteristics: The presence of multiple responding officers or specific officer training levels can shift the likelihood of an arrest during an incident.
