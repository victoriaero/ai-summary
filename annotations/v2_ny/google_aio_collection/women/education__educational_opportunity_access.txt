Several key economic, social, and cultural factors influence educational access and opportunities for women.
Economic Factors
• Poverty and Costs: Families with limited financial resources often prioritize the education of boys over girls when they cannot afford school fees, uniforms, or supplies.
• Opportunity Costs: Girls are frequently required to stay home to perform domestic chores or care for younger siblings, lowering their physical and temporal availability for school.
Social and Cultural Norms
• Child Marriage and Early Pregnancy: Traditional practices like child marriage and restrictive re-entry policies for adolescent mothers force many girls to drop out permanently.
• Gender Stereotypes: Cultural biases often devalue women's academic and professional potential, steering them away from lucrative fields like STEM (Science, Technology, Engineering, and Mathematics) into lower-paying arts or humanities roles.
Infrastructure and Safety
• Physical Safety and Distance: Long travel distances to schools and the risk of gender-based violence or harassment during transit deter families from sending girls to school.
• Lack of Facilities: The absence of gender-segregated toilets and proper menstrual hygiene management infrastructure heavily impacts attendance, especially past puberty.
