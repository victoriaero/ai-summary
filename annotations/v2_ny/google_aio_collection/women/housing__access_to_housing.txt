Access to safe and stable housing for women is heavily influenced by economic inequalities, gender-based violence, systemic discrimination, and broader societal and policy factors.
Economic Factors
• Gender Wage Gaps: Persistent disparities in pay mean women earn less over their lifetimes, reducing their purchasing power and compounding wealth at a slower rate than men.
• Higher Rent Burdens: Due to lower median incomes, women often spend a significantly higher percentage of their earnings on rent, leaving them more vulnerable to housing instability and eviction.
• Intersectional Disparities: Women of color, single mothers, and female-headed households face dramatically lower homeownership rates and higher rates of poverty and rental arrears compared to white men or male-headed households.
Safety and Gender-Based Violence
• Domestic and Intimate Partner Violence: Abuse is a leading driver of homelessness among women. A lack of safe, accessible alternative housing or transitional support blocks abuse survivors from leaving dangerous environments.
• Financial Control: Abusers often sabotage victims' credit scores, restrict access to household funds, or forbid them from working, creating severe economic hurdles to securing independent housing.
Systemic and Societal Factors
• Housing Discrimination: Practices like "pinklining" (charging women or female-headed households higher interest rates or fees) and social stigmas against single mothers hinder fair access to mortgages and rental properties.
• Caregiving Responsibilities: Women shoulder a disproportionate amount of unpaid caregiving and household work, which can limit employment flexibility, earnings, and housing choices.
• Older Age Vulnerabilities: Gendered wealth gaps and the loss of a partner or income leave older women at heightened risk of poverty and late-life homelessness when living costs soar.
