Harassment and bullying for women in education—whether students or staff—are influenced by a combination of institutional, social, and interpersonal factors outlined by UNESCO and social-ecological research.
Key Influencing Factors
• Gender-Based and Social Norms: Entrenched patriarchal attitudes, sexist values, and traditional stereotypes create environments where gendered harassment and relational aggression (such as rumors or social exclusion) are normalized or overlooked.
• Institutional Climate and Tolerance: School cultures that lack clear anti-harassment policies, tolerate microaggressions, or feature weak administrative responses allow bullying to persist.
• Power Imbalances and Hierarchies: Structural power dynamics—such as senior versus junior staff ("upward" or downward bullying) or hierarchical peer statuses in schools—increase vulnerability to coercion and targeted mistreatment.
• High-Pressure Working and Learning Environments: High-stakes testing, under-resourcing, and intense workloads create stressful atmospheres prone to blame and interpersonal friction among educators and students alike.
• Intersectionality and Marginalized Identities: Women with intersecting marginalized identities—including race, sexual orientation, disability, or lower socioeconomic status—face heightened rates of bias-based harassment and victimization.
