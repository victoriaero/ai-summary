Police use of force involving women is shaped by officer communication styles, racial disparities, and the specific type of call or encounter.
Factors Involving Female Officers (Use of Force By Women)
• Communication skills: Female officers often rely on verbal de-escalation to calm aggressive behavior before using physical force.
• Lower force rates: Research indicates that women officers use physical or deadly force less often than their male peers.
• Department culture: Some studies suggest that high-stress environments or pressure to fit in can change how officers react in critical moments.
Factors Involving Female Citizens (Use of Force Against Women)
• Rising encounters: Data from the Prison Policy Initiative shows that police use of force against women increased significantly from 1999 to 2015.
• Racial disparities: Black women experience higher rates of traffic stops, searches, and arrests compared to white women, raising the risk of escalation.
• Type of call: Encounters involving domestic disputes often carry a higher risk of physical resistance and heightened conflict during police arrivals.
