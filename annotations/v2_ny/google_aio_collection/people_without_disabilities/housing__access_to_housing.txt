Access to housing for people with disabilities is heavily influenced by severe affordability gaps, a critical shortage of physically accessible units, and widespread housing discrimination.
(Note: While your prompt mentioned "without," research and data focus heavily on the systemic barriers faced by individuals with disabilities, which are outlined below.)
Affordability and Income
• Fixed Incomes: Many individuals rely on Supplemental Security Income (SSI), which often falls far below average rental costs.
• Cost Burden: Millions of disabled renters pay more than 30% or even 50% of their income toward rent, raising the risk of eviction and homelessness.
• Lack of Assistance: The majority of low-income disabled renters do not receive federal housing assistance due to funding shortages.
Physical Accessibility
• Scarce Accessible Stock: Less than 5% of U.S. housing meets basic accessibility needs, and less than 1% accommodates wheelchairs.
• Aging Infrastructure: Much of the federally assisted housing was built before the 1970s and is exempt from modern accessibility laws.
• Modification Barriers: Landlords frequently deny or complicate reasonable physical modification requests.
Discrimination and Bias
• Discriminatory Practices: Disability-based complaints make up over half of all fair housing violations, including direct harassment and indirect restrictions.
• Landlord Lack of Knowledge: Housing providers often lack understanding of accessibility needs, creating unnecessary friction during the rental search.
• Source-of-Income Bias: Landlords frequently reject tenants who use housing vouchers or public assistance.
Systemic and Policy Barriers
• The "Housing Labyrinth": There is no single, centralized database to find and apply for accessible, affordable units.
• Zoning Laws: Single-family zoning restrictions limit the construction of diverse, multi-unit housing options.
