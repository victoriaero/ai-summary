Several key factors influence whether people without disabilities use preventive healthcare services, including financial access, personal health beliefs, and logistical constraints.
Financial and Insurance Factors
• Health insurance status: Uninsured individuals are much less likely to receive routine check-ups and screenings compared to those with private coverage.
• Cost and affordability: Out-of-pocket expenses, copays, and fears of unexpected medical bills lead many people to delay or skip preventive visits.
Personal and Behavioral Factors
• Perceived health status: People who feel healthy often believe they do not need preventive screenings or check-ups.
• Health literacy: A lack of awareness or understanding regarding which screening tests are recommended for a specific age or gender reduces participation.
• Emotional barriers: Fear of a negative diagnosis, anxiety about medical procedures, or personal embarrassment cause many individuals to avoid preventive care.
Logistical and System Factors
• Time and work conflicts: Inflexible work hours, difficulty taking time off, and long appointment wait times act as major practical deterrents.
• Primary care connection: Having a regular primary care provider or a usual source of ongoing care strongly increases the likelihood of receiving up-to-date preventive services.
