📅 Key Factors Influencing Healthcare Coverage for Nondisabled Adults
For adults without disabilities, health insurance coverage and plan costs are primarily driven by socioeconomic, geographic, and regulatory factors rather than medical history. Under the Affordable Care Act (ACA), individual market health insurance premiums are legally determined by specific rules.
📌 Main Determinants of Coverage and Costs
• Employment Status
	• Primary driver of private, employer-sponsored health insurance.
	• Full-time workers access group plans at much higher rates.
	• Part-time or gig-economy workers often rely on public marketplaces.
• Income Level (FPL)
	• Determines eligibility for marketplace subsidies and Medicaid expansion.
	• Lower income brackets face higher baseline uninsurance risks.
	• Income dictates cost-sharing subsidies (reductions in deductibles).
• Geographic Location
	• State-level policies (like Medicaid expansion choices) alter local uninsurance.
	• Local healthcare market competition impacts monthly premium pricing.
• Age
	• Older adults without disabilities pay higher premiums than younger adults.
	• Pricing scaling is legally capped by ACA rating rules.
• Tobacco Use
	• Insurers can charge higher tobacco surcharges (up to 50% more) in most states.
