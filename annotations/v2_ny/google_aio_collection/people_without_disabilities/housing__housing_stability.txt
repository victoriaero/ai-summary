Housing stability for people without disabilities is primarily influenced by economic factors, housing market conditions, and personal social support networks.
💰 Economic Stability and Income
• Income level: Steady employment and income determine the capacity to pay rent or mortgages on time.
• Rent burden: Paying over 30% of income on housing increases the risk of eviction or foreclosure.
• Emergency savings: Having cash reserves prevents displacement during sudden job loss or medical emergencies.
🏠 Housing Market and Quality
• Affordability: The local supply of low-cost housing units dictates whether residents can avoid overcrowding or homelessness.
• Housing quality: Well-maintained properties without structural, pest, or environmental hazards reduce health crises that threaten stability.
• Location: Proximity to reliable transportation, grocery stores, and job opportunities supports long-term residency.
🤝 Social and Institutional Support
• Social networks: Family or community safety nets offer emergency shelter or financial assistance during crises.
• Public assistance: Access to housing vouchers or rental assistance programs mitigates severe poverty impacts.
• Legal protections: Fair rental laws and clear tenant rights prevent unlawful evictions and displacement.
