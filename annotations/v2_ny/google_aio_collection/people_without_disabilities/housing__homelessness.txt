For people without disabilities, homelessness is primarily driven by economic hardship, structural housing market failures, and sudden personal or relational disruptions.
Economic and Structural Factors
• Lack of Affordable Housing: Rising rent costs and a severe shortage of low-income units force many households out of their homes when rent outpaces stagnant wages.
• Job Loss and Poverty: Sudden unemployment, reduced work hours, or a lack of personal savings make it impossible to cover basic living expenses and rent.
• Insufficient Safety Nets: Declining public assistance programs and a lack of adequate housing vouchers leave low-income families vulnerable during financial shocks.
Personal and Relational Circumstances
• Domestic Violence: Fleeing an abusive partner or unsafe home environment is a leading trigger for unhoused individuals and families.
• Relationship and Family Breakdown: The loss of a co-signer, divorce, or family conflict can suddenly strip away a person's shared housing support system.
• Debt and Financial Shocks: General consumer debt or unexpected emergency expenses are frequently cited by individuals without disabilities as a major push factor into housing loss.
Systemic Barriers
• Justice System Involvement: Individuals re-entering society after incarceration often face severe discrimination and roadblocks in securing stable employment and rental housing.
• Systemic Discrimination: Racialized groups and marginalized communities experience disproportionate rates of housing instability due to historic and ongoing economic disparities.
• Lack of Identification: Missing essential identification documents prevents people from accessing jobs, public benefits, and official housing applications.
