For students without disabilities, key factors that influence the risk of being bullied or engaging in harassment in educational settings include perceived differences, social isolation, and emotional or behavioral vulnerabilities.
According to guidelines from StopBullying.gov and general educational research, the primary risk factors for being targeted include:
• Perceived Differences: Students who are seen as different from their peers—such as differences in weight or body appearance, clothing, financial status, race, sexual orientation, or being "new" to a school—face a higher risk of harassment.
• Perceived Vulnerability: Students who are viewed as physically weaker, less able to defend themselves, or passive are more frequently targeted by peers.
• Social Isolation: Having few friends, being unpopular, or lacking strong social connections reduces peer support and increases a student's targetability.
• Mental Health and Emotional Factors: Students experiencing depression, anxiety, or low self-esteem are at a greater statistical risk of being victimized.
Factors that influence a student engaging in bullying behavior include:
• Social Power and Status: Some students bully to assert dominance, gain popularity, or maintain social standing within peer groups.
• Personal and Environmental Stressors: Aggressive behavior is linked to external factors like a lack of parental involvement, exposure to violence at home, frustration, or associating with friends who already bully.
