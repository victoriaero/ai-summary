Academic preparation, financial resources, and family background are the primary factors that influence college access and admission for students without disabilities.
Academic Factors
• High school performance: Grade point average (GPA) and the rigor of completed courses (such as AP or honors classes) serve as core evaluation metrics for admissions.
• Standardized testing: Scores from exams like the SAT or ACT often impact admission decisions and merit-based scholarship eligibility.
• Academic counseling: Guidance counselors help students navigate application timelines, course selection, and college requirements.
Economic and Financial Factors
• Family income and wealth: Affordability dictates whether students can pay application fees, hire test prep tutors, or attend expensive private institutions.
• Cost-benefit analysis: Students and families weigh the immediate costs of tuition against anticipated future earnings and student loan repayment.
• Financial aid and institutional support: The availability of grants, scholarships, and loans heavily shapes which colleges a student can realistically consider attending.
Social and Cultural Factors
• Parental education and background: Families with college-experienced members possess greater cultural capital to guide students through the complex admission process.
• Community and peer networks: Surrounding expectations, high school college-going programs, and peer influences shape a student's predisposition to apply to higher education.
