Socioeconomic disadvantage, racial disparities, and substance use are the primary drivers of incarceration for people without diagnosed disabilities.
Key Factors Influencing Incarceration
• Poverty and Economic Instability: People with low incomes often cannot afford cash bail, forcing them to wait in local jails before trial. This pressure frequently leads to accepting plea deals just to get out of custody.
• Racial and Ethnic Disparities: Black, Hispanic, and Indigenous individuals face higher rates of aggressive policing, stops, and searches in their neighborhoods. These systemic biases result in higher rates of arrest and prosecution.
• Substance Use and Drug Offenses: Problematic use of alcohol and other drugs leads to frequent arrests under strict drug laws, funneling non-violent offenders into the criminal legal system.
• Lack of Education and Employment: Low literacy, dropping out of high school, and chronic unemployment limit economic survival options and increase the risk of criminalized survival behaviors.
• Housing Instability: Experiencing homelessness or living in unstable housing makes individuals much more visible to law enforcement in public spaces, raising their chances of arrest.
• Early Life Contact: Growing up in disadvantaged locations, experiencing out-of-home or foster care, or having early contact with police creates a pathway into the justice system.
