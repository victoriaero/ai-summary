General workplace risk factors—such as a lack of workforce diversity, power disparities, and isolated work settings—influence workplace harassment regardless of a person's disability status.
According to the U.S. Equal Employment Opportunity Commission (EEOC), key organizational and environmental risk factors that foster general workplace harassment include:
• Power disparities: Imbalances in authority make vulnerable or lower-ranking employees hesitant to report misconduct.
• Homogenous workforces: Uniform environments lacking diversity can make minority or distinct employees more susceptible to targeted mistreatment.
• Isolated workplaces: Physical or scheduling isolation reduces oversight and limits an employee's access to complaint channels.
• Decentralized or remote management: Operations distant from direct leadership oversight increase the likelihood of inappropriate conduct going unchecked.
• Customer service roles: High-tolerance policies for external clients ("the customer is always right") expose workers to unchecked third-party harassment.
• Young or inexperienced workforces: Newer and younger employees often lack familiarity with reporting protocols, making them easier targets.
