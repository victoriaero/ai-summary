AI OVERVIEW TEXT
============================================================

Access to government benefits for people without disabilities is primarily influenced by socioeconomic status, administrative burdens, geographic location, and strict income eligibility rules.
Socioeconomic Status
• Income and Education: Individuals with higher education levels and stable incomes navigate complex application systems more easily.
• Poverty Levels: Low-income workers often face higher hurdles because safety net programs targeted at them involve stricter verification processes than universal programs.
Administrative Burdens
• Paperwork Requirements: Complex forms, confusing instructions, and high documentation demands reduce application and renewal rates.
• Communication Barriers: Relying strictly on physical mail for recertification causes lost paperwork when low-income individuals move frequently.
• Digital Access: Poorly designed government websites and digital illiteracy create obstacles for applicants.
Geographic Location
• Rural vs. Urban Gaps: Rural areas often experience lower enrollment in federal and state programs due to limited broadband access and long travel distances for in-person help.
• State-Specific Rules: Benefit availability and income thresholds change depending on the state where a person lives.
Eligibility Rules
• Means-Testing: Programs like SNAP or Medicaid enforce strict income and asset caps.
• Information Gaps: A lack of clear information about available programs and eligibility criteria prevents eligible people from applying.
