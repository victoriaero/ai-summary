Housing stability for men—particularly those facing vulnerability, low income, or reentry—is influenced by a combination of economic, personal, systemic, and social factors.Economic and Financial FactorsEmployment and Job Stability: Steady income and secure employment are primary drivers, whereas job insecurity, low wages, or underemployment make consistent rent payments difficult.Housing Affordability & Cost Burden: Spending a high percentage of income on rent or utilities leaves minimal reserves for emergencies, increasing the risk of eviction or arrears.Savings and Credit History: Limited personal savings and poor credit histories restrict access to formal housing markets and security deposits.Health and Personal CircumstancesMental and Physical Health: Chronic physical illnesses, disabilities, or untreated mental health conditions can impair a person's ability to work, earn an income, and manage daily living responsibilities.Substance Use Challenges: Co-occurring substance use disorders often complicate housing retention, though targeted service engagement (such as Assertive Community Treatment) helps improve stability.Past Incarceration: A history in the criminal justice system creates severe structural barriers, including landlord discrimination and restrictive background checks.Social and Systemic SupportSocial Support Networks: Isolation and a weak or absent family/friend support system make navigating emergency resources and unexpected life crises much harder.Service Engagement: Active participation in supportive housing programs, case management, and community health services strongly correlates with maintaining long-term housing.Institutional Gaps: Declining public assistance programs, lack of accessible legal aid during landlord disputes, and tight local rental markets with few affordable vacancies exacerbate instability.
