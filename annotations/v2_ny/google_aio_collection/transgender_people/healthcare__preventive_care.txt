Preventive care for transgender people is heavily influenced by a combination of systemic barriers, provider education gaps, anatomical considerations, and social determinants of health.
Provider Education and Training Gaps
• Lack of training: Many medical and nursing school curricula lack comprehensive training on transgender health and gender-affirming care.
• Provider knowledge: Patients frequently struggle to find clinicians who understand their specific clinical needs, leading to missed or delayed screenings.
Stigma, Discrimination, and Fear
• Avoidance of care: Stigma, discrimination, and fear of disrespect cause many transgender individuals to delay or completely avoid routine preventive visits like pelvic exams or STI screenings.
• Marginalization: Economic hardship, social stigma, and lack of inclusive intake forms or gender-neutral language further isolate patients from receiving timely primary care.
Anatomy and Hormonal Status
• Retained anatomy: Preventive screenings (such as cervical cancer or prostate checks) depend on a patient's specific retained organs and surgical history rather than their gender identity alone.
• Hormone therapy impacts: Hormone use can alter screening baselines; for example, long-term feminizing hormone therapy can increase breast density, which impacts mammography results.
• Cardiovascular and metabolic health: Monitoring risks associated with long-term hormone therapy is a critical component of routine maintenance.
Insurance and Structural Barriers
• Insurance coverage: Transgender people face higher rates of being uninsured or underinsured, restricting access to regular wellness exams.
• Geographic and state policies: Local and state-level healthcare policies heavily dictate the availability of and regional access to inclusive, competent providers.
