Transgender people face disproportionate rates of incarceration driven by systemic, economic, and law enforcement factors.
Socioeconomic Marginalization and Survival Economies
• Poverty and Joblessness: Transgender individuals experience significantly higher rates of poverty and unemployment due to widespread discrimination in housing, education, and employment.
• Underground Economies: Due to economic exclusion, many are pushed into survival-oriented or criminalized economies, such as survival sex work or the illicit drug trade, increasing their exposure to the legal system.
• Youth Homelessness: High rates of rejection and instability lead to disproportionate homelessness among LGBTQ+ youth, elevating their risk of early juvenile justice contact.
Law Enforcement and Policing Biases
• Targeted Profiling: Police bias, stereotyping, and profiling disproportionately subject transgender people—particularly transgender women of color—to frequent street stops and surveillance.
• Pre-existing Disparities: Disproportionate rates of early arrests and interactions with law enforcement heavily compound lifetime likelihoods of incarceration, particularly for Black and Native American transgender individuals.
