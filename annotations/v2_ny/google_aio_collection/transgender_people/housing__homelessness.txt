Multiple systemic, financial, and social factors drive high rates of housing instability and homelessness among transgender individuals.
Family Rejection and Conflict
• Forced departure: Family conflict and rejection are primary drivers of homelessness, particularly for transgender youth who are frequently forced to run away or get kicked out after coming out.
• Loss of support: The lack of a familial safety net leaves young people vulnerable before they can establish financial independence.
Discrimination and Economic Instability
• Housing and job bias: Widespread discrimination in the rental market and employment leads to lower incomes and higher rates of poverty.
• Cost burden: Transgender households pay more than 50% of their income on housing at much higher rates than cisgender counterparts, increasing eviction risks.
Barriers in Shelter and Social Services
• Mistreatment and denial: Many emergency shelters turn away transgender people or misgender them by forcing placements that do not match their gender identity.
• Safety concerns: Fear of harassment, physical violence, and sexual assault in rigid, gender-segregated facilities cause many unhoused transgender people to avoid shelters entirely and remain unsheltered.
Rigid Identification Requirements
• Mismatched documents: Outdated identity documents (IDs) that do not reflect a person's chosen name or gender presentation create administrative hurdles.
• Access denial: These discrepancies prevent individuals from securing formal leases, jobs, or public assistance.
Intersection of Marginalization
• Compounded risk: Transgender people of color, transfeminine individuals, and Indigenous populations experience significantly higher rates of poverty, discrimination, and unhoused status compared to white or cisgender peers.
