Several compounding structural, economic, and social factors influence the arrest and detention of transgender people by law enforcement within the criminal justice system.
Key Factors Influencing Arrest and Detention
• "Walking While Trans" and Profiling: Police profiling and broad discretion under antiquated loitering or vagrancy statutes often result in transgender individuals—particularly transgender women of color—being targeted and arrested on mere suspicion of engaging in sex work.
• Survival Economy and Poverty: High rates of housing instability, discrimination in employment, and lack of family or social support push some transgender individuals into the underground economy or survival sex work, which significantly increases exposure to police interaction and arrest.
• Geographic and Systemic Disparities: Research from the Williams Institute and public health studies indicate that transgender people face higher risks of arrest or incarceration in specific regions (such as the U.S. South) and when navigating extreme poverty or prior incarceration history.
• Call-for-Service Dynamics: Data analyses on police stops show that transgender and gender-nonconforming individuals are frequently stopped following public calls for service and face a higher relative risk of subsequent arrest even when they do not match formal suspect descriptions.
• Distrust and Reluctance to Seek Help: Due to documented histories of verbal harassment, misgendering, and physical or sexual misconduct by officers during routine encounters, many transgender people distrust law enforcement and are hesitant to report crimes committed against them, altering the nature of their interactions with the justice system.
