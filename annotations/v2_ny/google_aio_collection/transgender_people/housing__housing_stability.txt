Housing stability for transgender people is heavily influenced by systemic discrimination, family rejection, economic factors, and policy barriers. Data from the National Alliance to End Homelessness show that gender-expansive households face severe cost burdens and higher rates of housing instability compared to cisgender counterparts.
Key Factors Influencing Housing Stability
• Discrimination and Bias: According to the Urban Institute, transgender individuals face routine discrimination, including being lied to about unit availability, outright denial of housing, or harassment by landlords and neighbors.
• Family Rejection: Research highlighted by The Trevor Project indicates that family rejection drives high rates of homelessness and housing instability among transgender and nonbinary youth, removing vital safety nets early in life.
• Economic Insecurity and Poverty: Employment discrimination and limited job opportunities lead to disproportionate poverty and severe rent burdens, restricting options for stable housing and homeownership.
• Identity Document (ID) Barriers: Rigid requirements for matching name and gender markers on official IDs block access to public assistance, employment verification, and housing applications.
• Shelter and Safety Net Access: Transgender individuals seeking temporary or emergency shelter often encounter harassment, violence, or policies that misgender them in segregated facilities, forcing many to avoid shelters entirely.
• Intersectionality: Transgender people of color, particularly transfeminine individuals, experience compounding impacts of racism, transmisogyny, and structural inequality that drastically elevate their risk of housing precarity.
