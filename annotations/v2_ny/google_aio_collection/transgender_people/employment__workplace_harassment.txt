Workplace harassment for transgender employees is heavily influenced by individual, organizational, and broader cultural factors rooted in systemic bias and rigid institutional norms.
Core Influencing Factors
• Cisnormativity and Gendered Environments: Workplaces that enforce strict binary expectations, traditional grooming codes, or gendered facilities create frequent points of tension and surveillance that increase vulnerability to harassment.
• Masculinity Contest Cultures: Organizational climates that reward hyper-masculinity or rigid gender presentation show particularly high rates of overt bias and hostility toward non-conforming identities.
• Absence of Inclusive Policies: A lack of explicit anti-harassment policies covering gender identity and expression—coupled with absent or ineffective diversity training—leaves employees unprotected and coworkers uneducated.
• Intersectionality and Marginalization: Vulnerability is compounded by intersecting identities, where transgender people of color, nonbinary individuals, and trans women frequently face disproportionately severe rates of discrimination and targeted hostility.
• Broader Cultural and Social Norms: External societal stigmas, strict religious or traditional gender roles, and political rhetoric often bleed into the workplace, shaping interpersonal prejudice among coworkers.
