Several interpersonal, systemic, and cultural factors influence preventive care access and utilization for homosexual and sexual minority individuals.Provider Competence and StigmaAffirming Care: Access to LGBTQ+-Affirming Health Care Providers increases timely preventive screenings, vaccinations, and disease management.Provider Bias and Discrimination: Fear of or previous experiences with bias, intolerance, or denial of care lead many patients to postpone or avoid routine medical visits.Lack of Provider Awareness: Many primary care clinicians fail to take comprehensive, risk-based sexual histories or remain unaware of a patient's sexual orientation, missing crucial preventive screening opportunities.Disclosure and TrustPatient Disclosure: Openly sharing sexual orientation with a primary care physician is directly linked to higher rates of appropriate screenings, HIV/STI testing, and vaccinations.Internalized Stigma: High levels of internalized stigma or minority stress are negatively associated with routine checkups and preventive health utilization.Economic and Systemic BarriersCost and Insurance: Financial constraints, high out-of-pocket costs, and a lack of insurance coverage specifically for preventive services deter regular care.Geographic and Provider Shortages: Rural or underserved regions often suffer from a shortage of culturally competent providers and inclusive healthcare facilities.
