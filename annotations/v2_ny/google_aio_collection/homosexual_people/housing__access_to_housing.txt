Access to housing for homosexual and LGBTQ+ individuals is shaped by systemic discrimination, economic disparities, family rejection, and inconsistent legal protections.
Housing and Lending Discrimination
• Rental Bias: Landlords sometimes show fewer available apartments to same-sex couples or quote them higher rent prices compared to heterosexual applicants.
• Mortgage Hurdles: Same-sex couples face mortgage approval rates that are 3% to 8% lower, along with higher fees from some lenders.
• Research Data: Studies from the Urban Institute show that housing agents often treat gay male applicants differently than straight applicants by withholding unit availability.
Economic Disparities and Homeownership
• Lower Homeownership: According to data from the Williams Institute, about 49.8% of LGBT adults own their homes, compared to 70.1% of non-LGBT adults.
• Poverty Rates: LGBTQ+ adults experience higher rates of poverty (around 21.6%) compared to cisgender straight adults (15.7%), making it harder to save for deposits or down payments.
• Cost of Living: Many LGBTQ+ individuals move to major cities with stronger social protections, where the local cost of living is much higher.
Family Rejection and Youth Homelessness
• Family Conflict: Rejection from relatives after coming out forces many young people out of their family homes.
• High Risk: LGBTQ+ youth make up between 20% and 45% of the total homeless youth population, putting them at high risk for housing instability early in life.
Legal Protections
• Patchwork Laws: Federal, state, and local laws offer uneven protection against housing bias.
• Enforcement Gaps: Where anti-discrimination laws are weak or poorly enforced, landlords and lenders can turn away queer applicants more easily.
