Hiring outcomes for homosexual and LGBTQ+ individuals are influenced by a complex mix of recruiter demographics, gender-role stereotypes, and organizational policies.
Recruiter Gender and Bias
• Female recruiters: Research highlighted by the University of Sussex shows that female hiring managers sometimes rate gay and lesbian applicants more favorably than equally qualified straight candidates, often perceiving them as higher in competence and warmth.
• Male recruiters: Conversely, studies indicate that male managers may exhibit a slight preference for heterosexual applicants or rate homosexual applicants lower depending on the perceived masculinity or femininity of the role.
Gender-Typed Roles and Stereotypes
• Gender-role violations: Gay men applying for traditionally "masculine" jobs face lower interview callback rates if perceived as violating traditional gender presentations.
• Perceived workplace harmony: Experimental studies noted in IZA World of Labor find that applicant perceptions vary; lesbian candidates are sometimes viewed more favorably for being pleasant to work with, while gay men can encounter implicit bias regarding social and leadership skills in specific industries.
Disclosure and Signals on Resumes
• Overt disclosure: Including signals of sexual orientation on a CV (such as leadership in an LGBTQ+ student organization) can trigger dual responses: positive evaluation by inclusive employers or discriminatory scoring by biased recruiters.
Organizational Culture and Policies
• Inclusive frameworks: Companies with explicit non-discrimination policies, inclusive benefits, and transparent diversity commitments create safer environments for disclosure and generally attract and retain homosexual talent more effectively.
