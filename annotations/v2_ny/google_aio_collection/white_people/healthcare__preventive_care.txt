AI OVERVIEW TEXT

Socioeconomic status, health insurance coverage, and having a regular doctor are the primary factors influencing preventive care utilization for White individuals in healthcare.
Socioeconomic Status (SES)
• Income and Education: Higher levels of income and education strongly correlate with increased use of preventive screenings, routine checkups, and Medical Expenditure Panel Survey wellness visits among White adults.
• Cost Barriers: Out-of-pocket costs and lack of financial resources reduce the likelihood of utilizing recommended preventive services.
Health Insurance Coverage
• Insurance Status: Having health insurance significantly increases access to and utilization of routine preventive care, such as blood pressure checks, cancer screenings, and annual wellness visits.
• Plan Type: Beneficiaries enrolled in Medicare Advantage often show higher rates of preventive care use compared to those in traditional Medicare.
Healthcare Access and Providers
• Usual Source of Care: Having a regular doctor or a consistent primary care provider is one of the strongest predictors for receiving timely preventive services.
• Geography: Rural versus urban residence can impact the availability of and physical access to specialized preventive care clinics and providers.
