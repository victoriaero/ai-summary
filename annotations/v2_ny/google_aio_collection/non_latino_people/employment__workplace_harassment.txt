Workplace harassment for non-Latino employees is driven by general systemic, organizational, and interpersonal risk factors identified by the U.S. Equal Employment Opportunity Commission (EEOC) rather than Latino-specific national origin dynamics.
Key Organizational Risk Factors
• Power Disparities: Hierarchical structures where supervisors hold unchecked authority can foster an intimidating environment and make lower-level or younger employees fear retaliation if they report misconduct.
• Homogenous Workforces: Workplaces lacking demographic diversity can inadvertently marginalize individual minority employees or normalize insular group behaviors.
• Isolated or Decentralized Settings: Remote work sites, isolated physical locations, or decentralized management reduce direct supervision and limit accessible complaint channels.
• Monotonous or Repetitive Tasks: Workplace boredom or routine jobs can sometimes degrade interpersonal boundaries and foster informal, exclusionary teasing or bullying.
Cultural and Interpersonal Dynamics
• Coarsened Social Discourse: Charged external political or social attitudes often bleed into the workplace, increasing offensive commentary or friction among coworkers.
• Customer Service and Client Interaction: Roles heavily dependent on external clients where management adopts a "customer is always right" approach can expose employees to unmitigated harassment from the public.
• Impaired Judgment: Alcohol consumption at company events or offsites frequently reduces inhibitions and correlates with spikes in inappropriate workplace conduct.
