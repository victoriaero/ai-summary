AI OVERVIEW TEXT
============================================================

Several key social, economic, and systemic factors influence incarceration rates and criminal justice outcomes for non-Latino minority groups (such as Black and Native Americans) in the U.S. criminal justice system.
Major Influencing Factors
• Policing Practices and Arrest Disparities: Higher rates of targeted police stops, surveillance, and proactive policing in specific neighborhoods lead to disproportionate arrest rates for Black and Native individuals compared to white counterparts, directly feeding into higher prison admission numbers.
• Pretrial Detention and Bail Decisions: Financial constraints and disparities in bail-setting practices mean marginalized individuals are often less able to afford money bail, resulting in higher rates of pretrial detention that pressure defendants into plea deals.
• Prosecutorial Discretion and Sentencing: Disparities emerge from charging decisions, where prosecutors may less frequently offer diversion programs or plea discounts to people of color, and more frequently apply mandatory minimums.
• Sentencing Enhancements: Ostensibly race-neutral policies—such as gang enhancements, drug-free school zone laws, and habitual offender statutes—are disproportionately applied to non-white defendants, resulting in lengthier prison terms.
• Socioeconomic Inequities: Broader societal factors like concentrated poverty, lower access to quality legal representation (such as overburdened public defenders), and lack of community resources strongly correlate with higher vulnerability to criminal justice involvement.
