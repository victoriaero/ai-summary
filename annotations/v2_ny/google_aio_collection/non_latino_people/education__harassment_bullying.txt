For non-Latino students in educational settings, harassment and bullying are influenced by a combination of individual, social, and environmental factors.
Key Influencing Factors
• Racial and Ethnic Identity: According to data from the National Center for Education Statistics (NCES), bullying based on race is reported at varying rates across demographics, affecting students from diverse backgrounds such as Black, Asian American, and multi-racial youth. Perceptions of difference in majority-minority group dynamics often drive targeted harassment.
• Visible Religious and Cultural Markers: As outlined by StopBullying.gov, students who wear visible religious attire (such as a hijab, kippah, or patka) or practice specific faith traditions face heightened risks of discriminatory harassment.
• Academic and Extracurricular Involvement: Research indicates that minority students involved in high-achieving academic settings or specific extracurriculars may experience unique peer pressures or vulnerability to victimization.
• Immigrant Status and Acculturation: First- or second-generation immigrant youth—regardless of specific regional background—frequently experience higher rates of social exclusion and bullying compared to native-born peers.
• Environment and School Climate: Unsupportive school environments, peer delinquency, and broader social or political events significantly elevate the likelihood of identity-based harassment.
