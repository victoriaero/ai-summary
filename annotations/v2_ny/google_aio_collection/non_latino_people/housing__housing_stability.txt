Housing stability for non-Latino populations (including Black, white, and other demographic groups) is shaped heavily by baseline economic resources, systemic credit access, and historical wealth gaps.
📊 Key Economic & Asset Factors
• Liquid Savings: Prepandemic liquid assets heavily buffer against eviction and rent delinquency risks.
• Income Shocks: Unpredictable employment or wage drops directly scale housing precarity.
• Cost Burden: Spending over 30% of income on rent creates severe vulnerability.
📉 Structural & Historical Disparities
• Intergenerational Wealth: Inheritance and family gift gaps limit down-payment assistance and homeownership durability.
• Credit & Lending: Historical banking disparities and underwriting practices affect credit scores and mortgage terms.
• Appraisal & Returns: Lower neighborhood appreciation and appraisal bias reduce equity generation for specific minority groups.
💡 Neighborhood & Physical Conditions
• Property Quality: Physical inadequacy or overcrowding limits stable long-term asset building.
• Neighborhood Poverty: High-poverty areas experience elevated rates of evictions and municipal stressors.
