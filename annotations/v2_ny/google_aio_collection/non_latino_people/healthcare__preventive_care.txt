Health insurance coverage, socioeconomic status, and having a regular source of care are the primary factors influencing preventive care utilization for non-Latino populations in the United States.
Key Influencing Factors
• Health Insurance Status: Having coverage is one of the strongest predictors of receiving regular wellness visits, vaccinations, and cancer screenings. Uninsured individuals delay or skip preventive services due to direct costs.
• Socioeconomic Status (SES): Higher household income and education levels consistently correlate with better access to health information, paid time off for medical appointments, and guideline-recommended preventive screenings.
• Usual Source of Care: Having a designated primary care provider or regular clinic facilitates continuous health monitoring, routine check-ups, and early disease detection.
• Health Literacy and Awareness: Understanding the purpose, availability, and scheduling guidelines for screenings (such as for colorectal, breast, or cervical cancer) heavily dictates whether individuals pursue preventive care.
• Age and Sex: Demographic variables dictate specific preventive needs; younger adults or specific cohorts sometimes underutilize baseline screenings like blood pressure checks or routine immunizations relative to older demographics.
