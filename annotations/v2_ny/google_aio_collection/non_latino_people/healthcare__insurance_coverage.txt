AI OVERVIEW TEXT

Health insurance coverage for non-Latino individuals is primarily influenced by socioeconomic status, employment characteristics, and geographic location.
📊 Socioeconomic Status
• Income level: Higher household income relative to the federal poverty line strongly increases the likelihood of affording private or marketplace coverage.
• Education: Higher educational attainment correlates with better job stability and health literacy regarding insurance options.
• Wealth and assets: Liquid assets determine an individual's ability to pay for out-of-pocket premiums and deductibles.
💼 Employment Characteristics
• Employer-sponsored plans: Access to full-time work with health benefits is the leading driver of private insurance.
• Industry type: Jobs in service or part-time sectors often lack affordable health coverage options.
• Firm size: Smaller employers are less likely to offer subsidized health insurance compared to large corporations.
📍 Geography and Policy
• State Medicaid expansion: Residing in one of the states that adopted the Affordable Care Act's Medicaid expansion lowers uninsurance rates.
• Local safety net: Proximity to urban teaching hospitals or public clinics changes reliance on public versus private insurance.
• Administrative barriers: Local enrollment assistance and state-level outreach policies affect how easily eligible residents secure coverage.
