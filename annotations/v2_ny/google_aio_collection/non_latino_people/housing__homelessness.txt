High rental housing costs, a shortage of affordable housing, and personal or systemic economic challenges heavily influence homelessness risks for non-Latino populations.
According to research from organizations like the National Alliance to End Homelessness, several primary factors drive homelessness across demographic groups, including non-Latino individuals and families:
Economic and Housing Factors
• Affordable Housing Shortage: A lack of low-cost housing units and high rent burdens push low-income households toward housing instability.
• Income and Wages: Wages lagging behind rising housing costs and general poverty directly limit a household's ability to maintain permanent housing.
• Employment Status: Unemployment or underemployment reduces financial resilience against sudden life changes.
Personal and Social Triggers
• Health and Medical Issues: Uninsured healthcare costs, severe physical illness, untreated mental illness, and substance use disorders act as major risk factors.
• Social Isolation: Fragile or impoverished social support networks fail to provide a safety net during financial emergencies.
• Family Breakdown: Domestic disruption, divorce, or the loss of a primary wage earner can instantly trigger housing loss.
