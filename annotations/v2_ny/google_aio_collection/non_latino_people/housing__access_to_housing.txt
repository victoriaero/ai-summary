Housing access for non-Latino groups is primarily shaped by historical policies, generational wealth gaps, income differences, and ongoing mortgage market discrimination.
Historical and Systemic Policies
• Redlining: Past government policies and Habitat for Humanity outline how discriminatory zoning and redlining confined Black and other minority families to specific areas while denying them equitable home loans.
• Exclusionary Zoning: Current local zoning rules often limit the construction of affordable multifamily housing, which perpetuates neighborhood segregation and concentrated poverty.
Wealth and Inheritance Gaps
• Intergenerational Wealth: Median white family wealth significantly exceeds that of Black families, creating massive disparities in the ability to afford large down payments.
• Inheritance and Gifts: White homebuyers are far more likely to receive financial help or inheritance from family compared to Black or other minority buyers, easing the path to homeownership.
Financing and Appraisal Disparities
• Mortgage Denials: Black and minority applicants face higher mortgage denial rates and varying borrowing costs compared to white applicants with similar financial profiles.
• Appraisal Bias: Homes located in predominantly Black and Hispanic neighborhoods are frequently undervalued by appraisers, which limits property tax bases and household wealth accumulation.
