Patient safety in healthcare is influenced by general human, system, and patient-related factors that apply across diverse populations, alongside specific social determinants.
General Factors Influencing Patient Safety
According to the World Health Organization (WHO), patient safety events are driven by core systemic and individual elements:
• Communication breakdowns: Miscommunication among healthcare workers, clinical teams, and patients.
• Teamwork and workflow issues: Ineffective teamwork, provider fatigue, burnout, and cognitive bias.
• Patient-level factors: Limited health literacy, lack of active engagement in care plans, and treatment non-adherence.
Broader System and Social Determinants
Research on healthcare quality metrics highlights that structural and social conditions shape safety and care outcomes:
• Social Determinants of Health (SDOH): Negative SDOH—such as unstable housing, poverty, or environmental hazards—are strongly linked to delayed medical care and lower-quality healthcare experiences.
• Access and Institutional Quality: Geographic location, insurance coverage status, and the availability of resources at specific healthcare facilities (such as underfunded safety-net clinics versus major teaching hospitals) introduce variability in patient safety and monitoring.
