The primary factors that influence police arrest and detention for non-Latino individuals include the legal severity of the alleged offense, the person's prior criminal record, and the geographic concentration of police patrols in specific neighborhood "hot spots".
According to data from the Prison Policy Initiative and studies published in public health and criminology journals, arrest and detention outcomes vary widely among non-Latino racial groups due to a combination of legal, geographic, and systemic factors.
Key Factors Influencing Arrest and Detention
• Offense Severity and Criminal History:
	• The type and violence level of the alleged crime heavily drive immediate arrest and subsequent pretrial detention.
	• Prior criminal records increase the likelihood of both arrest and higher bail or detention rates across racial groups.
• Neighborhood and Geographic "Hot Spots":
	• Arrests are heavily concentrated in specific urban block groups or high-crime areas.
	• Higher police presence and proactive policing in these specific locations lead to elevated arrest rates for everyone living or traveling through them, regardless of individual behavior.
• Racial and Skin Color Disparities:
	• Research shows that Black and Native American individuals experience significantly higher rates of police stops, arrests, and pretrial detention than White individuals.
	• Studies on skin tone (colorism) indicate that perceived darker skin color increases the likelihood of arrest for individuals within minority populations.
	• White individuals generally experience lower baseline arrest and pretrial detention rates nationally compared to Black and Native American counterparts facing similar charges.
