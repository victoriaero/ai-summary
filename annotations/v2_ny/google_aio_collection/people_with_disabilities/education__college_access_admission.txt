Several key factors influence college access and admission for people with disabilities, spanning academic, financial, legal, and institutional dimensions.
Academic Preparation and K-12 Transition
• High School Achievement: High school GPA is a strong positive predictor of college access and success, but students with labeled disabilities often receive fewer required credits or less college-preparatory coursework.
• Transition Planning: High school transition plans that explicitly identify postsecondary accommodations and legal rights make students much more likely to seek and secure support in college.
• Standardized Testing: Using standardized tests like the SAT or ACT can disadvantage disabled students if proper accommodations (such as extended time) are not provided or if testing conditions trigger stress-induced speed barriers.
Disclosure and Self-Advocacy
• Self-Determination: Personal autonomy and self-advocacy skills strongly correlate with a student's ability to request and utilize college support services.
• Stigma and Disclosure: Many students choose not to disclose invisible disabilities (such as psychological or cognitive conditions) due to fear of social stigma, which prevents them from accessing critical institutional support or accommodations.
Financial and Resource Disparities
• Cost of Evaluations: Obtaining professional documentation or psychoeducational evaluations for a disability can cost thousands of dollars. This creates a major barrier for lower-income students, skewing formal accommodation access toward wealthier demographics.
• Family Support: Active guidance and financial backing from families and third-party associations serve as primary catalysts for entering higher education.
Institutional and Cultural Barriers
• Ableism and Faculty Attitudes: Entrenched academic cultures and negative faculty attitudes toward accommodations can create psychological strain and deter students. Instructors sometimes lack training regarding their legal responsibilities.
• Physical and Digital Infrastructure: Inaccessible campus buildings, physical layout limits, and inaccessible digital learning technologies or online portals restrict college choice and participation.
• Legal and Support Frameworks: While laws like the Americans with Disabilities Act (ADA) prohibit discrimination, the transition from the entitlement-based K-12 system to the eligibility-based higher education framework requires students to independently navigate complex disability resource offices.
