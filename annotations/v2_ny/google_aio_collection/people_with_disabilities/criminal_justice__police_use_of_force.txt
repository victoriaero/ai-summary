Several key behavioral, systemic, and situational factors influence the use of police force against individuals with disabilities during criminal justice encounters.
Key Influencing Factors
• Misinterpretation of Behaviors: Officers frequently mistake the physical or behavioral manifestations of a disability—such as avoiding eye contact, repetitive movements, unresponsiveness, or delays in processing verbal instructions—as signs of non-compliance, defiance, or intoxication.
• Communication Barriers: A lack of effective alternative communication strategies or failure to recognize sensory impairments (such as deafness or being hard of hearing) can lead officers to escalate an encounter when an individual does not respond to verbal commands.
• Reliance on Command-and-Control Tactics: Traditional police training prioritizes physical compliance and pain-compliance tools, which can become dangerous or fatal when deployed against individuals experiencing psychiatric crises or intellectual and developmental disabilities (IDD) who cannot safely process or obey immediate directives.
• Lack of Disability Competency and Training: Many law enforcement agencies lack adequate, ongoing de-escalation, disability recognition, and crisis intervention training, leaving officers unequipped to handle non-violent behavioral health or cognitive scenarios safely.
• Intersectionality and Marginalization: The risk of severe police violence multiplies when disability intersects with other marginalized identities, particularly for Black disabled individuals and other people of color, due to compounded racial biases and systemic ableism.
