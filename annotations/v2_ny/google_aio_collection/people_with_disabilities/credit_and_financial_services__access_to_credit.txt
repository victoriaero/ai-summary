AI OVERVIEW TEXT

Access to credit for people with disabilities is primarily influenced by income stability, credit history gaps, physical and digital accessibility barriers, and lender misperceptions of risk.
📉 Income & Financial Stability
• Earnings volatility: Episodic or degenerative conditions can cause sudden work interruptions.
• Medical expense burden: High out-of-pocket costs reduce disposable income for loan repayments.
• Benefit dependency: Reliance on fixed disability benefits (SSDI, SSI) alters traditional debt-to-income evaluations.
📊 Credit Health & History
• Lower credit scores: Statistical gaps leave fewer disabled individuals with prime scores.
• Alternative borrowing: Heavy reliance on high-cost payday or title loans due to bank denials.
• Debt aversion: Intentional avoidance of mainstream credit products limits positive credit building.
⚠️ Accessibility & Technology
• Physical barriers: Inaccessible branch locations and non-compliant ATMs restrict in-person banking.
• The digital divide: Incompatible screen readers or poorly designed web banking interfaces hinder applications.
• Staff training gaps: Lenders often lack the specialized support required to service disabled applicants properly.
💡 Policy & Behavioral Factors
• Stigma and bias: Fear of disclosure or direct discrimination prevents fair credit negotiation.
• Regulatory protections: Enforcement of laws like the Equal Credit Opportunity Act prevents explicit benefit discrimination.
• Actual default risk: Research shows disabled borrowers often exhibit lower default rates than non-disabled peers despite perceived risks.
