AI OVERVIEW TEXT
============================================================

Incarceration for people with disabilities is heavily influenced by early school discipline, a lack of community mental health care, and the misinterpretation of neurodivergent or cognitive behaviors by law enforcement.
Data from the Penn LDI show that disabled individuals make up a major portion of correctional populations due to several connected systemic factors.
Early Life and School Disparities
• School Suspensions: Children with disabilities are twice as likely to receive out-of-school suspensions as non-disabled peers.
• Early Arrests: Students with learning or developmental disabilities face a significantly higher chance of being arrested while still in elementary or secondary school.
• Lack of Support: Unmet educational accommodations and missing Individualized Education Programs (IEPs) push vulnerable youth away from school and toward the justice system.
Mental Health and Crisis Response
• Reliance on Police: Communities often rely on armed police officers to respond to mental health or behavioral health crises instead of medical teams.
• Criminalization of Illness: Jails and prisons act as default holding facilities for individuals experiencing untreated psychiatric or cognitive crises.
• Lack of Diversion: Courts and local justice systems often fail to provide proper diversion programs or fast-track medical care.
Law Enforcement and Court Interactions
• Misunderstood Behaviors: Neurodivergent traits—such as autism, ADHD, or traumatic brain injuries—can cause sensory overload or communication barriers that officers mistake for aggression or disobedience.
• Procedural Barriers: Court processes are often rigid and complex, failing to provide d/Deaf, hard of hearing, or cognitively disabled defendants with the legal accommodations they need to understand charges or proceedings.
• Inadequate Intake Screening: Jails frequently miss or fail to record invisible disabilities during booking, preventing individuals from receiving necessary support.
Intersectionality and Social Exclusion
• Racial Disparities: The risk of arrest and harsh treatment multiplies when disability intersects with minority race, poverty, or gender.
• Poverty and Housing: A lack of stable community housing, public health funding, and therapeutic support networks leaves disabled individuals more exposed to policing and arrest.
As highlighted by the Prison Policy Initiative, detailed studies on PMC, and updates from the MacFound, addressing these drivers requires shifting from carceral punishment to community-based health and social safety nets.
