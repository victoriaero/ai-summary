Housing stability for people with disabilities is primarily influenced by financial limitations, physical accessibility barriers, systemic discrimination, and the availability of supportive services.
Key Factors Influencing Housing Stability
• Affordability and Fixed Incomes: Many individuals rely on fixed incomes or inadequate benefits like Supplemental Security Income (SSI). Average rental costs vastly exceed what SSI covers, leaving millions of disabled renters severely cost-burdened and spending over 50% of their income on housing. Higher health-related expenses further strain these limited financial resources.
• Lack of Accessible Design: There is a severe shortage of housing built with universal design or physical accessibility features (such as wheelchair ramps, wider doorways, or roll-in showers). Modifying existing homes often requires costly renovations that low-income tenants or owners cannot afford.
• Discrimination and Ableism: People with disabilities frequently encounter direct and indirect housing discrimination, including landlords denying reasonable accommodation requests, failing to share information, or exhibiting negative biases. Disability-related complaints make up a majority of fair housing grievances.
• Gaps in Federal and Community Assistance: The vast majority of low-income disabled individuals who qualify for federal housing assistance do not receive it due to underfunded programs and long waitlists.
• Caregiver and Support Transitions: Aging family caregivers or shifts in required personal care can lead to sudden instability if adaptable community-based supportive housing or Permanent Supportive Housing (PSH) is unavailable. Without these supports, individuals risk institutionalization or homelessness.
