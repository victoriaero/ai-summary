Credit and loan approval for people with disabilities are influenced by standard financial metrics combined with systemic and legal factors regarding disability income and accommodations.
📊 Standard Financial Factors
• Payment History (35% of score): Tracks past on-time debt and loan repayments.
• Debt-to-Income (DTI) Ratio: Compares monthly debt obligations to gross monthly income, including benefits.
• Credit Utilization: Measures the percentage of available revolving credit currently being used.
• Length of Credit History: Evaluates the age of oldest and newest accounts and average account age.
⚠️ Disability-Specific and Systemic Factors
• Income Stability & Verification: Government benefits like SSDI or SSI are legally valid and stable sources, though some lenders incorrectly demand excessive proof of continuation.
• Additional Cost-of-Living Pressures: Higher baseline expenses for adaptive equipment, care, or accessible housing can strain disposable income despite stable benefit streams.
• Alternative Financial Reliance: Underbanked individuals often utilize high-cost non-traditional options if mainstream credit is initially inaccessible.
✅ Legal Protections and Regulations
• Equal Credit Opportunity Act (ECOA): Prohibits lenders from discriminating against applicants using public assistance or disability income.
• Disability Status Omission: A person's disability status or history is not a factor recorded inside a standard credit scoring model.
