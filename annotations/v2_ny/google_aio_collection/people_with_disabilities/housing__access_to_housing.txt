Access to housing for people with disabilities is primarily influenced by financial constraints, physical accessibility limits, and landlord discrimination.
Affordability and Income Constraints
• Low fixed incomes: Many disabled individuals rely on Supplemental Security Income (SSI) or disability benefits that fall far short of average rental costs.
• Severe cost burdens: Millions of disabled renters spend more than 30% to 50% of their income on rent and utilities, driving high rates of housing insecurity and homelessness.
• Gaps in federal assistance: A large majority of low-income disabled households qualify for federal housing assistance but never receive support due to underfunded programs and long waiting lists.
Physical Accessibility and Design
• Severe supply shortages: Less than 5% of housing stock meets moderate mobility needs, and under 1% accommodates wheelchairs.
• Structural barriers: Missing ramps, narrow doorways, multi-step entries, and unadapted bathrooms prevent safe and independent living.
• Modification costs: While the Fair Housing Act allows tenants to make reasonable modifications, the financial burden typically falls on the renter rather than the landlord.
Discrimination and Systemic Barriers
• Landlord discrimination: Providers routinely turn away applicants with disabilities or subtly steer them elsewhere to avoid requests for accommodations.
• Support network reliance: Many adults rely on aging family caregivers; when those caregivers age or pass away, the risk of displacement or institutionalization sharply rises.
• Intersectionality: Disabled people of color—particularly Black and Hispanic renters—face compounded economic marginalization and disproportionately higher rates of eviction.
