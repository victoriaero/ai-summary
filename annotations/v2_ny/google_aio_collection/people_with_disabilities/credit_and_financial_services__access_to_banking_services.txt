Access to banking and credit services for people with disabilities is significantly restricted by economic disparities, physical and digital barriers, and systemic credit health challenges.
📉 Economic and Income Factors
• Low-Income Earning Capacity: Adults with disabilities experience higher rates of unemployment and lower average incomes, reducing their demand for or ability to maintain formal bank accounts.
• Higher Living Expenses: Increased costs related to medical care, housing adaptations, and assistive devices leave less disposable income for traditional banking fees or savings.
• Alternative Financial Services: Lower income and reduced bank availability drive reliance on costly non-bank alternatives like payday loans and check cashers.
⚠️ Credit Health and Scoring Barriers
• Lower Credit Scores: Individuals with disabilities report worse credit scores across all income levels due to historical financial exclusion or medical debt distress.
• Stricter Bank Lending Criteria: Traditional underwriting models penalize irregular income patterns common among disability benefit recipients, forcing them into alternative credit.
🏛️ Physical and Infrastructural Obstacles
• Inaccessible Branch Locations: Over half of disabled customers report physical difficulties traveling to traditional brick-and-mortar bank branches.
• Unusable ATMs: A large percentage of users encounter physical or interface barriers when attempting to use standard cash machines.
💻 Digital and Technological Divides
• Inaccessible Digital Interfaces: Websites, mobile apps, and security authentication features (such as visual CAPTCHAs) often fail to comply with universal design or accessibility standards.
• Communication Hurdles: Many customers experience confusion or lack of support when dealing with phone-based customer service or automated voice assistants.
📋 Institutional and Cultural Deficits
• Inadequate Staff Training: Frontline bank personnel frequently lack training in inclusive policies, empathy, or assistive communication.
• Lack of Product Awareness: Programs designed to help, such as specialized ABLE Accounts, suffer from widespread under-notification and outreach gaps.
