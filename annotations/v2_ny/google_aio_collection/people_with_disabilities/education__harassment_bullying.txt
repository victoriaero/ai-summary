Students with disabilities face a significantly higher risk of being bullied or harassed in educational settings due to perceived differences, vulnerability, and communication challenges.
According to data from the U.S. Department of Education and public health guidance compiled by agencies like StopBullying.gov, several core factors influence this increased risk:
Key Influencing Factors
• Perceived Differences and Vulnerability: Students with physical, sensory, developmental, or intellectual disabilities are often viewed as different or less powerful physically, intellectually, or emotionally, making them primary targets for peers seeking control.
• Social and Communication Challenges: Many affected students struggle to interpret social cues or express themselves clearly, which can lead to unintentional social missteps or an inability to effectively signal or defend against peer aggression.
• Behavioral and Emotional Difficulties: Research indicates that students with conditions like autism spectrum disorder (ASD), attention-deficit/hyperactivity disorder (ADHD), or emotional disturbances experience higher rates of victimization because behavioral challenges can complicate peer interactions.
• Inappropriate Prevention Programs: General school-wide anti-bullying programs often fail to accommodate different intellectual or reading levels, leaving vulnerable students unable to fully grasp or utilize the taught coping strategies.
• Systemic and Environmental Gaps: Inadequate staff training, failure by educators to recognize subtle harassment, or dismissing cruel behavior as "kids being kids" creates an unmonitored environment where bullying can thrive.
