Poverty and income barriers are primary drivers of housing instability, as people with disabilities experience poverty at roughly double the rate of nondisabled individuals.
Key factors influencing homelessness among people with disabilities include:
• Inadequate Financial Benefits: Federal disability payments and supplemental security income (such as SSI) frequently fall short of covering market-rate rent, while strict asset limits and earnings caps restrict financial cushioning.
• Employment and Wage Disparities: Disabled adults face severe workplace barriers, higher rates of unemployment, and federal policies that legally permit subminimum wages for certain workers with disabilities.
• Acute Shortage of Accessible Housing: Less than 5% of housing stock is physically accessible for people with mobility needs, and universal design principles are frequently overlooked by builders and landlords.
• Housing Discrimination: Landlords and housing providers frequently engage in direct or indirect discrimination, such as denying accommodation modification requests, complicating rental processes, or acting on negative stereotypes.
• Unmanaged Health and Support Needs: A lack of community-based care, personal care assistants, or mental health and behavioral support makes it difficult for individuals to sustain independent living.
• Inaccessible Emergency Shelters: Traditional homeless shelters often lack adaptive bathrooms, wider entryways, or trauma-informed quiet spaces, leaving disabled individuals unsafe or turned away.
• Violence and Abuse: Individuals with disabilities—particularly women—face a higher risk of losing housing due to interpersonal violence or abusive domestic environments.
Detailed insights and policy breakdowns can be reviewed via the Center for American Progress and resources from the Administration for Community Living.
