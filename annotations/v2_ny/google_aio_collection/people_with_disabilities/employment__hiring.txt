Hiring decisions for people with disabilities are primarily influenced by employer attitudes, workplace accessibility, and company knowledge regarding legal frameworks and accommodations.
Employer Attitudes and Perceptions
• Productivity beliefs: Many hiring managers hold false expectations that workers with disabilities are less productive. Data shows most employees perform equally well or better than peers.
• Cost assumptions: Employers often fear high costs for workplace changes. Research from TransCen Inc. shows that over half of workplace accommodations cost nothing, and others average around $300.
• Stigma and bias: Negative stereotypes and social stigma from leadership or staff create hidden barriers to entry.
Company Knowledge and Resources
• Legal awareness: Understanding rules like the Americans with Disabilities Act (ADA) improves hiring rates.
• Agency partnerships: According to the vrtac-qe Employer Practices Factsheet, companies that partner with local disability service agencies hire at much higher rates.
• Financial incentives: Tax credits and financial grants often sway smaller businesses to open roles for applicants with disabilities.
Workplace Accessibility
• Physical access: Missing ramps, narrow doors, or poor layouts stop candidates from applying or succeeding.
• Digital tools: Inaccessible software, uncaptioned videos, and poor communication tech block full participation.
• Flexible options: Remote work and flexible schedules make it easier to hire and retain disabled workers.
