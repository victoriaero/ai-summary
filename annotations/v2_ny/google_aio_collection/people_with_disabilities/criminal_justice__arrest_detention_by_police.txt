Arrest and detention rates for people with disabilities are driven primarily by officers misinterpreting disability-related behaviors as non-compliance and a widespread lack of specialized police training.
Key Factors Influencing Arrest and Detention
• Invisible Disabilities: Conditions like autism, mild intellectual and developmental disabilities (IDD), or traumatic brain injuries are often hidden. Because officers do not see a physical sign, they fail to recognize the disability.
• Misread Behaviors: Neurological responses—such as delayed reactions, repetitive movements ("stimming"), or sensory overload—are frequently mistaken for physical resistance or active defiance.
• Lack of De-escalation Training: Many law enforcement officers do not receive adequate training to safely identify or communicate with individuals experiencing a mental health or cognitive crisis.
• Racial and Intersectionality Disparities: The risk of arrest increases sharply when disability intersects with race. Studies show that Black individuals with disabilities face a significantly higher cumulative arrest rate by age 28 (55%) compared to white individuals with disabilities (40%).
• Reliance on Police for Crises: Communities often dispatch armed police officers as first responders to mental health or behavioral health calls instead of dispatching trained medical or social service professionals.
