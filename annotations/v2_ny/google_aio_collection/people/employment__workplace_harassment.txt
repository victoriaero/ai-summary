Organizational conditions and environmental risk factors—such as power disparities, isolated workspaces, and a lack of diversity—are the primary influences on workplace harassment.
According to guidance from the U.S. Equal Employment Opportunity Commission (EEOC) and workplace safety studies, several key environmental and organizational factors increase the risk of harassment:
Organizational and Environmental Risk Factors
• Power Disparities: Environments where supervisors or high-value employees hold significant authority over vulnerable workers create fear of retaliation or inaction.
• Customer Service Roles: Jobs where employee pay or retention depends on client satisfaction can lead workers to tolerate inappropriate or harassing behavior from customers.
• Isolated Workplaces: Remote sites, night shifts, or solitary work arrangements reduce the presence of witnesses and give harassers easier access.
• Workplace Culture Tolerating Alcohol: Social events or work environments that encourage drinking can impair judgment and lower social inhibitions.
• Monotonous or Low-Intensity Work: Repetitive or boring tasks leave employees with idle time, which can foster bullying or frustration-venting.
• Homogenous or Segregated Workforces: Workplaces lacking diversity can foster unchallenged stereotypes, while poorly integrated diverse groups can suffer from cultural or language tensions.
• Young or Inexperienced Workforces: Younger employees may be more vulnerable or naive about what constitutes acceptable conduct.
• Decentralized Workplaces: Locations with limited communication or oversight between management tiers can allow local misconduct to go unchecked.
