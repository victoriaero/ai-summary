A wide range of personal, financial, and organizational factors influence whether people receive proper preventive care.
Financial and Insurance Factors
• Health insurance coverage: People with insurance or no-cost preventive options get checkups much more often.
• Cost and copays: Even tiny fees or out-of-pocket costs stop many lower-income patients from seeking care.
Access and System Factors
• Primary care availability: Having a regular primary care provider (PCP) improves screening and vaccination rates.
• Location and travel: Living far from clinics, lacking transport, or facing poor neighborhood conditions reduces use.
• Continuity of care: Seeing the same doctor regularly builds trust and leads to better long-term prevention habits.
Personal and Social Factors
• Demographics and background: Age, gender, ethnicity, and housing stability affect health priorities and access.
• Health literacy and awareness: Knowing which screenings or vaccines are needed makes people more likely to ask for them.
• Social support: Family encouragement and community norms strongly shape attitudes toward healthy habits.
