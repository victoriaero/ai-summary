Police use of force is primarily influenced by suspect behavior, legal standards, and situational or demographic factors.
Suspect Behavior and Threat Level
• Suspect Resistance: Active or aggressive resistance by a suspect is the strongest predictor and primary factor increasing the likelihood of police use of force.
• Immediacy of Threat: The perceived or real danger to the safety of officers or the public dictates the level of force applied.
• Severity of Crime: Higher-severity offenses or violent crimes often elevate the potential for escalated police intervention.
• Flight or Evasion: Attempting to run or evade arrest influences whether officers pursue or deploy physical restraint.
Legal and Departmental Guidelines
• Objective Reasonableness: Under the legal standard established by Graham v. Connor, actions are judged based on what a reasonable officer would do in the same split-second situation.
• Force Continuum: Departments train officers on a graded range of options—from verbal commands and empty-hand control to less-lethal tools and lethal force.
• Department Policies: Specific internal rules and de-escalation protocols shape how much force is authorized in a given jurisdiction.
Suspect Demographics and Health Status
• Mental Illness or Impairment: Suspects showing signs of mental illness or substance impairment experience higher rates of force due to perceived risks or communication barriers.
• Demographic Disparities: Research notes that people of color and individuals at the intersection of marginalized identities (such as low income or mental illness) encounter higher rates of force.
