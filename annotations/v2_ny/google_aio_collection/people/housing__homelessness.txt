Homelessness for people currently in housing is primarily influenced by severe economic pressure, housing cost burdens, and sudden life crises.
Economic and Housing Factors
• Severe Rent Burden: Households spending more than 30% to 50% of their income on rent are at high risk of losing their housing when costs rise unexpectedly.
• Income Shocks: Sudden job loss, reduced work hours, or stagnant wages make it impossible to keep up with basic monthly expenses.
• Lack of Savings: Widespread poverty means many families are a single paycheck, emergency, or unexpected bill away from eviction.
Personal and Crisis Factors
• Domestic Violence: Fleeing an abusive household often forces individuals—especially women and children—to leave immediately with no financial backup plan.
• Health and Medical Debt: A major illness or disability can drain savings, cause job loss, and lead to medical-debt-induced eviction.
• Untreated Behavioral Health: Severe mental illness or substance use disorders, combined with a lack of community support, can erode a person's ability to maintain stable tenancy.
Systemic and Institutional Barriers
• Eviction and Housing History: A past eviction or involvement with the criminal justice system creates major screening hurdles when trying to secure or retain safe rental housing.
• System Failures: Inadequate discharge planning when leaving foster care, hospitals, or correctional facilities leaves vulnerable individuals without a bridge to stable housing.
• Discrimination: Systemic racism, racial disparities, and discrimination based on sexual orientation or gender identity restrict fair access to stable jobs and housing options.
