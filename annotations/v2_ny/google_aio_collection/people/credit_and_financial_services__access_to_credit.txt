Access to credit is directly influenced by individual credit scoring metrics, socioeconomic background, systemic geographic factors, and institutional barriers. [1, 2, 3] 
## 📊 Key Credit Score Factors (FICO® / VantageScore)

* Payment History (35%):
* On-time payments build higher scores.
   * Late or missed payments severely lower eligibility.
* Amounts Owed / Utilization (30%):
* Lower revolving debt ratios improve approval odds.
   * Maxed-out limits signal high financial risk.
* Length of Credit History (15%):
* Older accounts establish reliable borrower track records.
* Credit Mix (10%):
* Managing multiple credit types (cards, installment loans) helps.
* New Credit Inquiries (10%):
* Frequent new applications temporarily depress scores.

## ⚠️ Socioeconomic & Systemic Drivers

* Childhood Environment:
* Upward mobility and hometown resources heavily shape long-term borrowing behavior. [2, 4] 
* Income Visibilities:
* Lower-income consumers often enter the credit system via debt collections rather than proactive credit cards. [5] 
* Alternative Financial Reliance:
* Reliance on non-reporting services (payday loans) fails to build traditional prime credit history. [6] 

## 💡 Institutional & Behavioral Barriers

* Financial Literacy:
* Gaps in understanding how to interact with regulated banks restrict access. [1] 
* Burdensome Requirements:
* Complex paperwork, technical language, and documentation hurdles deter low- and moderate-income applicants. [1] 
* Geographic Disparities:
* Branch closures and historical redlining reduce mainstream regulated credit availability in specific communities. [6]
