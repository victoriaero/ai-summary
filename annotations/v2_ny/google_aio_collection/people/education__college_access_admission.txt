College access and admission depend heavily on a student's academic record, family income, and guidance support from school staff.
Academic Performance
• Grades: High school grades in college prep classes are the top factor for college admission.
• Curriculum: Taking hard classes, like advanced placement courses, shows readiness for college work.
• Test Scores: Standardized tests like the SAT or ACT matter less now because many schools are test-optional, but high scores still help.
Financial and Wealth Factors
• Cost and Aid: High tuition prices and lack of financial aid stop many students from enrolling. You can read more about these hurdles in the NASFAA Report.
• Family Wealth: Wealthy students buy test prep, private coaching, and help with essays. Poorer students often lack these paid tools.
Counseling and Support
• School Counselors: Good counseling helps students finish financial aid forms and college applications.
• Counselor Shortages: Many public schools have too many students per counselor. This leaves little time for college planning.
• Mentor Roles: Teachers and adult guides play a major part in encouraging students to apply to college. Findings on this are detailed in the Determining Factors Study.
Equity and Background
• Demographics: Black and Latinx students face more barriers and enroll at lower rates than Asian and white peers.
• Systemic Gaps: Cultural stereotypes and unfair resource splits hurt minority and low-income student readiness. Insights on these systemic hurdles are outlined in the NYU Steinhardt Review.
