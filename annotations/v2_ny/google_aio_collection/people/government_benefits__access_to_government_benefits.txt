Access to government benefits is heavily influenced by administrative barriers, eligibility structures, and individual or regional factors. [1, 2, 3] 
## 📋 Administrative and Compliance Costs

* Paperwork volume: Extensive documentation requirements delay or deter applications.
* In-person hurdles: Mandatory office visits and interviews reduce participation. [1] 
* Recertification cycles: Frequent renewal rules cause accidental benefit loss. [1] 
* System integration: Siloed agency databases prevent automatic enrollment across programs. [4] 

## 📉 Financial and Policy Thresholds

* Income caps: Strict earning limits create abrupt cutoff points. [5] 
* Benefits cliffs: Small wage increases can trigger total benefit loss. [5] 
* Asset limits: Restrictions on personal savings or vehicle ownership block eligibility. [5] 
* Work mandates: Required hourly employment tracking limits access for caregivers. [5] 

## 🧠 Psychological and Social Factors

* Social stigma: Perceived demeaning attitudes deter potential applicants.
* Information gaps: Difficulty learning about available programs limits uptake.
* Language barriers: Non-English speakers face navigation challenges.
* Digital exclusion: Poor internet access restricts online application use. [2, 3, 6, 7]
