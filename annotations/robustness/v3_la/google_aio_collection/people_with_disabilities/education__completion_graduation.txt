Multiple individual, systemic, and environmental factors influence high school and college graduation rates for students with disabilities.
Key Risk Factors and Barriers
• Academic Disengagement: Chronic absenteeism, course failures, and low ninth-grade performance strongly predict dropping out.
• Disciplinary Exclusion: High rates of suspensions and punitive discipline lower graduation likelihood.
• Low Expectations: Deficit mindsets and unrealistically low expectations from educators and caregivers hinder student achievement.
• Bullying and Isolation: Social disengagement, bullying, and lack of integration with non-disabled peers impede success.
• Systemic Hurdles: Inadequate teacher training, poor transition support, and abrupt shifts from high school legal frameworks (like IEPs) to voluntary college disclosure rules create major roadblocks.
Factors Supporting Completion and Success
• Inclusive Practices: Mainstreaming and spending time with typically developing peers increase graduation rates.
• Early Intervention: Tracking attendance, behavior, and grades via Multi-Tiered Systems of Support (MTSS) allows for timely help.
• Mentoring and Relationships: Strong connections with faculty, staff, or community mentors significantly boost retention and degree completion.
• Family Involvement: Active parental support and high caregiver expectations correlate with better attendance and higher grades.
• Holistic Accommodations: Tailored academic adjustments, assistive technology, and clear postsecondary support services help students persist through graduation.
