Credit scores for people with disabilities are influenced by standard financial factors like payment history and credit utilization, but systemic socio-economic barriers shape their overall credit health. [1] 
## 📊 Standard Credit Scoring Factors
Credit scoring models (like FICO and VantageScore) do not look at disability status or public assistance benefits. They rely entirely on your credit report data: [2] 

* Payment history (35%): Paying bills and loans on time. [3] 
* Credit utilization (30%): Keeping credit card balances below 30% of your limit. [3] 
* Length of credit history (15%): How long your accounts have been open. [3] 
* New credit and inquiries (10%): Applying for new loans or cards. [2] 
* Credit mix (10%): Having a mix of cards and installment loans. [4] 

## ⚠️ Systemic and Disability-Related Challenges
While the scoring math is neutral, people with disabilities often face distinct structural hurdles:

* Income Constraints: Lower rates of employment or reliance on fixed incomes (like SSI or SSDI) can lead to "thin" credit files or lower borrowing limits. [1, 2] 
* Medical Debt: High out-of-pocket medical and care expenses can cause missed payments that drop credit scores.
* Alternative Financial Services: Over-reliance on non-bank services (check cashers, payday loans) fails to build positive credit history. [1, 5] 
* Episodic Impairments: Active phases of certain psychosocial or cognitive conditions can occasionally lead to missed payments or poor financial choices. [6] 

## 💡 Credit Building Strategies

* Benefit Exclusions: Federal benefits like SSI and SSDI do not count as negative or positive data—they are not factored into scores at all.
* Alternative Reporting: Use tools like Experian Boost or rent-reporting services to get credit for utility and phone bills.
* Secured Cards: Open a secured credit card or a credit-builder loan to establish positive payment history safely. [1, 2, 3, 7]
