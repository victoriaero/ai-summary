Administrative burden for men seeking government benefits is primarily driven by three cost categories: learning, compliance, and psychological costs. [1] 
## Key Cost Factors

* Learning Costs: Men face challenges finding clear information about eligibility rules and program existence, especially in complex safety-net programs like unemployment insurance or veterans' assistance. [2] 
* Compliance Costs: Often called a "time tax," these include the paperwork, document gathering, phone wait times, and recurring recertifications required to maintain benefits. Work requirements and mandatory check-ins add heavy compliance hurdles. [2, 3, 4] 
* Psychological Costs: Social stigma and traditional masculine norms around self-reliance can create acute stress, embarrassment, or a perceived loss of autonomy when asking the government for financial assistance.

## Structural and Policy Influences

* Program Fragmentation: Shared control across federal, state, and local levels creates varied rules, making navigation confusing and inconsistent. [4] 
* Bureaucratic Design: Onerous verification tasks, redundant forms, and a lack of personalized assistance amplify the difficulty of completing applications. [5, 6, 7] 
* Intersection with Status: Factors like low income, minority status, or veteran/parolee social constructions alter how street-level bureaucrats treat applicants and how severe the administrative hurdles feel. [8]
