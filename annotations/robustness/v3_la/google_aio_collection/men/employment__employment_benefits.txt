Several key personal, occupational, and cultural factors influence the employment benefits and priorities for men in the workplace: [1, 2, 3] 

* Life Stage and Family Status: Millennial and younger male workers, particularly fathers, place a high value on family-forming benefits, paternity leave, and childcare support compared to non-parents. [1, 4] 
* Occupational Segregation and Industry: Men are statistically more likely to work in male-dominated, physically demanding, or high-status industries, which shape the availability and necessity of specific risk, health, and wellness benefits. [3, 5] 
* Evolving Health Needs: Awareness around holistic well-being has shifted benefit designs; men increasingly seek mental health support (such as EAPs and therapy), preventative care, and reproductive health options like paternity leave and fertility coaching. [2] 
* Financial and Career Priorities: Research indicates that men often prioritize direct compensation, earnings growth, retirement security, and professional upskilling when evaluating total rewards packages. [6, 7] 
* Workplace Culture and Inclusion: Inclusive corporate cultures directly impact how comfortably men can access flexible work arrangements, mental health resources, and paternal leave without facing traditional masculine stigma. [8, 9]
