Graduation and completion rates for Black students in education are heavily shaped by systemic opportunity gaps, socioeconomic status, pre-college academic preparation, and institutional support systems, according to the [U.S. Department of the Treasury](https://home.treasury.gov/news/featured-stories/post-5-racial-differences-in-educational-experiences-and-attainment) and [Equity in Higher Education](https://www.equityinhighered.org/resources/ideas-and-insights/segregation-in-higher-education-and-unequal-paths-to-college-completion-implications-for-policy-and-research/). [1, 2] 
## Socioeconomic and Financial Factors
Financial instability and debt create major hurdles for student persistence.

* Family Wealth Gaps: Unequal parental wealth linked to housing markets limits financial safety nets.
* Debt Burdens: Prohibitive student loan debt impacts Black students at higher rates, forcing many to work off-campus.
* Work and Family Responsibilities: Over one-third of Black bachelor's students manage heavy work or family duties, increasing the risk of stopping out. [1, 3, 4, 5] 

## K-12 and Pre-College Preparation
Early educational foundations strongly predict ultimate graduation success. [2] 

* School Segregation and Funding: Documented by the [Southern Education Foundation](https://southerneducation.org/wp-content/uploads/final-miles-to-go-report-2024_r15-1.pdf), underfunded schools and racial segregation restrict access to advanced coursework like Advanced Placement (AP) classes.
* Discipline and Tracking: Harsh disciplinary practices and systemic tracking lower academic momentum early on.
* Academic Preparation: Pre-college test scores and study habits heavily account for later college completion gaps. [1, 2, 4, 6] 

## Institutional Climate and Support
The day-to-day environment on campus or in school influences whether students stay or leave.

* Discrimination and Microaggressions: Research highlighted by [EBSCOhost](https://www.ebsco.com/research-starters/ethnic-and-cultural-studies/impact-race-and-ethnicity-high-school-graduation/) shows that campus microaggressions and racial discrimination lower academic readiness and persistence. Roughly 20% to 33% of Black postsecondary students report frequent discrimination. [5, 7] 
* Faculty Diversity: Low numbers of minority faculty can depress academic expectations and reduce mentorship opportunities. Same-race teachers and mentors positively correlate with higher graduation rates. [1, 2] 

## Gender Gaps
Outcomes vary significantly by gender within the Black student population.

* The Gender Gap: According to [Brookings](https://www.brookings.edu/articles/racial-disparities-in-the-high-school-graduation-gender-gap/), gender graduation gaps are largest among Black students (a 9 percentage point difference).
* Black Men in Higher Education: Data from the National Student Clearinghouse shows Black men face the lowest completion rates and highest stop-out rates across multiple college sectors compared to their female peers. [8, 9]
