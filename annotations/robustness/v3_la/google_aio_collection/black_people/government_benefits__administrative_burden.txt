Black people experience administrative burdens in government benefit programs influenced by bureaucratic discrimination, systemic policy designs, and organizational practices. [1] 
Research from social policy and public administration studies (such as works published in the [Journal of Public Administration Research and Theory](https://academic.oup.com/jpart/article/33/1/139/6517284)) identifies several key factors shaping these experiences:
## Bureaucratic Discrimination and Interpersonal Treatment

* Staff Interactions: Studies on public benefit programs like SNAP and Medicaid show that Black adults are significantly more likely (roughly 40% higher) to report feeling racially or ethnically discriminated against by program staff compared to White adults. [2] 
* Psychological Costs: Negative, biased, or punitive treatment by agency workers increases the psychological toll of navigating the system, adding a distinct barrier to retention and enrollment. [1, 3] 

## Racialized Organization and Policy Design

* Bureaucratic Discretion: High levels of staff discretion allow formal rules to be decoupled from actual practice, facilitating the racialized administration of benefits. [4] 
* Punitive Rules: Historical policy choices and "deservingness" tropes often build more burdensome, restrictive requirements (such as intensive verification or work reporting rules) into means-tested programs that disproportionately serve marginalized racial groups. [4, 5] 
* Racial Resentment: Public opinion and political science research (such as analyses by Simon Haeder and Donald Moynihan) demonstrate that higher levels of racial resentment among portions of the public and policymakers correlate with sustained support for more punitive and complex administrative procedures. [5, 6] 

## Resource and Structural Inequities

* Reliance on Means-Tested Aid: Because of broader economic disparities, Black families experience higher rates of job loss and economic insecurity, leading to higher reliance on means-tested programs (like TANF and Unemployment Insurance) that frequently entail higher compliance and learning hurdles. [4, 7, 8] 
* Unequal Access to Resources: Factors like access to reliable digital tools, transportation for in-person appointments, and flexible work schedules interact with rigid bureaucratic demands, amplifying compliance costs for minority and low-income applicants. [9]
