AI OVERVIEW TEXT
============================================================

Neighborhood quality for Black Americans is shaped by a mix of historical public policies, ongoing market discrimination, and unequal access to essential community resources. [1, 2] 
According to research from organizations like the [Urban Institute](https://www.urban.org/racial-equity-analytics-lab/structural-racism-explainer-collection/causes-and-consequences-separate-and-unequal-neighborhoods) and [Brookings](https://www.brookings.edu/articles/homeownership-racial-segregation-and-policies-for-racial-wealth-equity/), several key factors drive these disparities in housing and neighborhood quality:
## Historical Policies and Disinvestment

* Redlining: Past government maps marked Black neighborhoods as "hazardous" for investment, cutting off loans and funding.
* Restrictive Covenants: Past rules banned Black families from buying homes in certain neighborhoods.
* Urban Renewal: Past highway and building projects often destroyed Black neighborhoods and businesses.
* Legacy of Disinvestment: These past choices left many Black communities with aging infrastructure and fewer public funds. [3, 4, 5, 6] 

## Real Estate and Lending Discrimination

* Appraisal Bias: The [U.S. Department of the Treasury](https://home.treasury.gov/news/featured-stories/racial-differences-in-economic-security-housing) notes that homes in majority-Black neighborhoods are often undervalued compared to similar homes in white neighborhoods. [4, 7] 
* Mortgage Hurdles: Black homebuyers face higher rates of mortgage denials and higher costs for loans. [4] 
* Racial Steering: Real estate agents sometimes push Black buyers away from predominantly white areas and toward segregated neighborhoods. [2, 8] 

## Resource and Socioeconomic Access

* Quality Schools: Lower property tax bases and unequal funding result in poorly resourced schools in many Black neighborhoods.
* Community Services: Neighborhoods with high poverty rates often lack full-service grocery stores, safe parks, and reliable public transit.
* Job Proximity: Segregation limits easy access to stable, well-paying employment centers. [1, 2, 7] 

## Environmental and Physical Conditions

* Environmental Hazards: As reported by [Opportunity Starts at Home](https://www.opportunityhome.org/resources/racial-equity-housing/), zoning laws historically allowed toxic industries near Black residential areas.
* Substandard Housing: Black households experience higher rates of indoor hazards like lead paint, mold, and poor air quality. [3]
