Socioeconomic background, academic preparation, and family structure are primary factors influencing educational completion and graduation rates for non-Latino groups (such as White, Asian, Black, and Indigenous students).
## Key Factors Influencing Graduation and Completion

* Socioeconomic Status and Affordability
* Family income and household wealth strongly predict college persistence and completion.
   * Financial constraints, debt aversion, and the rising cost of tuition relative to federal aid like Pell Grants force many lower-income students to stop out or work long hours while enrolled. [1, 2, 3] 
* Academic Preparation and Admissions Metrics
* High school grade point averages (GPAs) and standardized test scores serve as major predictors of bachelor's degree completion.
   * Controlling for academic qualifications in high school significantly narrows racial completion disparities across demographic groups. [4] 
* Family Structure and Support
* Living with married birth parents during high school substantially increases graduation odds and overall college completion rates.
   * First-generation college status—frequently tracked across various non-Latino minority subgroups—introduces hurdles in navigating institutional bureaucracies and financial aid. [4, 5] 
* Institutional Type and Resources
* Enrollment in well-resourced four-year public or private non-profit institutions correlates with higher completion rates compared to under-resourced community colleges, for-profit entities, or institutions with high student-faculty ratios. [6, 7] 
* Gender and Enrollment Patterns
* Females consistently graduate at higher rates than males across most racial and ethnic demographics.
   * Part-time enrollment status and heavy work commitments sharply increase stop-out and dropout risks. [1, 3, 4, 7]
