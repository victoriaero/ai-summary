## Key Factors Influencing Police-Initiated Contact

* Neighborhood and Community Context: Criminological research, including minority threat and conflict theories, indicates that economically disadvantaged neighborhoods and areas with high social vulnerability experience heavier police deployment and higher rates of proactive, police-initiated stops. * Individual Demographics: Studies analyzing data like the Police-Public Contact Survey (PPCS) show that Age and Gender significantly affect police interaction rates. Younger individuals and men face higher odds of police-initiated contact and subsequent perceptions of procedural friction. [3] 
* Socioeconomic Status: Lower household income levels strongly correlate with an increased likelihood of being approached or stopped by law enforcement. [3] 
* Social and Familial Environment: Research tracking early adolescence and criminal justice contact demonstrates that an individual's immediate social circle—such as having family members or peers involved in the criminal legal system, or experiencing prior school disciplinary actions—substantially raises the probability of a police-initiated encounter. [1]
