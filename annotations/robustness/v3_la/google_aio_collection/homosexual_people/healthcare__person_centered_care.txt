Person-centered care for homosexual and queer individuals is heavily influenced by provider communication, institutional environment, cultural humility, and the psychological impact of minority stress. [1, 2, 3] 
## Provider Communication and Empathy

* Affirming language: Using inclusive terminology and avoiding heteronormative assumptions about a patient’s partners or lifestyle builds immediate trust. [2, 4] 
* Discretion and safety: Asking about sexual orientation only when clinically relevant and strictly protecting confidentiality prevents patient alienation. [5] 
* Active listening: Engaging in collaborative, open-ended conversations supports shared decision-making and respects patient choices. [6] 

## Institutional and Environmental Factors

* Inclusive spaces: Displaying non-discrimination policies, rainbow markers, or safe-space signage signals a secure environment. [2, 4] 
* Chosen family recognition: Allowing patients to define their support networks and make decisions regarding visitation empowers individuals during care transitions. [5, 7] 
* Staff training: Implementing routine, ongoing education on cultural humility and conduct standards ensures consistent, respectful treatment across all clinic or hospital staff. [2, 5] 

## Psychological and Social Dynamics

* Minority stress: Past or anticipated experiences of stigma, prejudice, or discrimination often cause patients to delay or avoid necessary medical care. [1] 
* Intersectionality: Factors like race, religion, age, and socioeconomic status intersect with sexual orientation, changing how a person perceives and experiences healthcare. [5, 7] 
* Social support: Strong community ties and comfort with identity disclosure increase the likelihood that a patient will openly share health concerns and seek preventative screenings. [8]
