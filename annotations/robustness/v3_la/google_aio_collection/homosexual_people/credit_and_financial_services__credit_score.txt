## 📊 Standard Credit Score Factors
Credit scoring models like FICO and VantageScore evaluate the exact same financial habits for everyone, regardless of identity: [2, 3] 

* Payment history (35% - 40%): Tracking on-time or late payments on loans and cards. [4, 5, 6] 
* Credit utilization (20% - 30%): The ratio of used credit versus total available limits. [5, 6] 
* Length of credit history (15% - 21%): The average age of open accounts. [5, 6] 
* Credit mix (10% - 21%): A combination of installment loans and revolving credit. [5, 6] 
* New credit (5% - 11%): Recent hard inquiries from new applications. [5, 6] 

## ⚠️ Indirect Systemic and Economic Factors
While algorithms do not use sexual orientation, external socioeconomic barriers heavily impact these underlying score components for LGBTQ+ people: [1, 7] 

* The LGBTQ+ Wage Gap: Earning roughly 90 cents per dollar compared to cisgender or heterosexual peers restricts cash flow, leading to higher credit utilization. [1, 7, 8] 
* Loss of Family Support: Younger individuals coming out may lose financial safety nets early, forcing reliance on credit cards for basic survival or medical needs. [1] 
* Higher Out-of-Pocket Costs: Disproportionate spending on healthcare, legal name/gender marker changes, and specialized family estate planning strains monthly budgets. [1, 9] 
* Administrative Disruptions: Legal name changes during transition processes can cause mismatch errors or reporting delays across major credit bureaus, temporarily lowering perceived credit stability. [1, 8]

* Utilize Alternative Data: Opt into services like Experian Boost to get credit for on-time utility and phone payments.
