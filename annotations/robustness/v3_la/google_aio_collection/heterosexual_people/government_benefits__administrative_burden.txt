Administrative burden in government benefits is primarily influenced by three core cost categories—learning, compliance, and psychological costs—which are affected by socioeconomic, structural, and personal factors rather than sexual orientation alone. [1] 
While research on administrative burden extensively examines intersections of race, gender, class, and age, the specific administrative hurdles faced by heterosexual individuals are driven by the same foundational factors that impact the general population. [2] 
------------------------------
## Key Types of Administrative Costs

* Learning Costs: The difficulty of knowing a program exists, understanding complex eligibility rules, and figuring out how to navigate the system. [1] 
* Compliance Costs: The time, effort, paperwork, and documentation required to apply for and maintain benefits. [3] 
* Psychological Costs: The stress, anxiety, loss of autonomy, and social stigma tied to interacting with government agencies. [1, 4] 

------------------------------
## Factors Influencing the Burden## 1. Socioeconomic Status and Class

* Income and Resources: Lower-income heterosexual individuals often experience higher relative compliance and learning costs due to stricter means-testing and documentation requirements in safety-net programs like SNAP or Medicaid. [1, 5] 
* Financial Buffers: Access to outside financial resources or paid assistance (such as private navigators or family help) reduces the friction of gathering documents and filing complex paperwork. [2, 6] 

## 2. Human Capital and Cognitive Resources

* Education and Literacy: Navigating bureaucratic forms requires high levels of literacy and digital literacy. Lower educational attainment increases the difficulty of understanding instructions and notices.
* Cognitive Load: Factors like scarcity, chronic stress, or time poverty deplete executive functioning, making it harder to complete multi-step bureaucratic tasks. [7] 

## 3. Age and Health Status

* Aging and Cognitive Decline: Older adults often face higher learning and compliance barriers, especially when digital-only applications replace in-person or paper options. [7, 8] 
* Physical and Mental Health: Disability, illness, or poor health reduces the physical and mental energy required to wait in lines, attend appointments, or re-certify benefits. [7, 8] 

## 4. Program Design and Digital Access

* System Complexity: Programs with frequent re-certification rules, ambiguous guidelines, and poor plain-language communication impose heavier burdens on all applicants.
* Digital vs. In-Person Barriers: A lack of reliable internet access, mobile-optimized portals, or accessible physical offices increases compliance friction. [1, 9]
