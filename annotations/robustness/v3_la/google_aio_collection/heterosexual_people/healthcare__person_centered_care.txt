Person-centered care (PCC) for heterosexual individuals in healthcare is shaped by interpersonal communication, provider attributes, and systemic environment factors.
## Key Interpersonal and Communication Factors

* Patient-Provider Communication: Clear, empathetic dialogue and active listening build trust and improve satisfaction. [1] 
* Provider Gender Preferences: Patients often prefer female providers for empathy and intimate examinations, and male providers for specific procedures like surgery. [1] 
* Shared Decision-Making: Actively involving patients in comparing clinical choices and treatment planning enhances engagement. [2, 3] 

## Systemic and Sociodemographic Influences

* Heteronormative Assumptions: Because heterosexual orientation aligns with default societal norms, care is rarely impeded by heterosexism, but providers must still avoid making rigid assumptions about individual family structures or sexual health needs. [4] 
* Insurance and Socioeconomic Status: Having health insurance and stable financial resources directly increases access and patient satisfaction. [1] 
* Consultation Costs: High out-of-pocket or consultation fees negatively affect the perception of care quality and overall satisfaction. [1] 

## Organizational and Environmental Elements

* Time Constraints: Limited appointment times restrict thorough dialogue and shared decision-making. [5] 
* Privacy and Confidentiality: Clear protocols and private spaces reinforce safety and trust during physical and sexual health assessments. [3]
