Person-centered care (PCC) is influenced by a combination of patient-specific, provider-level, and systemic organizational factors. [1] 
## Patient-Specific Factors

* Health Literacy and Education: A patient's ability to understand medical information affects their participation in shared decision-making. [1] 
* Demographics: Factors like age, gender, and multi-morbidity status shape individual care preferences and expectations. [1] 
* Psychological and Behavioral Traits: Personal motivation, emotional state, and clear personal goals dictate how actively a person engages as an equal partner in their care. [2] 

## Provider and Team Factors

* Communication Skills: Clear, empathetic communication from doctors and nurses builds trust and facilitates collaboration. [1, 3] 
* Empathy and Training: Formal training in collaborative care and high professional empathy directly improve the delivery of person-centered interactions. [1, 4] 
* Interdisciplinary Teamwork: Strong collaboration among multi-professional healthcare teams supports coordinated care delivery. [3, 4] 

## System and Environmental Factors

* Work Environment and Culture: Supportive nursing work environments, positive organizational cultures, and strong leadership replace rigid, paternalistic hierarchies. [4, 5] 
* Time Constraints: High work pressure and limited appointment times act as major barriers to meaningful engagement. [1, 3] 
* Infrastructure and Workflows: Integrated medical records, 24/7 access protocols, and chronic care management frameworks make continuous, coordinated care possible between regular visits. [6, 7]
