Administrative burden for people seeking government benefits is driven by three main types of costs: learning, compliance, and psychological costs, which are shaped by policy design and bureaucratic implementation. [1, 2] 
## Core Types of Costs

* Learning Costs: The effort and time required to find out that a program exists, understand eligibility rules, and figure out how to apply. [2, 3] 
* Compliance Costs: The "time taxes" and physical demands of providing documents, filling out complex forms, waiting on hold, or traveling to in-person interviews. [3] 
* Psychological Costs: The emotional toll, stress, loss of personal autonomy, and social stigma associated with proving need to the government. [3, 4] 

## Key Influencing Factors

* Complex Policy Design: Intricate rules, frequent re-certification requirements, and strict asset or work mandates increase paperwork and error rates. [5, 6] 
* Administrative Fragmentation: Overlapping programs across local, state, and federal levels lack data sharing, forcing applicants to submit the same information repeatedly. [1, 3] 
* Bureaucratic Implementation: Onerous verification practices, poor digital tools, opaque communication, and underfunded agencies create long wait times and processing backlogs. [5, 7] 
* Involvement of Third Parties: Delegating service delivery to private contractors or complex intermediaries can add extra layers of rules and friction. [1, 4] 
* Socioeconomic Disparities: Limited internet access, rigid hourly work schedules, lack of transportation, and lower baseline resources magnify the impact of these burdens on low-income and marginalized groups. [3, 5]
