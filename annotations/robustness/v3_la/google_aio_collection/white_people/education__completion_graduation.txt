Socioeconomic status, neighborhood poverty levels, and academic performance strongly influence educational completion and graduation rates for White students. [1, 2, 3] 
## Key Factors Influencing Graduation

* Family and Community Wealth: Parental socioeconomic status and living in affluent or middle-class neighborhoods significantly lower the risk of dropping out, while concentrated neighborhood poverty increases it. [1, 2] 
* Early Academic Proficiency: Third-grade reading skills act as a major predictor; White children from high-poverty communities who lack reading proficiency face higher non-graduation rates compared to those from wealthy settings. [1] 
* Family Structure: Living in a single-parent family or experiencing parental divorce can increase the risk of failing to finish high school on time. [2] 
* Enrollment Intensity and Performance: In higher education, maintaining a high cumulative grade point average (GPA) and enrolling full-time are the most vital elements driving degree completion. [3, 4]
