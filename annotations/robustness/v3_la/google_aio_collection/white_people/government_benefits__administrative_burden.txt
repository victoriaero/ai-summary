Administrative burdens in public benefit programs like SNAP and Medicaid are generally categorized into three types of costs: learning, compliance, and psychological costs. According to research on racial and ethnic differences in public benefits (such as findings from the Workforce Economic Inclusion and Mobility Survey), low-income White adults frequently report higher rates of learning and compliance burdens compared to some minority groups, even after controlling for demographic and economic factors. [1, 2, 3] 
## Key Factors Influencing Burdens for White Beneficiaries

* Learning Costs (System Complexity): Navigating eligibility rules, understanding program availability, and figuring out application procedures require substantial effort. Studies indicate that White applicants often report higher navigation and informational hurdles when trying to figure out program requirements. [1, 2] 
* Compliance Costs ("Time Taxes"): Fulfilling procedural requirements—such as submitting verification documents, completing renewals, and waiting in lines or on hold—creates tangible compliance challenges. Research shows White participants frequently report dealing with these operational hurdles at higher rates than certain minoritized groups in specific means-tested programs. [3, 4, 5] 
* Interactions with Agency Staff: While minority applicants (such as Black and Asian adults) report significantly higher rates of perceived racial or ethnic discrimination from program staff, White adults report lower rates of staff-inflicted discrimination interactions, which shapes the overall psychological and compliance experience. [2, 3] 
* Socioeconomic and Geographic Variables: Factors such as local agency resourcing, digital access, fluctuating income, and disability status heavily dictate the weight of compliance and learning barriers across all racial demographics. [6]
