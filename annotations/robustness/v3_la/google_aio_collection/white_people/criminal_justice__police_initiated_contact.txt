Police-initiated contact for White people is primarily driven by driving exposure and traffic infractions, neighborhood crime levels, and individual behavioral or situational factors. [1, 2] 
## Key Influencing Factors

* Traffic Violations and Driving Exposure: Traffic stops represent the most frequent type of police-initiated contact. Because White individuals make up a large share of the overall population and driving demographics, they account for a high absolute volume of traffic-related stops. [1, 3] 
* Neighborhood and Environmental Context: Local crime rates, community disadvantage, and geographic patrol allocation strongly influence the frequency of police encounters regardless of demographics. [2] 
* Behavioral and Situational Triggers: Observable infractions, suspicious behavior, or direct calls for service involving specific individuals prompt officer intervention.
* Age and Demographics: Younger adults (particularly ages 18 to 24) experience a disproportionately higher rate of police-initiated contact across all racial groups compared to older demographics. [3, 4]
