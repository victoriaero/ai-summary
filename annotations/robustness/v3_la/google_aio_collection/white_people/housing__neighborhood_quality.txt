Several socioeconomic, historical, and perceptual factors shape neighborhood quality and residential choices for White people in housing. Research in sociology and urban studies identifies the primary drivers as follows:
## Key Factors Influencing Neighborhood Quality and Choice

* Socioeconomic Proxies: According to urban research highlighted by the [Kinder Institute for Urban Research](https://kinder.rice.edu/urbanedge/residential-segregation-rewards-whites-while-punishing-people-color), White homebuyers and renters often prioritize low crime rates, high-performing schools, stable or appreciating property values, and well-maintained infrastructure. Sociologists note that these tangible community assets frequently function as "racial proxies," where neighborhood selection is heavily steered away from areas perceived to lack these traits. [1] 
* Racial Composition and Preferences: Experimental and demographic studies indicate that neighborhood racial makeup influences residential desirability. Research on neighborhood satisfaction shows that some White residents report lower satisfaction as the proportion of minority residents increases, a trend linked to both explicit and implicit biases or stereotyped assumptions about diverse areas. [2, 3, 4] 
* Social Networks and Real Estate Steering: Housing searches for White households often operate within predominantly White social, familial, and professional networks. Realtors, family recommendations, and localized peer groups frequently direct White buyers and renters toward majority-White neighborhoods, reinforcing patterns of segregation even when stated preferences include nominal desires for diversity. [1, 5] 
* Historical and Structural Advantages: Decades of legacy public policies—including redlining, discriminatory lending, and subsidized housing placement—historically concentrated resources, green spaces, and single-family zoning into predominantly White neighborhoods. These structural advantages result in White renters and buyers more readily accessing higher-quality housing units, lower crime environments, and higher property appreciation rates. [6, 7, 8, 9, 10, 11]
