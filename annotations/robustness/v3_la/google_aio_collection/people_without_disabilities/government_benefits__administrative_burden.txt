Administrative burden for people without disabilities accessing government benefits is driven by three core types of costs: learning, compliance, and psychological factors. [1, 2] 
## Types of Costs Influencing Burden

* Learning Costs: The time and effort needed to find out about program rules, eligibility requirements, and how to apply. Frequent changes in policy or moving across different state and local jurisdictions increase these costs. [1, 3] 
* Compliance Costs: The paperwork, documentation, travel, and appointments required to prove and maintain eligibility. Redundant forms and strict bureaucratic demands add to this strain. [1, 2, 4] 
* Psychological Costs: The stress, stigma, shame, and loss of autonomy felt when dealing with complex or unpopular public programs. [1, 2] 

## Key Socioeconomic and Institutional Factors

* Socioeconomic Status: Individuals with lower income or lower levels of education often face steeper hurdles, as they may have fewer resources or less digital access to navigate complex systems. [3, 5, 6] 
* Scarcity and Financial Insecurity: Being financially insecure forces applicants to spend significant daily energy on immediate survival tasks, increasing the emotional toll of bureaucratic demands. [5, 7] 
* Program Design and Infrastructure: Fragmented agency coordination, unfamiliar rules during crises, and confusing application portals amplify the burden. Conversely, policies that shift tasks to the state—such as auto-enrollment or pre-filled forms—reduce these barriers. [4, 8, 9]
