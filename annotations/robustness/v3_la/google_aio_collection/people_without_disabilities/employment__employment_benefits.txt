Employment benefits for workers without disabilities are primarily influenced by organizational size, industry sector, job role, and geographic location.
## 🏢 Company Size and Structure

* Large Enterprises: Typically offer comprehensive health insurance, retirement matching, and wellness packages due to economies of scale.
* Small Businesses: Often provide basic or scaled-down benefit options due to higher per-employee administrative and insurance costs.
* Corporate Philosophy: Driven by executive priorities regarding talent retention and competitive positioning in the labor market.

## 🏭 Industry and Occupation

* High-Demand Sectors: Tech, finance, and specialized engineering feature robust perks (stock options, remote work stipends, elite health plans) to attract top talent.
* Hourly or Retail Sectors: Frequently focus on mandated minimums or basic medical plans with limited supplementary perks.
* Unionization: Collective bargaining agreements heavily mandate precise pension tiers, health coverage standards, and paid leave policies.

## 🌍 Location and Legal Frameworks

* State and Federal Mandates: Requirements like the [Affordable Care Act](https://mi.db101.org/glossary.htm) establish baseline rules for large employers regarding health coverage offerings. [1] 
* Regional Labor Markets: Local costs of living and regional competition compel employers to sweeten benefit packages to reduce turnover.
