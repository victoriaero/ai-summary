Key factors that influence high school and college completion or graduation rates for students without disabilities include:
• Attendance and Early Milestones: Early attendance patterns (such as regular attendance in elementary school like 5th grade) and staying on track with attendance and course credits in key transition years (like 9th grade) strongly predict high school graduation according to research published in the Journal of School Nursing via PubMed.
• Racial, Ethnic, and Gender Identity: Disparities exist across demographic groups; research highlights that students identifying as Black, Hispanic, or Native American historically face lower graduation odds compared to White peers, though these gaps narrow when controlling for attendance and school engagement factors.
• Behavioral, Emotional, and Cognitive Engagement: A student's sense of belonging, connection, purpose, and active participation in school life directly lowers the risk of dropping out before completion.
• Pre-College Academic Preparation: For higher education, solid academic foundation and preparation prior to entering college significantly shape first-year performance, GPA, and eventual bachelor's degree completion as documented via PubMed Central.
• Socioeconomic and Family/Parental Factors: Family support, parental educational expectations, and stable housing or financial conditions play a major role in keeping students enrolled and progressing toward a degree.
