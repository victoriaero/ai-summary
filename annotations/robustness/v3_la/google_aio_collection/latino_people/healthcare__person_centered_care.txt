Person-centered care for Latino people is heavily influenced by communication barriers, core cultural values, institutional resources, and socioeconomic factors. [1, 2, 3] 
## Language and Communication

* Limited English Proficiency (LEP): Language hurdles hinder accurate medical history-taking and diagnosis without professional medical interpreters. [2, 4, 5] 
* Provider Fluency: Access to Spanish-speaking clinicians greatly improves patient satisfaction and trust. [1, 6, 7] 
* Rapport Building: Spending time on introductory pleasantries before diving into medical questions fosters better connection. [8] 

## Core Cultural Values

* Familismo: Decision-making often involves the broader family rather than just the individual patient. [2, 9] 
* Personalismo: Patients look for a warm, personal, and respectful approach rather than a purely transactional interaction. [5, 8, 9] 
* Respeto: Deference to authority and formal courtesy (such as avoiding first names unless invited) shape clinical dynamics. [5, 9] 
* Fatalismo and Traditional Beliefs: Belief that health outcomes are pre-determined or combined use of folk remedies alongside Western medicine can influence treatment adherence. [1, 2] 

## Institutional and Systemic Factors

* Insurance and Cost: High uninsured rates and out-of-pocket costs restrict regular access to preventative care. [1, 2, 7] 
* Documentation Status: Fear of immigration enforcement or lack of legal status creates severe medical mistrust and delays acute care seeking. [4, 10] 
* Clinic Environment: Availability of bilingual support staff, diverse workforces, and longer visit durations improve care quality. [1, 6]
