While credit scoring models (such as FICO and VantageScore) do not explicitly use race or ethnicity as a calculation factor, structural and socioeconomic factors heavily influence the credit scores of Latino consumers. [1] 
## 📊 Key Credit Scoring Factors
Standard credit scoring models rely on universal mechanics:

* Payment history (35%): Tracking whether bills are paid on time. [2] 
* Credit utilization (30%): The amount of credit used relative to total limits. [2] 
* Length of history (15%): How long credit accounts remain active. [2] 
* Credit mix (10%): Having a combination of revolving and installment accounts. [3] 
* New credit (10%): Recent inquiries or newly opened accounts. [3] 

## ⚠️ Structural Disparities and Root Causes
Although the formulas are race-neutral, broader economic factors create score gaps (such as a median score of 667 for Hispanic consumers compared to 727 for white consumers): [1, 4] 

* Credit invisibility: Roughly 12% to 15% of Latino consumers lack enough credit history to generate a traditional score. [5] 
* Alternative reliance: Greater dependence on alternative financial services or non-reporting lenders that do not build standard credit histories. [1] 
* Generational wealth: Historical wealth gaps reduce access to traditional prime-tier banking and affordable credit products. [6] 

## 💡 Actionable Steps to Build Credit

* Secured cards: Open a secured credit card backed by a cash deposit to establish baseline history.
* Rent reporting: Utilize services that report on-time rent and utility payments to credit bureaus.
* Credit unions: Partner with community-focused financial institutions that offer culturally tailored credit-building loans. [1, 4]
