Several interconnected socioeconomic, institutional, and cultural factors influence educational completion and graduation rates for Latino students. [1, 2] 
## Financial Pressures and Affordability

* Cost of attendance: Latino students are more likely to cite high expenses as a primary reason for leaving school without graduating. [3] 
* Balancing work and study: Many Latino learners operate as post-traditional students, frequently working while enrolled to support themselves or their families, which can extend time-to-degree or lead to stopping out. [2] 
* Financial aid navigation: As a high share of Latino undergraduates are first-generation college students, navigating complex financial aid systems often occurs without parental familiarity or guidance. [3, 4] 

## Institutional and Systemic Factors

* Type of institution: Latino students disproportionately enroll in open-access, two-year, or less-selective institutions where graduation rates and resource levels are lower compared to selective universities. [5, 6, 7] 
* Remedial coursework: Placement in remedial classes at community colleges can delay credit accumulation and hinder progression toward a degree or transfer. [7, 8] 
* Institutional support: Research from organizations like [Excelencia in Education](https://www.edexcelencia.org/latino-college-completion) shows that intentional practices at Hispanic-Serving Institutions (HSIs) and certified programs improve retention and completion outcomes. [4, 9] 

## Family and Background Influences

* First-generation status: Over half of Latino college students are first-generation, meaning they lack family precedent for navigating higher education systems.
* K-12 preparation: Disparities in early academic preparation, resource-limited local high schools, and systemic biases in the classroom can impact readiness for higher education. [4, 8, 10]
