Police-initiated contact for Latino individuals in the criminal justice system is influenced by factors such as racial profiling, geographic and neighborhood policing strategies, and local immigration enforcement collaboration. [1, 2] 
## Key Influencing Factors

* Racial Profiling and Biased Policing: Perceptions and data indicate that Latino individuals often experience proactive or officer-initiated stops due to subjective visual identification, perceived ethnicity, or biased neighborhood policing practices. [1, 2, 3] 
* Immigration Enforcement Partnerships: Collaborations between local law enforcement and federal agencies like Immigration and Customs Enforcement (ICE) increase the likelihood of police-initiated stops or inquiries during routine encounters. [1] 
* Neighborhood Demographics and Patrol Allocation: High-density Latino neighborhoods or areas with concentrated poverty frequently experience higher levels of saturation patrols, increasing the structural opportunity for police-initiated contacts.
* Language Barriers: Limited English Proficiency (LEP) can escalate misunderstandings or complicate communication during initial officer encounters, sometimes altering the trajectory of a police stop.
