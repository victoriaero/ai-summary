Administrative burden for cisgender people in government benefits is primarily driven by three cost categories—learning, compliance, and psychological costs—which are heavily shaped by intersecting factors like race, gender roles, socioeconomic status, and institutional design. [1, 2, 3] 
According to administrative burden frameworks by scholars Pamela Herd and Donald Moynihan, the public encounters structural hurdles that vary based on individual and group resources: [1, 4] 
## Key Cost Factors

* Learning Costs: The time, effort, and information required to find out if one is eligible for a program and how to navigate application procedures. Cisgender applicants with lower socioeconomic status or less social capital often experience higher learning barriers. [1, 5] 
* Compliance Costs ("Time Taxes"): The operational friction of proving eligibility, including gathering multi-agency documents, waiting on hold, or traveling to in-person interviews. These impose heavy financial penalties on hourly or low-wage workers. [1] 
* Psychological Costs: The emotional toll, stigma, loss of autonomy, and stress associated with interacting with government agencies. [1, 6] 

## Intersecting Influences

* Gender and Reproductive Labor: Research on gendered administrative burdens indicates that cisgender women shoulder a disproportionate share of compliance and psychological costs due to obligations tied to care labor and reproductive support systems. [3] 
* Race and Ethnicity: Studies on public benefits like SNAP and Medicaid show that administrative burdens are racialized. While minority applicants navigate specific community support networks that can alter learning costs, Black and Asian individuals report facing significantly higher rates of staff discrimination during program interactions. [2, 4, 5] 
* Socioeconomic and Human Capital: Access to digital infrastructure, transportation, stable housing, and higher education directly mitigates compliance burdens, whereas economic disadvantage magnifies every administrative hurdle. [4, 7]
