Cisgender student graduation and completion rates are primarily influenced by school belonging, teacher expectations, personal educational aspirations, and socioeconomic background. [1, 2, 3] 
General educational research looks at the broader student population—the vast majority of whom are cisgender—to identify the main drivers of academic success and completion:
## Key Psychological and Social Factors

* School Belonging: Feeling accepted and connected to a school community heavily supports academic achievement and persistence.
* Teacher Expectations: High confidence and positive performance expectations from educators directly increase student success rates.
* Educational Aspirations: Long-term goals and personal motivation strongly predict whether students transition successfully to college and finish degrees. [2, 3, 4] 

## Demographic and Gender Trends

* The Gender Gap in Completion: Women currently outperform men in college enrollment and completion. For example, 44% of women aged 25–29 have completed a bachelor’s degree compared to 35% of men, a gap influenced by differing early aspirations, behavioral norms, and social pressures. [3, 5, 6] 
* Intersection with Race and Class: Gender gaps in completion vary across racial and ethnic groups, being particularly wide among Black and Hispanic men, and heavily moderated by family socioeconomic status. [1, 5]
