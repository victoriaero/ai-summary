Police-initiated contact for women in the criminal justice system is heavily influenced by intersecting factors of race, socioeconomic crisis, and specific situational triggers like traffic and street stops. [1, 2] 
## Key Influencing Factors

* Racial and Demographic Disparities: Race heavily shapes the likelihood and nature of police-initiated contact. Data from the [Prison Policy Initiative](https://www.prisonpolicy.org/blog/2019/05/14/policingwomen/) shows that Black women face traffic stops at higher rates than white women and experience arrests during those stops at triple the rate. [1, 2] 
* Crises and Social Vulnerability: Situations such as domestic violence, behavioral health challenges, homelessness, and substance use frequently draw law enforcement intervention. While these crises drive police contact, they often indirectly lead to charges like drug possession or theft rather than resolving the root crisis. [3, 4] 
* Type of Involuntary Encounter: Police-initiated contact typically occurs through traffic stops, street stops, or the execution of arrest warrants. [5] 
* Perceived Legitimacy and Location: Neighborhood contexts and community norms regarding law enforcement shape how women experience and report these interactions. Marginalized women in low-income urban areas navigate a complex paradox between needing emergency help and fearing discriminatory or overzealous police responses. [2, 6]
