Gendered divisions of labor, intersectional identities, and psychological, learning, and compliance costs heavily influence the administrative burden women face when accessing [Administrative Burden: The Woman's Work](https://debtekawa.com/administrative-burden-the-womans-work/). [1, 2] 
## Core Dimensions of Administrative Burden
Administrative burden consists of three primary types of friction when citizens interact with government welfare or safety-net programs like Medicaid, SNAP, and WIC: [3, 4, 5] 

* Learning Costs: The effort required to find out that a program exists, understand eligibility guidelines, and figure out how to apply.
* Compliance Costs: The "time taxes" and resource demands of gathering documentation, filling out complex forms, waiting in lines or on hold, and attending interviews.
* Psychological Costs: The emotional toll, stress, anxiety, and social stigma associated with seeking public assistance. [5, 6, 7, 8] 

## Key Factors Influencing Burden for Women

* Gendered Division of Labor: Studies show that women absorb a disproportionate share of routine household management, health care coordination, and caregiving tasks. Government benefit compliance lands on top of this pre-existing domestic labor distribution. [1] 
* Intersectional Identities: A woman's experience is shaped by overlapping inequalities. Race, class, immigration status, age, ability, and criminal legal system contact create stratified and often severe hurdles in securing benefits like housing and health care. [2] 
* Human Capital and Scarcity: Access is mediated by cognitive resources and time flexibility. Women experiencing financial scarcity, lower baseline human capital, or single motherhood face intense trade-offs when trying to satisfy bureaucratic demands during working hours. [9, 10] 
* Program Design and Fragmentation: Complex rules, frequent recertification demands, lack of digital optimization, and poor agency coordination amplify the time and stress required to maintain vital safety-net supports. [3, 6, 11]
