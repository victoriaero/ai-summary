Neighborhood quality for women in housing is heavily influenced by a combination of safety, economic resources, social support, and physical infrastructure. [1, 2, 3] 
## Safety and Freedom from Disorder

* Crime and Violence: Low rates of crime and street violence directly reduce risks like intimate partner violence and make public spaces feel secure. [2, 3] 
* Social and Physical Disorder: The absence of vacant buildings, visible trash, graffiti, and public drug use helps women feel safer and less stressed. [3, 4] 

## Economic and Resource Factors

* Financial Affordability: Low housing cost burdens and affordable rents leave women with enough income for basic needs like food and healthcare, reducing severe financial strain. [1, 4, 5] 
* Collective Socioeconomic Resources: Neighborhoods with higher relative socioeconomic resources for women foster informal social control that protects women’s well-being and reduces safety risks. [2] 

## Social Environment and Support

* Social Capital and Trust: Strong ties, mutual aid, and trust among neighbors create a reliable local support network.
* Family and Demographic Vulnerability: Single mothers and women under 65 face higher risks of financial stress and housing instability, making local community support systems vital. [1, 6] 

## Physical Infrastructure and Amenities

* Public Services and Mobility: Reliable public transit, walkable sidewalks, and good street lighting allow for safe and easy daily travel.
* Green Spaces: Access to clean parks, recreational areas, and natural views supports mental health and daily well-being. [7, 8, 9]
