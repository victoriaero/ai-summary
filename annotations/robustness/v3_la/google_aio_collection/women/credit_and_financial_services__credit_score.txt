Credit scores are legally gender-neutral and do not directly use gender as a calculating factor, but systemic economic differences heavily influence the credit outcomes of women. [1] 
## 📅 Core Credit Factors (FICO Weighting)

* ✅ Payment History (35%): On-time bill payment record. [2, 3] 
* ⚠️ Amounts Owed / Credit Utilization (30%): Ratio of revolving debt to total limits. [2, 4] 
* 🔎 Length of Credit History (15%): Age of oldest and average accounts. [2, 3] 
* 📊 Credit Mix (10%): Variety of credit cards, installment loans, and mortgages. [2, 3] 
* 📌 New Credit (10%): Recent hard inquiries and newly opened accounts. [2, 3] 

## 💡 Systemic & Behavioral Influences for Women

* 📉 The Gender Pay Gap: Lower average earnings can restrict disposable income, forcing higher proportional reliance on revolving credit. [5, 6] 
* 📈 Credit Utilization Discrepancies: Wage disparities often result in higher credit utilization ratios if spending limits remain tight relative to debt carried. [5, 6] 
* 🗒 Credit Attribute Variances: Studies from the [Federal Reserve Note on Gender Differences](https://www.federalreserve.gov/econres/notes/feds-notes/gender-related-differences-in-credit-use-and-credit-scores-20180622.html) show women often manage more tradelines and longer histories, but utilization and income disparities drive historical score gaps. [7] 

## 📈 Action Plan to Optimize Scores

   1. Phase 1 (Immediate): Set up automatic minimum or full payments to protect [Payment History](https://www.experian.com/blogs/ask-experian/credit-education/score-basics/what-affects-your-credit-scores/).
   2. Phase 2 (Short-Term): Pay down revolving card balances to keep utilization below 30% (ideally under 10%).
   3. Phase 3 (Long-Term): Request credit limit increases without spending more to instantly lower your utilization percentage. [1, 4, 5, 8]
