Police-initiated contact and heightened scrutiny for transgender people are driven by systemic profiling, socioeconomic vulnerability, and law enforcement perceptions of gender non-conformity.
Key Influencing Factors
• Perceptions of Gender Non-Conformity: Law enforcement officers frequently subject individuals perceived as disrupting normative gender expectations to increased surveillance, stops based on reasonable suspicion rather than traffic violations, and higher rates of searches and arrests.
• Involvement in the Underground Economy: Engagement in survival sex work or the underground economy—often driven by housing discrimination and employment barriers—strongly predicts police interaction and vulnerability to profiling. Officers frequently use gender expression or perceived sex work as a pretext for stops.
• Housing Instability and Public Space Presence: High rates of homelessness and lack of safe shelter force many transgender individuals to spend significant time in public spaces, increasing visibility and exposure to " walking while trans" profiling and loitering enforcement.
• Intersectional Racial and Socioeconomic Disparities: Transgender people of color, particularly Black transgender individuals, face compounded risks of aggressive police contact, physical force, and mistreatment compared to their White counterparts.
• Over-Policed Neighborhoods: Living in or visiting economically disadvantaged, heavily policed geographic areas naturally elevates the baseline probability of police-initiated stops and surveillance.
