Person-centered care for transgender people is heavily influenced by provider education, systemic stigma, and inclusive facility practices. [1, 2, 3] 
## Provider Competence and Training

* Clinical Knowledge: Providers who receive targeted training on gender-affirming care and unique medical needs deliver more respectful and accurate treatment. [3, 4] 
* Implicit Bias: Negative provider attitudes, heteronormative beliefs, and lack of awareness often lead to postponed care, misgendering, or outright denial of services. [2, 4, 5] 
* Communication: Using inclusive language and allowing patients shared control over clinical procedures fosters trust. [4] 

## Systemic and Institutional Factors

* Electronic Health Records (EHR): Systems that incorporate self-reported gender identity, chosen names, pronouns, and anatomical inventories help clinicians provide tailored, timely, and responsive care. [6] 
* Environment and Policies: Welcoming physical spaces, non-discrimination policies, and insurance coverage for gender-affirming treatments reduce structural barriers. [3, 4, 7] 
* Peer Support and Community Collaboration: Health programs designed and delivered in partnership with transgender peers or community organizations improve health outcomes and trust. [8] 

## Intersectional and Social Determinants

* Multiple Marginalizations: Transgender people of color, disabled individuals, and youth often face compounded discrimination and higher rates of care refusal. [3, 9] 
* Socioeconomic Pressures: Financial strain, housing instability, and lack of public or private insurance limit regular access to primary and mental healthcare. [10, 11]
