Transgender individuals' credit scores are driven by standard credit metrics combined with unique administrative hurdles like legal name-change file fragmentation and economic disparities. [1, 2] 
## 📌 Standard Scoring Weights
Credit bureaus calculate scores using standard financial habits. [3] 

* Payment history
* On-time bill payments (35% impact) [4] 
* Amounts owed
* Credit utilization ratio (30% impact) [4, 5] 
* Credit length
* Age of active accounts (15% impact) [4] 
* Credit mix
* Types of loans used (10% impact) [4] 

## ⚠️ Administrative Barriers
Legal transitions introduce unique system failures. [1, 2] 

* Legal name changes
* Bureau files can fragment into unconnected reports
   * Deadnames can trigger 100-point score drops [1, 2, 6, 7] 
* Identity mismatches
* Outdated markers cause lender confusion and rejections [8, 9] 

## 📉 Economic Disparities
Broader financial strain indirectly lowers scores. [5, 10] 

* Wage gaps
* Trans workers earn less per dollar [2, 9, 11] 
* High medical costs
* Gender-affirming care drives up credit card balances [2] 
* Lost family support
* Younger adults rely heavily on revolving credit [2] 

## ✅ Action Steps
Protect your credit health during transition. [11] 

* Monitor reports
* Check all three major bureaus annually [11] 
* Update names
* Submit legal name changes to bureaus immediately [11] 
* Build alternative credit
* Use secured or credit-builder cards [3, 11]
