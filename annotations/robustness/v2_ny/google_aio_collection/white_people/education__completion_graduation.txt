Socioeconomic status, parental education background, and family structure are the primary factors influencing educational completion and graduation rates for White students.
## Key Influencing Factors

* Socioeconomic Status (SES): Higher parental income and wealth strongly correlate with higher K-12 achievement, college enrollment, and bachelor's degree completion. Wealth ties directly to better-funded school districts and residential stability. [1, 2] 
* Parental Background and Education: Family educational history heavily dictates student support systems, college readiness expectations, and navigation of higher education application processes.
* Family Structure: Living with married birth parents during high school significantly raises the odds of high school completion and college graduation. [3] 
* Gender Disparities: A growing gap exists where White women finish college at higher rates than White men. Studies from the [Pew Research Center](https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2021/11/08/whats-behind-the-growing-gap-between-men-and-women-in-college-completion/) note that non-college-graduate White men are more likely than White women to report leaving school simply because they did not want to continue or felt their career path did not require a degree. [4, 5] 
* Institutional Type: White students disproportionately enroll in four-year public or private non-profit universities rather than for-profit or two-year institutions, which statistically yields higher completion rates. [6, 7]
