## 📌 Race-Neutral Scoring Factors
Credit scores (such as FICO and VantageScore) do not include race, gender, age, income, or zip code as direct calculation factors. For White consumers—and all consumers—credit scores are determined by standard financial data reported to credit bureaus: [1, 2] 

* Payment history (35%): Timeliness of past credit card, loan, or mortgage payments. [3] 
* Amounts owed (30%): Credit utilization ratio and total debt balances. [3] 
* Length of credit history (15%): Age of oldest accounts and average account age. [3] 
* New credit (10%): Frequency of recent credit inquiries and newly opened accounts. [3] 
* Credit mix (10%): Variety of credit types used, such as revolving credit and installment loans. [3] 

------------------------------
## 📊 Structural and Socioeconomic Context
According to analysis from organizations like [Investopedia](https://www.investopedia.com/average-credit-scores-by-race-5214521) and the [National Consumer Law Center](https://www.nclc.org/wp-content/uploads/2016/05/20240227_Issue-Brief_Past-Imperfect.pdf), average credit scores vary across racial groups due to broader socioeconomic disparities rather than algorithmic inclusion of race: [1, 4] 

* Homeownership rates: Higher baseline homeownership allows more White consumers to benefit from installment loan history and asset stability, which positively influence credit scores. [1, 5] 
* Wealth and debt gaps: Access to generational wealth reduces reliance on high-cost revolving debt or alternative fringe financial services that can negatively affect scores. [2, 6] 
* Credit visibility: Statistical data from regulatory bodies indicates lower rates of "credit invisibility" (having no credit record) among White and Asian Americans compared to other demographic groups. [4]
