Person-centered care (PCC) for White patients is primarily influenced by general foundational factors like effective provider communication, shared decision-making, health literacy, and insurance coverage.
According to comparative studies on patient-centered care and health disparities, core interpersonal preferences—such as wanting an attentive, empathetic, and listening provider—are shared across racial and ethnic groups, including White, Black, and Hispanic patients. However, White patients' experiences and priorities in health systems are shaped by specific dynamics:
• Communication and Shared Decision-Making: Transparent communication, active listening, and provider empathy serve as the foundation of high-quality PCC, directly impacting patient satisfaction and adherence for White patients just as they do for other groups.
• Systemic Alignment and Navigation: Research indicates that White patients generally experience health systems that align with dominant cultural norms, meaning they face fewer subtle barriers regarding institutional trust, navigating the system, or having their cultural definitions of health understood by mainstream providers.
• Insurance and Socioeconomic Factors: Financial factors, type of insurance coverage, and socioeconomic position significantly dictate access to specialized care and out-of-pocket burdens, influencing the overall quality and continuity of person-centered interactions.
• Provider Concordance and Comfort: Because the majority of the U.S. healthcare workforce is White, White patients frequently experience cultural and linguistic concordance with their providers by default, which research associates with higher baseline comfort and perceived respect in clinical settings.
