Administrative burden for heterosexual people—like other groups—is primarily driven by three types of costs: learning costs (understanding eligibility and finding information), compliance costs (the time, paperwork, and "time tax" required to prove and maintain eligibility), and psychological costs (stress, stigma, and frustration when dealing with agencies).
While administrative burden research often looks at intersections of race, class, gender, and disability, the specific experience of heterosexual individuals is heavily influenced by general socioeconomic and structural factors rather than sexual orientation itself.
Key Factors Influencing Administrative Burden
• Income and Social Class: Lower-income heterosexual individuals face much higher compliance and psychological hurdles. They often navigate more heavily conditioned safety-net programs (such as SNAP or Medicaid) that require frequent recertifications and rigorous asset or income documentation.
• Family Structure and Parenting Status: Parents—including heterosexual parents—experience significantly higher administrative stress and compliance demands compared to non-parents due to added eligibility rules regarding childcare, household size, and dependents.
• Human Capital and Cognitive Resources: Access to internet technology, literacy levels, and available free time directly affect a person's ability to shoulder compliance and learning costs. Scarcity and demanding work schedules create friction when trying to complete long forms or wait on telephone holds.
• Disability and Health Status: Individuals with chronic illnesses or disabilities face compounded psychological and compliance burdens. Proving ongoing medical eligibility requires extensive documentation, creating a "catch-22" where the people who need assistance the most have the least cognitive and physical bandwidth to clear bureaucratic hurdles.
• Social Capital and Networks: Communities with robust information-sharing networks or social capital experience lower learning costs because members help each other navigate complex application systems.
