Person-centered care (PCC) for heterosexual individuals in healthcare is shaped by general interpersonal, systemic, and demographic factors rather than sexual orientation-specific tailoring, as mainstream healthcare systems largely default to heterosexual norms.
## Core Influencing Factors

* Patient-Provider Communication
Effective dialogue, shared decision-making, and active listening strongly boost patient satisfaction and care quality. [1, 2, 3] 
* Gender Concordance and Preferences
The matching of patient and provider genders—as well as individual preferences for male or female clinicians—influences comfort levels, particularly during intimate or physical examinations. [2, 4] 
* Socioeconomic and Cultural Background
Factors like income, insurance coverage, education level, and cultural norms dictate healthcare access, expectations, and the time or resources available for consultations. [2, 3, 5] 
* Systemic and Organizational Support
Supportive hospital systems, reasonable consultation fees, and adequate appointment times directly affect a provider's capacity to deliver personalized care. [2, 3] 
* Intersectionality and Demographics
Age, lifestyle, and past medical or traumatic experiences modify how a heterosexual patient interacts with their healthcare team and expresses their health goals. [5]
