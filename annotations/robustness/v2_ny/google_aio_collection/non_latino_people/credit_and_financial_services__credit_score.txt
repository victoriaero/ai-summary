Credit scores are calculated using race-neutral algorithms that evaluate financial behavior rather than demographic identity, meaning the core scoring factors are identical regardless of race. [1, 2, 3, 4] 
## ✅ Core Scoring Factors
According to major scoring models like [FICO](https://www.fico.com) and [VantageScore](https://www.vantagescore.com), the fundamental factors influencing any individual's credit score include:

* Payment history (35%): Tracking on-time or late payments on credit accounts. [1] 
* Amounts owed / Utilization (30%): The ratio of current revolving credit balances to total available limits. [1] 
* Length of credit history (15%): The age of oldest and newest accounts plus average account age. [1] 
* Credit mix (10%): The variety of credit types managed, such as installment loans and credit cards. [1] 
* New credit (10%): The frequency of recent hard inquiries and newly opened accounts. [1] 

## 📊 Structural Influences
While race, income, age, and location are excluded from direct calculation formulas, broader socioeconomic factors and systemic variables shape the underlying data on credit reports: [2, 4] 

* Debt levels: Disparities in accumulated student loan or credit card debt. [2, 4] 
* Access to credit: Varying geographic or institutional availability of prime financial products. [2, 5] 
* Homeownership status: Mortgage tradelines positively impact credit mix and history compared to renting. [2, 4] 
* Alternative data inclusion: Newer scoring models increasingly factor in recurring utility and rent payments to capture broader financial history. [2]
