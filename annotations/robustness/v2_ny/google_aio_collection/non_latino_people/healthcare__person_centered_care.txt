Person-centered care for non-Latino groups (such as Black and non-Latino White patients) is primarily influenced by patient-provider communication quality, clinician unconscious biases, and shared decision-making involvement. [1, 2, 3] 
## Key Influencing Factors

* Patient-Provider Communication: Active listening, clear explanations, and being genuinely understood strongly predict positive care experiences for Black and White patients. Deficits in communication often lead to unmet needs regarding treatment decisions. [1, 2] 
* Implicit Bias and Discrimination: Clinician unconscious or perceptual biases can create affective distance or misunderstandings, negatively affecting how minority patients experience warmth, empathy, and respect. [4, 5] 
* Cultural and Individual Values: Factors like finding personal meaning in illness, specific religious or spiritual beliefs, and varying preferences for autonomy versus family/social integration shape how patients view quality care. [2, 6] 
* Health Literacy and Education: Individual health literacy levels affect a patient's capacity to navigate dominant medical systems and engage in complex, long-term chronic disease management. [1, 7] 
* Care Coordination and Continuity: Accessible first points of contact, integrated services, and consistent, long-term provider relationships build the structural trust necessary for effective person-centered delivery. [8]
