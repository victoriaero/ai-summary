Socioeconomic background, academic preparation, and family structure are key factors that influence educational completion and graduation rates for non-Latino groups (such as White, Asian, and Black students). [1, 2] 
## Core Factors Influencing Graduation

* Academic Preparation: High school grade point averages (GPAs) and standardized test scores strongly predict college completion across racial and ethnic groups. Controlling for these academic indicators significantly narrows completion disparities. [2] 
* Family Structure: Living with married birth parents during high school increases the statistical odds of graduation and overall educational attainment. [2] 
* Socioeconomic Status: Household income, parental education levels, and access to stable financial resources determine whether students can afford continuous enrollment without facing severe work or financial interruptions. [3] 
* Institutional Type and Resources: Students enrolled in public four-year universities or well-funded private non-profit institutions graduate at higher rates compared to those in under-resourced or for-profit programs. [4, 5] 
* Gender Disparities: Across many demographic categories, female students complete associate, bachelor's, and advanced degrees at notably higher rates than male students. [2, 6]
