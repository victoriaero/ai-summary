Employment benefits for non-Latino workers are primarily influenced by structural, occupational, and economic factors rather than ethnicity alone. [1, 2] 
## 📊 Key Factors Influencing Benefits

* 🏢 Firm Size and Industry
* Large corporations offer comprehensive health and retirement plans more often than small businesses.
   * Industries like technology, finance, and government provide higher rates of non-wage compensation than retail, construction, or agriculture. [1] 
* ⏰ Employment Status (Full vs. Part-Time)
* Full-time permanent positions routinely qualify for employer-sponsored health insurance and retirement matching.
   * Part-time, gig, or subcontracted roles frequently lack standard benefit eligibility. [1, 3] 
* 🎓 Education and Skill Level
* Higher educational attainment correlates strongly with access to professional-tier corporate benefits and specialized perks. [4] 
* 📍 Geographic Location and State Policy
* State-mandated sick leave, paid family leave, and local labor regulations shape baseline benefit offerings.
