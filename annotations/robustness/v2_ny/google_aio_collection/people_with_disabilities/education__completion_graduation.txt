Multiple academic, social, and institutional factors influence completion and graduation rates for students with disabilities across K-12 and higher education. [1, 2, 3] 
## Key Influencing Factors

* Academic Preparation and Accommodations: Access to targeted accommodations—such as flexible assignment dates, distraction-reduced testing environments, and assistive technology—greatly increases completion likelihood. Conversely, gaps in early academic preparation or reliance on intensive, restrictive interventions can sometimes correlate with lower completion odds if foundational skill gaps are not met. [3, 4, 5] 
* Inclusive Practices and Mainstreaming: Spending portions of the school day with typically developing peers ([Mainstreaming](https://enablingdevices.com/blog/five-strategies-for-raising-graduation-rates-for-students-with-disabilities/)) and being included in the general education curriculum raise graduation rates. Holding capable students to standard academic expectations rather than relying on alternate diplomas improves long-term outcomes. [6, 7] 
* Attendance and Early Disengagement: Chronic absenteeism, disciplinary exclusion, and low school engagement are major risk factors for dropping out. Tracking attendance and behavioral data early via [Multi-Tiered System of Supports (MTSS)](https://www.gradpartnership.org/resources/a-pathway-to-change/) helps schools intervene before students fall off track. [1, 3] 
* Mentorship and Relationships: Meaningful connections with faculty members, mentors, or staff at [Office of Disability/Special Services](https://njcommonground.org/college-students-with-disabilities-what-factors-influence-successful-degree-completion/) strongly support persistence and degree completion. [2] 
* Self-Advocacy and Disclosure: Personal qualities like self-awareness, resilience, and the ability to advocate for one's needs are vital. However, many postsecondary students choose not to disclose their disabilities due to privacy concerns or lack of awareness, meaning they miss out on crucial college-level support. [2, 8] 
* Intersectionality: Individual identity factors—including racial or ethnic background, socioeconomic status, and the specific type of disability (e.g., cognitive versus physical conditions)—interact with the school environment and significantly impact attendance and graduation trajectories. [4, 9]
