Administrative burden for people with disabilities in government benefits is driven by three main cost categories: learning costs, compliance costs, and psychological costs. [1, 2] 
## Key Factors Influencing Administrative Burden

* Learning Costs: People with disabilities spend significant time and effort trying to figure out if they qualify, what benefits exist, and how to apply . Complex rules and fragmented programs make this harder. Living in rural areas with few local offices adds extra hurdles. [2, 3, 4] 
* Compliance Costs: Applicants must provide massive amounts of paperwork, such as decades of medical records and strict proof of income or assets . Frequent recertification requirements and low asset limits (like the $2,000 cap for single SSI recipients) force ongoing, difficult proof . [5, 6] 
* Psychological Costs: Navigating invasive bureaucratic systems causes stress, frustration, and a loss of personal autonomy . Low institutional trust and societal stigma around receiving public assistance increase these mental health impacts . [2, 6] 
* Agency Funding and Staffing Levels: Underfunded administrative budgets at agencies like the Social Security Administration (SSA) lead to long wait times, processing backlogs, and errors . This reduces staff capacity to offer personalized help . [7, 8]
