Several key physical, social, and economic factors influence neighborhood quality for people with disabilities.
## Physical Accessibility and Infrastructure

* Walkability and Sidewalks: Intact, well-maintained sidewalks with curb cuts and ramps are essential for wheelchair users and people with mobility devices. [1] 
* Public Transportation: Reliable, accessible public transit (like buses with lifts and accessible subway stations) determines how easily residents can reach jobs, healthcare, and community spaces.
* Public and Open Spaces: Access to nearby parks, recreational areas, and safe community spaces positively affects overall well-being and reduces isolation. [2, 3] 

## Social Environment and Safety

* Crime and Safety: High crime rates disproportionately affect disabled households and limit safe outdoor mobility. [4, 5] 
* Community Support and Collective Efficacy: Strong neighborhood social cohesion, helpful neighbors, and mutual trust improve independence and mental health. [4] 
* Proximity to Services: Close access to medical care, grocery stores, and social services reduces the negative psychological and physical impacts of a disability. [6, 7] 

## Economic and Systemic Factors

* Affordability: High housing and rental costs relative to fixed incomes (like Supplemental Security Income) force individuals into lower-quality neighborhoods with fewer resources. [1, 8, 9] 
* Systemic Discrimination: Ableism, lack of accommodation enforcement, and historical housing injustices restrict choices, trapping disabled individuals in under-resourced areas. [3, 9, 10]
