Women currently outpace men in college enrollment and completion rates in the United States, with a national six-year graduation rate of roughly 67.9% compared to 61.3% for men. [1, 2] 
Several key financial, social, and background factors influence college completion and graduation for women:
## Financial and Economic Factors

* Affordability and Debt: Affordability is the single most common barrier cited by women for not completing or pursuing higher education (roughly 44% of women cite cost). Furthermore, women disproportionately carry student loan debt, with 71% holding debt post-graduation compared to 64% of men. [3, 4] 
* Labor Market Returns: Improvements in women's expected career earnings and labor market returns for holding a college degree serve as major economic drivers encouraging persistence and completion. [5] 

## Family and Social Background

* Parental Education: Family background—especially the educational attainment of mothers—strongly influences completion. Women with college-educated mothers experience higher utility and motivation to finish college.
* Support Networks: Mentoring, peer connections, and parental support positively correlate with academic resilience and higher institutional graduation rates. [5, 6, 7] 

## Health and Well-being

* Mental Health Challenges: Cisgender women report higher rates of anxiety and depression during college, which research links to higher consideration rates of dropping out if unaddressed by campus mental health resources. [4, 8]
