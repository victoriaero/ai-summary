Person-centered care for women in healthcare is shaped by individual, interpersonal, and systemic factors that dictate how well a patient's preferences, values, and needs are met. [1, 2, 3] 
## Patient and Demographic Factors

* Identity and Background: A woman's age, cultural background, socioeconomic status, and lifestyle significantly impact her expectations, health literacy, and lived experience in healthcare settings. [4, 5] 
* Diverse and Specialized Needs: Factors such as gender diversity (e.g., transgender women), reproductive life stages, incarceration, or histories of domestic abuse require uniquely tailored, non-judgmental approaches. [5, 6] 
* Trauma History: Past negative or traumatic medical experiences can create barriers to trust, requiring clinicians to approach care with extra sensitivity and transparency. [5] 

## Interpersonal and Provider Dynamics

* Communication and Empathy: Active listening, acknowledging emotions, avoiding a rushed demeanor, and validating health concerns prevent patients from feeling dismissed. [7, 8] 
* Autonomy and Shared Decision-Making: Fostering a therapeutic alliance where women retain choice and control over their treatment plans promotes empowerment and satisfaction. [3, 9] 
* Provider Attributes and Relationships: Establishing collaborative partnerships, building mutual trust, and offering continuity of care—especially across primary and specialized services—greatly enhance the quality of care. [4, 9, 10] 

## Systemic and Organizational Policies

* Institutional Culture and Resources: Widespread access to comprehensive services, supportive management, and clear organizational policies dictate whether [patient-centered care for women](https://www.valueinhealthjournal.com/article/S1098-3015%2820%2932103-3/fulltext) is successfully implemented. [1, 8, 11] 
* Provider Training and Staffing: Equipping all healthcare staff with robust relational and technical skills, reducing provider fragmentation, and ensuring manageable workloads prevent depersonalized or rushed treatment. [12, 13]
