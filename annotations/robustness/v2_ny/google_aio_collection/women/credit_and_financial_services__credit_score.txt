📊 Credit scores are officially gender-neutral by law (the Equal Credit Opportunity Act prohibits using gender as a scoring factor), but behavioral and systemic economic factors create differences in how scores trend for women. [1, 2, 3] 
## 📌 Core Credit Score Factors

* ✅ Payment History (35%): Timely payment of bills is the single largest driver. [4, 5] 
* ✅ Credit Utilization (30%): The ratio of revolving debt to available limits. [4, 6] 
* ✅ Length of History (15%): Age of open and active accounts. [4, 5] 
* ✅ Credit Mix (10%): Variety of credit types like cards and installment loans. [4, 5] 
* ✅ New Credit (10%): Recent hard inquiries and newly opened lines. [4, 5] 

## ⚠️ Systemic and Behavioral Influences

* 📉 The Gender Pay Gap: Lower average earnings relative to credit needs can elevate revolving utilization ratios if balances are carried. [3, 7] 
* 🔎 Credit Usage Patterns: Research shows differences in tradeline volume and debt-to-limit management drive score discrepancies, which disappear when history attributes are controlled. [1] 
* 💡 Model Calibration: Studies suggest traditional scoring models can exhibit slight mis-calibration or lower predictive accuracy nuances across specific alternative borrowing groups. [8] 

## 🗓 Action Plan to Optimize Scores

* Phase 1: Automate Payments — Set up auto-pay for minimums to protect your 35% payment history.
* Phase 2: Lower Utilization — Keep revolving balances under 30% of total limits (ideally under 10%).
* Phase 3: Use Tools — Link utility and phone bills via [Experian Boost](https://www.experian.com/blogs/ask-experian/credit-education/score-basics/what-affects-your-credit-scores/) to add positive on-time payment data. [6, 7, 9, 10]
