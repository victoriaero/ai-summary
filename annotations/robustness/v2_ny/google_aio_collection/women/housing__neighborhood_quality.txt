Women's perceptions and experiences of neighborhood quality are shaped by a combination of safety, socioeconomic resources, infrastructure design, and social cohesion. [1, 2] 
## Safety and Social Disorder

* Public Harassment: Fear of sexual harassment and crime in public spaces heavily influences how women perceive neighborhood safety and social disorder. [1] 
* Socioeconomic Buffers: Higher aggregate socioeconomic resources for women in a neighborhood help reduce the negative psychological impact of visible social disorder (like vandalism or loitering). [1] 
* Neighborhood Stability: Declining or unstable neighborhoods correlate with lower health-related quality of life and higher stress, particularly for women facing financial constraints. [3] 

## Infrastructure and Urban Design

* Proximity to Daily Needs: Gender-sensitive urban planning—exemplified in cities like Vienna—integrates housing with childcare facilities, elder care, medical offices, and grocery shops within immediate walking distance. [2] 
* Passive Surveillance: Architectural choices like cantilevered windows allow parents (frequently mothers) to monitor children playing outdoors and foster connection to community life. [2] 
* Physical Conditions: Freedom from structural inadequacies (such as dampness, mold, and poor thermal comfort) directly impacts maternal mental health and overall satisfaction. [4, 5] 

## Economic and Social Factors

* Housing Affordability: Women experience higher rent burdens and systemic gaps (such as wage disparities and historical lending practices) that restrict them to lower-quality neighborhoods.
* Social Support Networks: Access to community centers, local organizations, and inclusive spaces builds a stronger sense of belonging and collective safety.
