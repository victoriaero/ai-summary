Police-initiated contact for women in the criminal justice system is heavily influenced by intersecting factors of race, socioeconomic vulnerability, and situational contexts such as domestic or behavioral health crises. [1, 2, 3] 
## Key Factors Influencing Police-Initiated Contact

* Racial and Ethnic Disparities: According to data highlighted by the [Prison Policy Initiative](https://www.prisonpolicy.org/blog/2019/05/14/policingwomen/), Black women face higher rates of traffic and street stops compared to white women, and they experience disproportionately higher rates of arrest during those encounters. [1] 
* Crises and Vulnerabilities: Law enforcement contact is frequently triggered by underlying crises, including intimate partner violence, behavioral health challenges, homelessness, and substance use disorders. [2, 4] 
* Situational and Environmental Context: Research examining police-community encounters indicates that situational characteristics—such as the type of neighborhood, visible indicators of conflict, guardianship, and perceived resistance—shape how and when police initiate contact. [5, 6] 
* Intersectional Bias: Studies on marginalized women show that structural inequities, neighborhood policing strategies, and implicit or explicit biases lead to heightened involuntary surveillance and intervention for women of color. [3, 5]
