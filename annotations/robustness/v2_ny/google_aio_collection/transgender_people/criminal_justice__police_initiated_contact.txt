Several key factors influence police-initiated contact and interactions for transgender people within the criminal justice system, driven by systemic profiling, socioeconomic vulnerability, and intersecting identities. [1, 2] 
## Key Influencing Factors

* Survival Sex Work Profiling: Engagement or perceived engagement in the underground economy and sex work strongly predicts police interaction. Law enforcement officers frequently profile transgender individuals—particularly trans women—as sex workers simply for being visible in public spaces, a phenomenon often described as "walking while trans". [2, 3, 4] 
* Public Visibility and "Walking While Trans": Routine public presence can trigger police stops. Officers often use gender identity, non-conforming gender expression, or clothing choices as a proxy or sole basis for suspicion and detentions related to prostitution offenses. [4, 5] 
* Racial and Ethnic Disparities: Transgender people of color (TPOC), particularly Black transgender individuals, face significantly higher rates of police stops, searches, and physical force compared to White transgender respondents. Intersecting racism and transphobia heavily compound the probability of aggressive police intervention. [2, 3, 6, 7] 
* Housing Instability and Homelessness: High rates of homelessness and lack of family acceptance force many transgender people—especially trans youth—to spend significant time in public outdoor spaces. This increased street-level exposure raises their baseline likelihood of encounters with law enforcement in over-policed neighborhoods. [4, 8] 
* Socioeconomic Status: Low income and poverty restrict access to private spaces and private legal resources, increasing reliance on public spaces and public assistance where police contact is more prevalent. [1, 9]
