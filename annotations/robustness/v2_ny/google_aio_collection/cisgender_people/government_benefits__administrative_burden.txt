AI OVERVIEW TEXT
============================================================

Cisgender people experience administrative burden in government benefits through three primary cost categories: learning costs, compliance costs, and psychological costs. [1] 
While transgender individuals often face distinct identity-based surveillance and misgendering, cisgender individuals' burdens are primarily driven by intersecting socioeconomic, institutional, and demographic factors. [2, 3, 4] 
## Core Cost Factors

* Learning Costs: The time and effort required to find out about a program, figure out eligibility, and understand application procedures. These are influenced by an individual's digital literacy, access to internet infrastructure, and availability of clear program guidance. [5] 
* Compliance Costs: The "time taxes" spent fulfilling bureaucratic mandates, such as gathering documentation, filling out repetitive forms, waiting on hold, and traveling to offices. These disproportionately penalize hourly workers who lose wages while complying. [1, 5] 
* Psychological Costs: The emotional toll, stress, and stigma associated with navigating safety-net programs. Research shows that applicants with children or lower income levels often report significantly higher stress and frustration during these interactions. [1, 6] 

## Broader Influencing Variables

* Socioeconomic Status and Resources: Access to personal resources—such as reliable transportation, stable housing, paid time off, and digital devices—directly dictates how easily an applicant can clear compliance hurdles. [4, 7] 
* Intersection with Race and Class: Administrative rules that appear neutral often disproportionately burden low-income and racially minoritized cisgender populations due to systemic economic inequality and varying access to community social capital. [4, 8] 
* Agency Design and Staffing: The complexity of application forms, availability of language accommodations, and the quality of client-caseworker interactions heavily influence the overall weight of the burden. [7, 9]
