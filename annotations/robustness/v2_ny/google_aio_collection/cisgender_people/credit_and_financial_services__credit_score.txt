Credit scores are legally gender-neutral and do not directly use gender as a scoring factor, but models are influenced by behavioral and economic factors that can vary across demographic lines. [1, 2, 3] 
## 💳 Core Factors Influencing Credit Scores

* ✅ Payment History (35%): Tracking your record of paying past debts on time. [4, 5] 
* ✅ Amounts Owed / Utilization (30%): Measuring the ratio of used credit to total available limits. [4, 5, 6] 
* ✅ Length of Credit History (15%): Factoring in the age of oldest and average accounts. [4, 5] 
* ✅ Credit Mix (10%): Evaluating a combination of installment loans and revolving credit. [4, 5] 
* ✅ New Credit (10%): Accounting for recent hard inquiries and newly opened accounts. [4, 5] 

## 📊 Structural and Behavioral Influences

* 🔎 Proxy Leakage: Variables like age, education, and geographic location can indirectly correlate with demographic patterns in credit data. [3] 
* ⚠️ Utilization Discrepancies: Research from institutions like the [Federal Reserve Note on Credit Differences](https://www.federalreserve.gov/econres/notes/feds-notes/gender-related-differences-in-credit-use-and-credit-scores-20180622.html) shows that differences in revolving utilization and debt-to-income realities drive variations in score outcomes rather than explicit demographic weighting. [1] 
* 💡 Economic Flexibility: Higher baseline income and larger credit limits can make maintaining a low credit utilization ratio easier. [6, 7]

* 🗓 Long-Term (6+ Months): Automate minimum or full payments to build a consistent, unblemished history. [6, 8, 9]
