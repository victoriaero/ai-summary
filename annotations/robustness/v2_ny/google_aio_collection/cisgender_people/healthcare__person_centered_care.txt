Person-centered care for cisgender people in healthcare is primarily influenced by interpersonal communication, provider empathy, cultural competence regarding social determinants of health, and the mitigation of systemic barriers like cost and insurance coverage. [1, 2] 
## Key Influencing Factors

* Provider Communication and Empathy: Clear communication, active listening, and strong relational skills from healthcare professionals heavily drive patient satisfaction and trust. [2] 
* Intersectional Identities: Factors such as sexual orientation (e.g., cisgender sexual minority women), race, socioeconomic status, and substance use history shape unique experiences of bias, discrimination, or culturally competent support within clinical settings. [1, 3] 
* Systemic and Financial Access: Out-of-pocket consultation fees, insurance limitations, and geographic availability directly restrict or facilitate continuous, high-quality engagement with care. [2] 
* Gender and Professional Preferences: Patient perceptions are sometimes influenced by provider attributes, including perceived professionalism and matching demographic preferences for specific types of sensitive care. [2]
