Person-centered care for homosexual and LGBTQ+ individuals is shaped by provider communication, clinic environment, and systemic training factors. [1, 2, 3] 
## Communication and Provider Attitude

* Affirming Language: Using inclusive terms and avoiding heteronormative assumptions during intake improves trust. [2, 4] 
* Provider Empathy: Compassionate, non-judgmental listening reduces patient fear and hesitation. [5, 6] 
* Open-Ended Questions: Asking neutral questions about relationships and lifestyle rather than presuming sexual orientation encourages self-disclosure. [4, 5] 

## Environment and Policies

* Visible Safe Spaces: Displaying non-discrimination policies, rainbow symbols, or inclusive signage reassures patients. [2, 4] 
* Chosen Family Recognition: Allowing partners or chosen family members to participate in care decisions and visitation honors patient autonomy. [1, 6, 7] 
* Privacy Controls: Protecting sensitive sexual orientation and gender identity (SOGI) data from unauthorized access prevents breaches of confidentiality. [1, 2] 

## Education and Systemic Factors

* Cultural Humility Training: Ongoing staff training on LGBTQ+ health needs prevents stereotyping and replaces finite cultural competence. [1, 2, 8] 
* Addressing Health Disparities: Acknowledging elevated risks for mental health struggles, substance use, and specific cancers ensures targeted, relevant clinical care. [4, 8, 9] 
* Institutional Accountability: Enforcing strict anti-discrimination policies builds a reliable climate of safety. [3]
