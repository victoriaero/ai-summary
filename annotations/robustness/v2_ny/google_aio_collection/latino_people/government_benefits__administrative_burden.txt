Language and communication barriers, mixed-status household concerns, and restrictive state-level policy designs heavily influence the administrative burden Latino people face when accessing government benefits. [1, 2] 
## Key Influencing Factors

* Language and Literacy Barriers: Finding out about benefit programs and reading complex application forms creates high learning costs when materials are not offered in Spanish or other primary languages. [1, 2, 3] 
* Immigration Status and Policy Rhetoric: Fear surrounding national anti-immigrant policies or interactions with government agencies increases psychological costs and discourages eligible mixed-status families from applying for benefits like TANF, SNAP, or Medicaid. [2, 3, 4] 
* Documentation and Compliance Demands: Requiring excessive types of paperwork and complex verification steps creates high compliance costs or "time taxes" that disproportionately penalize hourly or low-wage workers. [1, 3] 
* State Policy Variations: States with large Latino populations often feature stricter policy hurdles, though localized practices—such as staff training on documentation guidance and simplified application processes—can help reduce these burdens. [1]
