Employment benefits for people without disabilities are shaped by a combination of macroeconomic, organizational, and individual factors.
## 📊 Industry and Company Size

* Sector norms: Technology and finance sectors offer richer benefits than retail or hospitality.
* Firm scale: Large corporations provide comprehensive health and retirement plans compared to small businesses.
* Profitability: High-margin companies allocate more capital to competitive perks and bonuses.

## 📌 Job Role and Employment Status

* Full-time vs. part-time: Full-time roles unlock statutory and employer-sponsored packages.
* Hierarchical level: Executive and specialized roles command customized incentive structures.
* Market demand: High-demand skill sets force employers to upgrade compensation offerings.

## 💡 Economic and Labor Market Conditions

* Labor scarcity: Tight labor markets drive companies to expand wellness and insurance benefits.
* Inflation trends: Rising costs prompt adjustments in cost-of-living allowances and stipends.
* Regional standards: Local labor laws dictate mandatory minimums for paid leave and healthcare.

## 🔎 Individual and Demographic Factors

* Educational attainment: Higher degrees correlate with professional roles offering robust benefit tiers.
* Negotiation leverage: Experience and alternative offers allow candidates to secure enhanced terms.
* Career tenure: Seniority within an organization yields accrued vacation and vested retirement matches.
