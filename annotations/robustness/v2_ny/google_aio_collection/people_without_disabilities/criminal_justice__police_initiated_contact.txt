For individuals without disabilities, police-initiated contact in the criminal justice system is primarily driven by a combination of systemic, demographic, situational, and geographic factors. When cognitive, physical, or mental health vulnerabilities are not present, law enforcement interactions typically stem from the following influences: [1, 2, 3] 
## 1. Demographic and Socioeconomic Factors

* Race and Ethnicity: Extensive data, such as findings from the [Stanford Open Policing Project](https://openpolicing.stanford.edu/findings/), demonstrates that Black and Hispanic individuals without disabilities face significantly higher rates of proactive police stops and searches compared to white individuals. [4, 5] 
* Age and Gender: Young adults—specifically males aged 18 to 24—experience the highest volume of police-initiated contact, accounting for a disproportionate share of proactive street and traffic stops. [6, 7] 
* Socioeconomic Status: Individuals from low-income backgrounds are subject to higher rates of police contact due to increased exposure to heavily policed public spaces and systemic disadvantages. [8] 

## 2. Geographic and Environmental Context

* Neighborhood Crime Rates & Disinvestment: Police departments routinely concentrate personnel in areas designated as "high-crime" or economically disadvantaged. Consequently, individuals living in or traveling through these neighborhoods face a baseline higher probability of proactive police encounters. [1, 2] 
* Proactive Policing Zones: Areas with high commercial density, public transit hubs, or specific government agencies often see elevated levels of "broken windows" or quality-of-life policing, leading to more frequent citizen stops. [1, 2] 

## 3. Institutional Practices and Policy Drivers

* Pretextual Traffic Stops: A significant catalyst for police-initiated contact is enforcement of minor, non-safety violations (e.g., expired registration, tinted windows, or broken taillights). These function as "fishing expeditions" to search for contraband. [9, 10] 
* Departmental Performance Metrics: In some jurisdictions, unofficial or official organizational "production goals" or quotas incentivize officers to initiate frequent stops to meet statistical targets rather than responding strictly to public safety threats. [3, 11] 

## 4. Situational Factors

* Time of Day: Proactive stops change significantly depending on visibility. Research regarding the "Veil of Darkness" effect shows that racial disparities in traffic stops decrease after sundown because officers cannot easily determine the driver's demographic profile beforehand. [3, 9] 
* Discretionary "Reasonable Suspicion": For the general population, contact is frequently initiated based on an officer’s subjective evaluation of "suspicious" behavior, loitering, or presence in a specific location at unusual hours. [12]
