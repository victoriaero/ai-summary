Person-centered care (PCC) for individuals without specific disabilities is primarily influenced by patient characteristics, clinician behaviors, and the broader healthcare environment. [1, 2] 
## Patient-Specific Factors

* Health Literacy and Education: Patients with higher health literacy and educational backgrounds navigate medical information more easily and engage more actively in shared decision-making. [1] 
* Demographics and Expectations: Age, gender, and cultural background shape individual preferences for how much control or autonomy they want in their care. [1] 
* Active Participation: A patient’s willingness to adopt an active role rather than acting as a passive recipient heavily drives successful collaborative care. [3, 4] 

## Clinician and Provider Factors

* Communication and Empathy: Clinicians who practice attentive listening, use plain language, show empathy, and build trust create an environment where patients feel safe sharing personal goals. [1, 4, 5] 
* Professional Training and Attitude: Formal education in shared decision-making and moving away from traditional, physician-led hierarchies reduces paternalism. [1, 3, 6] 
* Interdisciplinary Teamwork: Collaborative teams shift power dynamics and distribute the responsibility of holistic patient support across various professionals. [5, 6] 

## Health System and Environmental Factors

* Time and Resource Constraints: Rigid appointment schedules and heavy workloads limit the time providers can spend engaging in deep, personalized dialogue. [1, 6, 7] 
* Organizational Leadership: Strong leadership and a supportive workplace culture encourage staff to prioritize patient-reported outcomes and collaborative planning. [5, 6] 
* Technology and Documentation: Interoperable digital health records and accessible online patient portals improve care coordination, whereas fragmented documentation systems act as major barriers. [6, 8]
