Several key economic, academic, and environmental factors influence high school and college completion for students without disabilities. [1, 2] 
## Academic Preparation and Early Indicators

* Early Reading and Attendance: Proficiency in reading by third grade and consistent attendance in elementary and middle school strongly predict on-time high school graduation. [1, 3] 
* Ninth-Grade Success: Being on track with credits and attendance during the first year of high school serves as a major milestone for finishing secondary education. [3] 
* First-Year College Performance: For higher education, first-year college grade point average (GPA) and avoiding course failures are the largest determinants of whether a student finishes a bachelor's degree. [4] 

## Socioeconomic and Family Factors

* Household Income and Poverty: Students from low-income families face significantly higher dropout rates and lower completion milestones compared to higher-income peers.
* Parental Involvement and Education: Active parental support, engagement in school activities, and high parental educational expectations strongly correlate with student graduation. [1, 5] 

## Demographic and Social Disparities

* Racial and Ethnic Identity: Statistics from government and academic studies show gaps in graduation rates, with Black, Hispanic, and Native American students experiencing lower on-time graduation odds compared to White and Asian peers, though many gaps narrow when controlling for attendance and economic supports.
* Teen Parenthood: Students who experience teen parenthood have markedly lower high school completion rates than those who do not. [1, 3] 

## School Environment and Institutional Support

* School Climate and Safety: Access to safe school environments, positive peer relationships, and smaller school sizes reduce the risk of dropping out.
* Teacher Quality: Experienced, high-quality teachers and supportive school staff improve student engagement and retention. [1, 6]
