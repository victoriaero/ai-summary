Credit scores are calculated using the same core financial data for all consumers, meaning the primary factors that influence a credit score do not change based on disability status. Major scoring models like FICO and VantageScore rely on five core factors from your credit reports: [1, 2] 
## Core Credit Score Factors

* Payment History (35% to 40%): Whether you pay your credit cards, loans, and other accounts on time. Late payments, collections, defaults, or bankruptcies lower your score. [2, 3] 
* Credit Utilization / Amounts Owed (20% to 30%): The total amount of revolving credit you are using compared to your total available credit limits (ideally kept under 30%, and preferably under 10%). [2, 4] 
* Length of Credit History (15% to 21%): How long your credit accounts have been open, including the age of your oldest account, newest account, and average age of all accounts. [2] 
* Credit Mix / Types of Credit (10% to 11%): The variety of credit accounts you manage, such as credit cards, retail accounts, installment loans, and mortgages. [2, 5] 
* Recent Credit Activity / Inquiries (5% to 10%): How often you apply for new credit (hard inquiries) and recently opened accounts. [2, 5] 

## What Is Not Used

* Income and Benefits: Federal assistance programs like SSI (Supplemental Security Income) or SSDI (Social Security Disability Insurance)—as well as general earned income—do not factor directly into credit score calculations. [6] 
* Personal Demographics: Your age, address, employment history, race, education, or marital status are excluded from credit reports. [3] 

You can pull your free credit reports anytime through [AnnualCreditReport.com](https://www.annualcreditreport.com) to review the data shaping your score. [4, 7]
