AI OVERVIEW TEXT

Structural racism, occupational segregation, and systemic gaps in data tracking and policy enforcement strongly influence employment benefits and compensation for Black workers. [1, 2] 
## Occupational Segregation and Industry Sector

* Private vs. Public Sector: According to the [Economic Policy Institute](https://www.epi.org/unequalpower/publications/understanding-black-white-disparities-in-labor-market-outcomes/), Black workers are often disproportionately represented in the public sector because it offers greater pay equity, stricter anti-discrimination accountability, and better access to defined-benefit pensions than the private sector. [3, 4] 
* Job Concentration: [Center for Economic and Policy Research (CEPR)](https://cepr.net/publications/the-continuing-power-of-white-preferences-in-employment/) notes that Black workers are more frequently concentrated in lower-paying or manual labor industries with fewer comprehensive benefits, limiting overall economic mobility. [5, 6] 

## Employer Data Tracking and Benefits Utilization

* Lack of Disaggregated Data: Research from [Mercer](https://www.mercer.com/en-us/insights/talent-and-transformation/diversity-equity-and-inclusion/how-employee-benefits-and-dei-initiatives-affect-black-employees/) shows that 85% of employers do not track health benefits utilization by race or ethnicity, and 77% do not monitor retirement or savings deferral rates by race or ethnicity. [1] 
* Limited Health Equity Initiatives: Only 10% of organizations implement targeted health equity initiatives, leaving disparities in healthcare access and wellness programs unaddressed within workforces. [1] 

## Policy Design and Structural Supports

* Unemployment Insurance (UI): Studies by the [Congressional Black Caucus Foundation](https://www.cbcfinc.org/capstones/economic-opportunity/a-net-with-gapping-holes-unemployment-insurance-and-racial-inequality/) indicate that state-level UI regulations—especially in Southern states with large Black populations—feature stricter eligibility rules and lower weekly caps, disproportionately harming Black workers during job loss. [7] 
* Collective Bargaining: Union representation strongly correlates with improved wages, health coverage, and retirement security for Black employees, whereas the historical decline of organized labor has widened benefit disparities. [2, 4]
