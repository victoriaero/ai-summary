While credit scoring models like FICO and VantageScore do not explicitly use race as a factor, systemic economic disparities and structural elements heavily influence the credit profiles and scores of Black consumers. [1, 2] 
## 📊 Structural & Economic Factors

* Racial Wealth Gap: Lower median household wealth and income limit emergency savings, making it harder to absorb financial shocks without missing payments. [3, 4, 5] 
* Exclusion of Alternative Data: Traditional scoring models historically omitted rent, utility, and phone payments—types of recurring payments more common in under-resourced communities. [1, 6] 
* Access to Credit: Black consumers face higher rates of being "credit invisible" or lacking traditional credit lines early in life, leading to shorter or thinner credit histories. [3, 5, 7] 

## ⚠️ Market & Institutional Disparities

* Predatory & Dual-Credit Markets: Over-reliance on high-cost fringe lenders or subprime loans due to historic redlining and fewer mainstream banking options in majority-Black neighborhoods. [1, 8] 
* Pricing Discrimination: Studies indicate higher interest rates and markups on auto and mortgage loans for minority borrowers, increasing debt burdens and utilization ratios. [6, 9, 10] 
* Collections and Legal Judgments: Disproportionate exposure to debt-collection lawsuits and default judgments that severely damage payment history metrics. [1]
