Administrative burdens in government benefit programs—such as SNAP, Medicaid, TANF, and Unemployment Insurance—are shaped by three main categories of costs and systemic factors that impact Black applicants and recipients: [1, 2, 3] 
## Core Types of Administrative Costs

* Learning Costs: The time, effort, and information required to find out about a program, understand eligibility rules, and figure out how to apply. [4, 5] 
* Compliance Costs: The concrete hurdles of proving eligibility, such as filling out lengthy forms, gathering extensive paper documentation, waiting in lines or on calls, and repeating verification during periodic renewals. [4, 6] 
* Psychological Costs: The emotional toll, loss of autonomy, and stigma associated with navigating bureaucratic systems, including feeling judged, devalued, or mistreated. [4, 6] 

## Key Factors Influencing Disparities

* Racialized Interpersonal Discrimination and Staff Interactions: While studies (such as research from the [Center for Social Development](https://openscholarship.wustl.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1978&context=csd_research)) find that Black applicants sometimes report lower baseline technical learning or compliance tasks than White counterparts, Black beneficiaries are roughly 40% more likely to report experiencing racial or ethnic discrimination directly from program staff. [7] 
* Program Design and Policy Stringency: Programs with higher structural barriers—such as TANF and Unemployment Insurance, which have seen increased administrative friction over time—disproportionately lower income replacement rates and economic security for Black and Latino families experiencing job loss compared to White families. Conversely, streamlining programs like SNAP reduces some of these gaps. [1, 3] 
* Bureaucratic Discretion and "Racialized Organizations" Theory: Local agencies often operate with wide discretion that decouples formal rules from actual practice, allowing systemic biases to shape how rules are enforced. Tropes around "deservingness" and fraud are frequently weaponized to justify stricter verification hurdles on means-tested programs that serve higher proportions of Black and minority families. [8] 
* State-Level Demographic and Political Context: Research shows that states with higher shares of White residents or higher levels of racial resentment among non-Hispanic White populations tend to maintain more restrictive administrative burdens and show higher political tolerance for making public assistance difficult to access. [1, 9, 10] 
* Policy Feedback and Loss of Trust: When administrative burdens lead to the wrongful or procedural termination of public benefits, it triggers a "racialized policy feedback loop"—disproportionately eroding trust in government and civic engagement among Black and minoritized recipients who lose critical support. [11]
