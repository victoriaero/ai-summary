Person-centered care (PCC) is influenced by patient characteristics, clinician skills, teamwork, and the surrounding healthcare system. [1, 2] 
## Patient-Specific Factors

* Health Literacy & Education: Patients with higher health literacy and education navigate shared decision-making more easily. [1] 
* Age & Demographics: Age, gender, and personal background shape individual patient preferences and expectations. [1] 
* Multi-morbidity Status: Managing multiple chronic conditions requires more complex, tailored coordination. [1, 3] 

## Clinician and Staff Factors

* Communication Skills: Clear dialogue and active listening build trust and improve collaboration. [1, 4] 
* Empathy & Compassion: Emotional intelligence and treating patients with dignity foster strong, respectful relationships. [5, 6] 
* Training & Knowledge: Formal education on shared decision-making directly impacts how well staff implement PCC. [1, 5] 

## Teamwork and Environment

* Interprofessional Collaboration: Strong multidisciplinary teamwork ensures holistic care across different providers. [4, 5] 
* Workplace Support: Positive work environments and manageable workloads reduce staff stress and encourage better patient engagement. [2, 7] 

## Health System and External Factors

* Time Constraints: High work pressure and short appointment times make true patient partnership difficult to sustain.
* Documentation & Systems: Integrated electronic records and care continuity frameworks (like Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services Chronic Care Management) help coordinate care between visits.
* Cultural Context: Organizational and community cultures dictate how openly providers and patients share decision-making. [1, 4, 8, 9]
