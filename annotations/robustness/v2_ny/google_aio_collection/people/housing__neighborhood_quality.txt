Neighborhood quality is shaped by a combination of physical, social, and economic factors that directly impact residents' health and daily well-being. [1, 2] 
## Physical Environment and Infrastructure

* Cleanliness and Upkeep: Well-maintained streets, proper waste management, and low levels of blight or abandoned buildings increase resident satisfaction.
* Environmental Health: Low exposure to air pollution, noise pollution, and extreme temperatures protects physical and mental health.
* Urban Green Space: Access to clean parks, trees, and recreational areas encourages physical activity and reduces stress. [2, 3, 4] 

## Safety and Social Dynamics

* Crime and Violence: Low rates of crime, vandalism, and neighborhood disorder prevent constant stress and feelings of vulnerability.
* Social Cohesion: Strong community networks, neighborly support, and a sense of belonging help residents handle emotional challenges. [1, 5, 6] 

## Services and Accessibility

* Amenities: Access to grocery stores, healthcare, libraries, and cultural centers improves convenience and daily life.
* Walkability and Transit: Safe sidewalks, bike lanes, and reliable public transportation make it easy to travel without a car. [4, 7, 8] 

## Economic Factors

* Housing Affordability: Lower financial strain from rent or mortgage burdens leaves room for other essentials like food and healthcare.
* Local Opportunities: Proximity to good schools and stable jobs supports long-term financial security and upward mobility. [9, 10, 11]
