According to the Bureau of Justice Statistics, males experience a higher rate of police-initiated contact (such as traffic stops, street stops, and arrests) compared to females.
Several key factors influence the likelihood and frequency of police-initiated contact for men in the criminal justice system:
Demographic and Social Factors
• Age: Young men, particularly those aged 18 to 24, experience the highest rates of police-initiated contact and traffic or street stops across age groups.
• Race and Ethnicity: Studies and national data show that men of color—specifically Black and Hispanic men—face a disproportionately higher rate of police-initiated stops, searches, and arrests compared to white men, even when accounting for underlying neighborhood crime rates.
• Social and Family Environment: Individual risk factors such as prior school disciplinary actions, a family history of criminal justice contact (e.g., parents or siblings who have been arrested), and association with peers involved in illegal behavior significantly increase the likelihood of police encounters.
Environmental and Geographic Factors
• Urban Density and Neighborhood Context: Men living in densely populated urban centers or neighborhoods with high perceived levels of crime and social disorder experience significantly higher police visibility and proactive policing strategies, leading to more frequent officer-initiated stops.
• Policing Priorities: Shifts in department-level deployment, "broken windows" enforcement, and proactive street-level enforcement practices routinely increase the volume of involuntary, officer-initiated contacts in specific geographic areas.
