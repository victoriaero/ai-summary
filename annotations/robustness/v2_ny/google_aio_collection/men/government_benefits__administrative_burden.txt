Administrative burden for men—and all applicants—in government benefits is driven by learning, compliance, and psychological costs shaped by specific program designs and individual resources. [1, 2, 3] 
## Core Costs of Administrative Burden

* Learning Costs: The time and effort required to find out about benefit programs, understand eligibility criteria, and figure out how to apply.
* Compliance Costs: The paperwork, documentation, wait times, and in-person interviews required to submit and maintain claims.
* Psychological Costs: The stress, frustration, and social stigma associated with interacting with government bureaucracies. [2, 4] 

## Key Influencing Factors

* Program Rules and Design: Stringent requirements—such as work mandates, frequent recertification, and asset tests—significantly increase compliance burdens. [5, 6] 
* Human and Cognitive Capital: Individuals with lower literacy, limited digital access, or high stress/scarcity face steeper learning and compliance hurdles. [1] 
* Bureaucratic Fragmentation: Complex, multi-agency coordination or decentralized federal systems make navigating programs harder for applicants. [3, 4] 
* Stigma and Gender Norms: Societal expectations around self-reliance can heighten the psychological and emotional costs for men seeking social safety-net assistance. [2]
