Administrative burden consists of the-learning, compliance, and psychological costs people face when trying to get or keep government benefits. [1] 
## Core Categories of Cost

* Learning Costs: Time and effort to find programs, figure out eligibility rules, and learn how to apply. [1] 
* Compliance Costs ("Time Taxes"): Filling out forms, gathering proof, waiting on hold, traveling to offices, and completing renewals. [1] 
* Psychological Costs: Stress, anxiety, loss of autonomy, and social stigma from interacting with bureaucracy. [1] 

## Key Influencing Factors

* Policy Design & Rules: Strict asset limits, work requirements, frequent recertification, and complex verification mandates. [2, 3, 4] 
* Administrative & Institutional Choices: Fragmented programs across agencies, poor customer experience (CX) design, confusing paperwork, and lack of digital/mobile access. [5, 6] 
* Involvement of Third Parties: Outsourcing or contractor/private-sector delivery that adds friction or misaligned incentives. [6] 
* Individual Resources & Scarcity:
* Human & Cognitive Capital: Literacy, digital access, and executive function.
   * The Scarcity Trap: People facing poverty, illness, or crisis need benefits most, yet high stress and constrained cognitive bandwidth make navigating complex systems hardest. [7] 
* Intentional Gatekeeping: Policy design sometimes aims to restrict rolls or curb fraud/errors, prioritizing exclusion over access. [3, 8]
