Multiple academic, financial, environmental, and institutional factors influence completion and graduation rates in education. [1, 2, 3] 
## Academic Preparation and Performance

* Early performance: Ninth-grade grades and first-year college performance are strong predictors of on-time graduation.
* Rigorous coursework: Taking advanced high-level math and rigorous courses before college heavily increases completion odds.
* Attendance: Regular daily attendance keeps students from falling behind at both high school and college levels. [3, 4, 5, 6] 

## Financial Stability

* Socioeconomic status (SES): Family income and poverty levels strongly correlate with dropout risks.
* Financial planning: Proactive financial planning, scholarships, and targeted aid help students persist through emergencies. [1, 2, 5, 7, 8] 

## Social and Personal Support

* First-generation status: Students whose parents did not attend college face higher risks of leaving school and struggle more with mental health prioritization.
* Life circumstances: Part-time enrollment, working full-time, independent status, and single parenthood reduce completion rates.
* Mental health: Unaddressed stress and mental health challenges heavily drive decisions to drop out. [1, 5] 

## School and Institutional Environment

* Relationships: Positive, supportive connections with teachers and mentors foster higher student engagement and graduation intentions.
* Institutional resources: Access to counseling, tutoring, and clear academic advising significantly improves retention. [2, 3, 9, 10]
