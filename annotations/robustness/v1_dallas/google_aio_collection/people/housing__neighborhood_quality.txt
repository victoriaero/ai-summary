Neighborhood quality is shaped by a combination of physical, environmental, social, and economic factors that directly impact residents' daily lives and well-being. [1] 
## Physical Environment & Infrastructure

* Housing Conditions: Structural integrity, maintenance levels, absence of hazards like mold or lead, and proper insulation or plumbing.
* Access & Mobility: Street connectivity, walkability scores, and reliable public transportation options.
* Local Amenities: Proximity to grocery stores, healthcare facilities, schools, and recreational spaces. [2, 3, 4, 5, 6] 

## Environmental Quality

* Cleanliness & Order: Absence of litter, graffiti, abandoned buildings, and high vacancy rates.
* Green & Blue Spaces: Access to [parks, trees, and open natural areas](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10687291/) that support mental and physical health.
* Pollution & Noise: Low exposure to chronic noise, heavy traffic, and poor air quality. [3, 4, 5, 7, 8, 9] 

## Social & Economic Factors

* Safety & Crime: Low crime rates, active neighborhood watch programs, and responsive emergency services.
* Social Cohesion: Strong community bonds, supportive neighbor networks, and active civic participation.
* Economic Stability: Area-level income distribution and affordable [housing cost burdens](https://nursing.jhu.edu/magazine/articles/2025/03/new-insights-into-connection-between-housing-quality-and-population-health/) relative to resident income. [3, 4, 6, 7, 10]
