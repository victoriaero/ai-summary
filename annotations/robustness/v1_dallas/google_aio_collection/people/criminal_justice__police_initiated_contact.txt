Police-initiated contact is shaped by demographic, geographic, and behavioral factors that vary across communities. [1, 2] 
## Key Factors Influencing Police-Initiated Contact

* Demographics and Age: Data from the [Bureau of Justice Statistics](https://bjs.ojp.gov/library/publications/contacts-between-police-and-public-2022) show that young adults (ages 18 to 24) and males experience higher rates of police-initiated contact, such as traffic or street stops, compared to older demographics and females. [3, 4] 
* Race and Ethnicity: Studies on policing disparities indicate that people of color—particularly Black and Hispanic residents—experience a higher frequency of police-initiated stops and searches relative to white residents, even when controlling for other variables. [1, 5] 
* Neighborhood and Social Environment: Individuals living in economically disadvantaged or high-crime neighborhoods experience heightened police presence and targeted enforcement strategies, which directly increase the likelihood of police-initiated interactions. [2, 6] 
* Prior Criminal Justice Involvement: Individuals with personal or familial histories of criminal justice contact (such as a parent or sibling who has been arrested) report higher rates of subsequent police engagement. [2] 
* School and Community Discipline: Academic research highlights that youth subject to frequent school disciplinary actions face a higher statistical probability of early police contact. [2]
