Person-centered care (PCC) in healthcare is directly influenced by a combination of patient-specific, clinician-specific, and systemic factors that shape how collaborative and holistic care is delivered. [1] 
## Patient-Specific Factors

* Health literacy and education: Clear understanding of health conditions helps patients engage in shared decision-making. [1] 
* Demographics: Age, gender, and cultural background shape individual preferences and expectations for care. [1] 
* Clinical complexity: Multi-morbidity (having multiple chronic conditions) increases the need for coordinated care but can complicate collaborative planning. [1] 

## Clinician and Team Factors

* Communication skills: Active listening, transparent dialogue, and empathy build trust between providers and patients. [1, 2] 
* Formal training: Education on shared decision-making and relationship-centered care directly improves implementation quality. [1] 
* Interprofessional teamwork: Coordinated collaboration among doctors, nurses, and specialists ensures holistic support. [2] 

## Health System and Environmental Factors

* Time and workload constraints: High work pressure and short visit times restrict a clinician's capacity to practice PCC. [1] 
* Organizational culture: Supportive leadership and workflows (such as chronic care management programs) sustain care continuity between visits. [3] 
* Infrastructure: Accessible documentation systems and integrated care networks facilitate smooth transitions and shared access to health data. [1, 4]
