Key factors that influence high school and college completion or graduation for students without disabilities include academic preparation, socioeconomic status, and school engagement.
## Academic Performance and Preparation

* Early Literacy and Numeracy: Proficient reading by third grade and solid foundational skills heavily predict on-time high school graduation.
* Attendance Patterns: Regular attendance starting as early as fifth grade and continuing through critical milestones like ninth grade strongly correlates with staying on track to graduate.
* Course Performance: Passing foundational classes during the freshman year of high school or freshman year of college prevents credit deficiencies and stopping out. [1, 2, 3] 

## Socioeconomic and Demographic Factors

* Family Income and Poverty: Students from low-income households experience significantly higher dropout rates and face barriers tied to resource availability. [1] 
* Racial and Ethnic Disparities: Systemic and structural inequalities mean students identifying as Black, Hispanic, or Native American often show lower graduation odds compared to White and Asian peers, though active attendance tracking and support help narrow these gaps. [2] 
* Parental Involvement and Education: Higher levels of parental education and active participation in a student's schooling life improve grades, attitudes, and completion rates. [1, 3, 4] 
* Life Events: Major disruptions like teen parenthood sharply reduce the likelihood of finishing high school on time. [1] 

## School Environment and Engagement

* Student Engagement: Behavioral, emotional, and cognitive connectedness—such as a sense of belonging and purpose at school—prevents students from dropping out.
* School Climate and Size: Smaller school sizes, safe learning environments, and positive peer or social supports encourage continuous enrollment.
* Teacher Quality: Access to effective educators and supportive school staff directly influences student retention and academic success. [1, 5, 6]
