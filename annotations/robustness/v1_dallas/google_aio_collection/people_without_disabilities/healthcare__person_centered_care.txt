Person-centered care (PCC) for people without disabilities in healthcare is primarily influenced by patient characteristics, clinician communication, and health system environments. [1, 2] 
## Patient-Specific Factors

* Health Literacy: A patient's knowledge and understanding of health information directly affects their ability to engage in shared decision-making. [2, 3] 
* Demographics: Age, gender, and educational background shape individual preferences, expectations, and active participation in care plans. [2, 3] 
* Multimorbidity: Managing multiple chronic conditions increases cognitive overload and information limits, complicating personalized goal-setting. [3, 4] 

## Clinician-Specific Factors

* Communication Skills: Empathetic listening, avoiding medical jargon, and providing tailored explanations foster trust and collaboration. [2, 5] 
* Professional Attitude: Moving away from traditional paternalistic models toward treating patients as equal partners. [6, 7] 
* Empathy and Training: Formal training in shared decision-making protocols enhances a provider's capacity to deliver person-centered interactions. [1, 2] 

## Health System and Environmental Factors

* Time Constraints: Rigid scheduling and high work pressures limit the time clinicians can spend on open dialogue and collaborative care planning. [2, 7] 
* Workplace Culture & Teamwork: Supportive interdisciplinary teams and positive organizational environments strongly correlate with higher implementation of PCC. [1, 7] 
* Resource Availability: Financial pressures, public versus private sector differences, and fragmented documentation systems create structural barriers or enablers. [7, 8, 9]
