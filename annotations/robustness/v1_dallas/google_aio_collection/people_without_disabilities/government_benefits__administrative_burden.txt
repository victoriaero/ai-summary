AI OVERVIEW TEXT

Administrative burden for people without disabilities accessing government benefits is driven by three core cost categories—learning, compliance, and psychological costs—shaped by operational, socioeconomic, and policy design factors.
Core Cost Factors
• Learning Costs: The time and cognitive effort required to find out programs exist, understand complex eligibility rules, and figure out how to apply. Variations in rules across state or local jurisdictions increase these costs when individuals move or search for information.
• Compliance Costs: The "time taxes" and monetary expenses of proving and maintaining eligibility. These include gathering extensive documentation, filling out repetitive forms, scheduling in-person or phone interviews, and enduring long wait times.
• Psychological Costs: The emotional toll of interacting with bureaucratic systems, including the social stigma of receiving public assistance, loss of autonomy through intrusive oversight, and frustration or distrust toward government agencies.
Influencing Structural and Socioeconomic Drivers
• Program Design and Verification Strictness: Requirements intended to prevent fraud and payment errors—such as frequent income recertification, work requirements, and asset limits—directly increase compliance and paperwork burdens.
• Digital vs. Physical Access: The availability and usability of online portals, mobile-optimized applications, and universal logins can lower barriers, whereas reliance on clunky websites, mandatory in-person visits, or limited phone line availability raises them.
• Socioeconomic and Resource Constraints: Individuals with lower incomes, lower baseline digital literacy, or hourly jobs without paid time off experience compliance costs more acutely because taking time to wait on hold or travel to an office creates a direct financial penalty.
• Geographic and Infrastructure Disparities: Rural residents or those living far from physical agency offices (such as Social Security or health department field offices) face higher travel and communication barriers, limiting their ready access to reliable guidance.
• Language and Communication Barriers: Notices and application forms written in complex bureaucratic jargon—or a lack of readily available translation and interpretation services for people with limited English proficiency—significantly amplify the difficulty of completing enrollment or renewal processes.
