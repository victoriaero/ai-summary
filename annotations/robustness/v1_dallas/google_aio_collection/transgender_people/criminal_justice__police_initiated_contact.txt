Police-initiated contact for transgender individuals is primarily driven by biased profiling of gender nonconformity, racial disparities, and socioeconomic vulnerabilities such as housing instability or survival sex work.
Research from studies highlighted by the [Williams Institute at UCLA](https://williamsinstitute.law.ucla.edu/publications/law-enforcement-lgbtq-people/) and the [U.S. Transgender Survey via PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11196069/) identifies the core factors influencing these law enforcement interactions: [1, 2] 
## Key Influencing Factors

* Gender Expression and Profiling: Officers frequently target people for "walking while trans," meaning they stop or question individuals simply for being visibly transgender or gender-nonconforming in public. [3, 4, 5] 
* Racial Disparities: Transgender people of color, especially Black and Latine individuals, experience significantly higher rates of police stops, searches, and aggressive tactics compared to white transgender individuals. [1, 2, 6] 
* Sex Work Assumptions: Law enforcement often incorrectly assumes that transgender people—particularly trans women and transgender people of color—are engaging in sex work, leading to proactive stops, harassment, and false arrests. [1, 4, 6] 
* Housing Instability and Poverty: High rates of homelessness and lack of family acceptance force many transgender people to live or spend time in public spaces and over-policed neighborhoods, increasing their exposure to police surveillance. [3, 7, 8]
