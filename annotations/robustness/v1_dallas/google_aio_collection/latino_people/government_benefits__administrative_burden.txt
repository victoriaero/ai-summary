Language barriers, complex documentation requirements, and fear related to immigration enforcement and public charge policies heavily influence administrative burdens for Latino people accessing government benefits. [1, 2, 3] 
## Key Factors Influencing Administrative Burden

* Language and Literacy Barriers: A lack of application materials, program information, and bilingual staff in Spanish limits accessibility and raises learning costs for applicants with limited English proficiency. [1, 2] 
* Documentation and Compliance Rules: Strict requirements for multiple types of verification documents, complex household compositions, and rigid interaction rules increase compliance costs. [1, 2, 4] 
* Immigration Status and Fear: Concerns regarding immigration enforcement, national anti-immigrant rhetoric, and "public charge" rules create high psychological costs, causing eligible families to avoid utilizing safety net programs. [2, 3, 5, 6] 
* State and Local Implementation Practices: Decentralized administration, varying local frontline worker discretion, and differing state policy designs either exacerbate or mitigate burdens (such as California and New Mexico providing streamlined Spanish-language assistance). [1, 2, 7, 8]
