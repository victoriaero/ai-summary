While credit scoring models do not explicitly consider race or ethnicity, structural economic disparities and systemic factors significantly influence credit scores and access to financial services for Latino consumers. [1, 2, 3, 4] 
## 🔍 Credit Invisibility and Thin Files

* Higher Rates: Roughly 12% to 15% of Latino consumers are "credit invisible", meaning they lack a traditional credit history. [5, 6] 
* Unscored Records: An additional 12% have records that cannot be scored due to insufficient history. [6] 
* Limited Access: Reliance on alternative financial services (like check-cashing) fails to build traditional credit. [6] 

## 📊 Wealth and Income Disparities

* The Wealth Gap: Median Latino household wealth represents a fraction of white household wealth, reducing emergency buffers. [5] 
* Paycheck Pressures: A significant percentage of Latino Americans report holding more debt than emergency savings, increasing missed-payment risks. [7] 
* Lower Homeownership: Lower rates of homeownership mean fewer opportunities to benefit from the positive credit mix of a mortgage. [2, 4] 

## 💡 Reliance on Alternative Financial Services

* Nonbank Credit: Limited access to traditional banks pushes consumers toward higher-cost payday or alternative loans. [6, 8] 
* No Reporting: These alternative services often do not report positive payment behaviors to major credit bureaus. [6] 

## 🗒 Credit Literacy and Misinformation

* Initial Guidance: Over a quarter of Hispanic respondents report feeling misinformed or tricked during their early experiences with credit. [1] 
* Systemic Barriers: Many feel the financial system was not designed to help them succeed from the start. [1]
