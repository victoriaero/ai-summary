Administrative burdens for Black people navigating government benefits are shaped by interpersonal discrimination, bureaucratic discretion, and structural program designs. [1, 2] 
As defined by administrative burden researchers, these obstacles generally fall into three categories: learning costs (understanding rules and availability), compliance costs (paperwork, verification, and time), and psychological costs (stigma and stress). [3] 
## Key Influencing Factors

* Interpersonal Discrimination and Staff Interactions: Research indicates that Black applicants are significantly more likely—roughly 40% higher—to report feeling discriminated against based on race or ethnicity by program staff during public benefit interactions. [4] 
* Bureaucratic Discretion: High levels of staff discretion allow formal rules to be decoupled from actual organizational practices, creating opportunities for the racialized administration of benefits. [2] 
* Program Type and Policy Design: Administrative burdens have increased over time in programs like Temporary Assistance for Needy Families (TANF) and Unemployment Insurance (UI), contributing to lower income replacement rates for Black families experiencing job loss compared to White families. Conversely, burdens have decreased in programs like the Supplemental Nutrition Assistance Program (SNAP). [5] 
* Racial Resentment and Public Tolerance: Public support for higher administrative hurdles in safety-net programs is strongly correlated with racial resentment from non-Hispanic White individuals directed at Black people, affecting how punitive or complex program designs remain. [6] 
* Dependency on Means-Tested Programs: Racially marginalized groups are more frequently funneled into means-tested programs that demand disproportionate time, verification steps, and effort to establish and maintain eligibility. [2]
