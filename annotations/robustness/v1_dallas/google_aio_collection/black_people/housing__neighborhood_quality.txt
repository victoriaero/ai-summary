Neighborhood quality for Black people is heavily influenced by historical redlining, ongoing housing discrimination, environmental disparities, and unequal access to community resources. [1, 2] 
## Historical and Systemic Policies

* Redlining: Past government maps marked Black neighborhoods as risky, cutting off investment.
* Zoning laws: Allowed factories and toxic sites near Black communities.
* Urban renewal: Destroyed Black homes and businesses for highways and public projects. [2, 3] 

## Financial and Real Estate Practices

* Appraisal bias: Homes in Black neighborhoods are valued much lower than similar homes elsewhere.
* Racial steering: Agents push Black buyers toward segregated or lower-resource areas.
* Predatory lending: Higher interest rates and unfair loan terms drain family wealth. [3, 4, 5, 6] 

## Community Resources and Services

* School funding: Property taxes tie school quality to home values, underfunding Black neighborhoods.
* Local amenities: Fewer full-service grocery stores, safe parks, and reliable transit options.
* Municipal services: Poorer street upkeep, delayed trash pickup, and heavy policing. [1, 7, 8] 

## Environmental and Health Conditions

* Pollution exposure: Higher rates of poor air quality and industrial waste nearby.
* Substandard housing: Older homes with lead paint, mold, and pests that harm health. [2]
