Person-centered care for homosexual and LGBTQ+ individuals in healthcare is shaped by provider knowledge, institutional inclusivity, and the presence or absence of discrimination. [1, 2] 
## Key Factors Influencing Care

* Provider Knowledge and Cultural Humility: Clinicians who understand LGBTQ+ health needs, terminology, and minority stress provide safer, more effective care. Ongoing self-reflection helps providers manage personal biases. [1, 3, 4] 
* Inclusive Communication: Asking open-ended questions about relationships and using non-judgmental, inclusive language builds trust and encourages patients to disclose their sexual orientation safely. [5, 6] 
* Recognition of Chosen Family: Allowing partners and chosen family members to participate in decision-making and bedside visits supports patient autonomy and emotional well-being. [7, 8] 
* Systemic Stigma and Discrimination: Fear of mistreatment, misgendering, or receiving substandard care often leads patients to delay or avoid seeking medical attention. [8, 9] 
* Intersectionality: Factors like race, ethnicity, socioeconomic status, and geographic location compound barriers, making care access more difficult for diverse individuals. [10, 11] 
* Institutional Policies: Facilities with nondiscrimination policies, inclusive intake forms that record sexual orientation, and staff training create welcoming environments. [6, 12]
