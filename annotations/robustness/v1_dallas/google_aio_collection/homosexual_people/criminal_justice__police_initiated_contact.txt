Police-initiated contact for homosexual and LGBTQ+ individuals is heavily influenced by intersecting factors involving gender non-conformity, demographic inequalities, selective enforcement of specific laws, and institutional bias. [1, 2, 3] 
According to research from organizations like the [Williams Institute](https://williamsinstitute.law.ucla.edu/publications/law-enforcement-lgbtq-people/) and the [ACLU](https://www.aclu.org/publications/policing-progress-findings-from-a-national-survey-of-lgbtq-peoples-experiences-with-law-enforcement), queer individuals—particularly sexual minority youth and transgender people—experience significantly higher rates of police stops and public encounters than the general public. [1, 2, 4] 
## Key Influencing Factors

* Gender Non-Conformity and Profiling: Law enforcement officers frequently associate gender non-conformity (such as effeminate men or butch and androgynous women) with sexual deviance or criminal behavior, leading to increased surveillance and profiling. [3] 
* Selective Enforcement of Laws: Officers often selectively enforce specific statutes—such as public lewdness or solicitation laws—disproportionately targeting gay men and transgender women based on archaic stereotypes. [3] 
* Intersection of Race and Class: Contact rates and hostile interactions escalate sharply for LGBTQ+ people of color, low-income individuals, and homeless youth, who face compounded vulnerabilities and overpolicing in public spaces. [1, 2, 5] 
* Place-Based Targeting: Sexual minorities are often overrepresented in specific public areas or urban spaces where intensive foot patrols or proactive policing operations occur, driving up the frequency of non-vehicle stops. [4, 6] 
* Implicit and Explicit Bias: Entrenched masculine subcultures and individual homophobic or transphobic animus among officers can influence discretionary decisions regarding who gets stopped, searched, or questioned. [6, 7, 8]
