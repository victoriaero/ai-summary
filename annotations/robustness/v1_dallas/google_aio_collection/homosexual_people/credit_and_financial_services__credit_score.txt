Standard credit scoring models (like FICO and VantageScore) do not track sexual orientation or gender identity. They evaluate identical financial mechanics for all consumers: [2]

* New credit (10%): Frequency of recent hard credit inquiries and newly opened accounts. [2] 

## ⚠️ Indirect and Systemic Influencing Factors
While algorithms are identity-blind, systemic socioeconomic barriers create downstream challenges for building and maintaining strong credit within the LGBTQI+ community: [1] 

* Wage and Income Disparities: An ongoing earnings gap (where LGBTQI+ workers often earn less per dollar compared to peers) reduces disposable income and liquid savings, increasing reliance on revolving credit. [1, 3] 
* Loss of Familial Support: Younger individuals who experience family rejection after coming out frequently lose crucial early financial safety nets, forcing heavier initial reliance on credit cards for survival or education. [3, 4] 
* Out-of-Pocket Healthcare & Legal Costs: Higher self-funded expenses—such as specialized medical care, legal name changes, or estate planning/parentage security documentation—can drain cash reserves and spike debt utilization. [3, 5] 
* Employment and Housing Bias: Historical and ongoing discrimination in hiring, promotions, or housing stability can cause income interruptions, leading to missed payments or higher debt loads. [1, 4]

* Monitor reports: Regularly pull free credit reports via annualcreditreport.com to catch errors or track progress. [2]
