A combination of school climate, mental health, and social support significantly influences educational completion and graduation rates for sexual minority individuals. [1, 2, 3] 
## School Climate and Safety

* Harassment and Bullying: Hostile environments, verbal harassment, and physical victimization lead to higher rates of absenteeism and school dropout.
* Institutional Support: Inclusive policies, [Queer Resource Centers](https://www.pcc.edu/queer/district-efforts/research/), and visible safety measures improve student engagement and retention.
* Geographic Choice: Many LGBTQ+ students select colleges in different cities or states specifically to find more welcoming climates. [3, 4, 5] 

## Psychosocial and Mental Health Factors

* Depression and Anxiety: Elevated rates of stress, anxiety, and depression—often linked to social rejection or family rejection—negatively affect academic motivation and GPA.
* Educational Expectations: Perceived discrimination in schools and workplaces can lower long-term educational aspirations and daily participation. [2, 3, 5] 

## Demographic and Gender Asymmetries

* Gender Variations: Research from the [American Sociological Association](https://www.asanet.org/for-press/press-releases/what-role-does-sexuality-play-in-the-academic-gender-gap/) shows that lesbian and bisexual women often face steeper academic disadvantages and higher dropout risks, whereas gay men frequently demonstrate higher college completion rates. [1, 6] 
* Family Background: Disparities vary depending on intergenerational educational mobility and whether students experience family rejection or relocate to escape unsupportive households. [4, 7]
