Credit scores for women are determined by the standard five credit-scoring factors, though macroeconomic realities like the gender wage gap can disproportionately increase credit utilization ratios. [1, 2] 
## 📊 Core Credit Factors
The primary components affecting credit scores universally include:

* Payment history: 35% of overall score.
* Credit utilization: 30% of overall score.
* Length of history: 15% of overall score.
* Credit mix: 10% of overall score.
* New credit: 10% of overall score. [3] 

## ⚠️ Systemic Discrepancies
Statistical analyses show distinct behavioral or economic patterns:

* Lower average starting incomes impact utilization.
* Higher revolving balances relative to limits.
* More active tradelines managed over time.
* Minor historical scoring model miscalibrations. [2, 4, 5] 

## 💡 Optimization Strategies
Practical steps to protect and build your score:

* Keep credit card utilization below 30%.
* Automate monthly minimum or full payments.
* Retain older, inactive credit line accounts.
* Spread out new credit card applications. [2, 6, 7]
