Safety, proximity to essential care services, and financial affordability are key factors that heavily influence neighborhood quality and residential satisfaction for women in housing. [1, 2, 3] 
## Safety and Security

* Crime and Violence: Low crime rates and active community policing directly reduce fear and increase a sense of personal control.
* Physical Lighting and Infrastructure: Well-lit streets, functional sidewalks, and visible public spaces prevent isolation and allow women to navigate their surroundings safely. [4, 5] 

## Integrated Amenities and Care Access

* Childcare and Elder Care: Neighborhoods that integrate childcare facilities, schools, and elder care services within walking distance minimize travel burdens, heavily benefiting female-headed households and working mothers. [2] 
* Healthcare and Daily Essentials: Easy access to grocery stores, basic medical offices, and public transit reduces reliance on cars and supports daily independence. [2, 6] 

## Financial Affordability and Housing Security

* Cost Burden: Low proportions of household income spent on rent or mortgages prevent severe financial strain, leaving resources for healthcare and nutrition. [1, 7] 
* Flexible and Stable Housing: Availability of affordable housing options reduces housing insecurity, which directly correlates with lower rates of anxiety and depression among women under 65 and lone parents. [1] 

## Social Environment and Community

* Social Cohesion: Active community centers, neighborhood groups, and inclusive social environments foster a strong sense of belonging and mutual support. [8, 9] 
* Property Management: Responsive maintenance and clean public spaces prevent neighborhood decay, litter, and broken windows that degrade overall well-being. [4, 9]
