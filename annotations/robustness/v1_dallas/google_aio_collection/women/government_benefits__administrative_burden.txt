Gendered divisions of labor, intersectional identities, and cognitive and psychological strains heavily influence the administrative burden women face when accessing government benefits. [1, 2, 3] 
## Key Factors Influencing Administrative Burden for Women

* Gendered Division of Labor: Women often shoulder a larger share of routine household management, caregiving, and health-related tasks, meaning government-required forms, appointments, and phone calls add directly to an existing unequal workload. [2] 
* Intersectional Social Location: A woman's experience with administrative burdens is shaped by overlapping factors like race, class, age, and ability, which can compound difficulties when navigating complex systems like public assistance, housing, and healthcare. [1] 
* Stigma and Psychological Costs: Marginalized or low-income women frequently encounter higher psychological tolls, including the stress, anxiety, and social stigma tied to claiming public safety net programs. [3, 4] 
* Strained Cognitive Resources: Women concentrated in low-wage jobs or juggling intensive caregiving responsibilities face restricted time and cognitive bandwidth, which amplifies the learning and compliance costs of sorting through convoluted paperwork. [3, 4, 5]
