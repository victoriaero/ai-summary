Multiple intersecting factors—including race, ethnicity, situational contexts, and underlying crisis circumstances—significantly influence police-initiated contact and subsequent outcomes for women in the criminal justice system. [1, 2] 
## Key Influencing Factors

* Race and Ethnicity Intersections: Research from organizations like the [Prison Policy Initiative](https://www.prisonpolicy.org/blog/2019/05/14/policingwomen/) shows that race and gender combine to affect policing outcomes differently. While overall police-initiated street stops are less frequent for women than men, racial disparities remain prominent during stops; Black and Latina women face higher rates of arrest or enforcement actions during stops compared to white women. [1, 3] 
* Underlying Crises and Pathways: According to assessments by the [Council on Criminal Justice](https://counciloncj.org/supporting-women-in-crisis/), police contact for women is frequently triggered by underlying crises such as domestic or intimate partner violence, behavioral health challenges, or homelessness. Although these crises initiate the environment or call for service, women are frequently subjected to enforcement or charges related to secondary factors like drug possession or theft. [2] 
* Marginalization and Immigration Status: Non-citizen and undocumented women face heightened barriers, including language difficulties and acute fear of deportation, which heavily influence how they interact with or avoid law enforcement. [4] 
* Perceptions of Legitimacy and Trust: A citizen's view of police legitimacy affects reporting and cooperative behavior. Studies noted in research on marginalized populations indicate that immediate safety needs often override general cynicism toward police, though past experiences of structural misconduct or perceived illegitimacy shape ongoing engagement. [5, 6]
