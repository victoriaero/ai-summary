Traffic stops, neighborhood demographics, and individual behavioral or social indicators heavily influence police-initiated contacts for non-Latino groups (such as Black and White individuals) in the criminal justice system. [1, 2, 3] 
## Key Factors Influencing Police-Initiated Contact

* Traffic Violations and Driving Context: According to data from the [Bureau of Justice Statistics](https://bjs.ojp.gov), traffic stops remain the single most common reason for police-initiated contact across racial groups, though Black drivers experience these stops at disproportionately higher rates than White drivers. [1] 
* Racial and Neighborhood Disparities: Research on adolescent and adult populations indicates that Black individuals are significantly more likely to experience police-initiated stops than White individuals, a disparity partially linked to heightened levels of school discipline, concentrated neighborhood disadvantage, and local policing strategies. [3] 
* Social and Environmental Background: Studies on early-adult and youth police contact show that individual risk factors—such as having a family member or parent with a prior arrest record, involvement in school behavioral infractions, or association with peers engaged in delinquency—increase the probability of proactive or officer-initiated intervention. [3] 
* Time and Visibility Factors: Large-scale analyses on police stop decisions reveal that stops for minority non-Latino drivers (particularly Black motorists) happen less frequently after dark when driver race is harder to distinguish, pointing to systemic visibility and potential perceptual bias in daytime officer-initiated stops. [4]
