Administrative burdens in government benefit programs like SNAP and Medicaid are primarily influenced by learning, compliance, and psychological costs that affect applicants across demographic lines. [1, 2] 
## Key Factors Influencing Administrative Burden

* Learning Costs: The complexity of finding out program rules, eligibility criteria, and application procedures. [1, 2, 3] 
* Compliance Costs: The time, effort, and documentation required to submit applications, complete strict eligibility checks, and maintain enrollment (such as managing enrollment churn and recurring paperwork). [1, 2, 4] 
* Psychological Costs: The stigma, stress, and negative messaging or a sense of diminished personal value communicated by navigating bureaucratic systems. [1, 2, 5] 
* Macro-Level and State Policies: Intentional or structural policy choices made by lawmakers—such as stricter verification requirements or automatic disenrollments—that shape the everyday friction of accessing safety-net resources. [4, 5] 

While studies (such as research from the [Center for Social Development at Washington University in St. Louis](https://openscholarship.wustl.edu/csd_research/979/)) indicate that White and non-Latino applicants often report experiencing high baseline levels of learning and compliance burdens in public benefits, the overall system mechanics remain driven by how easily rules can be navigated and how many procedural hurdles administrators put in place.
