Employment benefits for non-Latino workers are primarily shaped by structural, occupational, and individual factors that determine access to job-based coverage. [1] 
## 🏢 Firm Size and Industry

* Large corporations: More likely to offer comprehensive health and retirement packages.
* Small businesses: Frequently lack economies of scale to provide robust benefits.
* Sector concentration: Public and high-tech sectors offer higher benefit stability than low-wage service sectors. [1, 2] 

## ⏱️ Employment Status

* Full-time vs. part-time: Full-time schedules unlock standard eligibility for health insurance and 401(k) plans.
* Contingent labor: Temporary, gig, or independent contractor roles often exclude workers from statutory and employer benefits. [1, 3] 

## 📚 Human Capital and Education

* Educational attainment: Higher degrees correlate with professional roles offering rich fringe benefits.
* Skills and training: Specialized technical skills provide greater leverage in negotiating total compensation packages. [4] 

## 📍 Geographic and Regional Policy

* State labor laws: Local mandates on paid sick leave, family leave, and worker compensation vary widely.
* Local cost of living: Regional labor market competition influences baseline employer offerings to attract talent.
