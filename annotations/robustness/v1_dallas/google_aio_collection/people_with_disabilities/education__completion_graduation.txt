Several key factors influence educational completion and graduation for students with disabilities across K-12 and postsecondary levels, ranging from institutional support to personal self-advocacy.
Academic and Institutional Support
• Accommodations and Services: Access to specific accommodations—such as distraction-reduced testing environments, flexible assignment dates, and alternative format materials—significantly improves graduation odds.
• Mentorship and Relationships: Meaningful connections with faculty members or professional staff in Disability Services Offices strongly drive student persistence and success.
• Early Intervention Systems: Multi-Tiered Systems of Support (MTSS) that monitor attendance, behavior, and course performance help schools intervene before students disengage or drop out.
Student Factors and Engagement
• Self-Advocacy and Awareness: High personal insight into one's disability and strong self-advocacy skills empower students to secure and maintain necessary resources.
• Attendance and Participation: Consistent attendance—even as early as elementary and middle school—and active campus or school engagement heavily predict staying on track for graduation.
• Type of Disability: Research indicates varying completion likelihoods depending on the nature of the disability, with cognitive or mental health challenges often requiring more specialized and robust interventions compared to certain physical accommodations.
External and Systemic Influences
• Parental Involvement: Active caregiver and family engagement leads to better attitudes, higher grades, and increased rates of high school completion.
• Inclusive Environments: Proactive, inclusive school cultures that avoid disciplinary exclusion (like suspensions) and low teacher expectations foster higher achievement and graduation rates.
