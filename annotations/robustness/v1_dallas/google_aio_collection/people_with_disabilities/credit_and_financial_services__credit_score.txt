Credit scores for people with disabilities are calculated using the exact same standard financial factors as anyone else's, but individuals may face unique systemic and socio-economic hurdles. [1] 
## 📊 Core Credit Scoring Factors
Standard credit scoring models (like FICO and VantageScore) do not contain data regarding a person's medical status, disability, or receipt of public benefits. [2] 

* Payment History (35%): Timely payment of traditional credit lines, loans, and reported accounts. [3] 
* Credit Utilization (30%): The ratio of revolving credit used compared to total available limits (ideally under 30%). [3] 
* Length of Credit History (15%): The average age of open accounts and oldest active tradelines. [3] 
* Credit Mix & Inquiries (20%): Variety of credit types managed and recent hard applications for credit. [2, 3] 

## ⚠️ Disability-Specific Socio-Economic Hurdles
While the score calculation formula is neutral, systemic factors disproportionately affect credit health in the disability community: [4] 

* Income Constraints: Lower average employment rates can lead to "thin" credit files or an inability to qualify for traditional bank loans. [1, 5] 
* Unreported Payments: Essential recurring payments like rent and utilities historically bypassed major credit bureaus. [1] 
* Medical Debt Traps: Out-of-pocket healthcare or adaptive equipment costs can create unexpected collection accounts.
* Alternative Lending Reliance: Higher reliance on high-cost payday or pawn loans due to mainstream credit denials. [6, 7] 

## 💡 Protections and Building Strategies
Federal regulations safeguard applicants, and specific tools help establish positive credit profiles:

* Legal Protections: The Equal Credit Opportunity Act (ECOA) bans discrimination based on receiving Social Security Disability Income (SSDI) or Supplemental Security Income (SSI).
* Benefit Neutrality: Receiving SSI or SSDI does not directly lower or hurt a credit score.
* Credit Building: Utilizing secured credit cards, credit-builder loans, and services like Experian Boost to capture utility or SSDI-funded records. [2, 6, 8]
