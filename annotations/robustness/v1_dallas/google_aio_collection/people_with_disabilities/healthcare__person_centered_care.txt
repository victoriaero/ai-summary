Person-centered care for people with disabilities in healthcare is strongly influenced by communication practices, provider training, physical and administrative accessibility, and organizational culture.
Key Influencing Factors
• Communication and Interpersonal Relationships: Two-way, relational communication between patients, their caregivers, and the entire healthcare team—including administrative staff—improves care navigation. Conversely, fast-paced dialogue, complex medical jargon, and failure to communicate directly with the patient act as major barriers.
• Provider Knowledge and Training: Clinician familiarity with specific needs and diverse models of disability (such as social or functional models) fosters empathy and reduces biased assumptions. A lack of formal training often leads to provider hesitation, diagnostic overshadowing, or lower rates of routine preventive screening.
• Physical and Environmental Accessibility: The availability of accessible exam tables, clear physical layouts, adjustable lighting, and proper medical equipment dictates whether a patient can receive safe and dignified treatment.
• Administrative and Time Flexibility: Rigid scheduling and complex pre-visit paperwork hinder access. Facilitators include extended appointment times, alternative formats (large print, easy-read, visual aids), and flexible scheduling coordinated with support services.
• Organizational Leadership and Culture: Supportive clinic policies, interdisciplinary teamwork, and workplace cultures that prioritize patient empowerment and shared decision-making help operationalize person-centered planning.
