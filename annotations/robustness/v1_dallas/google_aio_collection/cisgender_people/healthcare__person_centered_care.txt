Person-centered care for cisgender people in healthcare is influenced by key interpersonal, systemic, and cultural factors that shape the quality, accessibility, and satisfaction of medical encounters.
## Key Factors Influencing Care

* Provider Communication: Effective listening, clear explanations, and empathetic interactions by healthcare professionals strongly drive patient satisfaction and trust.
* Interpersonal Professionalism: Respectful, competent, and dignified treatment by doctors, nurses, and administrative staff ensures patients feel valued and understood during visits.
* Socioeconomic Status and Insurance: Financial coverage, transparent consultation fees, and affordable access to services heavily dictate whether patients can consistently seek and receive continuous care. [1] 
* Gender and Demographic Preferences: Patient comfort is sometimes influenced by matching provider demographics (such as preferred gender for intimate or empathetic care), though foundational communication remains paramount. [1] 
* Intersectionality and Identity: For subgroups of cisgender individuals—such as sexual minority women—broader social determinants, inclusive clinical environments, and the avoidance of heteronormative biases shape how safe and patient-centered their care feels. [2]
