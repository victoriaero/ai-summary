Educational completion and graduation rates for cisgender individuals are primarily influenced by school belonging, teacher expectations, educational aspirations, and socioeconomic resources. [1, 2] 
## Key Factors Influencing Cisgender Completion and Graduation

* School Belonging: Feeling accepted and connected to the school community significantly increases academic achievement and persistence for all students, including heterosexual and cisgender populations. [1, 3] 
* Teacher Expectations: High academic expectations from educators act as a powerful catalyst for student success, boosting motivation and the likelihood of finishing courses or degrees. [1, 3] 
* Educational Aspirations: Personal and family expectations regarding future schooling serve as critical predictors for transitioning through and completing educational milestones, such as college entry and graduation. [2] 
* School Behavior and Engagement: Behavioral compliance and active classroom engagement heavily account for performance variations, particularly regarding the well-documented underperformance gaps among male students. [2] 
* Gender Disparities and Norms: Broader societal gender norms affect completion rates differently; data from organizations like the [National Center for Education Statistics](https://nces.ed.gov/) show that women currently outpace men in college enrollment and bachelor's degree completion rates.
