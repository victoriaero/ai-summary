Standard credit scores are legally gender-neutral, meaning cisgender status is never a direct factor; instead, identical core financial metrics determine every individual's score. [1] 
## ✅ Core Credit Scoring Factors
Traditional scoring models like FICO and VantageScore evaluate universal financial habits for all consumers.

* Payment history (35% of total score weight)
* Amounts owed and credit utilization (30% weight)
* Length of credit history (15% weight)
* Types of credit in use or credit mix (10% weight)
* New credit applications and inquiries (10% weight) [2, 3, 4, 5] 

## 📊 Indirect Socioeconomic Factors
While gender is excluded by law, secondary economic realities can indirectly shape credit attributes. [1, 6] 

* Wage and income differences alter revolving credit utilization [4] 
* Average debt loads vary across demographic data pools [4] 
* Community and regional banking access affect overall scoreability [7] 
* Proxy variables like age and marital status leak demographic data [6] 

## ⚠️ Legal Protections
Lenders are strictly barred from using gender or gender identity in credit decisions.

* The Equal Credit Opportunity Act prohibits sex-based discrimination [1] 
* Scoring models undergo regular testing for prohibited bias [8] 
* Credit limits and interest rates must rely solely on creditworthiness [8]
