Academic completion and graduation rates for White students are influenced by a combination of socioeconomic, academic, and family-level factors.
## Socioeconomic Status and Family Background

* Parental Education and Income: Higher levels of parental education and household wealth strongly correlate with higher rates of high school and college completion. Economic stability allows for better access to academic resources, tutoring, and stable housing markets linked to well-funded schools.
* Family Structure: Living with married birth parents during high school significantly increases the statistical odds and rates of high school and college graduation. [1, 2, 3] 

## Academic Preparation and K-12 Experience

* Prior Achievement: Controlling for high school grade point averages (GPAs) and standardized test scores explains a large share of academic success and degree completion tracking. [3] 
* Institutional Resources: White students more frequently attend well-resourced public four-year institutions and high schools with lower student-to-counselor ratios, greater college counseling availability, and advanced placement courses compared to many underrepresented minority peers. [4] 

## Gender Disparities

* The Completion Gender Gap: White women attain high school and college degrees at notably higher rates than White men. Research indicates that differing returns on education in the labor and marriage markets, alongside shifting behavioral and academic engagement trends, contribute to this gap. [3, 5, 6]
