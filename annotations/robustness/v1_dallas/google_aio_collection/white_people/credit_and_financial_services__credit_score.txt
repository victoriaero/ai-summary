Credit scoring models do not use race, gender, or income as direct inputs. They evaluate individual credit behavior. [2] 
## 📊 Standard Scoring Factors
The core components affecting these scores apply universally:

* Payment history: Timely debt repayment (35% weight). [5] 
* Amounts owed: Credit utilization ratio (30% weight). [5] 
* Credit length: Age of oldest and average accounts (15% weight). [5] 
* Credit mix: Variety of revolving and installment loans (10% weight). [5] 
* New credit: Recent inquiries and new accounts (10% weight). [5] 

## 💡 Contextual and Demographic Influences
While the formula is blind to race, broader financial advantages elevate average scores for White consumers: [2, 3] 

* Higher homeownership: Mortgages build positive installment credit history. [2, 6] 
* Generational wealth: Reduces reliance on high-cost emergency borrowing. [4, 7] 
* Lower credit invisibility: Only 9% lack traditional credit data. [1] 
* Prime access: Greater initial access to traditional bank loans. [8, 9]
