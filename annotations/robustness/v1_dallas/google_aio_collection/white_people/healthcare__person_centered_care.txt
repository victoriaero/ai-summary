Person-centered care (PCC) for White patients in healthcare is primarily influenced by general interpersonal, systemic, and socioeconomic factors that shape the standard patient-provider dynamic. [1, 2] 
## Core Influencing Factors

* Communication and Shared Decision-Making: Active listening, clear explanations from clinicians, and collaborative goal-setting directly shape the quality of person-centered interactions. [3, 4] 
* Socioeconomic Status and Health Literacy: Individual levels of education, health literacy, and financial stability influence a patient's ability to navigate health systems and engage in treatment plans. [3] 
* Insurance and Access: Type of health coverage and geographic proximity to primary or specialized care centers determine the continuity and personalization of services received. [5] 
* Provider Attitudes and System Alignment: General clinician empathy, adherence to the biopsychosocial model, and institutional focus on holistic, respectful treatment dictate how individualized the care experience feels. [1, 2] 
* Cultural and Individual Values: Personal preferences, family involvement in decision-making, and individual beliefs about health and wellness guide how care is tailored to the unique person. [1, 2]
