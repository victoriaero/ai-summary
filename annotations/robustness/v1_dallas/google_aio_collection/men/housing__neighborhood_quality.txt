Multiple core physical, social, and economic dimensions influence general residential and neighborhood quality, which heavily impact residents including men in housing arrangements. [1] 
## Safety and Security

* Crime and Violence: Low rates of local crime, vandalism, and gang activity are primary drivers of neighborhood satisfaction and personal safety. [2, 3] 
* Neighborhood Disorder: The absence of blight, graffiti, abandoned buildings, and high vacancy rates prevents feelings of vulnerability and stress. [4, 5] 

## Physical and Built Environment

* Housing and Structural Quality: Well-maintained housing free of physical inadequacies like mold, poor insulation, or plumbing faults safeguards physical and mental health. [4, 6] 
* Environmental Health: Low exposure to chronic noise pollution, poor air quality, and extreme heat reduces environmental stress and chronic health risks. [5, 7] 
* Urban Green Spaces: Access to clean parks, trees, and recreational waterways promotes mental restoration and physical activity. [1, 7] 

## Accessibility and Infrastructure

* Walkability and Mobility: Walkable streets, sidewalks, and active transportation options encourage physical movement and daily connectivity. [1, 8] 
* Proximity to Essentials: Convenient access to public transit, employment opportunities, healthcare facilities, and commercial amenities minimizes daily friction. [9, 10] 

## Socio-Economic and Social Capital

* Social Cohesion: Strong community trust, social support networks, and neighborly interactions act as protective factors against loneliness and anxiety. [1, 3, 5] 
* Economic Stability: Fair housing costs relative to income and stable local employment economics prevent severe cost burdens and financial strain. [1, 4]
