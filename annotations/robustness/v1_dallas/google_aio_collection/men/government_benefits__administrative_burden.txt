Administrative burden in government benefits is shaped by learning, compliance, and psychological costs, which interact with gender norms and personal resources. [1] 
## Core Cost Factors

* Learning Costs: The effort required to find out about programs, rules, and eligibility.
* Compliance Costs: The "time tax" of paperwork, documentation, travel, and appointments.
* Psychological Costs: The stress, frustration, and perceived stigma tied to seeking public assistance. [2, 3, 4] 

## Influencing Factors for Men

* Gender Norms and Stigma: Societal expectations around self-reliance and breadwinning can amplify the psychological costs and shame for men seeking welfare or safety-net benefits. [2, 3] 
* Program Design: Work requirements and frequent recertifications in programs like unemployment insurance or Medicaid impose heavier compliance demands. [5] 
* Cognitive and Resource Constraints: Financial insecurity, health issues, and limited digital or administrative literacy increase the difficulty of navigating complex bureaucratic systems. [6, 7]
